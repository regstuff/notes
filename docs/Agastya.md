# Agastya: The Great Sage

Sage Agastya, the son of Mitra and Varuna, was known across all the three worlds for his immense wisdom and virtue. One day, while wandering through a dense forest, he came upon a strange and disturbing sight: several men were hanging upside down over a deep pit. 
"Who could they be?" Agastya wondered. 
"We are your ancestors," the men replied sorrowfully. "We are hanging here like this because you are not yet married. Our souls will have no peace until we are assured of the continuance of our line. A son born of you can redeem us, and you too will escape this hell."

"O revered ones, I shall act for your redemption," Agastya promised. 
Determined to find a wife, Agastya searched long and hard across all three worlds but could not find the ideal woman. "I shall have to create her, taking in all that is most beautiful," he decided. Using his spiritual powers, Agastya created a beautiful baby girl. 

At that time, the King of Vidarbha was performing severe penances, praying for a child. Agastya arranged for his creation to be born to the king. The king and queen were overjoyed when they were blessed with a breathtakingly beautiful daughter, whom the Brahmans named Lopamudra. 

Lopamudra grew up into a virtuous and stunningly beautiful young woman. When the time was right, Agastya went to the King of Vidarbha and asked for her hand in marriage. The king was troubled, not wanting his delicate daughter to marry an ascetic, but he dared not displease the powerful sage. However, Lopamudra happily agreed, "Father, why do you hesitate? I am willing to marry the great sage."

After the wedding, Lopamudra cast off her costly royal robes and jewelry. "Henceforth I shall wear only bark and skin and rags, my lord," she declared, embracing the simple life of a sage's wife. They settled at a hermitage in Gangotri, where Lopamudra faithfully helped Agastya in his severe penances. 

One day, pleased with her devotion, Agastya asked if she had any desires. 
"Lord, I would like to live as I did in my father's house," she requested gently. 
Agreeing, Agastya set out to seek wealth from kings who could spare it. He visited King Srutarva, King Brihadastha, and King Trasadasyu. Although they offered him wealth, Agastya refused to take it when he realized it would empty their treasuries and deprive others. 

The three kings then suggested, "There is a wicked Asura called Ilvala who has a great deal of wealth. Let us go to him." 
Ilvala and his brother Vatapi hated Brahmans and used a dark trick to kill them. Vatapi would transform into a goat, which Ilvala would cook and feed to visiting Brahmans. Once eaten, Ilvala would call his brother's name, and Vatapi would tear through the guest's stomach, killing them. 

When Agastya and the kings arrived, Ilvala warmly welcomed them and served the special "goat" meat. Agastya, knowing their trick, ate the meal with relish. 
When he finished, Ilvala called out, "Vatapi, O Vatapi, come out!"
But Agastya simply let out a loud burp. "How can he come out?" Agastya laughed heartily. "I have already digested him!"

Terrified and defeated by the sage's power, Ilvala surrendered. Agastya demanded wealth for himself and the three kings. Ilvala silently planned to give them thousands of cows, gold coins, and his magnificent golden chariot. With his spiritual insight, Agastya immediately read the Asura's mind and claimed exactly what Ilvala had thought of. 
Agastya returned to Lopamudra with immense wealth, and they lived happily. A few years later, they were blessed with a son, fulfilling Agastya's promise to his ancestors. 

Sometime later, the mighty Vindhya mountain grew jealous of Mount Meru, whom the sun honored by revolving around it every day. When the sun refused to change its path for Vindhya, the angry mountain began to grow taller and taller, blocking the sun's path and threatening to plunge the earth into eternal darkness. 

The alarmed Devas (gods) approached Agastya for help. Agastya and Lopamudra traveled to the Vindhya mountain. 
"O great mountain, I wish to cross over you to the southern country," Agastya requested. "Wait till I return."
"O great sage, I will stop increasing my size till you return from the south," the mountain promised, bowing down to let the sage pass. 
Once across, Agastya told his wife, "Lopamudra, we will not return to the north. Then Vindhya will never be able to grow any taller." And so, they settled permanently in the south, saving the world from darkness.

Years later, a group of Asuras called the Kalkeyas, led by the terrible Vritra, began terrorizing the world. The Devas, led by Indra, obtained a powerful weapon called the *Vajra* (made from the bones of Sage Dadhichi) and attacked the Asuras. Indra successfully killed Vritra with the *Vajra*. 
Panic-stricken, the surviving Kalkeyas fled and hid deep at the bottom of the ocean. From there, they secretly emerged at night to murder good men and sages, slowly destroying the balance of the universe. 

The Devas searched everywhere but could not find the Kalkeyas. Lord Vishnu revealed their underwater hiding place and advised, "You must find some means to dry up the ocean. Who else can accomplish such a task but Agastya!"

Indra and the Devas rushed to Agastya, begging for his help. "Array yourselves for war while I drink the ocean," Agastya agreed calmly. 
Standing on the shore, the great sage cupped his hands and miraculously drank the entire ocean dry! 

With the ocean bed exposed, the hidden Kalkeyas were taken completely by surprise. Running helter-skelter without their watery shield, they were easily slaughtered by the well-prepared Devas. 

"My task is accomplished," Agastya smiled, watching the victory of the gods. "I may now go back to my penances. Peace be with you."
Thus, once more, the immensely powerful and wise Sage Agastya suppressed evil and protected the good in the three worlds.
