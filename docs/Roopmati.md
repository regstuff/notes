# Roopmati: The Tragic Story of a Rajput Princess

In the sixteenth century, Roopmati was the breathtakingly beautiful daughter of Thakur Thansingh, a proud Rathor Rajput who ruled the small principality of Dharampuri. Blessed with a voice as sweet as nectar, Roopmati excelled in classical singing and playing the *been* (a stringed musical instrument). 

One joyous spring day, while Roopmati and her friends were celebrating the spring festival by bathing in a beautiful forest pool, their sweet singing echoed through the dense woods. 

By sheer chance, **Baz Bahadur**, the young, romantic Sultan of Mandu, was hunting in the exact same forest. Captivated by the incredibly melodious voice, he followed the sound and quietly stepped out of the foliage. The moment he laid eyes on the beautiful Roopmati, and she on the handsome Sultan, they fell deeply, hopelessly in love. 

"So you are Baz Bahadur, the Sultan," Roopmati blushed. "You flatter well."

Returning to their respective palaces, both lovers were utterly consumed by thoughts of each other. At Mandu, Baz Bahadur completely neglected his royal duties, brooding silently in his apartments. At Dharampuri, Roopmati spent her entire days passionately singing melancholic love songs about the Sultan. 

Unfortunately, her strict, fiercely proud Rajput father, Thakur Thansingh, eventually overheard her singing. Realizing she was in love with a Muslim Sultan, the furious Thakur was absolutely livid. 

"Control your wrath," the royal priest tried to calm him. "She is just a child!"
"A child, yes—but one that threatens to bring terrible dishonor to our entire clan!" the Thakur roared relentlessly. "She will be punished. Prepare a strong draught of opium!"

Determined to brutally murder his own daughter to save his family's honor, the Thakur heavily imprisoned Roopmati in her room, fully intending to force her to drink the fatal poison that very night. 

Locked in her room, the terrified Roopmati sobbed herself into an uneasy slumber. Suddenly, a dazzling, radiant light pervaded the room. The River Goddess Rewa appeared before her. "Daughter, I know of your suffering," the Goddess spoke gently. "Fear not! Very soon a fresh spring shall burst forth in the city of Mandu... and there you shall be forever united with your lover."

Meanwhile, a deeply concerned Baz Bahadur had received urgent news of Roopmati's terrifying imprisonment. Refusing to lose his true love, the Sultan immediately marched his entire royal army toward Dharampuri. In a fierce, completely unexpected pre-dawn attack, the Mandu forces utterly routed the Rajput guards. 

Baz Bahadur triumphantly smashed open the heavy doors of Roopmati's chamber and finally freed his beloved. Proving his noble intentions, the Sultan did not conquer Dharampuri; he generously released the defeated Thakur and respectfully carried his beautiful bride away to Mandu with great royal regalia. 

The very next day, the royal couple joyously rode through the lush valley of the Rewa. Exactly as the Goddess had prophesied, a crystal-clear spring of pure water miraculously gushed forth from beneath a tamarisk tree. 

Overjoyed by the divine blessing, Baz Bahadur commissioned the immediate construction of a magnificent, serene palace for his new Queen on that exact, holy spot. Many ecstatic hours did the royal lovers while away on the high terraces of this beautiful palace, entirely lost in the supreme, heavenly delights of classical music and profound love. 

But their idyllic, peaceful paradise was tragically short-lived. 

Far away in Agra, the incredibly powerful Mughal Emperor, Akbar, had firmly consolidated his vast empire. Seeking to aggressively expand his borders, Akbar's hungry eyes fell upon the wealthy, peaceful kingdom of Mandu. He immediately dispatched his most ruthless general, Adham Khan, with a massive, unstoppable imperial army to conquer the kingdom. 

A great, incredibly bloody battle ensued on the borders of Mandu. Though he fought with the fierce, desperate courage of a true king defending his home and his love, Baz Bahadur's smaller army was utterly crushed by the massive Mughal forces. In the terrible, bloody slaughter, Baz Bahadur was tragically killed. 

The victorious, highly arrogant Adham Khan marched triumphantly into Mandu and arrogantly strode directly into Queen Roopmati's private chambers. Struck by her legendary beauty, the ruthless conqueror smirked. "Your loveliness is even greater than the poets have led me to believe," he declared. "Be my wife."

"Vile man! How dare you?" Roopmati spat fiercely, her eyes blazing with absolute, unyielding hatred. "You have brutally killed my husband!"
Laughing callously at her grief, Adham Khan left the chamber, highly confident that the helpless, captive queen would eventually surrender to his terrifying power. "I shall wait for the day when you are not as obstinate as now," he sneered. 

But the proud, utterly devoted Rajputni had already made her final, tragic decision. 

"Dress me up in my finest bridal attire," she quietly instructed her weeping maid. Dressed as a radiant bride, her broken heart filled entirely with beautiful reminiscences of Baz Bahadur, she sat down and played one final, incredibly plaintive, heartbreaking melody on her *been*. 

Late that night, when the palace had finally fallen silent, Queen Roopmati calmly poured herself a fatal dose of powdered diamond and swallowed it without a single moment of hesitation. 

The next morning, when the arrogant Adham Khan strutted exultantly into her royal bedchamber, fully expecting to claim his beautiful prize, he found Queen Roopmati lying peacefully on her royal bed, her face a picture of great, eternal serenity. She was dead. 

She had chosen to follow her beloved husband into the next world rather than submit to the cruel murderer who had destroyed their beautiful paradise. 

The tragic, immortal lovers were eventually buried side by side in a grand, beautiful tomb at Sarangpur. Even today, hundreds of people visit the sacred tomb of these legendary lovers, and it is said that in this magical, tragic place, when you call out the name *'Roopmati'* into the wind, the echo that gently answers back is *'Baz Bahadur'*.
