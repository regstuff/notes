# Sahasramalla: The Master Thief

In an ancient Indian city, there once lived a highly notorious, incredibly skilled thief named **Sahasramalla**. 

One dark night, while expertly breaking into a wealthy jeweller's shop through the roof, he accidentally woke the jeweller's son. The brave boy managed to grab Sahasramalla's leg as he dangled from the ceiling! Thinking incredibly fast, Sahasramalla violently smashed a heavy book onto the boy's head, forcing him to let go, and managed to narrowly escape back into the night. 

Shaken by the terrifying close call, Sahasramalla returned home to his mother. "It's all in the game, my son," she comforted him cheerfully. "You are incredibly talented. Let this experience teach you to make your plans more carefully in the future!"

Heartened by his mother's highly supportive words, Sahasramalla walked into a local barber's shop the very next morning. Dressed in fine clothes, he arrogantly demanded a full shave, haircut, and manicure. 
"This man is sure to pay me handsomely!" the greedy barber thought excitedly. 

But when the elaborate grooming was finally finished, Sahasramalla simply walked out. "Let your son come with me to collect the payment," he told the barber smoothly. 

Sahasramalla took the barber's young son directly to a high-end cloth merchant's shop at the other end of the city. He carefully selected the absolute costliest, most luxurious silks in the entire store. 
"I don't have the money with me right now," Sahasramalla told the merchant smoothly. "If you agree, I'll go home and bring the gold. My boy here will remain in your shop as a guarantee until I return." 
Seeing the innocent boy, the trusting merchant happily agreed. 

Sahasramalla casually walked out of the shop with the incredibly expensive cloth and went straight home to his delighted mother. 
Meanwhile, back at the shop, the merchant waited for hours. When the angry barber finally arrived looking for his son, both men suddenly realized they had been masterfully duped by a brilliant trickster! 

They immediately rushed to the royal palace and complained directly to the King. 

Furious that a common thief was operating so brazenly in his capital, the King offered a handsome reward. An arrogant dancing-girl and a boastful horse-trader both stepped forward, proudly claiming they could easily catch the thief. 

Hearing of their boasts through his mother, Sahasramalla decided to teach them a severe lesson. 

Dressing himself as a wealthy, traveling nobleman, Sahasramalla confidently strolled into the horse-trader's camp outside the city. "I am a fellow horse-trader," he lied smoothly. "I request your permission to spend the night in your camp." Flattered by the 'nobleman', the horse-trader happily agreed. 

"Before we sleep, let me take you to the house of a famous dancing-girl in the city," Sahasramalla suggested. 
When they arrived at the dancing-girl's lavish house, she warmly welcomed the two "rich men." Sahasramalla quickly pulled her aside. "My friend is extremely wealthy, but he is very tired," he whispered perfectly. "He wants to wash and rest first." 
The greedy dancing-girl eagerly gave the horse-trader her absolute best room. 

Once the horse-trader was resting, Sahasramalla approached the dancing-girl again. "I must urgently visit the King," he said, feigning great haste. "But in my hurry, I forgot to put on my royal ornaments. Would you lend me some of yours?" 
Hoping for a massive reward from the 'nobleman', she eagerly draped him in her most precious, expensive gold jewelry! 

Sparkling with stolen gold, Sahasramalla then walked straight into the horse-trader's room. "My lady wants to see you now," he smiled. "But I have a massive favor to ask. I urgently need a fast horse to take me to the palace!" 
"Take mine!" the horse-trader offered eagerly. "He is the absolute fastest in the country!"

Sahasramalla happily mounted the prized horse, dripping in the dancing-girl's gold, and simply rode away into the night! The very two people who had so arrogantly set out to catch the thief were themselves effortlessly caught in his brilliant, clever wiles. 

The next morning, the furious King summoned his Chief of Police. "This thief is becoming bolder and bolder!" the King roared. 
"I will easily produce him before you within five days, Your Majesty!" the Police Chief promised nervously. 

That very evening, Sahasramalla disguised himself as a wealthy, well-to-do citizen and strolled casually into a notorious gambling den heavily frequented by the Police Chief. Sitting down with the Chief, Sahasramalla deliberately challenged him to a game of high-stakes dice. Being a master gambler, Sahasramalla flawlessly won every single round, quickly winning all the Chief's money and even his official, heavy gold signet ring! 

"By the way," Sahasramalla casually mentioned to the utterly broke Chief, "the King wants to see you at the palace urgently." 

The moment the terrified Police Chief rushed off to the palace, Sahasramalla ran straight to the Chief's house and frantically banged on the door. 
"Madam, a terrible thing has happened!" he yelled to the Chief's wife. "Your husband has been officially arrested for treason! The King's men are coming right now to violently seize all his wealth! He sent me with his signet ring to tell you to quickly pack all your valuables into a large sack so I can securely hide them!"

Seeing her husband's official ring, the panicked wife hastily packed her entire life savings, gold, and jewels into a massive sack and handed it gratefully to the thief! 

When the helpless Police Chief returned home and discovered he had been utterly ruined by the very man he was supposed to catch, he collapsed at the King's feet in total, humiliating defeat. 

Realizing his entire police force was useless, the King himself angrily decided to take up the hunt. 

However, Sahasramalla was incredibly fearless. The very next day, he disguised himself as an expert, highly sought-after masseur and presented himself directly at the palace gates. "I have come to offer my soothing services to my sovereign," he bowed deeply. 

The exhausted, highly stressed King eagerly allowed the 'masseur' into his private royal bedchamber. Sahasramalla's incredibly skilled, genuinely expert hands quickly dispelled the King's weariness, gently lulling the monarch into a deep, heavy sleep. 

The absolute second the King began to snore, Sahasramalla quickly wrapped up the priceless Royal Crown, the King's personal jewelry, and his expensive royal robes. Tying them into a bundle, the master thief noiselessly slid down a long rope out of the high palace window and completely vanished into the night! 

When the King awoke hours later to find his crown and robes entirely missing, he was absolutely livid! "Guards! Guards! I have been robbed!" he screamed in fury. But Sahasramalla was already far, far away. 

"Son, you are truly the absolute greatest of thieves," his mother beamed proudly when he returned with the King's crown. "But now that you have literally robbed the King, I have absolutely no one else left to rob!" Sahasramalla laughed. "I have finally reached the end of the road."

Bored and looking for a new challenge, Sahasramalla went out for a stroll. He soon came across a massive, quiet gathering of people listening raptly to a serene, holy Jain monk named **Vasudda**. 

Curious, Sahasramalla slipped into the crowd. The monk was preaching a simple but profoundly powerful message. 
"Brothers, we should not hurt our fellow men by our selfish actions," the monk spoke gently. "If you strongly feel you have the right to the joys of life, so have others. Would you like it if someone violently stole your property? Then see to it that you absolutely do not steal someone else's property!"

The monk's incredibly gentle, deeply soothing words struck Sahasramalla like a massive thunderbolt. For the very first time in his entire life, the master thief genuinely grappled with his own conscience. He suddenly remembered the terrified face of the jeweller's son, the ruined dancing-girl, the broke police chief, and the deeply humiliated King. 

Overwhelmed with a sudden, massive flood of intense remorse, Sahasramalla fell weeping heavily at the holy monk's feet. He blurted out his entire, shameful story of crime and deceit. 

"If you have truly, genuinely repented, my son," the wise monk smiled compassionately, "then you must do exactly as I tell you."

The very next day, a massive crowd gathered outside the temple, and the furious King himself arrived with his heavily armed royal guards, seeking the notorious thief. 

To the absolute shock of the entire city, Sahasramalla calmly walked out of the temple, his hands folded in deep, sincere respect. He had brought back every single stolen item—the cloth, the ornaments, the horse, the police chief's valuables, and the Royal Crown! 

"I am completely ready to openly accept whatever harsh punishment you give me, Your Majesty," Sahasramalla bowed deeply, offering the King his crown. "I have brought back all the stolen goods to be completely restored to their rightful owners."

The King was utterly astounded by the incredibly dramatic transformation. "Well," the King smiled warmly, deeply moved by the genuine repentance, "since you have completely voluntarily given yourself up, and since the holy monk Vasudda personally intercedes for you... I completely pardon you, Sahasramalla! And I wish you profound happiness in your new life."

Sahasramalla, the absolute greatest master thief in the kingdom, completely abandoned his life of crime that very day. With his heart completely cleansed, he and his mother lived out the rest of their days in profound peace, absolute honesty, and deep, unwavering piety.
