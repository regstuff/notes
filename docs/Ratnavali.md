# Ratnavali

In the 7th century AD, Emperor Harsha wrote the famous Sanskrit play, *Ratnavali*. The story begins with a cunning political plot orchestrated by Yaugandharayana, the fiercely loyal and brilliant chief minister of King Udayana of Kaushambi. 

Yaugandharayana firmly believed that if King Udayana married Princess Ratnavali—the exceptionally beautiful daughter of King Vikramabahu of Simhala (Sri Lanka)—the king would undoubtedly become the supreme, undisputed emperor of the entire region. However, King Vikramabahu politely refused the royal proposal, not wanting to offend King Udayana's current, deeply beloved wife, Queen Vasavadatta. 

Determined to forcefully secure the alliance, Yaugandharayana secretly spread a completely false rumor that Queen Vasavadatta had tragically perished in a terrible fire at Lavanaka. Believing the heartbreaking rumor, the grieving King of Simhala finally agreed to the marriage and dispatched his daughter, Princess Ratnavali, to Kaushambi by sea. 

Tragically, a massive, violent storm struck her fleet, and Princess Ratnavali's ship was completely wrecked on the high seas. Fortunately, she miraculously clung to a broken wooden plank and was eventually rescued by a passing merchant ship bound for Kaushambi. 

The merchant safely delivered the rescued princess to Yaugandharayana. Recognizing her true royal identity by a precious, incredibly rare jeweled necklace she wore, the cunning minister decided to bide his time until the perfect political moment. He secretly entrusted the princess to the care of Queen Vasavadatta herself, falsely introducing her as a poor, shipwrecked maid named **Sagarika**. 

Unaware that 'Sagarika' was actually her own royal cousin, the kind-hearted Queen Vasavadatta made the beautiful girl her personal companion. 

One day, during the grand, joyous spring festival of Madana (the God of Love), Sagarika was secretly hiding behind an Ashoka tree in the Makaranda garden. From her hidden vantage point, she saw the handsome King Udayana approaching. She instantly fell deeply, hopelessly in love with him, suddenly realizing he was the very king she had originally been betrothed to! 

Heartbroken by her impossible situation, Sagarika secretly painted a beautiful, highly detailed portrait of the King from memory. Her playful, observant friend, Susangata, discovered the secret painting and mischievously painted a beautiful portrait of Sagarika right beside the King's image. 

Through a highly comical series of events involving a runaway talking bird named Medhavini, King Udayana and his loyal, bumbling court jester, Vasantaka, accidentally stumbled upon the hidden portrait in the garden. The King was absolutely fascinated by the breathtaking beauty of the maiden in the painting. When Sagarika and Susangata secretly overheard the King admiring the portrait, Sagarika's heart soared with desperate hope. 

The bumbling jester Vasantaka hatched a secret, highly daring plan: he would disguise Sagarika in Queen Vasavadatta's royal robes and arrange a secret, romantic rendezvous in the Madhavi bower that very evening! 

However, the Queen's sharp-eared maid, Kanchana Mala, secretly overheard the entire plot and immediately informed the furious Queen. 

That evening, a highly dramatic, incredibly tense comedy of errors unfolded at the bower. Queen Vasavadatta intentionally arrived at the bower completely disguised as Sagarika! Believing she was the beautiful maiden from the portrait, King Udayana began passionately confessing his deep love to her. When the furious Queen dramatically revealed her true identity, the utterly humiliated, deeply embarrassed King fell to his knees, desperately begging for her pardon. The deeply hurt Queen furiously stormed away. 

Moments later, the real Sagarika—desperately disguised in the Queen's royal robes—arrived at the bower. Deeply depressed and believing her love was utterly hopeless, she tried to tragically hang herself from a tree using a creeper vine! 

King Udayana, frantically returning to the bower and mistaking her for the furious Queen trying to commit suicide, rushed forward and desperately saved her. "Throw away that noose for my sake!" he begged tearfully, only to shockingly realize he was holding Sagarika! 

As the two lovers finally embraced, Queen Vasavadatta suddenly returned to the bower to forgive her husband—and caught them completely red-handed! 

Absolutely livid with uncontrollable jealousy and rage, the Queen ruthlessly ordered her royal guards to immediately arrest Sagarika and Vasantaka. Vasantaka was firmly bound, and Sagarika was violently dragged away and heavily locked in the dark dungeons. 

The next afternoon, while King Udayana was gloomily watching a spectacular performance by a famous traveling magician from Ujjayini in the royal courtyard, an urgent messenger announced the sudden arrival of Vasubhuti, the Chief Minister of Simhala. 

Before the King could even receive the visiting minister, absolute panic erupted! A terrifying, raging fire suddenly broke out in the inner royal apartments where Sagarika was heavily imprisoned. 

"My Lord! Save Sagarika!" the panicked Queen cried out, her sudden jealousy completely replaced by genuine, terrifying remorse. "Her hands and feet have been bound on my strict orders!" 

Without a single second of hesitation, the heroic King Udayana fearlessly dashed straight into the raging, roaring inferno. He violently kicked open the heavy dungeon doors and desperately freed the heavily bound Sagarika. 

However, as they emerged from the blazing rooms, the intense fire magically vanished into thin air! It had all been a breathtakingly realistic, terrifying optical illusion brilliantly conjured by the traveling magician! 

As the King and Sagarika stood safely in the courtyard, Vasubhuti, the visiting minister from Simhala, stared at the young maiden in absolute, utter disbelief. "Princess Ratnavali!" he gasped in shock. 

The brilliant, cunning Yaugandharayana finally stepped forward and proudly revealed his entire, elaborate master plan to the stunned royal court. 

Realizing that 'Sagarika' was actually her own royal cousin, Princess Ratnavali, the deeply moved and highly apologetic Queen Vasavadatta warmly embraced her. With the Queen's joyful, wholehearted blessing, King Udayana happily married Princess Ratnavali, thereby successfully fulfilling Yaugandharayana's brilliant political prophecy and becoming the undisputed, supreme Emperor of the entire land.
