# Monkey Tales (Jataka Stories)

The Jataka tales are ancient Buddhist stories that narrate the previous lives of the Bodhisattva (the Buddha) in both human and animal forms. In these particular tales, the Bodhisattva is born as a wise and noble monkey.

### The Monkey King's Sacrifice (Maha Kapi Jataka)

Once upon a time, a large tribe of monkeys lived happily on a massive, fruit-bearing mango tree on the banks of a river. Their leader, the Monkey King (the Bodhisattva), was incredibly wise and deeply cared for his subjects. 

One day, the Monkey King noticed that a group of humans had come to live near the forest. Anticipating grave danger, he called a meeting. "Men have never tasted this delicious mango fruit," he warned his tribe. "If they do, it will be incredibly dangerous for us. Make absolutely sure that not a single fruit falls into the river!"

The monkeys carefully plucked every bud from the branches that hung over the water. But despite their best efforts, one juicy, ripe mango slipped through the leaves, fell into the river, and was eventually caught in a local fisherman's net. 

The fisherman took the strange, delicious-looking fruit to the King of the land. When the King tasted it, he was absolutely delighted and demanded to know where it came from. Guided by a local forester, the King and his royal archers sailed up the river until they found the massive mango tree swarming with monkeys. 

Greedy for the fruit, the King callously ordered his archers, "When it is morning, shoot every single monkey. Tomorrow, along with the mangoes, we shall eat monkey flesh!"

Trapped and terrified, the monkeys turned to their King. "Don't panic," the Monkey King reassured them. "I will find a way out."

He grabbed a long, thick creeper vine, tied one end securely to a sturdy branch of the mango tree, and tied the other end around his own waist. With a mighty leap, he swung across the roaring river toward a large fig tree on the opposite bank. However, he miscalculated the distance—the vine was slightly too short! 

Without hesitating, the heroic Monkey King grabbed a branch of the fig tree with both hands, using his own stretched body to bridge the terrifying gap over the roaring river. "Cross over my back!" he ordered his tribe. 

One by one, the monkeys safely scurried across his back to freedom. However, the very last monkey in the tribe was a wicked, deeply jealous creature who hated the King. Seeing his chance for cruel revenge, the wicked monkey jumped heavily and ruthlessly onto the exhausted King's back with all his might. The tremendous impact snapped the vine, and the noble Monkey King plummeted down, crashing heavily onto a rock and breaking his back. 

The human King, who had been watching the incredible escape from his boat, was deeply moved by the Monkey King's supreme, selfless sacrifice. He immediately ordered his men to rescue the dying monkey. 

"You saved them at the cost of your own life!" the human King marveled gently. 
"I don't mind the pain," the dying Monkey King smiled peacefully. "I am happy that my subjects are safe." With those noble words, the great Monkey King breathed his last, leaving a profound lesson of selfless leadership.

### The Stupid Crocodile and the Monkey (Vanarinda Jataka)

In a dense jungle, near a wide river, lived a clever little monkey. In the middle of the river was a small, lush island filled with delicious fruit trees. Every morning, the monkey would jump from the riverbank onto a large rock protruding from the water, and from there, leap onto the island to enjoy his breakfast. 

Not far away lived a crocodile and his wife. The crocodile's wife had a wicked craving. "I am longing to eat that plump monkey's heart!" she told her husband. 

The crocodile hatched a cunning plan. "When the monkey is on the island eating his breakfast, I will lie perfectly still on that rock," he thought. "When he returns, he will mistake me for the rock. As soon as he jumps on my back, I'll kill him!"

The next evening, as the monkey prepared to jump back from the island, he noticed something strange. *That rock looks much bigger today,* the clever monkey thought suspiciously. *I'm sure it's that stupid crocodile up to his tricks again!*

Deciding to test his theory, the monkey called out loudly, "Hello, Rock! How are you today, my friend? Why aren't you speaking to me today? Have I annoyed you?"

The foolish crocodile, thinking the rock usually spoke to the monkey, quickly answered in a deep, gravelly voice, "What is it, monkey? I'm not a rock, I'm a crocodile, and I'm going to kill you for my dinner!"

The monkey knew he had to think incredibly fast. He remembered a crucial fact: whenever crocodiles open their massive jaws wide, their eyes automatically close tightly. 

"I'll tell you what, Crocodile," the monkey yelled back. "You open your mouth wide, and I'll jump right into it from here!"

"Good!" thought the dim-witted crocodile. "This is going to save me a lot of trouble." He opened his massive, terrifying jaws as wide as he could, which completely squeezed his eyes shut. 

Seizing his perfect chance, the agile monkey leaped through the air, landed directly on the top of the blind crocodile's snout, and used it as a springboard to bounce safely onto the riverbank! 

"You never will get my teeth into you!" the monkey laughed triumphantly from the safety of the trees. "You may be big and strong, Crocodile, but you are incredibly stupid!"

### The Demon Outwitted (Nalapana Jataka)

Long ago, a band of monkeys migrated to a new, unfamiliar forest. Their exceptionally wise leader warned them strictly, "There are certain poisonous trees here, and one of the beautiful lakes is haunted by a terrible, man-eating demon. You must not drink water or eat any fruit without asking me first!"

One hot day, while searching for firewood, the monkeys wandered deep into the forest and became intensely thirsty. They soon found a beautiful, sparkling lake. Remembering their leader's strict warning, they patiently waited on the banks. 

When the leader finally arrived, he carefully examined the muddy banks of the lake. "Look closely," he pointed out. "All the footsteps lead *into* the lake, but not a single one leads *out* of it! This lake is definitely haunted."

Suddenly, the water violently churned, and a terrifying, monstrous demon rose from the depths. "What are you doing near my lake?" the demon roared. "I eat all those who dare enter my waters!"

"Oh no! We will die of thirst!" the monkeys cried in despair. 
"Wait and see," the wise leader smiled confidently. "We will drink the water, but we will not enter the lake."

The leader instructed his tribe to collect dozens of hollow bamboo reeds growing nearby. By cleverly joining them together end-to-end, he fashioned a remarkably long, continuous hollow pipe. He slid one end far out into the clear water of the lake. Taking the other end, he sucked with all his incredible might. The water rushed smoothly up the long bamboo pipe! 

Using this brilliant, simple invention, all the monkeys safely quenched their terrible thirst from the riverbank, completely outwitting the furious, helpless demon who stomped back into the lake in absolute defeat.

### The Monkey's Heart (Sumsumara Jataka)

There once lived a clever monkey on the bank of a wide river. A little downstream lived a crocodile and his wife. The crocodile's wife, seeing the plump monkey jumping in the trees, desperately wanted to eat his heart. 

"Use your cunning," the wife nagged the crocodile. 
The next day, the crocodile swam up to the monkey. "Hello, Monkey! Aren't you sick of eating the fruit of the same tree every day? Why don't you go across to the opposite bank? There are incredibly delicious fruits there. You can ride on my back!"

The monkey, tempted by the thought of new fruit, foolishly agreed and hopped onto the crocodile's scaly back. 

When they reached the deepest, most treacherous part of the river, the crocodile suddenly began to dive underwater. 
"Hey! Are you trying to drown me?" the terrified monkey coughed, struggling to stay afloat. 
"Not at all," the crocodile sneered cruelly. "For I'm going to kill you. My wife wants to eat your heart!"

The monkey realized he was in mortal danger and had to think incredibly fast. 
"Ha! Ha! Ha!" the monkey suddenly burst into loud, uncontrollable laughter. "The joke's on you, Crocodile! You may kill me, but you will never get my heart!"

"What do you mean?" the confused crocodile stopped diving. 
"We monkeys hide our precious hearts in safe places before we venture out!" the monkey lied smoothly. "If my heart were removed from my body, I'd be dead! It's safe inside that tall, big tree on the bank where I live. Take me back, and I'll gladly climb up and give it to you."

The gullible crocodile, not wanting a heartless monkey, quickly turned around and swam back to the riverbank. The absolute moment they touched the shore, the agile monkey leaped from the crocodile's back and scrambled high up into the safety of his tree. 

"You poor fool!" the monkey laughed down at the furious crocodile. "If my heart were really removed from my body, I'd be dead! It's perfectly safe inside my chest, and you will never lay your clumsy hands on it!"

### The Monkeys and the Gardener (Armadusaka Jataka)

The King of Varanasi had a magnificent, sprawling royal garden, meticulously looked after by a highly loyal, hardworking gardener. One evening, the gardener heard that a grand, exciting week-long festival was starting in the town the very next day. He desperately wanted to go and enjoy the fun, but he couldn't leave the delicate plants unwatered. 

Looking around, the gardener spotted the leader of a friendly local monkey tribe. "Dear Monkey," the gardener pleaded, "I want to go to the festival. Will you and your friends water the garden for me while I am away?"
"Pooh! That's no problem," the monkey leader promised confidently. "We will happily water the garden for you."

The next morning, the monkeys enthusiastically gathered the watering cans and buckets. "Come on, my monkeys, we have very important work to do!" the leader announced proudly. 
"How much water should we give to each tree?" a younger monkey asked. 

The leader stroked his chin thoughtfully. "That's a very good question. We must be highly scientific! Some trees are small and some are big. All you have to do is pull up each plant completely from the soil and have a close look at the size of its roots. You pour less water for the smaller roots, and more water for the bigger roots!"

"You are really clever! What a wonderful idea!" the monkeys cheered in agreement. 

And so, with absolute enthusiasm and terrible, misguided dedication, the foolish monkeys spent the entire day systematically uprooting every single beautiful plant, flower, and sapling in the magnificent royal garden just to check the size of their roots before watering them! 

When the horrified gardener returned two days later, he found his beloved, meticulous garden completely destroyed, resembling a devastated wasteland. 
"What have you done?!" he cried in absolute despair. 
"We watered each plant exactly according to the specific needs of its roots!" the monkeys replied proudly. 

The weeping gardener sank to his knees amidst the withered, dead plants. "Whatever shall I tell the King?" he sobbed. "That's exactly what comes of depending on a fool. He may mean incredibly well, but he will inevitably end up doing far more harm than anything else."
