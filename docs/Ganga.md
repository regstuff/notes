# Ganga: The Divine Beauty

Beautiful, pure, strong-willed Ganga was the elder daughter of Mount Himavan. She had the power of purifying anything that touched her. 
One day, the Devas, led by Brahma, came to Mount Himavan. "Heaven needs the presence of young Ganga, sanctifier of all creatures," they said. 
"If Ganga goes, the earth will suffer. But it is my duty to look to the good of all the three worlds. I am willing to send Ganga," Himavan agreed. "Go with the Devas, Ganga, and serve them dutifully." And so, Ganga flowed in heaven. 

At about that time, life on earth was becoming impossible for all righteous people. Their sacrifices were ruined, their crops destroyed in a single night, and even their babes were murdered. Their mysterious tormentors were a group of Asuras who hid in the ocean by day and came out to harass them at night. The helpless people had to hide in caves. 

In heaven, the Devas were perturbed by the plight of the people. "We must help the good people on earth. But how can we, when we don't know who their tormentors are?" 
Brahma suggested they seek the help of Vishnu. 
"Lord, how can we destroy the mysterious enemies of the good men on earth? Where do they hide by day?" the Devas asked Vishnu. 
"In the ocean bed. To kill them you will have to dry up the ocean. Go to Sage Agastya. He alone can help you," Vishnu advised. 

When Sage Agastya heard the story, he agreed. "For the good of the world I will come with you and drink up the ocean." 
When they reached the ocean, Agastya bent down and began to drink. In a single gulp, he drank up the entire ocean! The evil Asuras were exposed on the dry ocean bed, and the Devas immediately fell upon them and vanquished them. 

"O Saviour. Now that the evil ones are dead, fill up the ocean again with the water you drank," the Devas requested Agastya. 
"I cannot. I have digested it. You will have to find some other means to fill the ocean," the sage replied. 
The Devas went to Brahma, who said, "A very long time must pass before the ocean will once again be full." 
Years passed, and the ocean remained dry. The Devas went to Vishnu again. "Do not worry," Vishnu assured them. "King Sagara and his descendants will cause the ocean to be filled up again. He has already begun penances to obtain sons."

At that moment on earth, Lord Shiva appeared before King Sagara and his queens, Keshini and Sumati. "I am pleased with your piety. You shall have many sons. One of your wives shall bear 60,000 proud and heroic sons who will all perish without heirs. Your race however will be continued by the one son that your other wife will bear."

Accordingly, Sumati gave birth to 60,000 strong and brave sons, while Keshini bore a son named Asamanja. However, Asamanja grew up to be a wicked prince who drowned children, and King Sagara was forced to banish him from the kingdom. But Asamanja's son, Amsuman, was a very kind and considerate boy loved by the people. 

Once, King Sagara was performing the Ashwamedha sacrifice. Indra, fearful of the king's growing power, stole the sacrificial horse. When the horse was found missing, Sagara turned to his 60,000 sons. "If we can't complete the sacrifice, we are all doomed. Go and find the horse."
The 60,000 princes searched the whole earth but could not find the horse. "There is only one place left to search - the nether world," they concluded. They began digging a massive tunnel through the earth. The panic-stricken inhabitants of the earth ran hither and thither. When they reached the nether world, their digging disturbed one of the eight elephants holding up the earth, causing a tremendous earthquake. 

Oblivious of the harm they had done, the sons of Sagara moved on and came to where Sage Kapila sat meditating. The stolen horse was grazing nearby. 
"There stands our horse. So this sage is the culprit. Thief! Return our horse!" the enraged princes shouted, preparing to attack him. 
"How dare you call me a thief!" Kapila's eyes opened in fury. Angered by their audacity, the powerful Kapila burned the 60,000 princes to ashes by uttering but one syllable. 

When the princes did not return, Sagara sent his beloved grandson, Amsuman, to find them. Amsuman followed their path into the nether world crater. He saluted the elephant and eventually reached Sage Kapila. 
"Have you seen my uncles?" Amsuman asked respectfully. 
"Yes. They were so insolent that I consumed them with the fire of my penances," Kapila replied. 
Amsuman let out a cry of anguish upon seeing the mountain of ashes. "Is there no way for them to enter heaven?"
"There is. Ganga in all her purity must be brought down from heaven. If she touches these ashes, your uncles will go to heaven," the sage revealed. "You are patient, truthful and righteous... you may take the sacrificial horse back with you."

Amsuman returned with the horse, and the sacrifice was completed. "I will not be able to accomplish such a feat in my lifetime," Sagara told Amsuman. "You must become king after me and strive to bring Ganga to earth."
When Sagara died, Amsuman became king. Later, he passed the throne to his son Dilipa and retired to the Himalayas to perform austerities, but he died without succeeding. Dilipa also spent his life trying to bring Ganga down, but he too failed. 
On his deathbed, Dilipa's son, Bhagiratha, made a vow. "Father, I will not ascend the throne without first bringing Ganga down to earth."

Bhagiratha entrusted the kingdom to his ministers and went to the forest to undergo the most severe penances. After years of meditation, Brahma appeared before him. "Ask for a boon, O steadfast one."
"Then let Ganga flow down to earth from the heaven and deliver the souls of my ancestors," Bhagiratha requested. 
"Your aim is a lofty one. But the earth cannot sustain the force of her fall. Only Shiva can withstand it," Brahma advised. 

For a year, Bhagiratha performed penances to please Shiva. Shiva appeared and agreed to receive the force of Ganga as she descended. 
In heaven, Ganga made ready to descend. But she was proud and thought, "The mighty one is off his guard. I shall sweep him away with me to the nether world."
Shiva divined her intentions. As Ganga fell with tremendous force, Shiva simply imprisoned her in his matted locks. She swirled about in her prison for many a year, unable to escape. 

Bhagiratha once again adored Shiva. "Lord! Have pity on me and release Ganga."
"Your devotion merits it," Shiva smiled. He released Ganga, who had become even more purified by her contact with the Lord's sacred head. As she flowed onto the earth, people ran to her to cleanse themselves of all sins. 

Bhagiratha led Ganga towards the ocean. On the way, she flowed near the hermitage of Sage Jahnu, who was performing a sacrifice. The irrepressible Ganga rushed forward, flooding the sacrificial fire and destroying the sacrifice. 
The sage was furious. "You shall be punished for this!" And the fuming sage consumed the entire river in one long gulp. 
Bhagiratha and the Devas pleaded with the sage. "Forgive her, holy one, and release her. Let not the endeavours of Bhagiratha be wasted."
Out of respect for Bhagiratha's penances, Jahnu released Ganga from his ear. "Henceforth, Ganga shall also be known as Jahnavi - your daughter, O holy one," the Devas declared. 

At last, Bhagiratha and Ganga reached the dry ocean bed and the nether world beyond. Ganga flowed over the ashes of the 60,000 sons of Sagara, redeeming their souls and sending them to heaven. As the waters filled the vast crater dug by Sagara's sons, the ocean was formed again. From that day onwards, the ocean came to be known as 'Sagara'. 

His promise fulfilled, Brahma appeared before Bhagiratha. "The sons of Sagara are in heaven and will remain there as long as Ganga flows on the earth. Since you brought Ganga to earth by your penances, she shall be known as Bhagirathi - your daughter."
Having fulfilled his vow, Bhagiratha returned to his kingdom, ascended the throne, and ruled wisely for many years.
