# Jataka Tales: Bird Stories

## 1. The Value of Friends

Long ago, on the shore of a large lake, there lived a hawk. One day, a she-hawk came to live on the opposite shore. When the hawk saw her, he flew over and asked, "Will you be my wife? Together we could raise a fine family."
"All right," she replied. "But tell me, do you have any friends?"
"No," the hawk admitted.
"Then you must make some friends," she advised. "In times of need, it is friends who help."
"I don't need friends. But I'll do as you say. Who shall we start with?"
"Our neighbors, of course! Go and call on the lion, the osprey, and the tortoise!"

The hawk agreed. He flew to the tiny island in the middle of the lake and befriended the tortoise. He then made friends with the osprey, who lived nearby, and finally called on the lion, who promised that no one would harm him now. 
Returning to the she-hawk, he announced, "I have three friends now."
Satisfied, they decided to make their home on the Kadamba tree on the island where the tortoise lived. Soon, two little fledglings were born to them. "This is the safest place we could find—far from the haunts of men," the she-hawk smiled. 

But the place was not as safe as they imagined. One day, two hunters who had caught nothing all day decided to swim across to the island to spend the night, hoping to catch a fish or a young tortoise at dawn. 
Plagued by mosquitoes, the hunters lit a fire under the Kadamba tree. The smoke made the fledglings cough and cheep. 
"Did you hear that? Fledglings! On this tree!" one hunter exclaimed, plotting to eat them. 

The parent hawks were terrified. "Friend tortoise is asleep. Quick, my dear, fly to the osprey and seek his help!" the she-hawk urged. 
The hawk flew to the osprey. "Have they climbed the tree yet?" the osprey asked. 
"Not yet. They're busy lighting a fire."
"Fly back and comfort her. I'll take care of the hunters." 

Under the cover of night, the osprey flew to the tree. When the hunters' fire blazed, the osprey suddenly dived into the lake, soaked himself, and shook his wet wings over the flames, putting out the fire! 
The hunters lit another fire, but the osprey put it out again. "It's no use catching the fledglings till the fire is ablaze again," a frustrated hunter grumbled. 

This went on till midnight. The she-hawk, feeling sorry for the exhausted osprey, sent her husband to ask the tortoise for help. 
The tortoise immediately crawled out of the lake towards the hunters. "Hey, look! A huge tortoise!" the hunters yelled, forgetting the birds. They greedily stripped off their waistbands and bound the tortoise to themselves to drag it away. 
But the massive tortoise was too strong! Instead of them dragging him, he dragged them straight into the deep lake! 

The hunters quickly loosened their cloth strips, thrashing in the water. "The fellow is dangerous! Let's leave him here and make another fire," they gasped, shivering as they climbed back ashore. They began collecting twigs again. 
"They're back! Go to the lion and seek his help," the she-hawk cried. 

Just as the hunters got their fire ready, they heard a terrifying roar. A massive lion charged at them! 
"Ee-a-ah! A lion!" the hunters screamed in pure terror. They ran for their lives, jumping back into the lake and swimming away as fast as they could. 

"Ah! My little ones, you are safe!" the she-hawk sighed in relief, turning to her husband. "Now do you see the value of having friends?"

## 2. Greed Does Not Pay

There once was a Brahmana who lived with his wife and three daughters. He loved them dearly and took good care of them. Then, suddenly, one day he died, leaving his family in dire poverty. Kind neighbors helped them so they wouldn't starve. 

Meanwhile, the Brahmana was reborn as a magnificent golden swan. One day, remembering his past life, he decided to check on his family. Flying to his old village, he saw his wife and daughters living on charity. "How degrading! I must do something to help them," the golden swan thought. 

He flew down to them. "I am your husband, reborn as a bird," he explained. "I can't bear to see your plight. I'll give you one of my golden feathers. Sell it and use the money to buy food and clothing." He plucked out a golden feather, gave it to his wife, and flew away. 
Week after week, he returned to give her another golden feather, and the widow soon became rich. 

But, alas! Like most rich people, she became greedy. "Suppose he stops coming? A mere bird is not to be trusted," she plotted. "The next time he comes, I'll pluck out all his feathers." Her daughters protested, but her mind was made up. 

The next time the golden swan landed, the widow grabbed him by the neck. Despite his struggling and pleas, she ruthlessly plucked him clean! "There! Now we'll never be poor again!" she gloated. 
But to her horror, she looked down and saw only ordinary white feathers in her hands! "I've been cheated! H-how did it happen?" she gasped. 

"I'll tell you how," the shivering, featherless swan said. "My golden feathers turned white because you plucked them against my will. I forgot to warn you never to do that."
"You forgot? How could you be so careless! We are ruined!" the angry widow screamed. She callously threw the featherless bird into the dustbin. 

Within months, the swan's feathers grew back, but they were entirely white. "What's the use of staying on here? I'd better go away," the swan thought, and flew away forever. As for the greedy widow, she soon spent all her money and fell back upon bad days, exactly as she deserved.

## 3. The Loyal General

Long ago in Varanasi, there lived a King Crow who had a mate he loved dearly. One day, as they flew past the palace of the King of Varanasi, the Queen Crow saw a royal feast being prepared. Her mouth watered. 
The next day, she refused to eat. "I want the food I saw in the palace on the king's table. If I can't have it, I'll die," she demanded stubbornly. 

The King Crow was worried, but his loyal General reassured him. "Oh! Is that all? Don't worry. I'll see that our queen has the food she craves for."
Taking eight of the best crows with him, the General flew to the palace kitchen roof. He instructed his team: "While the food is being taken to the king, I'll make the cook drop the dishes. Four of you must then fill your beaks with rice, and four with fish, and fly to our queen."

When the cook walked out carrying the royal dishes, the General swooped down, violently flapping and pecking at the cook's face. "Aieeee! Drop those dishes and catch that wicked crow!" the king shouted from his balcony. 
As the cook dropped the trays, the eight crows swooped in, filled their beaks, and flew away. 

The General, however, was caught by the guards and brought before the King of Varanasi. "O, crow! You have dared to displease me. And in this foolish venture you've risked your very life! Why did you do it?" the king asked. 
"For my king," the General replied proudly. "He told me about the queen's longing for the food she saw on your table. I promised to get it for her—and I have kept my promise."

The King of Varanasi was amazed. "The bird was willing to sacrifice his life for his king! Such loyalty is rare! He must be rewarded," the king declared. He ordered the General to be set free. "Henceforth, a portion of the food cooked for me shall be sent every day to you and your king and queen. And a large measure of rice shall be cooked for your king's following."

## 4. Practise What You Preach

Once, the King of the Birds took his flock to the Himalayas in search of food. "Now go and look for seeds and grain. When you find some, report to me, so we can all share it," he commanded. 

As they wandered off, one greedy bird found a highway where wagons loaded with grain frequently passed. Grain spilled everywhere! "What a feast! I won't tell the king about this find!" she thought. "But what if the others find it? I know! I'll tell them about it and yet keep them away!"

She flew back to the flock. "I very nearly lost my life! I happened to fly over the highway! Elephants, horses, and wagons drawn by fierce bullocks go along that route. There is grain, but once you alight it's difficult to soar up again. Don't go that way. It's dangerous."
Her trick worked; the other birds carefully avoided the highway. "That was clever of me! I have all this grain to myself now," she chuckled. 

One day, while greedily pecking grain on the highway, she saw a cart approaching from afar. "It's a long way off. There's enough time to peck a few more seeds," she thought greedily. 
What she didn't know was that it was a fast-moving express cart! Before she could even take wing, the heavy cart ran straight over her, killing her instantly. 

That evening, the King of the Birds noticed she was missing and sent a search party. They found her crushed body on the highway. 
"What a sad fate!" the King sighed to the flock. "She warned you not to go near the highway. But she could not control her own greed. Let this be a lesson to all of you!"

## 5. The Greedy Crow

A pigeon once made its home in a basket hanging in the kitchen of a rich merchant of Varanasi. One day, a greedy crow flew past and saw the cook preparing fresh fish. "I must find my way into this kitchen and make it my home," the crow plotted. 

Seeing the pigeon, the crow decided to pretend to be its friend. He followed the pigeon everywhere, claiming he just wanted company. "That doesn't matter," the crow insisted when the pigeon pointed out they ate different food. "While you peck grass seeds, I'll look for worms."
That evening, the merchant saw the crow follow the pigeon inside and kindly hung up a second basket for the new "friend."

The next evening, the merchant ordered his cook to prepare a large feast of fried fish for a banquet the following day. The crow's mouth watered. 
The next morning, when the pigeon called him to forage, the crow groaned, "I'm not coming today. I have a terrible stomachache."
The pigeon knew better. "It's the fish, isn't it? Take my advice. It is dangerous to touch men's food. Come, let's go." But the crow stubbornly refused. 

Once the pigeon flew out, the cook began frying the fish. When it was done, he covered the dishes and stepped out to rest. Seeing his chance, the greedy crow swooped down to steal a large piece of fried fish. But as he grabbed it, he clumsily knocked the heavy lid over with a loud *CLANG!*

The cook rushed back in and caught the thief red-handed. "Is this how you repay me for sheltering you?" the furious cook yelled. He grabbed the crow, plucked him entirely bald, and soaked him in a stinging mixture of sour buttermilk and burning spices! 
When the pigeon returned that evening and saw the bald, burning, groaning crow, he shook his head. "Alas, my greedy friend, if only you had listened to me!"

## 6. The Bulbul and the Hornbill

The Hornbill was once the king of the birds. However, he was a cruel tyrant who killed smaller birds for the slightest mistakes. The flock secretly met and decided they needed a new king. They chose the gentle Bulbul, who had a regal appearance and wouldn't hurt anyone. 

But how could they break the news to the fearsome Hornbill? 
The clever Owl came up with a plan and enlisted the help of the Woodpecker. The Owl then went to the Hornbill. "O king, we feel that you should undergo a test and prove your worth. You will have to sit on a thick branch and break it. If you don't succeed, whoever does shall be deemed worthier of ruling us."

The Hornbill scoffed arrogantly. "If I cannot break the branch, who can?" The Owl pointed out a specific, thick branch. The Hornbill flew up and landed on it with all his considerable might, but the sturdy branch didn't even bend. 

"The Bulbul is the next contestant," the Owl announced. "Is that branch over there thick enough?" 
"That branch is even thicker than this one," the Hornbill sneered. "If the Bulbul can break that branch, he certainly deserves to be king. Go on!"

What the Hornbill didn't know was that the Woodpecker had secretly bored entirely through the thick branch the Bulbul was to sit on! As soon as the lightweight Bulbul landed on it, the weakened branch snapped with a loud *CRACK!*

Stunned and humiliated, the Hornbill believed the Bulbul was incredibly powerful. Acknowledging defeat, the Hornbill flew away forever, and the gentle Bulbul became the new King of the Birds.
