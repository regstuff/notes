# Bheema and Hanuman

When the five Pandava brothers were deceitfully deprived of their kingdom by their jealous cousins, the Kauravas, they were forced into exile for thirteen long years along with their wife, Draupadi. They were accompanied by their wise preceptor, Sage Dhoumya. 

While they lived in the forest, Arjuna, the third Pandava, left for Mount Indrakeela to acquire powerful celestial weapons from the Devas. Nearly five years passed, but Arjuna did not return. 
"Everything in this forest reminds me of him. I can't bear this separation much longer," Draupadi sighed one day. 
"I, too, feel the same," Bheema agreed. "Without Arjuna, this forest seems empty."

Yudhishthira, the eldest, sought the advice of Sage Dhoumya. Just then, Sage Lomasha arrived with wonderful news. 
"Arjuna's mission has been successful!" Lomasha announced. "But the Devas need him for a little while longer. In the meantime, he has asked me to take you to all the important holy places, to the top of the Gandhamadana mountain, and finally, to Lord Kubera's gardens. He will join us there on his return."

Overjoyed by the thought of reuniting with Arjuna, the Pandavas and Draupadi eagerly set out. Soon, they reached the beautiful but treacherous Gandhamadana mountain. 
As they neared the snowy peak, a violent dust-storm suddenly broke out, followed by heavy, freezing rain. "Are the heavens coming down?" Nakula cried. 
Bheema quickly pulled Draupadi under a large tree to protect her. Soon, the sky cleared, but Draupadi was utterly exhausted. "Bh-ee-ma! He-elp me!" she gasped, before fainting on the rugged path. 

The brothers rushed to her side, panicked. They gently placed her on a deerskin and managed to revive her. 
"O Bheema, it will be hard for Draupadi to cross the snow-bound peaks still ahead of us," a worried Yudhishthira said. "Why don't you and Sahadeva stay here with her? The rest of us will proceed."
"I can't let you go," Bheema refused. "Let's have no more separations."
Suddenly, Bheema remembered his son, Ghatotkacha, born to the demoness Hidimba. "Ghatotkacha will carry her! I have only to think of him and he will be here."

Miles away, Ghatotkacha sensed his father's call and appeared instantly. "Command me!"
"Ghatotkacha, you can fly anywhere at will. Carry Draupadi and follow us to the mountain top. Fly low, so she does not become uneasy," Bheema ordered. 
Ghatotkacha and his Rakshasa followers lifted the entire party and flew them effortlessly to the serene hermitage of Nara and Narayana at the mountain's peak, where they rested. 

Some days later, Bheema and Draupadi were walking in the woods when a sudden breeze brought a beautifully fragrant, glowing lotus flower. 
"What an exquisite flower! What a sweet fragrance!" Draupadi marveled. "Oh, Bheema, get some more of them for me!"
"I would go to the very ends of this earth, if necessary, to get the flowers for you," Bheema promised, striding off in the direction the wind had blown from. 

Bheema searched high and low, venturing deeper and deeper into the wild, treacherous forest. He was so determined that when a massive rogue elephant attacked him, he simply grabbed it by the tusks and slammed it into the ground, killing it instantly. He strode on, terrifying lions and tigers, destroying everything in his path with his heavy mace. 

Finally, he saw a beautiful, clear lake near a banana grove. Hot and tired, Bheema blew his massive conch shell with all his might to announce his arrival, planning to take a refreshing dip before searching the grove. 

Unknown to Bheema, the banana grove was the home of Lord Hanuman, the mighty monkey-hero of the Ramayana. Hanuman, who was also a son of the Wind God (Vayu), instantly recognized the sound. "Only one being on earth can sound a conch so loudly—Bheema!" Using his spiritual powers, Hanuman divined Bheema's purpose. "It is Bheema! My dear brother! He has come for the celestial flowers. But he has always been too proud of his strength. For his own good, I must teach him to be humble."

Hanuman took the form of an old, frail monkey and stretched himself across the narrow path leading to the grove. To attract Bheema's attention, he lashed his tail against the ground, causing the entire mountain to shake and rumble with a deafening roar. 
Bheema rushed toward the sound, expecting a terrible monster. Instead, he saw an old monkey blocking his path. "Ha! Ha! Ho! Ho! It's only a monkey! I'll have some fun," Bheema laughed. 
He gave a loud, terrifying roar to scare the monkey away. But Hanuman simply turned and looked at him lazily. 
"I am ill. I was asleep and you woke me up," Hanuman complained. "Being a man, you ought to be more considerate. Who are you, and why are you here? Mere mortals may not go any further along this path. Be sensible, and go back."

Bheema was furious. "How dare this monkey talk to me like that! I am Bheema, the son of Pandu, born through the grace of Vayu! Let me pass or I shall thrash you!"
"I don't have the energy to move," Hanuman sighed. "If you must enter the grove, simply leap over me."
"If it were not a sin to leap over another living being, I would," Bheema boasted. "I would have leapt right over you and the mountain, just as my brother Hanuman leapt over the ocean!"
"Who is this Hanuman?" the old monkey asked innocently. 
"He is my handsome, strong, intelligent brother! And I am as strong as he is! So get up and let me pass!" Bheema yelled. 

"O mighty Bheema, I am old and weak. Have pity on me. Just move my tail aside and go across," Hanuman said. 
"For all his roaring, this monkey is a weakling! I can push it with my left hand!" Bheema thought contemptuously. He reached down and grabbed the monkey's tail. 

But to his utter shock, he couldn't move it even an inch! Sweating and straining, Bheema used both his massive, muscular arms, pulling with all his legendary might. The tail felt like an immovable mountain. Bheema collapsed, panting. 
"What's the matter with me? I, Bheema, am unable to move aside the tail of a mere monkey!" he realized with sudden clarity. "This is no mere monkey..."
Humbled, Bheema folded his hands. "O mighty one, forgive my harsh words. Pray tell me who you are."

Hanuman smiled warmly. "I am your brother, Hanuman!"
Bheema fell prostrate before him. "My brother! Show me the form in which you crossed the ocean! Please!"
Suddenly, Hanuman's body grew bigger and bigger, towering higher than the Gandhamadana mountain itself, glowing with blinding divine light. "Enough! I feel weak and faint. The light dazzles me!" Bheema cried. 

Returning to his normal size, Hanuman embraced his younger brother. Bheema felt a surge of incredible, strange power enter his body. "My fatigue has gone!" 
"Filled with your strength, we are sure to conquer our enemies," Bheema said gratefully. 
"I'll do more," Hanuman promised. "When you charge at the enemy in the great war, I shall sit invisible on Arjuna's flagstaff, and my roar shall strike terror into your enemies' hearts."
Hanuman then pointed Bheema toward the Saugandhika forest and Lord Kubera's private lake, where the celestial lotuses grew. "When you go back, you may speak of me, but don't tell anyone that I live here," Hanuman requested, before vanishing. 

Bheema rushed to the magnificent lake. As he bent to drink, thousands of fierce Rakshasa guards surrounded him. "Wait! This is Kubera's favourite playground. Mortals are not permitted here. You cannot drink the water or carry away the flowers without Kubera's permission!" they warned. 

"What nonsense! This lake belongs to all," Bheema retorted, and boldly plunged into the water. 
The furious Rakshasas attacked. But Bheema, energized by Hanuman's embrace, fought with terrifying fury, slaughtering over a hundred Rakshasas with his mace. The terrified survivors fled to Lord Kubera's palace. 

"Lord! Yudhishthira's brother has entered the lake without permission," they reported. 
To their astonishment, Lord Kubera smiled. "I know why Bheema is here! Return to your posts. Let him take the flowers."

Meanwhile, Bheema gleefully gathered the rare celestial flowers. Thinking of Draupadi's joy, he began to frolic in the lake with such immense power that he whipped up a violent storm, whirling water and massive boulders into the sky. 
Back at the hermitage, Yudhishthira saw the fiery meteors, heard the explosions, and panicked. "Arm yourselves! We may soon have to show our might!" he shouted, noticing Bheema was missing. 

Only then did Yudhishthira realize that Draupadi was smiling serenely. "He must have found the celestial lotuses," she said happily. 
Fearing Bheema had offended the gods, Yudhishthira urgently summoned Ghatotkacha. "Take us to the lake before he incurs their wrath!"
When they arrived, they found Bheema perfectly safe, grinning widely, holding a massive bunch of glowing celestial lotuses. 

"O Bheema, what have you done? You must never again commit such a rash act!" Yudhishthira scolded him. Bheema merely smiled and handed the beautiful flowers to a radiant Draupadi. 

Soon after, Kubera's Rakshasa guards arrived. Recognizing Yudhishthira, they bowed humbly. "Go to your lord and tell him that I, Yudhishthira, seek his permission to live for a while at this spot," the eldest Pandava requested. 
Lord Kubera happily granted his permission, and the Pandavas lived peacefully by the beautiful lake until Arjuna finally returned from his quest.
