# Narasimha Avatar

Vishnu, in the form of a magnificent boar, had killed the evil asura, Hiranyaksha. The devas were happy and there was much rejoicing in heaven and on earth, but not for long. 
Hiranyakashipu, Hiranyaksha's elder brother, seething with intense anger, was fully bent on avenging the death of his brother. 

"Vishnu thrives on virtuous souls and their virtuous deeds," Hiranyakashipu told his followers. He turned to the asuras who had come to offer him their condolences. "Go. Destroy all good people on earth. Put out their sacrificial fires and kill their cows... while I practise severe penances and become the absolute master of all the three worlds - heaven, earth and our own regions. Then shall I exalt sin and subdue virtue. I shall drive the devas and the sages to the nether regions and throw open heaven, the highest sphere, to the asuras."

And Hiranyakashipu went to Mount Mandara and began his penances. So he stood on one leg, arms raised to the sky, for years and years. Ant hills and grass grew on his motionless person but he did not move. 
The rivers and oceans trembled. "The day of Brahma must be over. The hour of dissolution is come," the fearful people whispered. 
The earth and her mountains quaked... and the stars and planets darted about in panic. So intense was Hiranyakashipu's penance that the fiery smoke emerging from his head began to literally scorch everything in its wake. 

The devas were utterly terrified. Led by Indra, they went to Brahma, the creator. 
"O merciful lord, pray do something, anything, before the three worlds of your creation are completely consumed by the fire of the asura's penances," Indra begged. 
Moved by their plight, Brahma went to Mount Mandara. A few sages accompanied him. 
"Enough, O asura. Arise. I have been conquered by your austerities. Any boon you seek shall be yours," Brahma offered. 

The holy water from the creator's kamandalu rejuvenated the scorched asura. He emerged, handsome and radiant. 
"Let not death come to me either by man or beast, by day or by night, indoors or outdoors, on earth or in the sky," Hiranyakashipu asked. "Grant me undisputed lordship over the material world."
"So be it," Brahma granted. 

Armed with the powerful boons, the asura lost no time in conquering and bringing under his complete sway, both heaven and earth. "I wish to occupy the palace of the lord of the devas. Drive him out," he ordered his armies. 
Betrayed by Brahma, the devas urgently approached Vishnu the preserver. Vishnu's voice reassured them from the heavens. 
"Have patience, O devas. I know the evil that is the asura. His son, the high-souled Prahlad, is deeply devoted to me. When the asura seeks to kill my devotee, I shall slay him."

The devas did not have to wait long. Even as Vishnu made his promise, Hiranyakashipu was screaming with exasperation at his son in his palace. 
"You will insist on defying me and worshipping my sworn enemy; me, before whom the denizens of all the three worlds quake with fear!"
Deep in contemplation of Lord Vishnu, Prahlad did not utter a word. 
Hiranyakashipu roared in fury. "You seem to be so confident in your faith. O fool, I cannot imagine where you get the strength from!"

At last Prahlad spoke calmly. "From whom not only I but also you and all other powerful beings derive theirs - the all-pervading Lord Vishnu."
Hiranyakashipu became livid with blind rage. "If he is all-pervading, he must be in this pillar. I am going to slay you now. Let him come and protect..."
He struck the pillar with his heavy mace. A resounding crash drowned his words. The pillar violently cracked open and... Lord Vishnu in the terrifying form of Narasimha (half-man, half-lion) emerged and substantiated his devotee's words. 

"He is neither a beast nor a human being. What might this strange creature be?" Hiranyakashipu thought, stepping back. 
As Narasimha advanced menacingly upon him, Hiranyakashipu tried to regain his composure. "It must be Vishnu. He has taken this strange form to kill me. He may have taken care of one condition but he still cannot harm me."
Emboldened by that thought, the asura picked up a mace and... roaring loudly rushed at Narasimha. 
But as he came near, Narasimha easily seized him, mace and all, in a close embrace. 

Hiranyakashipu however slipped through his powerful arms... and grabbing a sharp sword... charged once again. 
Watching his frantic movements, Narasimha sent forth a shrill peal of laughter followed by a weird, earth-shaking roar. And the next moment, the asura found himself helpless in the vice-like grip of Narasimha's claws. 

"It is the twilight hour! Neither day nor night..." Hiranyakashipu thought with sudden dread. 
Narasimha effortlessly carried the struggling asura to the threshold of the great hall. 
"We are neither indoors nor outdoors!" the asura realized in terror. 
And there, under the great arch of the doorway, Narasimha threw the asura across his massive thighs...
"Neither on earth nor in the sky. Alas! Vishnu has outwitted me!" Hiranyakashipu gasped his last thought. 
...And tore him to death with his razor-sharp claws. 

Thus did Vishnu, compelled by the pure faith of his young devotee, creatively circumvent the boons of Brahma to prevent the triumph of evil and establish virtue.
