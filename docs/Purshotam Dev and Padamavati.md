# Purushottam Dev and Padmavati

Long, long ago, Purushottam Dev, the young, brave, and handsome prince of Kalinga, set out on a grand tour of the whole country. One day, he and his royal companions camped on the lush outskirts of a deep forest within the neighboring kingdom of Kanchi. 

While resting, a beautiful deer suddenly dashed past the camp. Intrigued, Purushottam Dev grabbed his weapons and followed the deer deep into the dense forest, intending to track it down. 

Before he could go very far, two heavily armed guards suddenly stepped out from the thick foliage, firmly blocking his path. "Halt!" one of them barked. "How dare you disturb the peace in this forest? Don't you know that Princess Padmavati of Kanchi is resting here? We will bind you and take you to her!"

Amused by their arrogance, the highly trained prince easily overpowered both strong men in a matter of seconds. Hearing the brief scuffle, Princess Padmavati and her royal maids hurried to the spot. 

Seeing the beautiful princess, Purushottam Dev immediately bowed respectfully. "I am Purushottam Dev, Prince of Kalinga. I left your guards tied to a tree. I am sorry, but they were exceptionally rude."

Princess Padmavati remained entirely silent, but as she gazed at the prince, her heart fluttered. *How handsome and incredibly strong he looks,* she thought to herself, instantly falling in love. *What a noble bearing!*

When the King of Kanchi heard that the brave prince of Kalinga had single-handedly overcome two of his best guards, he was deeply impressed. He immediately invited Purushottam Dev to the royal palace as an honored guest. Over the next few days, the prince eagerly accepted the invitation, actively participating in royal sports and charming his hosts with fascinating tales of his distant travels. During this time, he and Princess Padmavati fell deeply in love. 

Their blissful time together was suddenly cut short. A frantic messenger arrived from Puri, the capital of Kalinga, carrying tragic news: the King of Kalinga had passed away. Bidding a hasty, sorrowful farewell to the royal family of Kanchi, Purushottam Dev rushed back to Puri, where he was formally crowned as the new King of Kalinga. 

Though he was now a king burdened with state affairs, Purushottam Dev simply could not forget the beautiful Princess Padmavati. Hundreds of miles away in Kanchi, Padmavati similarly pined for her handsome prince. 

Recognizing his daughter's deep, unmistakable love, the King of Kanchi happily decided to formally offer her hand in marriage to the young King of Kalinga. He proudly dispatched his Prime Minister to Puri with the royal proposal. 

The old, wise minister of Kalinga warmly welcomed the proposal. "Your proposal is most timely," the Kalinga minister smiled. "Our kingdom desperately needs a queen. But please, stay back and witness our grand Car Festival before you return to Kanchi."

The Prime Minister of Kanchi gladly agreed. A few days later, the famous Car Festival (*Rath Yatra*) dawned. It was held in grand honor of the presiding deities of Puri: Lord Jagannatha, his elder brother Balarama, and their sister Subhadra. 

According to ancient, sacred tradition, before the massive, towering wooden chariots were drawn by the millions of gathered devotees, the King of Kalinga had to perform a deeply symbolic act: he had to take a humble broom and personally sweep the platform of the Lord's chariot. 

Watching from a VIP balcony, the arrogant Prime Minister of Kanchi was absolutely horrified. "How incredibly humiliating!" he gasped in utter disgust. "The king holding a common broom like a lowly servant!"

Disgusted, the Kanchi minister immediately packed his bags, rushed back to his kingdom, and angrily reported the 'strange, humiliating sight' to his king. 

The King of Kanchi was equally outraged by the perceived insult to royal dignity. "It would never do!" he declared haughtily. "Our royal princess cannot possibly wed a king who sweeps chariots like a commoner. Never! Send word immediately that our proposal is completely withdrawn."

When the insulting, utterly arrogant rejection reached Puri, the old minister of Kalinga personally made the long, arduous trip to Kanchi to explain the sacred tradition. "Your Highness, my king has been terribly misunderstood," he pleaded respectfully. "The act merely emphasizes that even the greatest king on earth is ultimately only a humble servant of God."

"I am sorry," the King of Kanchi replied coldly, waving his hand dismissively. "I am absolutely not convinced. A daughter of the royal house of Kanchi cannot marry a king who wields a broom."

When the humiliated minister returned to Puri and reported the arrogant king's final refusal, Purushottam Dev's pride was deeply stung. "I shall take this up with him—on the battlefield!" he vowed furiously. 

Gathering his vast army, King Purushottam Dev marched directly into Kanchi. A massive, bloody battle was fought on the borders. However, that very night, a sudden, mysterious fire broke out in the Kalinga camps, completely destroying Purushottam Dev's vital ammunition and food provisions. 

Sensing the devastating weakness, the King of Kanchi ruthlessly attacked at dawn. Outnumbered and utterly unequipped, Purushottam Dev was humiliatingly forced to order a full retreat back to Kalinga. 

"You shall pay for this in our next battle, O King of Kanchi," Purushottam Dev swore as he rode away. 

Upon returning to Puri, the defeated, deeply frustrated king went straight to the magnificent temple of Lord Jagannatha. Refusing to eat or sleep, he stayed there for many days, fervently praying for the Lord's divine help and guidance. 

Meanwhile, back in Kanchi, the arrogant king decided to permanently end his daughter's infatuation. "I shall hold a grand *Swayamvara* for Padmavati," he told his minister. "See that all the kings of the land are invited... except, of course, the 'sweeper-king'." 

Hearing of the upcoming *Swayamvara*, Padmavati was absolutely heartbroken. *My heart is already given,* she wept silently in her chambers. *What shall I do, O Lord? How can I possibly choose another?*

In Puri, Purushottam Dev also heard about the *Swayamvara* and was consumed by despair. But that very night, Lord Vishnu (Jagannatha) miraculously appeared in his dream. "Do not despair, dear king," the Lord smiled reassuringly. "I am with you."

Filled with incredible, renewed hope, Purushottam Dev immediately mobilized his troops and marched toward Kanchi the very next morning. 

During the march, they passed a poor dairy-woman selling curds on the roadside. She flagged down the king's horse. "O King," she called out, "two incredibly handsome youths—one on a black horse and one on a white horse—passed by earlier. They ate some of my curds and gave me this diamond ring, telling me that if I showed it to you, you would pay for the curds."

The king took the ring and examined it. He was absolutely astounded. "But this is Lord Jagannatha's sacred diamond ring!" he gasped. In a flash of overwhelming divine realization, he understood: the two youths were Lord Jagannatha and Lord Balarama themselves! They had already ridden ahead to fight for him! 

Overjoyed by the Lord's direct, physical support, Purushottam Dev richly rewarded the poor woman with an entire village, naming it *Manik Patna* in her honor. 

Driven by an unstoppable, divine fervor, the Kalinga army reached Kanchi in a matter of days. They stormed the high, fortified city walls with absolute ease, crashing through the gates and completely surrounding the royal palace. 

Within moments, the defeated, humiliated King of Kanchi was dragged before Purushottam Dev. 
"What do you propose to do with me?" the captive king asked nervously. 
"Nothing. You shall be freed," Purushottam Dev replied coldly. "But... I will see to it that your arrogant daughter is married to a common sweeper!"

Padmavati, standing nearby, gasped in utter horror. 

Purushottam Dev rode triumphantly back to Puri, bringing the captive, weeping princess with him. "Minister, you will have to find a sweeper for Padmavati," he ordered his old minister sternly. 

Princess Padmavati was absolutely devastated. She secretly kept a lethal, poisoned ring on her finger, vowing to swallow the poison the moment she was forced to marry a sweeper. 

In private, the old, wise minister desperately pleaded with the king to change his mind, but Purushottam Dev's wounded pride would not let him back down. "Never! I have made a public announcement. I cannot go back on my own decision."

Nevertheless, Purushottam Dev was the one truly suffering. He still loved Padmavati deeply and couldn't bear the thought of her marrying anyone else, let alone a sweeper. Unable to sleep, he gave his minister a strict deadline: "I give you exactly one month's time to carry out my orders. Not a day more."

The month slowly passed. The minister desperately wondered how he could possibly make his stubborn young king see sense without forcing him to break his royal word. 

Finally, the glorious day of the annual Car Festival arrived once again. Milling crowds eagerly thronged the grand temple courtyard in Puri. The towering, magnificent wooden chariots were ready to be drawn. 

As royal musicians heralded the arrival of the king, the old minister quietly slipped into Princess Padmavati's apartment. "Get ready, my child," he told the trembling, tearful princess. "I will have to get you married. Today is the last day."

Believing her life was over, Padmavati quietly readied her poisoned ring, determined to swallow it at the altar. She was carried in a closed palanquin down to the crowded streets. 

Surrounded by eager, pushing crowds, King Purushottam Dev walked proudly toward the Lord's towering chariot, holding a humble broom in his hands to perform his traditional, sacred duty. 

As soon as the symbolic, sweeping act was completely over, the wise old minister suddenly rushed forward, leading the beautiful Princess Padmavati by the hand. 

"My Lord! My Lord!" the minister shouted, drawing everyone's attention. "Your Majesty, I have done it... I have finally found the perfect sweeper for Princess Padmavati!"

The minister gently pushed Padmavati forward. "My daughter, here is the most eligible sweeper for you. Garland him!"

Instantly understanding the brilliant, perfectly executed trick, Padmavati’s tears of sorrow vanished. Overjoyed, she eagerly threw the beautiful floral garland directly over King Purushottam Dev's neck! 

"What have you done, my minister?" Purushottam Dev asked, though a massive, relieved smile broke across his face. 
"Are you not a sweeper right now, my lord?" the wise old minister chuckled, pointing at the broom still in the king's hand. 

"Indeed I am!" Purushottam Dev laughed happily, pulling Padmavati into a joyful embrace. 

The massive crowd erupted into deafening, jubilant cheers, completely thrilled by the brilliant resolution. Their beloved king had kept his proud word, and more importantly, he had kept his true love. Together, the incredibly happy King and Queen of Kalinga turned to the towering chariot, offering their deepest, most fervent prayers to the ever-gracious Lord Jagannatha.
