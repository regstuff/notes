# Duryodhana Humbled

In the twelfth year of their exile, the Pandavas engaged in asceticism and the practice of archery in the Dwaita woods near a beautiful lake. A Brahmana who had visited them traveled to Hastinapura and described their pitiable living conditions to the Kaurava court. 

"Alas, the virtuous Pandavas who formerly slept on soft beds are now being made to sleep on the bare earth," Dhritarashtra lamented.

But Duryodhana, Shakuni, and Karna felt no pity. In fact, Karna was delighted. "O King, let us go to the Dwaita forest and look at the sons of Pandu," Karna suggested maliciously. "Let us see them shorn of their prosperity, the ones who never took orders and who never knew subjection. The happiness that one derives from beholding his foes in grief is great..."

Duryodhana was thrilled by the idea. "I have always wished glory and prosperity for Dhritarashtra and his sons. So, I bow to you and may that happen which is to happen to you! Let us invite them once again to a game of dice. Whoever loses now will be banished to the forest for twelve years..." wait, no, Duryodhana wanted to mock them. He said: "I want to subdue Yudhishthira without endangering my kin."

Shakuni devised a clever pretext. "It is the right season for the princes to go hunting," Shakuni told Dhritarashtra. "I beseech you to grant Duryodhana permission to go to the cattle-station at Dwaitavana to inspect the cattle."

Unaware of their true, malicious intentions, Dhritarashtra granted his permission, though he warned them not to provoke the Pandavas. 

Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni, and Duhshasana set out with a massive army of soldiers and courtiers. They camped near the Dwaita forest, spending their time hunting and reveling. When Duryodhana ordered his men to construct pleasure houses near the lake, they were stopped by heavily armed guards. 

"Stop! You cannot enter these woods," the guards warned. "Our king has come here to sport and we will not allow any intrusion."

The guards belonged to Chitrasena, the powerful King of the Gandharvas, who had occupied the lake with his Apsaras and attendants. When Duryodhana's soldiers tried to force their way in, the Gandharvas attacked. 

Duryodhana, enraged, ordered his entire army to attack the Gandharvas. A fierce battle erupted. Karna fought valiantly, raining sharp arrows upon the Gandharvas and killing hundreds. But Chitrasena unleashed his magic powers, bewitching the Kaurava soldiers and causing them to scatter in panic. Seeing the tide turn, Karna, unable to withstand the furious onslaught, fled the battlefield in his chariot. 

With his army routed and his greatest warrior gone, Duryodhana was quickly overpowered. Chitrasena seized him, Duhshasana, and the women of the royal household, binding them in heavy chains. 

The fleeing remnants of the Kaurava army rushed to the nearby Pandava camp, begging for help. 

Bheema laughed mockingly. "The Gandharvas have done what we should have done ourselves! The wretch got what he deserved."

But Yudhishthira, ever righteous, was stern. "O Bheema, blood relatives are often divided and disputes arise among them. But any insult to the family from a stranger should not be tolerated. Our family honor is at stake. Furthermore, these men have come to us for refuge. It is our duty as Kshatriyas to protect them."

Obeying their elder brother's command, Bheema, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva armed themselves and marched against the Gandharvas. A terrible battle ensued. The Pandavas fought furiously, but the Gandharvas took to the skies, fighting from the clouds. 

Arjuna, unleashing his newly acquired celestial weapons, covered the sky with a devastating shower of arrows, causing panic among the Gandharvas. Realizing the immense power of his adversary, Chitrasena rushed down from the sky and revealed himself. 

"Arjuna! Hold your fire!" Chitrasena called out. "It is I, your friend Chitrasena from Indraloka!"

Arjuna immediately lowered his Gandeeva bow and smiled. "O Chitrasena, why are you punishing the Kauravas? What purpose does it serve to capture Duryodhana and his wives?"

"I knew they had come to this forest to mock you and your brothers in your misfortune," Chitrasena explained angrily. "Lord Indra perceived their evil intentions and commanded me to bring Duryodhana in chains to him. I am only fulfilling that command."

"But the benevolent Yudhishthira has requested that they be released," Arjuna pleaded. "If my brothers and I are worthy of receiving your grace, please release him."

Chitrasena, respecting his friend, agreed. They went to Yudhishthira, and the Gandharva king formally released Duryodhana. 

"O child," Yudhishthira addressed the humiliated Duryodhana gently, "never again commit such a rash act. For you can never attain happiness then. Now you and your brothers go back to the capital cheerfully."

Duryodhana returned to his camp, completely shattered. The humiliation of being saved by his hated enemies was too much to bear. "The Pandavas have been severely humiliated by us. They will never forget how we molested Draupadi," he told Duhshasana. "Yet, we owe our lives to them. I cannot live with this shame. I will fast unto death."

Duhshasana, Shakuni, and Karna frantically tried to dissuade him. "You are foolish to take leave of your senses," Karna argued. "The Pandavas are your subjects. It was their duty to rescue their king." 

But Duryodhana remained stubborn, spreading Kusha grass on the earth and sitting down to meditate and fast. 

His desperate actions, however, were noticed by the fierce Daityas and Danavas in the netherworld. Fearing that Duryodhana's death would weaken their cause against the gods, they performed a dark ritual and summoned him to Patala. 

A female deity arose from a fire and carried Duryodhana down to the demons. "O Duryodhana, you are of celestial origin," the Danavas told him. "You are the creation of Maheshwara. We have also settled the means for slaying Arjuna. The soul of the slain Naraka has assumed the form of Karna, and he will slay Arjuna. Do not give up your life. Go, rule the earth."

Consoled and emboldened by the demons, Duryodhana returned to the surface and abandoned his fast. 

Back in Hastinapura, Karna, seeking to restore Duryodhana's pride, took a mighty oath. "With a large army at my disposal, I will conquer the world for you." 

Karna set out on a massive military campaign, subjugating kings in the North, East, South, and West. He returned victorious, having conquered all the kingdoms of the earth. To celebrate, Duryodhana performed the grand Vaishnava Yajna, rivaling Yudhishthira's Rajasooya. 

Following the successful sacrifice, Karna made a chilling, public vow in the royal palace. "And here I take a vow that as long as I don't slay Arjuna, I shall not allow anyone to wash my feet. Nor shall I taste meat or drink wine. If anyone asks for anything, I shall not say 'no'."

When the spies brought news of Karna's terrible oath to the forest, Yudhishthira grew deeply anxious. He knew that the inevitable war was drawing closer, and Karna's unyielding determination posed a grave threat to the Pandavas.
