# Heroes of Hampi: The Mythology of Kishkindha

In the early fourteenth century, the armies of the Delhi Sultanate attacked the Deccan, hoping to expand their territories. Two warrior brothers, Hakka and Bukka, were hunting with their hounds on the rocky banks of the river Tungabhadra (also known as Pampa), trying to find a strategy against the invaders. 

Suddenly, the rabbit they were chasing turned around and snarled, fighting back against their fierce dogs!
"What could make a rabbit act so brave?" Bukka wondered.
"It is the land here - a land of bravery. This is where you should set up your kingdom of victory. The rabbit is a sign," said an ascetic sitting nearby. The brothers listened spellbound as the sage told them the mythological history of the land they were standing on. 

"First," the sage began, "Shiva married Sati against the wishes of her father, Daksha. When Daksha held a great yagna, he invited all gods except Shiva and Sati. Sati attended anyway, but feeling insulted, she invoked the god of fire and burst into flames. Shiva's grief and anger knew no bounds. He tore off a strand of hair and threw it on the ground. From it emerged the fearsome god Veerabhadra."
Veerabhadra led his army to Daksha's court, destroyed the yagna, and wreaked havoc. To stop him, Vishnu worshipped Veerabhadra with offerings of lotuses. When Vishnu realized he was one lotus short of a thousand, he plucked out his own lotus-shaped eye and offered it. Touched by this devotion, Veerabhadra calmed down and restored Vishnu's eye, gifting him the Sudarshan Chakra. "The place where this happened is now called Chakratirtha," the sage explained. 

"Sati was reborn as Parvati, the daughter of Himavan," the sage continued. "In this region, she was known as Pampa (the river). She did severe penance to marry Shiva again. One day, Shiva appeared disguised as a starving ascetic. Pampa ran to the river to get food for him but was attacked by a crocodile. When the ascetic saved her, she realized it was Shiva himself, who then asked her to marry him. Gold rained down upon them from heaven, covering the entire hill, which came to be known as Hemakuta, meaning 'Peak of Gold'. We are standing on Hemakuta right now!"

The sage then told them the story of Hanuman. Anjana, cursed to be a monkey, was relieved of her curse when she gave birth to Hanuman, a son blessed by Shiva. The invincible monkey king Vali tried to kill the infant with a molten mixture of five metals (Panchaloha), but the child easily digested it. Realising Hanuman's strength, Vali stayed away from him. 
However, Hanuman was very mischievous. Once, he disturbed a meditating rishi, who cursed him to forget his great strength until someone reminded him of it in his time of need. 

Hanuman grew up and sought Lord Surya (the Sun God) as his guru. Since Surya was always moving across the sky, Hanuman flew backward in front of Surya's chariot, mastering the Vedas and Shastras in just sixty hours. In return for the teachings, Surya asked Hanuman to protect his son, Sugriva, from his powerful brother, Vali. 

Hanuman and Sugriva became close friends in Kishkindha. One night, a demon named Mayavi challenged Vali to a fight. Vali chased the demon into a cave, telling Sugriva to wait outside. "If you see blood coming out, it means I'm dead. Block the entrance and go home."
Sugriva waited for an entire year. When he saw blood flowing out and heard a roar, he assumed Vali was dead. Heartbroken, he blocked the cave and returned to Kishkindha, where he was crowned king, with Hanuman as his minister. 
But the blood was the demon's. Vali returned alive, furious that Sugriva had locked him in. Believing Sugriva was a traitor, Vali attacked him. Hanuman and Sugriva fled to Rishyamukha hill, a place Vali could not enter due to a curse from a rishi. 

While in exile, Hanuman and Sugriva met Rama and Lakshmana, who were searching for Sita after she was abducted by Ravana. Rama killed Vali, and Sugriva regained his kingdom. When it was time to cross the ocean to Lanka to rescue Sita, the monkey army was at a loss. It was then that Jambavan, the wise bear, reminded Hanuman of his forgotten strength. Remembering his power, Hanuman climbed to a high point and made his epic leap across the ocean. 

After Rama defeated Ravana and returned to Ayodhya, he offered boons to all the monkeys. Hanuman asked for a temple with a deity of his own where he could pray and gain strength. Rama directed him to ask Shiva. 
Hanuman did severe penance and Shiva appeared, granting his wish. "Ask Veerabhadra, he will help you," Shiva said. 
Veerabhadra appeared and a small idol of himself materialized on the spot. Suddenly, the idol began to grow taller and taller! Hanuman quickly built a temple around it, and finally, the idol stopped growing. "The idol is still here in the Uddana (large) Veerabhadra temple," the sage concluded. 

"This is a place steeped in valour," the sage told Hakka and Bukka. "It is a sacred place. This is a good place for a fortified city. The river will act as a barrier against invaders. Somebody needs to stand against all the invaders. Why not us?"
The brothers were deeply moved. "You are very wise, sir. Won't you be our advisor?"
"I will, but now I must go and complete my prayers," the sage said, and disappeared amongst the trees. 

Hakka and Bukka searched for him and eventually learned from villagers that the ascetic was the great sage Vidyaranya. With Vidyaranya as their advisor, Hakka and Bukka established their new capital exactly where the hare had turned on the dogs. They named the city Vijayanagara (City of Victory). 
Hakka became its first king, Harihara Raya I, and Bukka succeeded him as Bukka Raya I. Thus began the mighty Vijayanagara Empire, a kingdom of culture, learning, and valour that flourished for over three hundred years, defending the South.
