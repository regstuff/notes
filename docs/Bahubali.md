# Bahubali

Two thousand five hundred years ago, at Pavapuri in Bihar, on the dark night of *Kartika Amavasya*, the news of the salvation (nirvana) of Lord Mahavira—the 24th and final Tirthankara of the Jains—spread far and wide. Lord Mahavira had dedicated his entire life to human welfare, and his followers had infinite faith in him. Even today, the festival of his nirvana is celebrated as Deepavali. 

In the long tradition of Jain Tirthankaras, there were 23 Tirthankaras who preceded Mahavira. The first of them was Lord Rishabhadeva, also known as Adinatha. 

Nabhiraj, the father of Rishabhadeva, lived in a magnificent palace in Ayodhya. He brought up Rishabhadeva with immense love and arranged his marriage with great pomp and ceremony. In time, Rishabhadeva had two extraordinary sons. He made his elder son, Bharat, highly proficient in social affairs, governance, and administration, while his younger son, Bahubali, mastered the martial arts, especially archery. 

One day, Rishabhadeva grew deeply disillusioned with his regal splendor. Realizing the impermanence of worldly power, he decided to renounce the world and become a *muni* (ascetic). Before leaving, he divided his vast kingdom among his sons. Bharat was given the kingdom of Ayodhya, and Bahubali was made the king of Podanpur. 

But Bharat was deeply ambitious and unsatisfied with just Ayodhya. He had a burning desire to become the sole, sovereign king of the entire earth. 

One day, Bharat received highly auspicious news. "Oh Lord! A divine *Chakra* (wheel) has miraculously come into being in your armory!" a messenger announced. 
"General, state clearly what you mean?" Bharat asked. 
"Your majesty, a *Chakra* is the ultimate sign of a universal monarch (*Chakravartin*). This *Chakra* expects from you the victory of the universe."
"We understand," Bharat proclaimed. "Tomorrow itself, our army will march to gain victory over the entire earth."

Early the next morning, King Bharat's huge army was arranged in columns. Spearheaded by the floating, magical *Chakra*, the army marched forward. 
"First we shall advance towards the east and cross the Ganges," the General advised. 
The petty kings en route, seeing the terrifying army and the glowing *Chakra*, knew their defeat was certain. They immediately surrendered and presented precious gifts to Bharat. 

Bharat then marched to the sea. Traveling 12 *yojanas*, he shot an arrow inscribed with his name into the ocean. It fell on the abode of Magadhadeva, Lord of Lavandweep. 
"So King Bharat shot it! If he desires wealth, he shall get death," Magadhadeva raged. 
"My lord, it is unwise to fight a powerful king like Bharat," his minister advised. Heeding the advice, Magadhadeva peacefully submitted. 

Bharat's forces advanced relentlessly. They marched south, conquering the regions from Kalinga to Kerala. Turning west, they crossed the Vindhya mountains and the Narmada river, vanquishing all kings up to the Girnar mountain in Saurashtra. Crossing the Indus river, Bharat marched towards the Vijayardha mountains, subjugating non-Aryan kings, demons, and the rulers of the Himavana mountain regions. The entire world was now under his control. 

Victorious, Bharat triumphantly returned to Ayodhya. However, to his absolute shock, the magical *Chakra* suddenly stopped spinning at the main city gate and refused to enter. 
Panicking, everyone cried, "What has happened?"

Bharat summoned his head priest. "Oh learned one, please explain why this *Chakra* has stopped."
"Your majesty, it seems the conquest is not complete," the priest explained. "Pardon me, my lord, but your own invincible brothers have not yet offered salutation to you. Amongst them, the most valiant and wise Bahubali is the chief. Unless he surrenders, you cannot be called a sovereign king."

"Defiant Bahubali shall see the fury of our weapons!" Bharat raged. But his minister intervened. "My lord, do not be angry. We should write and explain that an elder brother is revered like a father. Let them accept you as the sovereign king peacefully."

When Bharat's emissaries reached the brothers, they respectfully replied, "Certainly Bharat is our revered elder, but our kingdoms have been given by our father, Rishabhadeva. We shall do as our father bids." 
The princes went to Mount Kailash and implored Muni Rishabhadeva. "Father, we have bowed down only before you. In his arrogance, Bharat wants us to offer him salutation. What shall we do?"
"Strife for kingdoms divides brothers and sons," Rishabhadeva advised calmly. "Someday, Bharat too will have to give up his kingdom. Why fight? Abandon your kingdoms and seek spiritual peace."
Following their father's wisdom, all the brothers gave up their kingdoms and became monks. 

All, except Bahubali, the king of Podanpur. 
When Bharat's emissary approached him and demanded his submission, Bahubali was furious. "What sort of salutation is owed to a brother who carries a sword in his hand to snatch the kingdom given by our father? Go tell him that he cannot become the sovereign king without defeating me in the battlefield!"

"Lord, you are wise. Amity amongst brothers leads to prosperity," the emissary pleaded. 
"You have conveyed the message well. But I smell arrogance in Bharat's words," Bahubali retorted. 

Soon, the two massive forces stood facing each other near Podanpur. Both brothers were ready with their bows, and the generals prepared to order the slaughter. 
Suddenly, a wise minister shouted, "Lord Bharat and Lord Bahubali, please wait! It is necessary that the ministers of both sides hold talks before starting this war."

The ministers assembled. "Why should the blood of thousands of innocent soldiers be shed just to determine which of the two brothers is a better warrior?" one argued. 
They finally agreed on a solution: "It would be best if the armies do not combat. Only the two brothers should fight. They should wage a sight-battle, a water-battle, and a wrestling match. Whoever wins these will be declared the victor."
"We all agree. Now there will be a war of righteousness," the kings and soldiers cheered. 

Bahubali smiled. "Let Bharat agree to this first."
"There is no harm in agreeing," Bharat thought confidently. "I possess the *Chakra*. Bahubali will be defeated."

The spectacular duel began. First was the sight-battle. The two mighty brothers stood staring hard into each other's eyes without blinking. Finally, Bharat's eyes dampened, his lashes dropped, and his neck ached from looking up at his taller brother. Bahubali won the sight-battle. 

Next, they descended into a pond for the water-battle. Bharat fiercely splashed water into Bahubali's face with all his might, but Bahubali stood perfectly still, taking the blows. Then, Bahubali retaliated. His massive splashes engulfed Bharat, entering his mouth, eyes, and nose. Overwhelmed, Bharat had to turn his face away. Bahubali won the water-battle too. 

Finally, the ultimate test: the wrestling match. 
Bharat's followers boasted, "In the wrestling match, the game will rest in Bharat's hands."
Bahubali's men retorted, "One who dreams of becoming a sovereign king on the strength of his army will realize what it is to encounter a truly great fighter!"

The brothers locked in a fierce grip. They clapped their thighs, clenched their fists, and showered blows on each other. Suddenly, Bahubali found an opening. He grabbed Bharat and lifted him high into the air with both hands! 
"Shall I throw him on the ground?" Bahubali thought. "No... he is my elder brother. I shall just make him sit on my shoulder."
Humiliated, Bharat bowed his head as Bahubali gently lowered him to the ground. 

But as soon as Bharat's feet touched the earth, his eyes reddened with uncontrollable rage and humiliation. Deceitfully, he summoned his divine *Chakra* and hurled it at Bahubali! 
"This is deceitful! Shame on Bharat!" the spectators screamed. 

But a miracle occurred. The glowing *Chakra* whirled around Bahubali, then stopped completely still and rested gently in his hands. Bahubali merely smiled. 
"Bharat, you forget that this *Chakra* cannot harm my mountain-like body," Bahubali said gently. "In your blind anger, you also forgot the divine rule that this *Chakra* is absolutely ineffective against one's own kin. You wish to become a sovereign king by destroying your own brothers. By deceiving me, you have tarnished our father's name."

Standing there, Bahubali suddenly felt a wave of intense disgust for the world. "My elder brother has behaved in such a shameful manner just for the greed of a kingdom. Kingdoms are a cause of misery. Of what use is this power and glory?"
The sky reverberated with cheers: "Hail to the Sovereign King Bahubali!"

But Bahubali turned to his brother. "Bharat, you did me good! For a kingdom, even brothers come to daggers. Take the kingdom. I am going to perform penance. I did not behave respectfully towards my elder brother by lifting him up. Therefore, brother, forgive me."
Bahubali cast aside his royal robes and jewels right there on the battlefield. "Look after the kingdom, Bharat. I am leaving now," he said. 

"Wait, Bahubali! I have been wicked. Please stop!" Bharat wept, falling to his knees. 
But Bahubali walked away and became a *muni*. Stripped of all worldly attachments, he stood perfectly still in the forest, performing severe penance on both legs. Months passed. His body became emaciated. Creepers wound tightly around his arms and legs, and serpents built anthills at his feet. Yet, despite his incredible austerities, Bahubali did not attain perfect knowledge (*omniscience*). 

Troubled by this, Bharat went to Mount Kailash and asked his father, Rishabhadeva. "Father, why has Bahubali not attained omniscience?"
"Something still disturbs him," Rishabhadeva explained. "He is not free from the nagging thought that he is standing on *your* domain. That tiny ego prevents his liberation."
"How can this be wiped, oh venerated one?"
"Bharat, go with your family. Offer him respect."

Bharat rushed to the forest and fell at the feet of the statue-like Bahubali. "We convey our salutations to you, Muni Bahubali. The ground on which you stand is not mine, and will never be mine... It belongs to everyone. Please show us the path."
Hearing this, the last trace of pique and ego in Bahubali's mind vanished. In that instant, he attained pure omniscience and achieved eternal liberation. 

To commemorate his great brother, Bharat installed a magnificent life-size statue of Bahubali in Podanpur. However, as centuries passed, creepers and forests swallowed the area, and the statue was lost to time. 

Many centuries later, in the year 983 A.D., in South India, the mother of a great general named Chamundaraya expressed a desire to see Bahubali's statue. "It is very difficult to find the lost statue in Podanpur," the general said. "But I shall erect a new, magnificent idol of Bahubali here on the Indra Giri hill."

On Chamundaraya's orders, artisans hewed a colossal, monolithic statue of Bahubali at Shravanabelagola, near Mysore. Even today, it stands tall and serene. The faint smile on Bahubali's lips is a gentle reproach to the senseless, bloody wars waged by greedy kings and emperors for transient power. 

Every 12 years, the spectacular *Mahamastakabhisheka* (grand anointment) is held there, where millions of pilgrims and tourists flock to pay homage to the great soul who conquered himself. 

*(Interestingly, Lord Bharat had a son named Marichi, who started an independent order of monks. Through a long cycle of rebirths, Marichi's soul eventually took birth as Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, whose 2500th Nirvana anniversary is still celebrated with great joy throughout India.)*
