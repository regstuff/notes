# The Bridegroom's Ring

Young Bhola lived in a little hut with his mother and his three unlikely pets — a cat, a dog, and a crow. One day, out hunting with his friends, the crow flying ahead of the group suddenly began circling excitedly over something on the ground. The dog dashed forward with the cat close behind and pounced — on a mouse. Just as the cat was about to strike, Bhola scooped it up off the ground before it could do the mouse any harm. "I'm sorry, dear. I won't let you harm the little one," he told the cat, setting it down gently, then cradled the trembling mouse in his hand. "Smile, little one. No harm will come to you. I'm taking you home."

The mouse turned out to be smart in its own right. Once settled into Bhola's house, it took to digging furiously in the yard — and each time, it surfaced with a treasure: first a gold mohur, then more, until the family had a small mountain of gold coins heaped on the floor. Soon Bhola and his mother, once so poor, moved into a fine big house.

"Now that we have a fine house," his mother said, "what you need is a wife." She soon found the right girl, and preparations for the wedding went ahead — new clothes, gifts, and finally the wedding ring itself, delivered and admired. "Keep it safe," Bhola told his mother, tucking it away. "I can hardly wait for the day," she said.

But on the eve of the wedding, disaster struck. Bhola found his mother in tears — the ring was missing. "I have looked everywhere," she said, "someone must have stolen it. One of the visitors I showed the wedding ornaments to must have walked away with it." Bhola turned to his three animal friends. "You must find the ring. Don't come back without it," he told them, and they set off at once — though the dog and cat had no idea where to even begin. "Sniff, sniff," said the dog, nose to the ground, picking up a scent. "Don't disturb him," the cat warned. "He's picked up the trail."

The trail led to a river, which the dog swam across with the cat riding on its back and the mouse clinging to the cat — only for the dog to lose the scent entirely on the far bank. "It's no use," it admitted. Just then the crow spotted their man from the air: "I think I've found him. Yes — he has it on his finger." They followed the thief at a distance through the day, waiting for him to stop and rest for the night, and finally he settled by an old temple, spread out his cloth, and lay down to sleep — but sat bolt upright, suddenly anxious, before he'd properly dozed off. "The ring!" He pulled it off his finger and, for safekeeping, popped it straight into his mouth before lying back down.

"Who will snatch the ring now?" the dog wondered aloud. "I will," the mouse volunteered — and when the cat and dog looked at it in astonishment, half-expecting it to crawl straight into the sleeping man's mouth after it, the mouse just laughed. "No, I don't mean that at all. I'll make him throw up the ring himself." Creeping up the sleeping thief's body, it climbed onto his chest, then his shoulder, and tickled his nose with its tail. The man sneezed — once, then a second time, hard enough that the ring flew straight out of his mouth. The cat pounced and snatched it up before it hit the ground.

The thief woke with a shout and lunged after the fleeing cat, only to run straight into the dog, who sent him sprawling. He scrambled up and gave chase again, tumbling into a ditch for his trouble — while the cat, still carrying the ring in its mouth, dashed onward until a stray piece of meat on the ground proved too tempting to resist. It dropped the ring to eat, and in that instant a hawk swooped down, snatched up the abandoned ring, and flew off with it before the cat even noticed what had happened.

The cat's growls of frustration told the others everything. "What happened? Where's the ring?" "Oh! The hawk has it!" Undeterred, the cat trailed the hawk and, spotting it settled on a branch gnawing at what looked like a dead mouse, waited for its moment — then leapt as the hawk took off again, wrestling the ring loose in midair with a satisfying *clink* as it hit the ground. The crow, arriving just as the dog caught up, filled the dog in on the whole chase, and the group agreed: the ring would be safest with the dog for the journey home.

It very nearly wasn't. Crossing the river again, the dog swallowed a mouthful of water along with the ring, and had to spit it back out — only to send the ring flying loose into the current instead. "I've lost the ring!" the dog admitted, mortified. "Never mind," said the mouse. "We'll get it back. Let's start digging canals and turn the course of the river" — and so the three of them, plus the crow keeping watch, set to work redirecting the water by hand until the riverbed ran dry.

The commotion didn't go unnoticed underwater. A fish investigating the strange draining reported back to the river's chief — a ring, lost by some troublesome creatures who were now literally rerouting the entire river to find it. "Find the ring and bring it to me," the chief fish ordered, and soon enough a smaller fish located it and delivered it up the chain. Surfacing with the ring in its mouth, the chief fish struck a bargain with the mouse waiting on the bank: "Will you leave our river alone now?" "Of course, we will," the mouse promised, and the ring changed hands (or fins) one final time.

With the ring safely entrusted to the crow for the last leg home, the four friends resumed their journey and reached the house without further mishap. "Here they come!" Bhola's mother cried, running to meet them. "They've brought back the ring! Oh, my darlings!" The wedding went ahead as planned — and amid all the celebration and finery, it was Bhola's four unusual friends, the mouse, the cat, the dog, and the crow, who quietly stole the show, perched together and watching the ceremony they alone had made possible.
