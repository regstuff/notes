# Urvashi

Urvashi, the peerless celestial dancer and the breathtakingly beautiful apsara, was the absolute favourite of Lord Indra, King of the Gods. 

One day, after performing a magnificent dance in the grand halls of Kubera, the god of wealth, Urvashi was returning home to heaven, accompanied by her close companion, Chitralekha, and a bevy of other apsaras. 

Suddenly, a group of terrifying Asuras (demons) ambushed them in the skies. The demons ruthlessly snatched Urvashi and Chitralekha. "Help! Help!" the terrified apsaras screamed as they were carried off. 

Just at that critical moment, the valiant King Pururavas of Pratishthana, a mortal friend and ally of King Indra, happened to be passing by in his flying chariot. Hearing their desperate cries, he saw the demons fleeing. "Do not worry," he assured the remaining apsaras. "I will rescue them. Wait for me on yonder hill-top."

Pururavas rode off on his daring mission, fearlessly attacked the Asuras, defeated them, and rescued the two celestial maidens. 

As his chariot gently touched the earth to return them to safety, Urvashi looked up, still trembling, and her eyes met those of her brave rescuer, Pururavas. In that single, breathless moment, the mortal King and the celestial dancer fell deeply and irrevocably in love. 

"Sweet Urvashi, having stolen my heart, you are taking my very being with you," Pururavas thought wistfully as Urvashi and her companions soared back into the sky on a Gandharva's chariot. 

When King Pururavas returned to his grand palace at Pratishthana, he was a changed man. For not a moment could he take his mind off Urvashi. He became moody, absent-minded, and morose. 

He confided his secret only to his closest friend and trusted adviser, Manavaka. But the King's strange behavior had not gone unnoticed by his devoted wife, Queen Aushinari. 

"Alas! Has my husband fallen in love with another woman?" she worried, feeling deeply hurt and angry. She sent her shrewd maid, Nipunika, to trick the truth out of Manavaka. The clever maid succeeded, confirming the Queen's worst fears—the King was hopelessly infatuated with the celestial apsara, Urvashi.

Meanwhile, high in her heavenly abode, Urvashi was equally love-lorn. Unable to bear the separation, she and Chitralekha secretly came down to earth and remained invisible in the royal gardens, just as Pururavas was discussing his heartache with Manavaka. 

Overhearing his confessions of love, Chitralekha nudged Urvashi. Urvashi quickly scribbled a passionate message on a birch leaf and let it gently fall between the two men. 

Pururavas read the leaf, his face lighting up with joy. "You are right, Manavaka! It is a declaration of her love," he cheered. 

At that very moment, Chitralekha and Urvashi revealed themselves. Pururavas rushed forward, "Urvashi! I am the most fortunate man on earth. I have won your love."

But their joyous reunion was agonizingly brief. A celestial messenger from the court of Indra suddenly materialized before them. "Chitralekha! Urvashi! Hurry. Indra awaits you. He wants you to perform immediately." 

Bound by her heavenly duties, Urvashi had to depart, leaving Pururavas heartbroken once again. In his distress, he carelessly dropped the leaf bearing her love letter, which was soon found by Queen Aushinari. Confronted with undeniable proof, the King hung his head in guilt.

Back in heaven, Urvashi's heart and mind were still entirely on earth with Pururavas. During a grand performance in Indra's court, she was supposed to play the role of Goddess Laxmi. Her co-actor asked her the script's pivotal question: "Of all the gods assembled here, to whom does your heart belong?"

Instead of replying with the correct name, the love-struck Urvashi dreamily uttered, "Pururavas!"

The director of the play, the great Sage Bharata, was furious at this humiliating blunder. "You have put me to shame! I banish you from heaven!" he cursed her. 

However, Lord Indra, who had always favored Urvashi and was moved by her profound love, softened the curse. "You are my favourite, Urvashi, and Pururavas is my greatest ally. You may go and stay with him on earth. But... there is a condition. If you bear him a child, the moment he sets eyes on it, you must return to my court."

Overjoyed, Urvashi flew back to earth. 

Meanwhile, at Pratishthana, Queen Aushinari had deeply repented for her earlier anger. Realizing she could not fight true love, she took a noble and selfless vow. Under the moonlight, she declared to her husband, "I call upon heaven to witness my vow. I shall cleanse my heart of jealousy. Henceforth I will welcome as my sister whichever woman my husband chooses to love."

Hearing this from her invisible hiding place, Urvashi revealed herself, touched by the Queen's immense grace. 

With the Queen's blessing, Urvashi and Pururavas were finally united. They spent many happy, blissful seasons together in the magical Gandhamadana gardens. 

However, apsaras are creatures of intense, volatile emotions. One day, Pururavas momentarily gazed admiringly at a beautiful young girl by the riverbank. Urvashi was suddenly seized with a fit of blinding, jealous anger. Pushing him aside, she stormed away and rushed recklessly into a nearby forbidden grove.

Alas! The grove belonged to Kartikeya, the fierce god of war, and women were strictly forbidden from entering it. If they did, they were instantaneously turned into creepers. And so, the beautiful Urvashi was transformed into a simple, flowerless vine. 

Pururavas searched frantically for her for days, wandering like a madman. Finally, he stumbled upon a glowing crimson jewel on the ground. A divine voice from the heavens called out, "Don't ignore the jewel. Pick it up. The one who owns it is soon united with a lost love."

Pururavas picked up the Sangamaniya jewel. As he walked on, he saw a forlorn creeper that strangely reminded him of his beloved. Following a desperate instinct, he encircled the creeper with his arms. The magic of the jewel worked! The creeper instantly transformed back into Urvashi. 

After a joyful reunion, they returned to their palace in Pratishthana. But their peace was short-lived. A swooping vulture suddenly snatched the magical crimson jewel from a maid's hands. Pururavas ordered the bird shot down. 

When the dead bird and the jewel were retrieved, the King examined the arrow that had killed the vulture. His eyes widened in absolute shock. The arrow bore a name deeply carved into it: *Ayus, the son of Urvashi and Pururavas.*

"Wonder of wonders! How could she have had a son I didn't know of?" the King gasped.

Just then, a venerable hermitess named Satyavati from Sage Chyavana's ashram arrived at the court, leading a handsome young boy. 

"Welcome, Mother," Urvashi greeted her, but her face suddenly went pale with dread. 

"Though the sage Chyavana has been his guru," Satyavati explained, "Ayus today broke the strict rules of our ashram. He killed a living creature—a vulture. So the sage commanded me to return him to his mother's care."

Urvashi embraced her son tightly, but burst into bitter tears. "I had forgotten myself in a mother's joy..." she wept, turning to her bewildered husband. "I must now tell you about the condition laid down by Indra. He decreed that the moment you set eyes on our child, I must return to heaven. I had hidden Ayus in the ashram to delay this terrible parting. But now... I will have to go back."

Pururavas was utterly devastated. "If you must leave to obey Indra, my life holds no meaning," he declared solemnly. "I shall crown Ayus king immediately, and I shall retire to the deep forest."

Just as the heartbroken couple prepared for their final separation, a sudden brilliant flash lit the sky, and the divine sage Narada descended to earth. 

"Greetings, Lord Narada," Pururavas bowed. 

"I bring a message from mighty Indra," Narada announced with a booming, joyous voice. "He urgently needs your valiant help against the Asuras once again. Therefore, he does not want you to retire to the woods. As a reward for your unwavering loyalty, he has lifted the curse. He has gifted Urvashi to you... for life!"

The heavy clouds of sorrow vanished instantly. The pangs in their hearts were stilled forever. "May you two live forever in conjugal bliss," Narada blessed them before returning to the heavens. 

And so, King Pururavas and the celestial apsara Urvashi lived happily together, ruling their kingdom with love and justice till the end of their days.
