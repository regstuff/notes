# Shrenik

Vanik Chandra was on his way home on a hot summer's day when he saw a very strange sight. A young man was sitting in the shade of a large tree, yet he still had his umbrella held open over his head! 

When the young man saw Vanik Chandra approaching, he stood up politely. "Sir, may I join you on your journey?"
"You are most welcome," Vanik Chandra replied, though he thought to himself, *What a peculiar lad! He must either be a genius or a fool.*

As they walked on, they came upon a small settlement. 
"Ah! I am hungry," Vanik Chandra said. "We should get something to eat in one of those houses."
He knocked at the door of the very first house on their way. "We are travellers, good sir. Could you..."
"Then keep travelling!" the man inside snapped, slamming the door.
Vanik Chandra tried another house. "Sir, we are hungry. We thought, perhaps..."
"I am sorry. We have no food left," the owner replied coldly.

As they walked away empty-handed, the young man observed, "This village is uninhabited."
*What does he mean?* Vanik Chandra wondered, looking at the houses filled with people. But he did not ask for an explanation lest he betray his own ignorance.

Further along their journey, they came upon a river. Vanik Chandra immediately took off his sandals to cross. But to his bewilderment, the young man waded through the water with his sandals still on!
*He is no doubt a fool! Or is he?* Vanik Chandra pondered.

Finally, they reached the outskirts of a town. "There! That is my village," Vanik Chandra pointed. "Please rest here for a while. I will send for you later."

As Vanik Chandra reached home, Nandashree, his beautiful and intelligent only daughter, came out to receive him.
"Nandashree! I cannot wait to talk to you about something," he said eagerly. He knew Nandashree might be able to make sense of his companion's strange actions.

He told her all about the young man on the journey. "I don't know whether he is a fool or a genius."
"Father, to me he seems to be a very intelligent person," Nandashree smiled.
"Why do you think so?"
"Father, an umbrella shields you not only from the sun but also from the droppings of birds in the trees. And a village where no one is hospitable to travellers is as good as deserted."
"Hm-m-m! You are right," Vanik Chandra nodded slowly.
"And your feet are more precious than your sandals when you wade through an unknown river with sharp rocks and thorns."
"How true! That never struck me!" her father marvelled. "His intelligence is certainly above average. And he is handsome too. Nandashree, why don't you..."
"Let me test him, father," Nandashree smiled mischievously.

"Yes. Send for him. He is waiting outside the village."
"I will, father, after I have made arrangements to receive him."
Nandashree went about setting up her test. She ordered the servants to pour muddy water all over the courtyard entrance. Then, she sent her cleverest maid to fetch the young man with specific instructions.

When the maid found the young man, she said, "Vanik Chandra's daughter Nandashree has sent for you."
"I have to go to the temple. I hope you will find your way to the house," she added.
"I could if you gave me directions," he replied.
The maid simply touched her ear and walked away.

The young man thought for a moment. *The ear... an ornament... a palm tree! The ear is the symbol for a palm tree.*
He walked into the village and soon spotted a house. *Ah, here is a house with a palm tree.* But then he remembered the rest of the clue. *And you find dirt in the ear.* 
He looked at the pristine, swept courtyard of the house. *But the surroundings are clean! This is not the house.*

He walked further and found another house with a palm tree outside. And at the entrance, there was plenty of dirty, muddy water.
*I had better remove my sandals,* he thought, slipping them off before stepping into the muck.

A servant greeted him. "Welcome, sir. My master will see you in a minute. Here is some water to wash your feet with, sir," he said, handing him a surprisingly tiny pot of water.

Hidden behind a window, Nandashree watched closely. *Now let's see how he washes the muck off his feet with that little potful of water.*
The young man did not pour the water over his feet. Instead, he dipped a small rag into the pot and carefully wiped his feet clean. The water in the pot was more than enough for a final rinse.
*He has passed this test too,* Nandashree smiled. *He can manage with the barest minimum.*

She stepped out to greet him. "Welcome, stranger. My father will see you now."
The young man was struck by her intelligence and beauty. 

Later that evening, Vanik Chandra spoke to him. "My parents are in Magadha," the young man shared. "But I cannot tell you who they are."
"You must have good reasons for keeping it a secret. Tell me your name."
"My name is Shrenik."
*Shrenik?* Vanik Chandra thought in surprise. *Why, you have the same name as our banished prince! Poor prince. He might be younger than the crown prince, but of the two he is definitely more fit to be king.*

"Have you also been banished?" Vanik Chandra joked, then quickly waved his hand. "Never mind, Shrenik. Don't answer that question. It was meant as a joke. But you must answer this one. Will you marry my daughter?"
Shrenik looked at Nandashree, whose eyes shone with intelligence. "I will!"

The young man who called himself Shrenik married Nandashree, and the couple spent many happy days together.

Then one day, while Shrenik was walking in the market, a disguised man approached him. "You! Sh...!" the man whispered urgently. "Pardon me, sir, we have been looking for you everywhere. Your father wants to see you. He is ill."

Shrenik hurried back to Nandashree. "My father is ill. I have to leave for Magadha at once," he told her sadly. "I will send for you as soon as he is well."
And Shrenik left.

Days, weeks, and months passed, but there was no sign or message from Shrenik. During this time, Nandashree was delivered of a beautiful baby boy. 
Seeing his daughter's constant sorrow, Vanik Chandra decided, "I am taking you and the child away from this village that has brought you so much misery." They moved away to start a new life.

The child, named Abhaya Kumar, grew up showing promise of outshining both his brilliant parents in intelligence. 
One day, as a young boy, he demanded, "Mother, where is my father? Today you must answer my question."
"Y-your father lives in Magadha," Nandashree hesitated.
"What is his name?"
"He called himself Shrenik."

"Shrenik!" Abhaya Kumar gasped. "Shrenik is our Maharaja's name!"
"Well, your father said his name was Shrenik. And that was all he would divulge about himself. He would not tell us who his parents were so we didn't know where to look for him in Magadha."
Vanik Chandra, listening nearby, added thoughtfully, "He must have given that name because around that time Shrenik the prince had been banished by the late Maharaja Prasenajit."
"When did he leave for Magadha?" Abhaya Kumar asked sharply.
"Before you were born. Around the exact time Prasenajit recalled Shrenik and crowned him king."
"And father called himself Shrenik... and never came back." Abhaya Kumar's eyes narrowed with determination. "Mother, I give you my word. Father will come here and take us with him."

Just then, the village chief walked in. "Abhaya Kumar, I have some news which I am sure will interest you. The king wishes to select a new minister from the villages. He will give candidates a ram which they have to look after for one month. And in that period, the ram must neither gain nor lose even a grain of weight. There is no one in the village willing to take up such an impossible challenge."
Abhaya Kumar smiled confidently. "Uncle, you take it up. Go to the capital and bring the ram."
"You are going to help your uncle become a minister," Vanik Chandra beamed. "Good. I knew you would."

Many villagers from across the kingdom aspired for the ministership, taking a ram home. 
A month later, they all returned to the court of King Shrenik. 
"Maharaj, this is the only ram which has neither gained nor lost weight," the officials reported, pointing to the village chief.
King Shrenik was amazed. "What did you feed him?"
"His normal diet, Maharaj—plenty of rich grass," the chief replied. "But... we tied a fierce wolf next to him. The ram did not lose weight as he was given his fill of food, but he did not gain weight because he was in a constant state of fear!"

"Remarkable!" King Shrenik laughed. "Was this bright idea yours? Did you think of it?"
"To be honest, no, Maharaj," the chief admitted.
"Take me to the man who did."

The king and the chief set out together. "This is where he lives, Maharaj."
As King Shrenik walked into the courtyard, he stopped dead in his tracks. "Nandashree!" 

Shrenik the stranger and Shrenik the king were indeed the same person. 
"I had lost hope of ever finding you!" Shrenik embraced her. "Soon after I became king, I was incredibly busy with a series of wars that had to be fought to secure the kingdom. Then, when I finally went back to your village, I found neither you nor your father there."

"I seek your blessings, father," Abhaya Kumar stepped forward and bowed.
"Maharaj, this is the wise Abhaya Kumar you have come to meet," Vanik Chandra smiled.
"My son!" Shrenik wept with joy.

King Shrenik took his brilliant wife and his genius son back to Magadha, where they lived happily and ruled wisely ever after.
