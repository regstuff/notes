# Vikramaditya

About 1,600 years ago, a vast part of India was ruled by the great and powerful King Samudragupta. Pataliputra was the grand capital of this sprawling Gupta Empire. The King had two sons: the elder, Ramagupta, and the younger, Chandragupta. 

The two princes were entirely different in nature. One day, while playing on the river bank with their friends, a fierce lion suddenly leaped out of the jungle, charging straight at them. 

"Run, Prince Chandragupta, run! A lion is coming this way!" Ramagupta screamed, turning and fleeing for his life in absolute terror. 

But young Chandragupta stood his ground calmly, drawing his sword. "Let it come. I'll face it," he said fearlessly. 

As the mighty beast lunged at him, Chandragupta expertly side-stepped the attack and, with a single, powerful swing of his sword, struck the lion dead. 

When Ramagupta safely returned and saw the dead lion, he tried to cover his cowardice. "He was just trying to show off," he sneered dismissively to the other boys. 

But King Samudragupta knew better. When his soldiers reported the incredible feat, the King smiled proudly. "That's like my son." When the cheering crowd brought the young hero back to the palace, the King decided it was time to officially name his heir. 

"How shall I reward you, my son? Ah! You will bear the burdens of this vast kingdom of mine!" the King declared, officially designating the brave Chandragupta as the Crown Prince, bypassing the cowardly elder brother. 

However, a few years later, when the great Samudragupta passed away, the peace of the kingdom was shattered. The ambitious but weak Ramagupta immediately demanded the throne. 

"It's too soon. Your father's ashes are still warm," Chandragupta advised gently. 

But Ramagupta would not wait. Fearing a devastating civil war that could destroy the empire his father had built, the noble Chandragupta made a massive personal sacrifice. "The right of succession is my elder brother's," he announced to the shocked royal priests. He voluntarily stepped down, allowing Ramagupta to be crowned King of the Gupta Empire. 

Ramagupta, thrilled to have power without fighting for it, soon married the incredibly beautiful Princess Dhruva Devi. Dhruva Devi, however, had heard of Chandragupta's heroic deeds and immense sacrifice. Though married to Ramagupta, her deep respect and admiration were reserved entirely for the noble younger brother. 

Some months later, King Ramagupta foolishly decided to go on a military expedition to the southern region of his kingdom. He took his entire royal camp, including the Queen and Chandragupta, with him. 

As they marched into a narrow mountain pass, the blare of enemy trumpets suddenly echoed around them. "Maharaj, the pass ahead is in the hands of the Sakas," a scout rushed back to report. "We can't go farther."

"We are trapped!" Ramagupta panicked instantly, his cowardice showing. "What will happen now? We will be massacred. Find some way out, minister!"

"Find out what the Saka King wants. Offer him a peace treaty," Ramagupta pleaded. 

When the messenger returned from the Saka camp, he was trembling. "Maharaj, I don't dare repeat it! His demand, sir, is shameful! He demands the hand of our Queen, Maharani Dhruva Devi!"

Any true king would have chosen to fight to the death to protect his wife's honor. But the cowardly Ramagupta was terrified of battle. "The security of our country must be our first concern... Then I have to give up Dhruva Devi. Messenger, inform him—I accept!"

When Dhruva Devi learned of this despicable betrayal, she was absolutely devastated. "Isn't there anybody to save me from this mortal insult?" she wept bitterly. Her loyal maid, Madhavi, rushed to Prince Chandragupta and told him everything. 

Chandragupta was furious. He stormed into the King's tent. "Please calm yourself. Nobody dare insult you while I am alive," he promised the weeping Queen. He turned to his brother. "I will never allow this!"

"Then—do you want me killed?" Ramagupta whined pathetically. 

"No, you will not be harmed—nor will the Queen be given up to the Saka King!" Chandragupta declared. "You will know all—in time! But the responsibility will be yours wholly!"

Chandragupta devised a daring, incredibly dangerous plan. On the day Dhruva Devi was to be handed over to the enemy, Chandragupta dressed himself entirely in the Queen's royal garments, heavy jewelry, and a thick veil. Hiding his sharp sword beneath the silks, he stepped into the royal palanquin. 

The palanquin was carried directly into the heavily guarded camp of the Saka King. Expecting to receive the beautiful Queen, the overconfident Saka King ordered all his guards and soldiers to leave his private tent. "Welcome, Maharani Dhruva Devi," he gloated as the veiled figure stepped out. 

Suddenly, the 'Queen' threw off the veil, drew a flashing sword, and lunged. "You scoundrel! Take this—your just meet!" Chandragupta roared, striking the Saka King down before he could even draw his weapon. 

"Be alert, soldiers! No Saka should escape!" Chandragupta shouted to his own hidden men outside. Without their King, the Saka army fell into chaos. In a short, brutal battle, thousands of Saka troops were put to death, and hundreds of officers were taken prisoner. Chandragupta banished the survivors from the land entirely. 

When Chandragupta returned to the Gupta camp victorious, the people went wild with joy. But their joy was mixed with deep anger towards their cowardly King. "Ramagupta is a coward. He has no right to be the King," the soldiers and citizens muttered openly. 

Ramagupta, humiliated and terrified of losing his power to his popular brother, decided to eliminate the threat once and for all. "I cannot for ever close my eyes to realities!" he muttered darkly to himself. 

That night, as Chandragupta slept soundly after the exhausting battle, a dark figure crept into his bed-chamber, raising a deadly dagger to strike. 

Hearing a sound, Chandragupta woke up instantly, grabbed his sword, and furiously fought the assassin in the dark. With a swift, powerful thrust, he fatally wounded the attacker. 

As the man collapsed into the light, Chandragupta gasped in horror. "Oh! God! It is you!" The assassin he had killed in self-defense was his own brother, King Ramagupta. 

The next morning in the royal court, the Chief Minister announced the grim news to the assembly. "Maharaj Ramagupta is dead. In the dead of night, he made a dastardly attempt on Prince Chandragupta's life!"

The crowd, already disgusted with Ramagupta's cowardice and betrayal, felt no pity. "The coward deserved it!" they shouted. "Victory to Chandragupta! He can safeguard the country and the Gupta traditions!"

The ministers unanimously agreed. "The honoured throne of the Guptas should not remain vacant for a moment. Prince Chandragupta must instantly be crowned." 

"And he has the right to marry Maharani Dhruva Devi!" the crowd demanded, knowing the deep, respectful bond between the two. 

The royal priests quickly performed the crowning ceremony, officially making Chandragupta the Emperor of the vast Gupta Empire. Immediately after, his joyful wedding to Dhruva Devi was solemnised amidst cheering crowds. 

Taking the throne, the new Emperor made a solemn vow to his people. "My countrymen, as you will be my witness, I will not rest until I have driven every single Saka out of our country."

Chandragupta kept his word brilliantly. He proved to be a magnificent, deeply cultured, and fiercely brave ruler. Under his reign, art and literature flourished, with great poets like Kalidasa gracing his court. 

"The people here adore you because you freed them from Saka domination," Dhruva Devi told him lovingly one evening. "They all refer to you as *Vikramaditya* (The Sun of Valour)."

"But the Sakas still rule in Gujarat," Chandragupta Vikramaditya reminded her, his eyes flashing with determination. "And near the borders of this country the Kushanas harass our people."

Spurred by his duty and his wife's encouragement, Emperor Chandragupta Vikramaditya soon left on a massive military campaign. Months later, joyous news arrived in Pataliputra. He had decisively crushed the Sakas of Gujarat, permanently removing the last vestige of their foreign rule from Indian soil. 

Later, his unstoppable armies marched even further, going as far as Bactria, beyond the towering Hindu Kush mountains, to utterly defeat the powerful Kushana Empire as well. 

Chandragupta Vikramaditya earned a legendary, immortal place in history. He proved to the world that the mere love of power is not all that is needed to create and sustain a great empire. Also needed are the love and support of a wise partner, immense personal courage, and good, old-fashioned honour.
