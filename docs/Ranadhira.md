# Ranadhira

Ranadhira Kanthirava Narasaraja Wadeyar (1638-1659) was a legendary, incredibly valiant king of Mysore. Known for his extraordinary physical strength, razor-sharp intellect, and immense generosity, he transformed Mysore into a major, formidable power in South India. 

But before he could even ascend the throne, the young prince had to survive a treacherous, deadly web of deceit. 

The story begins in the neighboring kingdom of Tiruchirapalli. The local chieftain was a massive patron of wrestling and proudly sponsored an undefeated, highly arrogant wrestler named Mahamalla. Boasting of his unmatched strength, Mahamalla brazenly hung his own loin-cloth over the main gateway of the city fort. "The one who dares to meet me in the ring will have to remove it!" he challenged. 

The insulting display deeply disgusted the local populace, but no one dared to touch it. One day, two travelers rode past the gates. The younger one, a fiery, muscular youth, was so outraged by the arrogant display that he drew his sword and effortlessly sliced the loin-cloth down! 

The furious chieftain immediately surrounded the young stranger with armed guards and forced him into the wrestling arena with the giant Mahamalla. 

As the match began, Mahamalla confidently planned to use a highly secretive, lethal strike—the *Vajra Mushthi* (Adamantine Fist)—to secretly cut the young man's throat during a grapple. But the young stranger moved with the blinding speed of lightning. Anticipating the dirty trick, the youth smoothly stepped back, snatched a sharp spear from a nearby guard, and lightly tapped Mahamalla on the head with it, instantly killing the giant! 

"Your wicked wrestler used to play precisely the same cowardly trick on others," the young man declared loudly to the stunned crowd. Before the furious chieftain could react, the mysterious visitor slipped away into the confusion, leaving behind a simple message scrawled on the fort wall: *“The man who killed that arrogant villain is a common wrestler from Mysore.”*

That "common wrestler" was none other than Prince Ranadhira Narasaraja of Mysore! 

As Ranadhira rode back to his quiet estate at Terakanambi, he intercepted a group of royal messengers from Mysore. They carried an official scroll from Vikramaraya, the powerful *Dalwai* (Chief Minister) of Mysore. 
*"The King of Mysore has died suddenly after a brief illness,"* the scroll read. *"As the deceased king had no direct heir, we have decided to crown you the new King."*

Ranadhira was immediately suspicious. Just moments later, a secret messenger from the Queen Mother arrived, begging Ranadhira to rush to the capital to *help* the young king, proving that the king was not actually dead! 

The messenger revealed the dark truth: For years, the wicked Chief Minister Vikramaraya had kept the pleasure-loving King as a mere puppet, ruling Mysore himself. However, ten days ago, the Queen Mother had finally shamed the King into asserting his true royal authority. When the King inspected the royal treasury, he discovered a massive deficit caused by Vikramaraya's rampant corruption! 

Realizing his absolute power was threatened, the desperate Vikramaraya secretly ordered the royal physician, Bommarasa, to prepare a highly toxic poison. Tragically, the Queen Mother, completely unaware of the plot, had personally forced her sick son to drink the "medicine." The King died in absolute agony, leaving Vikramaraya in total control. 

Furious at the cowardly assassination of his cousin, Ranadhira immediately rode to Mysore. 

Vikramaraya warmly received the young prince, secretly planning to assassinate him before the coronation. The next morning, Vikramaraya brought two massive, highly trained assassins disguised as wrestlers—Ranga and Channa—to the palace gymnasium, hoping they would easily crush Ranadhira. 

To Vikramaraya's absolute shock, Ranadhira completely overpowered both giant wrestlers with effortless ease. "Remember," Ranadhira advised the defeated giants gently, "your God-given strength is to be used to help others, never to cause harm." Deeply moved by his incredible strength and noble modesty, Ranga and Channa's loyalties instantly shifted. 

Later that day, Ranadhira confronted Vikramaraya in his private chambers. "I know exactly how the King met his end," Ranadhira stated coldly. He ordered the royal guards to arrest the Chief Minister for high treason. 

But the guards did not move. They were firmly under Vikramaraya's payroll. 
Vikramaraya laughed wickedly. "You are not yet a king, my prince. Nor will you ever be!" He stepped out, securely locked the heavy doors from the outside, and ordered Ranga and Channa to enter the chamber that night and murder the trapped prince. 

However, Ranadhira was not a man to be caged. Using his unbelievable, superhuman strength, he literally bent the thick iron window bars apart with his bare hands and escaped into the night! 

Meanwhile, Ranga and Channa, having completely switched sides, ambushed the wicked Vikramaraya outside his own residence. Before they could kill him, Ranadhira arrived. "Stop!" he commanded. With a single, devastating blow of his iron fist, Ranadhira knocked the treacherous Chief Minister unconscious. 

The next morning, the horrific truth of the King's assassination was publicly revealed. The people of Mysore wildly cheered their heroic savior. Vikramaraya was rightfully hanged for his heinous crimes, and Ranadhira Narasaraja Wadeyar was gloriously crowned the new King of Mysore. 

His spectacular reign was marked by unmatched bravery. When the powerful Sultan of Bijapur sent his massive army under Ranadulla Khan to invade Mysore, they successfully breached the walls of the capital, Srirangapatna. But King Ranadhira personally led a terrifying, ferocious frontal counter-attack on horseback, single-handedly cutting down the enemy ranks and forcing the entire Bijapur army into a humiliating retreat! 

Even his old enemies could not defeat him. The angry chieftain of Tiruchirapalli, still seeking revenge for Mahamalla's death, secretly dispatched twenty-five highly trained, heavily armed assassins to Mysore. They managed to corner Ranadhira alone in his bedchamber. 

Completely unarmed and unarmored, the fearless King didn't even flinch. Moving with the terrifying speed and power of a raging tiger, he fought all twenty-five armed assassins simultaneously with his bare hands, effortlessly breaking their bones and shattering their swords. By the time his royal guards finally broke down the doors, they found their invincible King standing calmly amidst twenty-five groaning, thoroughly defeated men! 

"Send their broken bodies back to the chieftain of Tiruchirapalli with my royal compliments," Ranadhira smiled grimly. 

Legends of King Ranadhira's superhuman might, brilliant intellect, and absolute fearlessness rapidly spread across India, chilling the hearts of rival kings and ensuring that the glorious kingdom of Mysore remained incredibly strong, prosperous, and utterly invincible throughout his legendary reign.
