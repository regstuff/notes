# The Mystery of the Missing Gifts - 1. Mystery of the Missing Gifts

Bhola was a lovable but incredibly lazy old man. His wife, Nimmo, was hardworking and short-tempered, always frustrated by her husband's idleness. 

One day, before leaving for the fields, Nimmo laid out some rice to dry in the sun. "Will you keep an eye on this rice and see that the sparrows don't get at it?" she asked Bhola. 
"You can trust me, Nimmo. I'll keep a close watch," Bhola promised. 

But as soon as she left, Bhola reasoned, "What is the need to watch the sparrows? They might pick up a grain or two. So what?" He promptly fell fast asleep. When Nimmo returned, she was furious to find that the sparrows had eaten every single grain of rice. 

To appease his angry wife, Bhola set a trap the next day using bird-lime (a sticky substance) and managed to catch the entire flock of sparrows. 

As he was about to collect them, the king of the sparrows spoke up. "Please, have mercy on us and set us free. I'll reward you if you do. Come to my house tomorrow."

Bhola kindly set the birds free. The next morning, he set out to collect his reward. On his way, he stopped at his daughter's house. Seeing her neighbour's large herd of cattle, he decided to ask the sparrow-king for a cow. 

The sparrow-king happily gave him a magical cow named *Sonar Barsa*. "Her dung turns into gold the moment it touches the ground," the king explained. 

Overjoyed, Bhola headed home. The cow dropped golden dung all along the way, and Bhola ran back and forth collecting it until he was exhausted. He decided to rest for the night at his daughter's house. 

He foolishly told his daughter the secret of the magical cow. That night, while Bhola slept, his greedy daughter stole *Sonar Barsa* and replaced it with an ordinary cow. 

The next day, Bhola proudly presented the cow to his wife, Nimmo. They waited patiently, but when the cow finally dropped its dung, it was just ordinary dung! Nimmo was furious, thinking Bhola was a fool. 

Confused and dejected, Bhola returned to the sparrow-king. Realizing someone had tricked Bhola, the king gave him another gift: a magic pot and spoon. "Stir water in this pot with this spoon, and it will turn into any food you desire!" 

Once again, Bhola stopped at his daughter's house and naively demonstrated the magic pot by turning water into delicious *dal* and *roti*. That night, the wicked daughter stole the magic pot and spoon, replacing them with ordinary ones. 

The next day, Bhola confidently told Nimmo to command the pot to make food. When nothing happened, Nimmo angrily threw the water in Bhola's face. 

Poor Bhola returned to the sparrow-king a third time. "My friend, someone is playing tricks on you," the sparrow-king said. This time, he gave Bhola a golden rope and a stick. "These should help you retrieve the other two gifts."

Bhola stopped at his daughter's house one last time. Assuming the new items were also incredibly valuable, the greedy daughter snuck into his room at night to steal them. 

But the moment she touched the golden rope, it magically coiled itself tightly around her! Then, the stick flew into the air and began whacking her soundly on the head and back. 

"OW-W-W! FATHER! HELP!" she screamed. 

Bhola woke up and saw his daughter trapped and being beaten by the magic stick. "I don't understand! Why have you coiled that rope around you?" he asked innocently. 

"FATHER! CALL IT OFF. I'LL RETURN ALL THE GIFTS I STOLE!" she cried, confessing to stealing the magic cow and the magic pot. 

Bhola finally understood her treachery. He called off the magic stick and rope, collected his rightful gifts, and returned home to Nimmo. 

When Nimmo saw the endless supply of gold and delicious food the gifts produced, she was overjoyed and never taunted her husband again.


# The Mystery of the Missing Gifts - 2. The Sound of Music

In the village of Ragihalli, there lived two brothers. The elder brother, Anna, was the richest man in the village, but he was incredibly greedy and cruel. The younger brother, Bada Tamma, was very poor but possessed a kind and generous heart. 

One day, a wandering *sanyasi* (holy man) came begging for alms. Anna chased him away angrily. But when the *sanyasi* went to Bada Tamma's humble hut, the poor farmer cheerfully shared what little food he had, going hungry himself. 

Grateful for the delicious meal, the *sanyasi* gave Bada Tamma a magical wooden flute. "Play it in your field. You will soon reap a rich harvest," he promised. 

Bada Tamma had the flute but no seeds to sow. He went to his rich brother, Anna, and borrowed a sack of paddy. Greedy Anna agreed only on the condition that Bada Tamma give him nine-tenths of his harvest in return. 

Bada Tamma sowed the seeds and then played the magical flute in his field from dawn to dusk. Miraculously, his crops ripened incredibly fast, yielding a massive, rich harvest in just a few days. 

When Anna saw this, he was amazed. Bada Tamma honestly explained the magical power of the flute. Immediately, Anna asked to borrow it. Bada Tamma gladly handed it over. 

Anna then demanded his nine-tenths share of Bada Tamma's harvest, taking away almost everything his poor brother had grown. 

Bada Tamma didn't mind. "I will sow my share and raise another crop with the help of my flute," he thought. He went to Anna to ask for his flute back. 

But Anna had deceitfully replaced the magical flute with an ordinary wooden one. "Here you are! And don't come to me again," Anna sneered. 

Bada Tamma played the new flute in his field, but nothing happened. He realized his brother had tricked him. When he confronted Anna, the greedy brother denied everything and chased him away. 

Dejected, Bada Tamma met the *sanyasi* again and told him his sad tale. The *sanyasi* couldn't give him another flute, but he gave him a magical drum instead. "Why don't you go to the *panchayat* (village council) and lodge a complaint against your brother?" the *sanyasi* suggested. 

Bada Tamma took the drum and went to the village elders. However, Anna, using his wealth and influence, easily convinced the elders that he was innocent. 

"Why should a rich man like him dupe anyone?" the elders agreed. "Bada Tamma is an ungrateful wretch! You shall be fined for bringing a false complaint against a respectable man."

Furious at the terrible injustice, Bada Tamma shouted, "I don't expect justice from you!" He raised his stick and struck the magical drum. *DUM!*

The moment the drum sounded, a strange magic took hold of the village elders. They were forcefully pulled to their feet. 

As Bada Tamma began to beat a steady rhythm on the drum— *DAMATAKA, DAMATAKA*— the elders' feet began to move uncontrollably. They were forced to dance wildly to the beat of the drum!

Bada Tamma kept beating the drum faster and faster. The exhausted elders danced harder and harder, their joints aching and backs hurting. They tried to sit, but the magic violently jerked them back to their feet. 

"OH BADA TAMMA! PLEASE STOP!" Anna screamed, dancing uncontrollably. "I ADMIT MY GUILT! I will return your flute. Stop beating the drum, please!"

Bada Tamma finally stopped playing. The exhausted elders collapsed in relief. Anna quickly returned the genuine magical flute. 

Bada Tamma went on to raise fresh, bountiful crops with his flute, while the *sanyasi* borrowed the magical drum to go "work wonders" in the neighbouring village!
