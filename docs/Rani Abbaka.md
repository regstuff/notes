# Rani Abbakka: The Queen Who Knew No Fear

In the sixteenth century, the small but incredibly wealthy principality of Ullal, located on the western coast of Karnataka, was ruled by Queen Hiriya Abbakka. Throughout her life, she had fiercely resisted the growing, oppressive power of the Portuguese, who aggressively demanded heavy tributes and sought to entirely monopolize the lucrative spice trade. 

Despite her incredible bravery, the Portuguese had brutally attacked Ullal, laid a devastating siege, and forced her into a humiliating surrender. As Queen Hiriya lay on her death-bed, her daughter and successor, Abbakka Devi, sat weeping by her side. 

"Alas, Abbakka!" the dying queen whispered bitterly. "I lie here helpless, while the Portuguese... The shame of that terrible defeat still rankles in my mind. Oh! Will my soul ever find peace?"
"Hush, Mother! You must not tire yourself," Princess Abbakka swore a solemn, tearful oath. "I am here. And I will not rest until I have completely driven them out of the west coast!"

Following her mother's death, Abbakka Devi was formally crowned the new Queen of Ullal. True to her unbreakable vow, her very first royal decree was shocking to her cautious court. She turned to her husband, King Veera Narasimha of the neighboring kingdom of Bangadi. 

"I will officially stop paying tribute to the Portuguese," Queen Abbakka announced firmly. "I will not sell them our spices and cloth either."

King Veera Narasimha was absolutely terrified by the bold decision. "Isn't that a rather rash decision?" he argued nervously. "The Portuguese might think that I am in league with you, and Bangadi too may have to face their terrifying wrath! Mangalore is just across the river Netravati. They could easily use their massive fort there to attack us. You must quit the fort of Ullal!"

"I absolutely refuse to be cowed by them," Abbakka replied coldly. "I am determined to completely drive them out. Their only real advantage is the fort they have heavily built for themselves at Mangalore."

Realizing his brave wife would not yield, the cowardly King Veera Narasimha furiously stomped out of the palace, completely abandoning her and returning to Bangadi. 

Unfazed by her husband's shameful desertion, Queen Abbakka bravely visited the ancient temple of Lord Lokeshwar in Mangalore the very next day. As she stepped out of the sacred temple, she was suddenly surrounded by a group of heavily armed men. 

"How dare you! Who are you?" the Queen demanded fiercely. 
"We have been secretly sent by our chieftain to aggressively escort you to his court," the leader sneered. 
To her absolute horror, Abbakka realized her own cowardly husband had sent armed mercenaries to kidnap her and forcefully hand her over to the Portuguese to save his own kingdom! 

But the cowardly King had severely underestimated his wife. A loyal band of Ullal soldiers had secretly followed their Queen. They violently fell upon the treacherous kidnappers, utterly routing them and safely escorting Queen Abbakka back to Ullal. 

"How could he stoop so incredibly low?" Abbakka thought in disgust. Having completely severed all ties with her treacherous husband, her very first move was to brilliantly restore the ruined fort of Ullal. She then commissioned the building of a massive, powerful fleet of merchant ships. 

When an arrogant Portuguese envoy arrived to demand the long-overdue annual tribute, Abbakka defiantly pointed to the door. "We are a sovereign country. We don't pay tribute to Portugal. And we absolutely don't take orders from the Portuguese!" 

Defying the Portuguese naval blockade, Abbakka's massive merchant fleet successfully sailed to Arabia, selling their precious spices and cloth for a massive, unprecedented profit. On their return journey, however, heavily armed Portuguese warships violently intercepted them on the high seas. 

A fierce, incredibly bloody naval battle was fought. While the majority of Abbakka's fleet successfully escaped back to Ullal, the brave commander's ship was captured. The ruthless Portuguese commander, Ribero, arrogantly demanded a massive ransom of one thousand gold coins for the commander's release. 

"We will not wait for them to attack," Queen Abbakka declared brilliantly to her alarmed ministers. "We'll carry the war directly into the enemy's camp. We'll take them by absolute surprise—tonight!"

Abbakka strategically allied herself with the incredibly brave Moplah sailors of the coast. Under the dark cover of night, a powerful, combined military expedition was launched against the heavily fortified Portuguese headquarters in Mangalore. 

The Moplahs attacked fiercely from the sea, raining deadly, fire-tipped arrows directly onto the Portuguese warships anchored in the harbor, setting the entire enemy fleet ablaze in a spectacular inferno! Simultaneously, Abbakka's elite land forces aggressively stormed the massive Mangalore fort from the rear. 

Caught completely off guard by the brilliant, two-pronged night assault, the Portuguese defenses completely crumbled. The gates of the fort were forced open from within, and Abbakka's soldiers violently stormed in. Commander Ribero barely managed to escape with his life, abandoning the fort. 

The victorious Ullal army successfully rescued their captured commander, forcefully seized all the massive gold reserves stored in the fort as rightful compensation for their fallen soldiers, and triumphantly returned to Ullal with captured Portuguese ships. 

When the humiliating, unprecedented news of the total fall of Fort Mangalore reached Goa, the furious Portuguese Governor reacted incredibly sharply. He immediately dispatched a massive, heavily armed military force led by General Coutinho to absolutely crush Ullal. 

Coutinho's massive army brutally laid siege to the fort of Ullal, relentlessly bombarding the walls with heavy cannons. But Queen Abbakka was entirely fearless. 

"My brave soldiers," the dauntless Queen addressed her men from the ramparts, "this shall be the absolutely decisive war. Let us fight with all our might and utterly defeat the Portuguese once and for all. Victory to Ullal!"

Instead of cowering behind the walls, Abbakka dramatically threw open the massive main gates of the fort and personally led a terrifying, fiercely aggressive frontal cavalry charge directly into the heart of the Portuguese army! 

The sheer, suicidal ferocity of the unexpected Rajput charge completely shattered the Portuguese lines. Unable to withstand the terrifying, relentless onslaught led by the fearless Queen herself, the massive Portuguese army completely broke ranks. After just four days of incredibly bloody fighting, the invading army beat a hasty, humiliating retreat, never daring to trouble Ullal again. 

"Mother, your greatest dream has finally come true," Queen Abbakka whispered, looking out over the free, unburdened sea. "Ullal is finally free!"

Queen Abbakka's legendary, superhuman courage broke the invincible myth of Portuguese naval supremacy. Years later, she actively helped King Venkatappa Nayak of Keladi to permanently capture the fort of Mangalore, ensuring that the ruthless Portuguese were eventually driven completely out of the western coast, cementing her immortal legacy as India's first female freedom fighter.
