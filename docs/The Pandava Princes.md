# The Pandava Princes - 1. The Pandava Princes

Thousands of years ago, the kingdom of Hastinapura was ruled by the blind King Dhritarashtra. He had a hundred sons, known as the Kauravas, the eldest and most wicked of whom was Duryodhana. 

Dhritarashtra's brother, Pandu, had previously ruled the kingdom but retired to the forest due to illness. There, his two wives, Kunti and Madri, gave birth to five strong sons: Yudhishthira, Bheema, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva. They were known as the Pandavas. Growing up in the harsh jungle under the guidance of great sages made them sturdy, fearless, and wise. 

When Pandu and Madri tragically died, Sage Vyasa and Kunti brought the five young Pandava princes back to the grand palace in Hastinapura. 

The blind King Dhritarashtra welcomed them, and their great-grand-uncle Bheeshma loved them dearly. However, as the boys grew, the Kauravas, especially Duryodhana and Duhshasana, became intensely jealous of the Pandavas' superior strength and skills. They were particularly terrified of Bheema's immense physical power and Arjuna's unmatched skill in archery, which he learned from their great tutor, Dronacharya. 

Consumed by hatred and fear that the noble Yudhishthira would rightfully be crowned the next king, Duryodhana constantly plotted against the Pandavas. 

Once, during a picnic, Duryodhana poisoned Bheema's food and threw his unconscious body into the Ganga river, hoping he would impale himself on iron spikes or be killed by poisonous water snakes. Miraculously, the snake venom counteracted the poison in Bheema's food, and he returned safely to the palace, much to Duryodhana's shock. 

Seeing the Pandavas' growing popularity among the people, Duryodhana convinced his blind father to send the Pandavas away to a town called Varanavati for a festival. Secretly, Duryodhana hired a sly architect, Purochana, to build a magnificent palace for them made entirely of highly flammable lac and jute. 

Fortunately, the wise minister Vidura warned the Pandavas of the trap. With the help of a loyal miner sent by Vidura, the Pandavas dug a secret escape tunnel. When Purochana finally set fire to the palace, he was caught in his own trap and burned alive, while the Pandavas and Kunti safely escaped into the dense forest. 

Back in Hastinapura, the Kauravas secretly rejoiced, believing their rivals were dead. 

Meanwhile, the Pandavas wandered through the perilous jungles. Bheema's great strength protected them. He defeated and killed the terrifying Rakshasa chief Hidimbasura, and later, while staying in the village of Ekachakrapura disguised as poor Brahmans, Bheema slew the monstrous giant Bakasura, freeing the villagers from his cruel tyranny. 

Sometime later, the Pandavas heard that King Drupada was holding a grand *swayamvara* (an archery contest) to find a suitable husband for his beautiful daughter, Draupadi. The challenge was seemingly impossible: to string a massive bow and shoot the eye of a revolving wooden fish, looking only at its reflection in a pool of water below. 

Hundreds of mighty princes, including Duryodhana and Karna, failed. But Arjuna, still disguised as a poor Brahman, stepped forward. With incredible skill, he effortlessly strung the bow and hit the target five times in quick succession! 

Draupadi garlanded the "Brahman," causing an uproar among the humiliated princes. A fight broke out, but Bheema and Arjuna easily fended them off and returned to their mother with their new bride. 

When Kunti, not knowing what they had brought, told them to "share it equally," the five brothers were bound to Draupadi as their shared wife. 

When the news reached Hastinapura that the Pandavas were alive and had formed a powerful alliance with King Drupada, Dhritarashtra was forced to invite them back. To avoid a civil war, the kingdom was divided. The Pandavas were given the barren wasteland of Khandavaprastha. 

With the help and guidance of Lord Krishna, their cousin and greatest ally, the Pandavas transformed the ruins into a splendid, magnificent new capital city called Indraprastha. 

To establish Yudhishthira as the supreme emperor, Lord Krishna advised them to perform the grand *Rajasuya* sacrifice. But first, they had to defeat the wicked and powerful King Jarasandha of Magadha. Disguised as Brahmans, Krishna, Bheema, and Arjuna challenged Jarasandha, and Bheema killed him in a fierce wrestling duel. 

With Jarasandha defeated, kings from all over the land gathered at Indraprastha for the *Rajasuya* sacrifice. When the first honour was given to Lord Krishna, the arrogant King Shishupala violently objected and attacked Krishna. In a flash, Krishna's divine discus (*Sudarshana Chakra*) severed Shishupala's head. 

With their enemies vanquished and their supreme authority acknowledged by all, the Pandava princes celebrated their victory, ruling their new kingdom with justice and righteousness.
