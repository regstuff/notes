# Pradyumna

Long ago, Kama, the handsome god of love, committed a grave error. He mischievously fired his arrows of love to distract Lord Shiva while the great god was deep in severe meditation. Enraged by the sudden interruption, Lord Shiva fiercely opened his blazing third eye, instantly burning Kama to a pile of cinders. 

Kama's beautiful wife, Rati, was absolutely inconsolable. Bereft and heartbroken, she built a funeral pyre and prepared to end her own life by leaping into the flames. Suddenly, a booming, heavenly voice stopped her. 
"Wait!" the divine voice commanded. "Kama shall be yours once more. When Lord Shiva marries, Kama's ashes will be revived. He will be reborn as Pradyumna, the eldest son of Lord Krishna and Rukmini. Go and wait for him."

Clinging to this desperate hope, Rati disguised herself as a humble maid named Mayavati. She traveled to the kingdom of the wicked Asura (demon) king, Shambara, and managed to secure a job working in the royal palace kitchens, silently biding her time. 

Meanwhile, in the glorious city of Dwaraka, immense joy filled the streets. A beautiful baby boy had been born to Lord Krishna and Queen Rukmini. The child was indeed Kama reborn, and they lovingly named him Pradyumna. 

But their joy was terribly short-lived. A week after the baby's birth, the evil Asura king Shambara had a terrifying, prophetic dream. *“Beware, wicked Shambara,”* a voice echoed in his nightmare. *“You shall be killed by Pradyumna, Krishna’s infant son.”*

Waking up in a cold sweat, the cowardly Shambara immediately used his dark magic to make himself invisible. He flew silently through the night sky to Dwaraka, crept into Queen Rukmini's bedchamber, and ruthlessly stole the sleeping baby right from his cradle. 

To ensure the terrifying prophecy would never come true, Shambara flew far out over the deep, churning ocean and mercilessly flung the helpless infant into the dark, raging waters below, believing the child would surely drown. 

However, destiny had other plans. As the baby sank into the ocean, a massive, monstrous fish swam by and swallowed little Pradyumna whole! Miraculously, the baby was completely unharmed inside the fish's massive belly. 

A few days later, a group of local fishermen caught that exact same giant fish in their heavy nets. "What a magnificent catch!" they cheered. "It will make a fine meal for King Shambara." 

The fishermen proudly carried the massive fish directly to the Asura king's palace kitchens. The head cook ordered the maid, Mayavati, to cut it open and clean it for the king's dinner. 

As Mayavati carefully slit the fish's stomach open, she gasped in sheer astonishment. Lying perfectly safe inside was a beautiful, gurgling baby boy! 
Utterly confounded, she quickly scooped the baby up and washed him. "Poor, poor baby," she whispered, holding him close. "Whose child are you? How did you come here?"

Suddenly, the great celestial sage Narada magically appeared in the kitchen. He smiled warmly at the confused maid. 
"He is none other than your husband, Kama!" Narada revealed. "When Kama was burnt to ashes, he begged Shiva that he might be reborn as Krishna's son. This is Pradyumna. Shambara stole him and threw him into the sea, but he is destined to be Shambara's doom. Hide him well, Rati, and rear him with care."

Overjoyed beyond measure, Rati (as Mayavati) secretly raised the baby in the hidden corners of the kitchen. As the years passed, Pradyumna grew into a spectacularly handsome, incredibly strong young man, looking exactly like his divine father, Lord Krishna. 

When Pradyumna finally came of age, Mayavati gently pulled him aside and revealed his true, glorious identity. "You are the son of Lord Krishna and Queen Rukmini," she explained, her eyes shining. "And I am Rati, your wife from your past life. You are destined to slay the wicked Shambara. But beware, he is a master of dark magic."

Over the next few weeks, Mayavati painstakingly taught Pradyumna powerful, ancient magical spells and illusions that she had learned, perfectly equipping him to counter the Asura king's dark trickery. 

When he was fully prepared, Pradyumna boldly marched directly to the front gates of Shambara's palace. 
"Come out, O valiant Asura!" Pradyumna roared, his voice echoing through the courtyard, dripping with sarcasm. "Come out, you coward who heroically stole a helpless baby from his gentle mother's side! Come out and fight me!"

Hearing the deafening taunts, Shambara burned with uncontrollable rage. He snatched up his massive, spiked mace and rushed out. A terrifying, earth-shattering combat ensued. The two mighty warriors clashed, their weapons striking like thunderbolts. 

Realizing he was losing the physical fight against the incredibly strong young prince, the cowardly Shambara resorted to his dark magic, instantly making himself completely invisible to strike from the shadows. 

But Pradyumna was perfectly prepared. Quickly chanting a powerful spell taught by Mayavati, he shattered the illusion, forcing the invisible Asura back into plain sight. 

Before the shocked Shambara could cast another spell, Pradyumna summoned a blinding, brilliant light that entirely dazzled the Asura's eyes. Seizing the moment, Pradyumna swung his heavy mace with all his might, smashing Shambara to the ground. In a flash, Pradyumna drew his shining sword and cleanly severed the wicked king's head from his body. 

With the tyrant finally dead, Pradyumna rushed back to Mayavati. "Come, my beloved," he smiled. "Let us not waste time. Mother Rukmini has suffered enough." 

Using their divine powers, the couple soared high into the sky, flying swiftly toward the golden city of Dwaraka. 

When they landed and walked into the inner apartments of Krishna's palace, the royal ladies gasped in utter confusion. The handsome youth looked so astonishingly like Lord Krishna that they initially mistook him for the Lord himself. 

Queen Rukmini stared at the young man, her heart suddenly pounding in her chest. *He looks exactly like my Lord,* she thought, tears welling in her eyes. *His features, his gait, his smile... My own son, if he were alive, would be exactly the same age as this boy.*

Just then, Lord Krishna walked into the room, accompanied by Sage Narada. The all-knowing Krishna smiled warmly at his bewildered wife. 

"Rejoice, Rukmini!" Narada announced happily. "Your long-lost son has finally returned to you. This is Pradyumna, who was stolen from his cradle, and the maiden beside him is none other than his devoted wife, Rati."

Overwhelmed with sheer, unimaginable joy, Queen Rukmini rushed forward and tearfully embraced her beloved son and her new daughter-in-law. 

The news spread like wildfire. The entire city of Dwaraka erupted into massive, joyous celebrations, proudly welcoming home their valiant prince who had miraculously returned from the dead to claim his rightful place.
