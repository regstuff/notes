# The Mother

The people of Liglig, a state in Nepal, were gearing up for a unique tradition. They were about to hold their annual race, a grueling run uphill and downhill, to choose their new king for the year. 

"What makes them think that a person who can run fast will make a good king?" mused Dravyashah, the younger brother of Narharishah, the King of neighboring Lamjung. Dravyashah was an adventurous, fearless, and ambitious young man. "What Liglig needs is a king who is wise and valiant."

"In that case, you would be the ideal ruler," his friend suggested enthusiastically. 

Dravyashah frowned. "But I am no good at running."

His friend grinned mischievously. "Who says you have to be? I have a plan. Listen..."

On Dussehra day, the day of the great race, the three friends rode to Liglig. As the race began at the foot of the hill, Dravyashah, as previously arranged, hid himself near the top of the hill, completely out of sight. The grueling race continued, but just as the exhausted lead runner flashed past his hiding spot, Dravyashah sprang into action!

Fresh and full of energy, he joined the race near the very end. Sprinting uphill, he easily overtook the tired runner, reached the temple atop the hill first, and triumphantly received the victory garland from the unsuspecting priest. Dravyashah then ran downhill to the cheering crowds below.

"Congratulations, young man! What's your name?" the people asked, amazed by his apparent stamina. 

"Dravyashah, sir," he replied confidently.

"You shall be our king for this year. Raja Dravyashah ko jai!" the crowd roared. 

Once installed on the throne, Dravyashah wasted no time. "What is your first command, Maharaj?" his ministers asked.

"From now on, the custom of selecting a king every year shall be stopped forthwith," Dravyashah declared firmly. "Under one permanent king, Liglig will prosper and flourish." 

With his rule secured, he raised a powerful army and swiftly began extending his territory, proving himself to be a highly capable leader despite his trickery. 

One day, Dravyashah made a visit back to Lamjung to meet his mother, Queen Basantvati. "Salutations, mother!" he said proudly, falling at her feet. "With your blessings, your younger son too has become a king today."

"May God bless you, my son," she smiled warmly.

Dravyashah then went to see his elder brother, King Narharishah. "Well done, Dravya!" Narharishah congratulated him. "You have brought glory to Lamjung—and to me! I, the Lord of Lamjung, will be the Lord of Liglig too! My treasury will now overflow..."

"Pardon me for interrupting you, brother," Dravyashah cut in. "As my elder brother, you are no doubt the master of the kingdom we inherited from our father. But the kingdom I have won shall be mine and mine alone."

Narharishah's face darkened with fury. "You are drunk with success! Go home and think it over!"

But Dravyashah walked out, returned to his capital, raised an even larger army, and continued extending his own territory. When Narharishah received news of this, he demanded tribute. Dravyashah refused. "I had made it clear to him long ago. There is no question of my paying any tribute."

Furious at the defiance, Narharishah plotted his revenge. "If he thinks he can bully me into surrendering, he'd better think afresh! We'll attack him in the month of Magha after the dip in the holy Chepe river."

When Queen Basantvati heard of this impending clash, she was horrified. "War between sons of the same mother! No! Never!"

When the holy month of Magha arrived, King Narharishah set out with the Queen Mother and his family for the annual dip in the river Chepe. King Dravyashah arrived at the opposite bank at the same time.

Determined to seek his mother's blessings, Dravyashah boarded a boat to cross the river. "My lord! Don't go alone. Take some soldiers with you," his men warned.

"Don't be ridiculous," Dravyashah replied. "I am going to ask the blessings of my mother."

He crossed the river alone and walked straight towards Basantvati. "May you live long, son. May you achieve greater glories!" she blessed him. 

Then, Dravyashah turned to his elder brother, seeking his blessing too. But Narharishah backed away with a hostile glare. The tension was thick, and violence seemed inevitable. 

Sensing the impending disaster, Queen Basantvati stepped between them. "I am going to the river. Follow me, Dravya!" she commanded. 

Wading into the chilly waters of the Chepe, she turned to her younger son. "Dravya, I command you to cross the river on my left. Please do as I ask you to!"

"As you wish, mother," Dravyashah agreed, stepping to her left side.

"If you want to extend your kingdom, do so towards the East," Basantvati instructed him firmly. "Don't ever cross Chepe and come towards my right." Dravyashah bowed deeply and accepted her decree.

She then called out to Narharishah, who stood on the bank. "And you, son, are free to extend your kingdom towards the West. But never, ever dream of crossing the river to extend your kingdom towards the East." 

She looked at both her sons with fierce, motherly love. "Chepe is your mother. Crossing her would mean trampling on your mother's bosom. Never do that."

Moved by their mother's powerful words, the two brothers laid down their hostility. They returned to their respective states, and a devastating war was averted. Many decades later, Prithvinarayan Shah, a descendant of Dravyashah, united the two states, laying the foundation for the mighty nation that came to be known as Nepal.
