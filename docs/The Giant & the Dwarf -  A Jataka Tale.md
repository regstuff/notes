# The Giant and the Dwarf - A Jataka Tale - 1. The Giant and the Dwarf

In ancient India there once lived a dwarf. Comical though he looked, he was exceedingly clever. And he had one great talent. He was an excellent archer and people called him Little Bowman. 

One day, he traveled to the city. "Where are you going with that great big bow, good sir?" a man asked. 
"I am going to the palace to offer my services to the king. I am an archer," Little Bowman replied. 

The people around him burst into laughter. "HO HO HO!"
"Be careful, or you may take off with one of the arrows you shoot!" a man mocked. 

*I can't blame them,* Little Bowman thought. *No one believes that a puny fellow like me can handle a bow and arrow. Hmmm... the king too, might throw me out without giving me a chance to prove myself. What I need is an assistant.*

Just then, he saw a huge man weaving baskets. *Like that man! Whew! He is a giant!*

"What are you staring at?" the giant asked. 
"You!" Little Bowman said. "How odd you look! A huge man like you sitting here and weaving baskets! You should be doing something more adventurous, my friend. What is your name?"

"Bhimasena," the giant replied. 
"Why don't you join the king's service as an archer, Bhimasena?" 
"An archer? I don't know anything about archery," Bhimasena said. 
"But I do. I'll do the work. All you have to do is... stand in front," Little Bowman explained his plan. 

Some time later, Bhimasena and Little Bowman went to the palace of the King of Varanasi. 

"Who are you? What business do you have at the palace?" the guards demanded. 
"I... I am Bhimasena, a mighty weav... I mean, archer. I have come to offer my services to the king," Bhimasena stammered, while Little Bowman hid behind him. 

The king was impressed by Bhimasena's massive size. "So you want to enter my services as an archer. You won't find a better archer anywhere," the king declared. "And who is that fellow?"
"He's my servant," Bhimasena replied. 
"Well, good archers are hard to come by these days. We'll find you a place here," the king said. 

So Bhimasena entered the king's service. 

Then some days later— "Did you send for me, Your Majesty?" Bhimasena asked. 
"Yes, Bhimasena. I have a task for you. A ferocious tiger is terrorising my people. None of the men I sent to slay him have come back. They must all be dead. Now I want you to track down the beast and destroy him."

"Whatever you say, Your Majesty," Bhimasena bowed nervously. 

Back at his quarters, Bhimasena panicked. "WHAT! You can't do this to me! I'm going back to my weaving!" he yelled at Little Bowman. 
"Keep calm, Bhimasena, keep calm. You don't need an archer to get that tiger. I'll tell you what to do. Listen..." Little Bowman explained a cunning plan. 

Soon, Bhimasena set out for the village where the tiger had last been seen. 

"Friends, I am a hunter. The king has sent me to rid you of the tiger," Bhimasena announced to the villagers. "But I need men to flush him out of his lair. Once he is out in the open, I'll do the rest."

"You can take all the able-bodied men of this village with you, my lord. We will send word to the other villages, too," they agreed eagerly. 

When Bhimasena finally entered the forest, he had several hundred volunteers with him armed with spears and clubs. 

"Be ready, sir! He'll come out any moment," a villager warned. 
"Did you hear that? It's him! It's the tiger!"

With a terrifying roar, the tiger charged out of the bushes! 

"Where's the hunter? Don't panic! We'll..." the villagers cried, turning to see Bhimasena hiding in the bushes. 

Forced to defend themselves, the massive crowd of villagers set upon the tiger... and beat him to death. 

"They've killed him. Good! Now for the second part of the plan," Bhimasena thought. Pulling out a strong creeper, he rushed back to the villagers. 

"Where is he? Where is he?" Bhimasena yelled. 
"There, sir! We clubbed him to death," a villager pointed proudly. 

"Oh, you fools! The king had commanded me to take him back alive," Bhimasena bellowed angrily. "I had planned to tie a creeper round his neck and... I would like to know who killed him before I got back with my creeper!"

The villagers were terrified. "Sir, we... we didn't know that you... that the king... wanted him alive," they pleaded. "Please don't tell the king we killed him, my lord. There's no telling what he'd do to us. We will pay you well for your silence."

"Oh, well! I'll tell the king I killed him," Bhimasena agreed reluctantly. 

Bhimasena took the dead tiger to the palace. "The mission is accomplished, Your Majesty."
"So I see. You are indeed a valiant hero, Bhimasena. Accept these gifts as a token of our gratitude," the king smiled. 

Later— "Do you still want to go back to your weaving, Bhimasena?" Little Bowman asked. 
"That's a silly question, Little Bowman. I can't imagine how I ever did it," Bhimasena laughed. 

The giant became popular at the court. Then a few weeks later— 

"Your Majesty, we crave your protection," travelers begged the king. "A wild buffalo is terrorising travellers on the southern road. Several men have lost their lives already."

The king sent for his champion. "Go and slay that beast! It should be child's play for you, Bhimasena."

Bhimasena returned to Little Bowman. "A wild buffalo can be more dangerous than a tiger. I hope the Little Bowman doesn't lose his nerve," he worried. 

But when he told the dwarf of his assignment— "It shouldn't be a difficult task for you, Bhimasena," Little Bowman said calmly. 

"For me! What are you trying to say? If you think I am going to wrestle with wild buffaloes you are mistaken," Bhimasena snapped. "I'm not going to endanger my life by doing your work for you. We agreed that you would do all the work, didn't we?"

"Nobody is asking you to endanger your life, Bhimasena. Did you endanger your life when you went to kill the tiger?" Little Bowman pointed out. 

"Ah! The creeper trick! Now why didn't I think of it myself?" Bhimasena realized. 

When Bhimasena came back to the palace a few days later, he had the dead buffalo with him. 

"Look at the size of that buffalo! Our hero is back," the courtiers cheered. 

Bhimasena became even more popular. Several feasts were given in his honour. 

"Tell us how you killed the buffalo, Bhimasena," the king asked. 
"Oh, it was nothing really," Bhimasena boasted loudly. "He did put up a terrific fight. The earth shook under us as we struggled. It took nearly all my strength to subdue him."

Just then, Little Bowman approached. "Sir! Your attendant has come to meet you," a guard announced. 

"Tell him to go away! Can't you see I'm busy?" Bhimasena yelled angrily. 

*Why is he behaving like this?* Little Bowman wondered. *He has been avoiding me ever since he came back. I'll wait for him.*

Later, when Bhimasena walked by, Little Bowman confronted him. "You don't seem pleased to see me. What's the matter?"

"Look, Little Bowman, let's get one thing straight. I don't need you any more," Bhimasena sneered. "All right, you taught me how to deal with the tiger and the buffalo. But what is so great about that, eh? If I had given it some thought, I'm sure I could have come up with a similar, or even better plan, myself. You have served your purpose. Now let us part."

"Oh, so that's it! Success has gone to his head," Little Bowman thought. "I only hope it does not land him into trouble. Anyway, I'll keep out of his way for a while."

Little Bowman, thereafter, kept out of Bhimasena's way. He spent his days in ease and comfort. 

Then one day— "Your Majesty, we are being attacked!" a guard rushed in. "The king of the northern kingdom is on his way here with a large army."

The king read a message from the enemy. "'Send out your champion to fight with our own or surrender.' We shall fight! We shall unleash the mighty Bhimasena on them!"

"Fear not, sir. I shall scatter them like chaff before the wind. I shall crush their champion like this melon!" Bhimasena boasted, smashing a melon with his fist. 

Little Bowman watched from afar. "For all his bravado, he knows nothing about war. I'll have to save that fool somehow," he thought. 

As Bhimasena swaggered out of the palace and walked majestically towards the war elephant which was waiting for him, Little Bowman approached. 

"Bhimasena, you'll need someone to carry your spear, won't you? I'll come with you," he offered. 
"Go, Bhimasena. Crush their champion and win laurels for yourself and our kingdom," the king cheered. 

Crowds cheered the giant as he set out to meet the enemy. 

"Do you see what a great man I've become?" Bhimasena told Little Bowman. "You can imagine the honours they'll heap on me on my triumphant return! Why, they might even want me to become their king! I shall refuse of course, but what if they insist..."

*TRA-TRA-TRA!* An enemy horn sounded in the distance. 

"Er... did you hear something?" Bhimasena asked, his face turning pale. 
"Yes, the sound of a horn," Little Bowman replied. "There they are! The enemy, of course."

Bhimasena began to tremble violently. *TINKLE TINKLE* went his armour. 

"Stop trembling, will you!" Little Bowman hissed. "You'll fall off the elephant and get trampled. Here, I'll tie you to your seat."

"What fierce-looking fellows!" Bhimasena whimpered, looking at the enemy army. 

"What's the matter, Bhimasena? You look troubled. Look at their champion, Little Bowman! He... he looks like a wild animal!" the giant cried. 

"I was so safe and secure when I was a weaver. What will happen to me now?" Bhimasena wept. 
"Leave all the fighting to me. Now let's go," Little Bowman said. 

"Are you ordering the elephant to charge? Yieeee! Stop, Little Bowman! Stop! Let me get down, please," Bhimasena begged. 

"You've turned out to be a great coward, haven't you?" Little Bowman sighed. "Oh, my! You've wet yourself too! Yes, you had better run away, Bhimasena. A battlefield is no place for you."

Little Bowman untied the giant, and Bhimasena scrambled down and ran away as fast as he could. 

"Farewell," Little Bowman called out. "Now to complete the task which my worthy friend set out to do."

He picked up his large bow. In the enemy camp, they watched the elephant approach. "What is that little man up to? Is he their champion? We shall soon know who he is. Let him come closer. Then I'll..."

*TWANG!* 

Little Bowman shot a perfectly aimed arrow that knocked the enemy champion clean off his mount! 

As the enemy soldiers and the elephants rushed about in a state of panic, Little Bowman charged into their midst... pulled the enemy king off his elephant... and sped away with his royal prisoner. 

"Save me! Save me!" the enemy king cried. 

Later, back at the palace— 

"You have performed an amazing feat, Little Bowman. Single-handed you have captured the enemy and put his army to flight," the king praised him. "From now on, you shall have an honoured position in my court— better even than that which was held by that cowardly Bhimasena."

Little Bowman became a great hero and his fame spread far and wide. As for Bhimasena, he was never heard of again.
