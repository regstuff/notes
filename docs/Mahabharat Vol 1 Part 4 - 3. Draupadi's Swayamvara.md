# Draupadi's Swayamvara

King Drupada of Panchala had secretly harbored a lifelong desire to marry his exceptionally beautiful daughter, Draupadi, to Arjuna, the greatest archer of the age. To ensure only a warrior of Arjuna's caliber could win her hand, Drupada devised an impossibly difficult test for her *Swayamvara*.

He constructed a massive, incredibly stiff bow that no ordinary man could string. High above the arena, he suspended a target, and right below it, he placed a rapidly revolving mechanism with a narrow gap. The successful suitor had to string the heavy bow and shoot five arrows simultaneously through the spinning mechanism to hit the target.

Kings and princes from far and wide—including Jarasandha, Shalya, and the Kauravas led by Duryodhana—flocked to the dazzling arena. The Pandavas, still disguised as simple Brahmanas, quietly slipped into the stadium and took their seats among the priests.

When the contest began, Dhrishtadyumna announced the rules. One by one, the greatest Kshatriya kings stepped forward. And one by one, they failed miserably. Some couldn't even lift the bow; others were thrown to the ground violently when the stiff bow snapped back as they tried to string it. 

As the kings grew disheartened, the magnificent Karna strode into the arena. With effortless grace, he picked up the bow, strung it easily, and prepared to fire. 

Seeing him, Draupadi immediately stood up. "I will not wed a Suta (charioteer's son)!" she declared loudly. 
Insulted and enraged, Karna threw down the bow with a bitter laugh and walked away.

When all the Kshatriya kings had failed, Arjuna—dressed in the simple white robes of a Brahmana—stood up from the priest's enclosure and walked toward the bow. The Brahmanas murmured in doubt, fearing that a priest attempting a warrior's task would bring them ridicule. But as Arjuna effortlessly lifted the heavy bow, a hushed awe fell over the stadium.

With fluid, lightning-fast motions, Arjuna strung the bow and fired five arrows straight through the revolving mechanism, striking the target perfectly and bringing it crashing to the ground. The arena erupted in cheers, and celestial flowers rained down from the heavens. Smiling, Draupadi stepped forward and garlanded the triumphant "Brahmana."

However, the defeated Kshatriya kings were incensed. "This is an insult to our warrior caste!" they roared. "How can a mere Brahmana win a princess?" Led by Karna and Shalya, the furious kings drew their weapons and charged at King Drupada to slay him.

Arjuna and Bheema instantly leaped to Drupada's defense. Bheema simply uprooted a massive tree and wielded it like a giant mace, standing beside his brother like the god of death himself. A fierce battle broke out. Arjuna's arrows effortlessly stopped the charging kings, matching even the great Karna shot for shot until Karna, realizing he was fighting an equal, withdrew in respect. Bheema engaged King Shalya in a brutal wrestling match, finally lifting the powerful king high into the air and tossing him to the ground.

Seeing the terrifying prowess of these two "Brahmanas," the kings admitted defeat and withdrew, leaving Arjuna and Bheema to proudly lead Draupadi away from the arena.

Returning to their humble potter's hut at dusk, Arjuna cheerfully called out to his mother from outside. "Mother, look what we have brought today!"
Without looking up from her prayers, Kunti automatically replied, "Enjoy it, all of you together."

When she turned around and saw the beautiful princess, Kunti gasped in horror. "What have I said? My words cannot be taken back, but how can one woman marry five men?"

Yudhishthira, knowing his mother's word was absolute law, made a solemn decision. "Draupadi shall be the wife of us all."

Meanwhile, Dhrishtadyumna had secretly followed the Brahmanas to the potter's hut. Eavesdropping from the shadows, he realized with joy that these "priests" were actually the mighty Pandava princes, whom everyone believed had perished in the Varanavata fire! He rushed back to the palace and excitedly informed his father. 

Overjoyed, King Drupada invited the Pandavas to his palace the next day. When Yudhishthira formally revealed their identities, Drupada was thrilled, knowing his dream of marrying his daughter to Arjuna had come true. 

But when Yudhishthira informed him that Draupadi would marry all five brothers to honor their mother's accidental command, Drupada was deeply shocked. "This is against all laws of society and morality!" he protested. 

Just then, the great Sage Veda Vyasa arrived. He took King Drupada aside and explained a divine secret. "In her previous life, Draupadi was a devout maiden who performed severe penance to obtain a husband," Vyasa revealed. "When Lord Shiva appeared, she excitedly asked for a husband five times! Shiva granted her wish, saying she would have five husbands in her next life. This is divine destiny."

Satisfied by the sage's explanation, King Drupada happily gave his blessing. In a grand, joyous ceremony, Draupadi was married to all five Pandava brothers, uniting the immensely powerful Kuru princes with the formidable kingdom of Panchala.
