# The Fall of Bheeshma

On the ninth day of the great war, with Bheeshma in command, the Kaurava army fell with unprecedented fury upon the Pandava forces. Evil omens appeared; the sun seemed blotted out, and a rain of meteors fell from the sky, foreboding disaster. 

The young hero Abhimanyu led a fierce counter-attack. Chariots, cavalrymen, and elephants fell to his sure arrows. The Kaurava soldiers fled in terror, prompting Duryodhana to send the magical Rakshasa, Alambusha, against him. Alambusha used his magical powers to envelop the Pandava army in absolute darkness. But Abhimanyu released the *Bhaskara* (Sun) weapon, dispelling the darkness and illuminating the battlefield. Having destroyed the illusion, Abhimanyu tormented the Rakshasa with arrows until Alambusha fled in mortal fear. 

Meanwhile, the Kauravas set an entire battalion of elephant-warriors upon Bheema. Descending from his chariot, Bheema attacked them with his massive mace, crushing skulls and shattering bones. The frightened elephants retreated in panic, trampling their own Kaurava soldiers underfoot. 

As the battle raged, Bheeshma continued his relentless slaughter of the Pandava army. Virata and Drupada confronted him, but Bheeshma easily destroyed Virata's banner and bow, and killed his horses and charioteer. The Pandavas were taking heavy losses. 

At the end of the day, a deeply distressed Yudhishthira called a conference. "Only you can help us out of this dilemma, O Krishna," Yudhishthira pleaded. "We do not wish to kill Bheeshma, but if we do not, he may bring about the annihilation of our army."

"Do not grieve," Krishna consoled him. "At your command, I am ready to kill Bheeshma. But Arjuna has vowed to kill him. And as your friend, it is my duty to ensure that his vow does not go unfulfilled."

Together, the Pandavas and Krishna went unarmed to Bheeshma's tent in the dead of night. They paid obeisance to the Grandsire and asked him the fateful question: "O omniscient one! Tell us how you can be killed on the battlefield so as to save our army from slaughter."

Bheeshma smiled, knowing his time had come. "It is true that you cannot win the war as long as I am alive. The Pandavas, along with Krishna, must attack me. But I have taken a vow not to fight against a woman, or one who was formerly a woman. Let Arjuna shoot his arrows at me standing behind Shikhandi. Since I will not attack Shikhandi, I will be vulnerable."

Arjuna was horrified. "How could I bring myself to kill this grand old man in whose lap I have played as a child? How can I attack him when he chooses to be unarmed?"

Krishna reminded Arjuna of his Kshatriya duty. "Remember your Dharma. You vowed to kill him. You cannot win the war without killing him. Moreover, he chose to oppose you in war."

The next morning, the tenth day of the battle, the Pandava army set out with Shikhandi at its head. When the two armies clashed, Shikhandi confronted Bheeshma. 

"I shall kill you today, O Bheeshma," Shikhandi challenged. 

"I shall not take up arms against you," Bheeshma replied calmly, lowering his Gandeeva. 

Seeing this, Arjuna, shielding himself behind Shikhandi, rained an incessant shower of arrows on Bheeshma. The great patriarch was pierced by countless arrows from head to toe. With a few hours to go before sunset, Bheeshma looked to the sky in the east and fell from his chariot. 

He did not touch the ground. His body was suspended in the air, resting entirely on the bed of arrows that pierced his flesh. 

The battle immediately stopped. The gods in heaven lamented the fall of the mighty hero. Holy sages from Mansarovar took the form of swans and came to visit him. Bheeshma assured them, "I shall not die till the sun travels northward (Uttarayan)."

Warriors from both armies, overcome with grief, gathered around the fallen Grandsire. "My head hangs loose," Bheeshma said. "One of you must give me a head-rest."

Several soft and excellent pillows were brought, but Bheeshma refused them all. "These are not fit for a soldier." He looked at Arjuna. "O Arjuna! You have given me the most appropriate bed. Now, give me a head-rest befitting my bravery and courage."

Understanding his Grandsire's wish, Arjuna set three arrows on his Gandeeva and shot them into the ground directly under Bheeshma's head, supporting it perfectly. Bheeshma was pleased. "Give up strife now, O Kings," he urged Duryodhana and the others. "Let my death be the harbinger of peace." But his words of wisdom did not appeal to Duryodhana.

Faint with exhaustion and pain, Bheeshma uttered one word: "W-A-T-E-R!"

The kings rushed to bring pitchers brimming with cool water, but Bheeshma would not accept them. "I do not desire these. I wish to see Arjuna."

As Arjuna arrived, Bheeshma said, "O Arjuna! You alone can provide me with the divine water I wish to drink."

Arjuna climbed up his chariot, invoked the *Parjanya* weapon, and shot an arrow deep into the earth. Instantly, a jet of pure, sweet water sprang up directly into Bheeshma's mouth, quenching his thirst. The people watched in sheer amazement. 

Under the cover of night, Karna, who had abstained from fighting while Bheeshma was commander, approached the fallen patriarch. He bowed to him, tears in his eyes. 

"O Karna," Bheeshma said affectionately, "you are not Radha's son, but Kunti's son by the God Surya. I do not despise you. I spoke to you harshly only to diminish your energy and morale, and to prevent a schism in the family. The Pandavas are your brothers. Join hands with them. This is the favor I ask of you at this hour."

"O Bheeshma!" Karna replied. "I know that I am the son of Kunti. But my mother abandoned me at birth. I will sacrifice my wealth, my family, my very life for Duryodhana. I wish to follow my Dharma and continue fighting Arjuna. Bless me, O Grandsire, that I may fight this war."

Seeing his unwavering resolve, Bheeshma blessed him. "Very well, O Karna, if you are so resolved. Fight the war and attain heaven." 

With Bheeshma's blessing, Karna finally entered the great war.
