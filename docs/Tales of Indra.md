# Tales of Indra - 1. Indra and the Flying Mountains

Long, long ago, in the ancient times, birds and gods were not the only things that flew. Hurtling through the skies were giant, incredibly heavy mountains, endowed with massive wings!

They soared carelessly through the sky and landed with ground-shaking force wherever they chose to. Unfortunately, they created terrible chaos in both heaven and earth. 

*THUD!* A giant winged mountain suddenly crashed right next to a small village, sending up a massive cloud of dust and terrifying the villagers. People constantly feared for their lives, never knowing when a mountain might drop on their homes.

"Let us urgently ask the gods to help us," a wise village priest suggested to the terrified crowd.

"Yes! Let us pray to Lord Indra, the King of the Gods and Lord of Thunder," another priest agreed. "He will surely help us."

The desperate people prayed to Indra for many long years. Finally, pleased with their devotion, Indra appeared before them in a blaze of heavenly fire.

"Do not fear," Indra promised the villagers. "I will speak to the mountains and ask them not to trouble you any longer."

But this was much easier said than done. When Indra flew up to the clouds and confronted the arrogant King of the Mountains, his request was met with sheer mockery. 

"Ha ha ha!" the giant Mountain King laughed, his rocky face sneering. "Puny little Indra, why should I ever listen to you?"

"Do not test me," Indra warned, his eyes flashing with lightning. "My true strength does not lie in my size. Stop harassing the innocent people below."

The Mountain King scoffed. "You are completely insignificant before me! Why should I take orders from you?"

"How dare you speak to me like that!" Indra roared in fury. 

Drawing back his arm, Indra forcefully threw his mighty, crackling thunderbolt, the Vajra, straight at the mountain. 

*AAAH!* the Mountain King screamed in pain. *THUD!* The powerful Vajra sliced cleanly through stone, completely severing the king's giant wings, sending them fluttering uselessly away while the wingless mountain plummeted to the earth below. 

Indra furiously pursued all the other winged mountains across the sky, mercilessly cutting off their wings with his flashing Vajra. *SLASH! SLASH!* The severed wings fluttered away into the sky. (Many people believe that these drifting mountain wings actually formed the very first clouds).

Only one mountain—Mainaka, the golden-hearted, the kindest mountain of all, and brother to Goddess Parvati—managed to escape the terrible slaughter. As he fled in terror, the Wind God, Vayu, swooped in to help him. 

"O Mainaka, brother of Parvati, I have come to rescue you," Vayu roared, wrapping the mountain in powerful gales. "Come with me. I will take you to a place Indra's Vajra cannot reach."

"O Vayu, I am forever indebted to you for your generous help," Mainaka cried. 

Vayu blew the tired mountain quickly towards the vast ocean... but Indra's sharp eyes saw them flee!

"Who dares fly away from me?" Indra shouted, chasing after them on his white elephant, Airavata. "Mainaka, you cannot escape!"

Vayu lifted Mainaka in his powerful winds and took him to the edge of the sea. "O Sagara, mighty God of the Ocean, please give Mainaka a safe hiding place!" Vayu pleaded.

Immediately, the great waters gurgled and whirled, opening up a massive chasm until there was space for Mainaka. The grateful mountain sank deep in and was soon completely swallowed up by Sagara... just in time to escape Indra's wrath.

Indra hovered over the swirling water, raising his Vajra. "Mainaka! You hide from me now, but some day you will have to reappear," Indra threatened the waves. "And that is the day I will finally cut off your wings!"

Deep underwater, Sagara consoled the trembling Mainaka. "Don't worry, mighty Mainaka. I know Indra well. He is terribly angry now, but soon his temper will cool and he might forgive you."

Not wanting to take any chance, Mainaka stayed hidden safely under the ocean for thousands of years. 

...Until one day, Sagara looked up towards the sky above the waves. 

"Look, minister, there is someone actually flying towards us!" Sagara pointed in amazement. "Who dares cross the unending ocean in one single jump?"

"It is Hanuman, the monkey god!" Sagara realized. "He is going to Lanka to help my kinsman, Rama, whose beloved wife, Sita, has been kidnapped by the evil demon king Ravana."

Sagara turned to his minister. "You must offer him some rest. He has a very long way to go before he finally confronts Ravana in Lanka."

"But how will he rest on the water?" Mainaka asked from the depths. 

Mainaka made a brave decision. *I have hidden from Indra all these years in fear. I cannot hide anymore. Helping the noble Hanuman is far more important to me than saving my own wings!*

"O Sagara, you have safely sheltered me all these years," Mainaka said respectfully. "But now I ask your permission to help him who flies fast towards us. Hanuman is the noble son of Vayu. It was Vayu who rescued me and brought me to you. This is my one chance to repay my great debt."

Sagara smiled proudly. "O Mainaka! I know now exactly why you are called the golden-hearted mountain! Go and offer both yourself and me to Hanuman. We will gladly help him in any way he seeks."

Just then, the waters heaved violently, and from deep below the ocean, the mighty, winged Mainaka dramatically rose up into the sky right in front of Hanuman.

Hanuman stopped mid-flight. "What is this I see?"

"O Hanuman, many years ago your great father, Vayu, rescued me from Indra's terrible wrath," Mainaka explained kindly. "Allow me to finally repay my debt to him by offering myself for you to rest on."

Hanuman smiled warmly. "You must be Mainaka. My father saved you because you are genuinely good and kind. You owe him absolutely no debt."

"If not for me, then please stop for the ocean god, Sagara," Mainaka pleaded. "He has sent me on his behalf to offer you whatever help you need."

Hanuman flew down, affectionately hugged the peak of Mainaka, and then prepared to resume his crucial mission. "I am deeply grateful to you both, but I simply cannot stop and rest now. Mother Sita desperately needs my help. I must reach her as fast as I can."

Meanwhile, high up in heaven, Indra had seen the entire brave exchange. 

*Hmmm....maybe I have completely misjudged Mainaka all these years,* Indra thought to himself, lowering his Vajra. *He bravely risked his own life and wings being seen by me when he came out to help Hanuman.*

Indra descended from the clouds and appeared before the mountain. 

Mainaka braced himself to lose his wings, but Indra smiled gently. "O Mainaka, you knew you could lose your wings and yet you let yourself be seen to help another. For this beautiful act of selflessness, I completely forgive you. I also bestow upon you the permanent boon of fearlessness. You are now free to go wherever you please."

Mainaka bowed his rocky head. "Thank you, my lord. But I do not wish to go anywhere else. I am very happy here below the ocean, if Sagara will still have me."

Sagara rose from the waves, only too pleased. "This is your true home, Mainaka. You must stay here forever."

And so the golden-hearted Mainaka lives happily in the deep ocean, the only mountain in the world to keep his magnificent wings.


# Tales of Indra - 2. Indra and the Many Indras

Indra, the powerful Lord of the Devas, had just returned from a massive, terrible battle. He had finally vanquished the mighty Asura, Vritra. 

For many long months, the terrible demon Vritra had wreaked absolute havoc on the gods. He had even swallowed all the water in the entire world, leaving the earth parched and dry. Finally, in a desperate battle, Indra had attacked Vritrasura with his lightning thunderbolt, the Vajra... and with a mighty blow, he killed the demon.

All the water that Vritra held came gushing powerfully out of his huge body, restoring life to the earth.

Indra returned to a tumultuous, roaring welcome in Indraloka. 

"Welcome Indra! All hail, Indra!" the massive crowd of Devas cheered wildly.

But despite his victory, Indra was deeply saddened. The beautiful city of Indraloka lay completely devastated by the demon's attack. 

Indra looked sadly at a ruined palace. "My beloved home, Indraloka, lies in absolute ruins! It has to be completely rebuilt. I want it to look just exactly as it used to."

A minister stepped forward. "Can I suggest something, my Lord?"

"Speak."

"You have won such a uniquely heroic victory," the minister praised him. "Surely you deserve a far grander palace than you had before."

Indra's chest swelled with pride. "You are absolutely right!"

And so, Indra sent a guard with orders: "Summon Vishwakarma immediately."

Vishwakarma was the incredibly skilled celestial architect and engineer of the gods. When Vishwakarma arrived, he bowed low.

"Vishwakarma, you must completely rebuild Indraloka and make it far more magnificent than it ever was," Indra commanded grandly. "Make it glitter brightly like the sun itself, and let its golden towers rise high above the clouds."

"It will be a great challenge and an absolute honour, Devendra," Vishwakarma promised. 

Vishwakarma went away, deeply excited by the rare opportunity to build the ultimate palace. He set to work at once.

After many months of back-breaking magical work, the palace was finished. 

"Beautiful!" Vishwakarma gasped, looking at the glittering towers. "This is absolutely the best work I have ever done. I think it's time to show Devendra."

But when Indra arrived to inspect it, he looked displeased. "Well..."

"Is something wrong, my Lord?" Vishwakarma asked nervously.

"O Vishwakarma, this is very beautiful, but..." Indra sighed, shaking his head. "...It is simply not lofty enough! Indraloka should be exactly like me—an awe-inspiring inspiration to anyone who sets eyes on it."

Vishwakarma hid his profound disappointment. "I will try again, O Mighty one."

Vishwakarma started work all over again... and this time he poured all his celestial magic into the stones, completely surpassing the best in himself and all three worlds. The new palace was breathtakingly beautiful.

Indra and his followers came to visit the second palace. 

"Oooh! Wondrous!" the followers gasped in awe.

But Indra still frowned. "O Vishwakarma, do you not understand who I am?" Indra demanded angrily. "I am Indra—the supreme King of the Gods. Surely I deserve something far grander than this!"

Vishwakarma was exhausted. *If this magnificent palace is not good enough for him, absolutely nothing will ever be,* the architect thought despairingly. *How can I make him understand?*

Desperate, Vishwakarma sought refuge with Lord Brahma, the creator. 

"O Lotus one, Indra is getting completely insatiable in his immense pride," Vishwakarma complained. "I cannot keep endlessly building and re-building Indraloka for him."

Brahma smiled gently. "I understand. Do not worry. Tomorrow you will be freed from your impossible task."

Vishwakarma returned home, greatly relieved.

Brahma then turned to Lord Vishnu, the preserver, who heard him out in complete silence. Finally, Vishnu nodded. "Your work will be done," Vishnu promised.

The next morning, Indra was inspecting his palace grounds when he heard a commotion. "What is that noise? What are all these strange children doing playing here?"

Among the children, Indra noticed a remarkably calm young boy wearing a small crown. *Who is he?* Indra thought, mesmerized. *He shines with a very strange, divine light!*

Intrigued, Indra welcomed the boy. "I will be happy to show you around my palace..." Indra boasted, "...but Vishwakarma has sadly not succeeded in creating a fitting tribute to me yet."

The boy smiled wisely. "You, O Indra, are indeed worthy of noble tributes and grand offerings... O highest of devas, no Indra before has ever succeeded in creating such marvels."

Indra frowned, confused. "Indra... before?"

Then Indra laughed loudly at the child's apparent ignorance. "Ha! Ha! Ha! No Indra before! And exactly how many Indras were there before me, little boy?"

But the boy just kept smiling calmly. 

Indra's laughter quickly turned to anger. "Impudent child! I am Indra! There never was and never will be another."

The boy didn't flinch. 

*He smiles instead of cowering before my mighty anger,* Indra thought nervously. *Why am I beginning to feel fear?*

Sensing the boy's divine power, Indra suddenly bowed his head. "O saintly one! I know you are no ordinary child. Please tell me what you know."

The boy's eyes seemed to hold the entire universe. "O King of Devas, you know that Lord Vishnu is our ultimate creator. But I have seen that the vast body of Lord Vishnu is made up of fathomless, pure waters."

The boy pointed to the sky. "From every single pore of Vishnu's infinite body, a complete universe bubbles up and breaks. Each bubble has its own distinct life, its own gods, and its own demons."

Indra listened in stunned silence.

"Who will possibly count these infinite bubbles, O Indra? These many universes that constantly form and break? How many Indras exactly like you co-exist side by side in the adjacent worlds?"

The boy continued, his voice echoing. "Even in this exact same world, one single Indra lasts only seven Yugas. 28 Indras of seven Yugas each make up only one night and one day in the incredibly long life of Brahma. But the lifetime of a Brahma is also severely limited and when one sinks, another arises. In this massive, grand canvas of time, who, O Devendra, can confidently say how many Indras or even Brahmas there are?"

Indra's immense pride completely vanished, completely crushed by the sheer scale of the infinite universes. 

The boy pointed down at a line of ants marching across the marble floor. "You see these ants? They can tell you the absolute truth."

"Ants? These insignificant creatures!" Indra gasped.

"Each of these tiny ants, my Lord, was actually once an Indra," the boy revealed.

"What? An Indra! An ant? It cannot be!" Indra cried in horror.

"It is our actual deeds that make us who we truly are," the boy taught him gently. "Good and kind acts make us noble, take us higher, while deceit, vanity, and wicked acts cause us to be reborn as lowly creatures."

The boy looked sadly at the ants. "Each of these ants rose to be an Indra by their incredible virtue, but once they became Indra, pride, vanity, and treachery caused them to fall lower and lower with each rebirth. And here they are, crawling in the palace they once reigned from, tiny enough to be crushed by my toe."

Then, the boy shed his disguise and revealed his true, glowing, blue-skinned form as Lord Vishnu.

Indra fell to his knees in tears. "Forgive me, Lord! Foolish pride made me completely blind. Vishwakarma has created a truly glorious palace, but I foolishly and arrogantly thought that I deserved better."

Indra learnt his painful lesson well. Vishwakarma finally completed Indraloka without any more complaints, and peace and harmony was fully restored in heaven.


# Tales of Indra - 3. Indra and Sarama

All was quiet and well in Amravati, the beautiful celestial city of the gods. Indra was enjoying a rare moment of peace, relaxing on a cushion and petting his loyal, beloved dog, Sarama. 

Just then, a herdsman burst into the room, looking absolutely frantic.

"My Lord! The royal cowshed is completely empty! All of your prized cows are missing!"

Indra sat up in shock. "How can that possibly be? Come, Sarama."

Indra and Sarama rushed quickly to the cowshed. It was completely empty. 

"Where are the guards?" Indra demanded furiously. "Were they not actively doing their duty?"

"We... we were, my Lord," a terrified guard stammered, bowing low. "We kept strict vigil all night long, as usual. But suddenly in the early morning, the cows were just completely gone."

"How can so many loud, heavy cows simply disappear without you seeing them go?" Indra yelled.

"There was a very strange, thick mist early this morning, my Lord," the second guard explained. "For a while, we could not see absolutely anything."

"The cows just magically disappeared, my Lord!" the third guard added helplessly.

Indra frowned. "Mist!! Disappeared! What absolute nonsense!" 

But Sarama, the faithful dog, was busy sniffing the ground. *They seem to be speaking the absolute truth, O Mighty Indra,* Sarama communicated telepathically to her master. *There are absolutely no footprints here. The thieves and so many heavy cows would have definitely left some marks, some clues in the mud...*

*Who dares secretly play with me like this!?* Indra thought angrily. *Who did this...some Asura, some wild Gana...?*

"Sarama, my beloved dog," Indra said, looking down at her, "can your sharp nose smell something? Do you secretly know who did this?"

*SNIFF! SNIFF!* 

*There is a very strange, magical smell, my Lord,* Sarama told him, *quite distinct from the smell of the cows. These are certainly no ordinary thieves.*

"They must not get away with this," Indra commanded. "You must actively follow their magical scent, Sarama."

*I will surely find the cows, my Lord,* Sarama promised.

Sarama set off immediately on a long quest that took her tracking across many steep mountains and vast meadows... until she finally crossed Rasa, a magical stream at the very end of the world. 

*SNIFF...The strange smell is definitely strongest here,* Sarama thought, looking around the rocky landscape. *The thieves must be very near.*

*Where exactly do you hide, you cowardly cow thieves?* she growled. 

Suddenly, the entire rocky area was thickly covered in a blinding, unnatural mist, and Sarama could not see anything at all. It was no ordinary mist. It was magically made by the wicked Panis, the terrifying demons of the mist.

"Who dares politely come here to disturb our peace?" a giant, ugly demon boomed from the mist.

"It is I, Sarama, faithful companion to Lord Indra," the brave dog announced into the fog. "If you are the wicked ones who have secretly taken his cows, you must release them at once."

The mist cleared to reveal several massive, ugly Panis. 

"Ha, ha, ha!" the leader mocked loudly. "Look exactly who the mighty Indra has sent to aggressively fetch his cows. A mere dog!"

"His cows must mean absolutely nothing to him," another demon laughed. "Maybe we should have safely stolen this little dog instead. Ha, ha, ha!"

"Let's playfully capture her now," the leader sneered, reaching down. "Then who will the mighty Indra send to rescue the dog? A mouse! Ha ha ha!"

The Panis grabbed Sarama and cruelly threw her around on the hard, rocky surface. *BAM!* 

But Sarama was completely unafraid and quickly scrambled back to her feet. 

"Tell your master that the clever Panis have hidden his cows forever," the demon leader mocked. "He will never, ever find them."

"But my master does not magically need to find them," Sarama growled confidently. "I can strongly smell them, and I know exactly where they are. They are magically sealed in that massive Vala!" she said, pointing a paw at a mighty, solid rock wall.

The demons gasped in shock. The leader quickly whispered to his friend, "How did she miraculously know that? We cannot let Indra find our secret rock cave. We will have to securely keep the dog here."

The demon leader turned back to Sarama with a fake, sweet smile. "Why do you aggressively fight, Sarama? You are much like our sister. Stay here peacefully with us and share our stolen treasures."

"I want absolutely no treasures or ill-gotten gains," Sarama barked fiercely. "My devotion purely lies with my Lord Indra."

The demons realized they couldn't bribe her. *She is perfectly loyal and true to Indra,* the leader whispered. *We will cleverly have to trick her.*

The Panis tried another deceptive ploy. They bowed deeply to the dog. "Forgive us, O wise Sarama," the leader pleaded falsely. "Your powerful master is very fortunate to have an ally exactly like you. It was extremely wrong of us to try and violently stop you here. Go back, tell Indra that we are willingly ready to make peace."

They brought out a bowl of fresh milk. "Before you go, O Sarama, please generously accept some milk from us as a sincere peace offering."

*They seem to have finally realised my master's might,* Sarama thought, her guard dropping. Thirsty from her incredibly long journey, Sarama happily lapped all the milk up. 

*Heh heh!* the demon whispered secretly to his friend. *Once she quickly drinks this, she will be so deeply intoxicated and drugged that she will not be able to get back to Indra!*

Sarama started on her long way home. But halfway there...

*Huh!? My eyes! I cannot see clearly,* Sarama panicked, her legs wobbling. *The Panis....they cruelly tricked me! The milk is magically making me terribly sleepy.*

She stumbled and fell to the ground. *No! I must absolutely not let them defeat me. I must reach my master, somehow!*

Fighting desperately to keep a hold over her fading senses, Sarama dragged herself slowly home... and finally collapsed at Indra's feet in Amravati.

"The Panis...they deviously gave me something to make me sleepy," she whined weakly. "They... they secretly didn't want me to safely reach you."

"The Panis!" Indra roared in fury, grabbing his Vajra. "I will not peacefully let them go!"

"The cows... are tightly sealed in ...in a Vala... over the Rasa stream," Sarama managed to say before closing her eyes.

"Shush....don't talk now, my incredibly noble friend," Indra said softly, stroking her head. "We will find them securely together."

Filled with absolute anger, Indra magically transported himself to the Panis' rocky hideout. 

"You devious Panis have hidden my cows securely behind this rock!" Indra screamed at the terrified demons. "I will aggressively smash it to tiny bits."

Indra threw his glowing Vajra. *WHAM!* It violently dashed open the solid Vala... and out poured, not just Indra's missing cows, but a massive horde of other stolen animals—horses, tigers, wolves, and deer!

The crackling thunderbolt flew obediently back to Indra's hand... and he furiously turned it on the Panis. 

"I will slay you.... every single one of you," Indra promised. "You stole my cows and cruelly drugged my faithful Sarama."

A fierce, lightning-filled battle ensued, but in the end, Indra electrocuted all the demons. "The Panis are completely dead! Long live Indra!!"

As a happy Indra and a fully recovered Sarama rode back to Amravati with all the safely rescued animals running behind them, it was a truly glorious sight, indeed!
