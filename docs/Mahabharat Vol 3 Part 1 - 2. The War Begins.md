# The War Begins

Upon Krishna's return to Upaplavya, he reported the failure of his peace mission to the Pandavas. Recognizing that Duryodhana was bent on war, Yudhishthira began his final military preparations. 

The Pandavas assembled an army of seven Akshauhinis. Yudhishthira asked his brothers whom they should appoint as the Commander-in-Chief. After considering Drupada, Virata, and Shikhandi, they unanimously chose Dhrishtadyumna, the valiant prince of Panchala. Arjuna selected Lord Krishna not as a warrior, but as his guide and charioteer. 

The massive Pandava army marched towards the sacred plains of Kurukshetra and pitched their tents. 

In Hastinapura, the Kauravas amassed a colossal force of eleven Akshauhinis. Duryodhana appointed the great patriarch, Bheeshma, as the Commander-in-Chief. Bheeshma accepted, but with strict conditions: "I will fight for you, but I will not slay the Pandavas. I will, however, kill ten thousand of their warriors every day. Also, there is another condition—I will not fight Shikhandi."

This decision angered Karna, who vowed not to fight as long as Bheeshma was the commander. 

Before the battle commenced, both sides established strict rules of warfare. Combat would take place only between equals—charioteer against charioteer, infantryman against infantryman. Fighting would cease at sunset, and the warriors would behave as friends in the evening. Those who surrendered, non-combatants, and those without weapons were not to be attacked. 

The great sage Veda Vyasa visited King Dhritarashtra and offered to grant him divine vision so he could witness the epic battle. Dhritarashtra, heartbroken at the thought of seeing his family slaughtered, declined. Instead, Vyasa bestowed the divine vision upon Sanjaya, the king's trusted advisor, so he could narrate the events of the war to the blind monarch. 

As the two massive armies arrayed themselves on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, a sudden gloom descended upon Arjuna. Seeing Bheeshma, his beloved grandsire, Drona, his revered teacher, and countless cousins and friends on the opposing side, his resolve shattered. 

"O Krishna," Arjuna said, his voice trembling. "The Gandeeva is about to fall from my hands. I can barely stand on my feet. I cannot bear to see the destruction of all the Kauravas. I shall not fight." He dropped his bow and sank into his chariot, overwhelmed by sorrow. 

It was then, on the battlefield, that Lord Krishna delivered the profound spiritual discourse that would become known as the Bhagavad Gita. He spoke to Arjuna of duty, righteousness, and the immortal nature of the soul. 

"The soul is indestructible," Krishna taught. "No weapon can pierce it, no fire burn it. One who is born is certain to die, and one who dies is certain to be reborn. There is nothing to grieve over. Rise up! Without making any distinction between victory and defeat, pain and pleasure, gain and loss, resolve to fight. Perform your duty with an evenness of mind, O Arjuna!"

Enlightened and freed from his delusions, Arjuna picked up the Gandeeva, his mind clear and ready to do Krishna's bidding. 

Just before the conches sounded, Yudhishthira did something entirely unexpected. Removing his armor and weapons, he walked barefoot across the battlefield towards the Kaurava army. He approached Bheeshma, Drona, Kripa, and Shalya, bowing respectfully to each and seeking their blessings for the impending war. Touched by his nobility, the elders blessed him with victory, though they remained duty-bound to fight for Duryodhana. 

As Yudhishthira returned, he called out, "I make an appeal to all Kaurava warriors. If any of you wish to come over to my side, he is welcome."

Yuyutsu, a son of Dhritarashtra, stepped forward. "O King, if you will accept me, I will join you." Thus, Yuyutsu crossed over to the Pandava side. 

The war-drums beat, the conches blew, and the two armies hurled themselves at each other. The first day of the great Kurukshetra war began with intense, hand-to-hand combat. Chariots confronted chariots, elephants clashed with elephants. The battlefield turned into a scene of unimaginable slaughter. 

Bheeshma fought with matchless valor, devastating the Pandava ranks. Abhimanyu, the young and heroic son of Arjuna, fought brilliantly against the Kaurava veterans, including Bheeshma himself, drawing praise from all. 

As the sun set on the first day, the weary soldiers returned to their camps. The Pandavas were deeply troubled by the heavy losses they had suffered at the hands of the invincible Bheeshma. The great war had truly begun, and its bloody toll was already evident.
