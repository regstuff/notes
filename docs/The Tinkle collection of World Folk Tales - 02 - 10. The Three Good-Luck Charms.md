# The Three Good-Luck Charms

It was a lovely day in Japan. A little boy-monk looked out of the monastery window at the beautiful snowy landscape. "Oh, how I long to go outside and gather chestnuts!" he sighed. 

Saying his prayers and finishing his morning chores quickly, he ran to the old Head Monk. "May I go and pick chestnuts today?" he asked eagerly. 

"No, you mustn't go out. What if the mountain witch catches you?" the old monk warned. 

"Oh, please, please let me go," the boy begged. 

"All right, go if you must..." the old monk relented. "...But take these three pieces of paper. They are good-luck charms. If you run into any trouble, ask them to help you."

The boy-monk roamed the mountain slopes happily, wandering farther and farther away from the safety of the monastery. "What a lot of chestnuts I've picked!" he smiled, his basket full. 

Suddenly, an old woman appeared from behind a tree. "Little boy, you are from the temple monastery, aren't you?" she smiled crookedly. "Come along with me. There are much better chestnuts growing in my garden."

"That sounds nice," the innocent boy thought. 

"I'll roast the big, juicy ones and boil the tiny ones for you," the old woman promised. 

She took the boy-monk to her secluded wooden house and gave him the promised treat. "They're delicious! But I can't eat any more..." he patted his full stomach. 

"You must be sleepy. Sleep here tonight. You can go back to the monastery tomorrow," she insisted. 

The boy-monk got under a thick quilt and was soon fast asleep. But at midnight, he suddenly awoke. *Pitter, patter...* "It's raining," he muttered, listening to the drops on the roof. 

The raindrops seemed to be whispering something... *Pitter patter, look and see, the old woman seems a witch to me...*

Startled, the boy-monk peeped out from under the quilt. "The old woman... there she is! What is she doing?"

The old woman was staring into a mirror, admiring herself. But she was no longer an old woman. She had transformed into a hideous monster! "How beautiful I am! How long and sharp my horns have grown!" she cackled. 

"It's the mountain witch! She'll eat me up! I must escape in the morning," the boy-monk panicked, laying as still as a stone. 

In the morning, the boy crept out of bed. "Old woman, I want to have a wash," he asked. 

"It's too cold for a wash. Stay where you are!" she snapped, boiling a massive cauldron of water over the fire. 

"But old woman, I must have a wash; I must! I simply must..." he insisted loudly. 

"Oh bother! How much you trouble me!" she growled. "All right, go! But I'll tie this rope round your waist so you don't run away."

"Now I must be quick..." the boy thought as he went out to the courtyard. He quickly untied the rope from his waist and tied it securely around a thick wooden pillar. 

"When the mountain witch calls out, answer for me," the boy whispered to the pillar, taking out one of his good-luck charms and sticking it to the wood. 

Then he tiptoed out the back door and ran off like the wind! 

Inside, the mountain witch waited and waited. "Hurry up! Haven't you finished?" she yelled. 

"Wait a little, wait a little!" the charm on the pillar called back in the boy's voice. 

"How long you take. Come out, now!"

"Wait a little, just a little..."

At last, she got tired of waiting and angrily kicked open the door. "What! He's gone! I've been tricked! I've been tricked! But I'll catch you still."

The witch flew after him and soon caught up with the boy-monk. Desperate, he brought out his second good-luck charm and threw it behind him. "Be a high sand mountain!"

*WHOOSH!* A massive mountain of loose sand erupted from the ground between them. Up the sand mountain went the witch. She slipped and sank, sank and shouted... but finally reached the top. "Wait, wait... I am going to eat you up!"

The boy-monk ran on and on. When he could finally see the monastery in the distance, he flung up his very last charm. "Be a big river!"

*SPLASH! WHOOSH!* A raging, deep river appeared. 

The witch dove in and tried to swim as fast as she could, but the roaring waves pushed her back... *Slish, slosh, slish, slosh.*

The boy-monk reached the monastery doors. "Save me, sir! Save me. The mountain witch is after me!"

"Quick! Get into the empty cupboard," the old monk instructed, shutting the boy inside. 

Seconds later, the dripping wet witch kicked open the door. "Where is that miserable boy? I'll eat him for breakfast!"

"Oh, the boy-monk? He went off to pick chestnuts yesterday. He hasn't come back yet," the old monk said calmly, toasting a rice cake over his fire. 

"LIES! LIES! Tell me where he is!" the witch screamed. But then, her huge nose twitched greedily. *Sniff, sniff...* "Rice cakes! Umm! I love rice cakes. Give me one, old monk," she drooled. 

"I'll give you as many as you want, but first you must let me test your magic powers," the clever monk smiled. 

"Anything! Ask me to do anything!" the hungry witch agreed. 

"All right, then, how big can you be? This I want to see."

"BIG... BIG..." the witch chanted, growing instantly until she was so tall her horned head touched the cloudy sky! 

"Good, good! Now show me how small... small... small you can be. This I want to see," the old monk chuckled. 

The witch shrank instantly until she was no higher than the fire tongs. 

"Can you get any smaller?" the monk asked. 

"Ah, ha! You are now as small as a soya-bean!" The witch shrank until she was just a tiny speck. 

Quick as a flash, the old monk pinched the tiny witch between his fingers, put her between two golden, roasted rice-cakes... and popped them into his mouth! 

And that was the very end of the terrifying mountain witch. 

To this day, village children in Japan sing a happy song while picking crisp and juicy chestnuts on a lovely autumn day: 
*Crisp and juicy chestnuts, go crunch! crunch! crunch!*
*The wise, old monk has gobbled up the mountain witch for lunch!*
