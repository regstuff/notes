# Krishna and Rukmini: The Bold Elopement

Krishna is celebrated as the great lover of Indian mythology. Yet the details of the women he wooed, won, and wed are often overshadowed by his conquests in battle. His conquest of Rukmini, however, is an unalloyed romantic tale of none but the brave deserving the fair. Here, Krishna plays the romantic hero par excellence, recklessly carrying away his lady love right from under the noses of his mighty rivals.

Rukmini serves as a perfect foil to Krishna in this idyllic tale. Though coy, it is she who confidently takes the first step by revealing her heart to her beloved. She masterminds the daring details of their escapade, proving just how resourceful women were in ancient India. This story has been so popular throughout the centuries that the word "Swayamvara" often brings to mind the story of Krishna and Rukmini—even though, ironically, it was hardly a conventional Swayamvara at all.

***

The story begins in Vidarbha, where Rukmi, the ambitious son of King Bheeshmaka, returned home from Mathura bursting with incredible news. As the members of the royal household surrounded him, he announced, "Krishna, the cowherd from Vrindavan, has slain Kamsa!"

"It appears that Krishna is really the eighth son of Vasudeva and Devaki, Kamsa's cousin," King Bheeshmaka mused. "He and his elder brother, Balarama, were secreted away to Gokul as soon as they were born and were brought up among the cowherds. So, in spite of all Kamsa's efforts, the prophecy has come true. Is Krishna really a divinity?"

Rukmi's beautiful sister, Rukmini, was a forthright girl. "Then he is no cowherd but a noble!" she interjected brightly.

"Rukmini!" Rukmi snapped. "He has murdered my friend, and you call him noble?"
"You will never admit that Kamsa was an evil king," Rukmini countered. "The Yadavas of Mathura had never been happy under his rule. Besides, don't forget he deposed his own father and usurped the throne."
"No! Krishna refused the crown," King Bheeshmaka added gently. "He insisted that his grandfather, Ugrasena, be reinstated."

"He refused the crown!" Rukmini exclaimed, thoroughly impressed.
"Yes," Rukmi admitted grudgingly. "But King Ugrasena and the nobles have requested—nay, insisted—that he remain in Mathura and..."
"Who wouldn't!" Rukmini smiled. "Did he agree?"
Ignoring his sister, Rukmi turned back to his father. "He has agreed to stay for a while—to learn the scriptures and the princely arts."

"What about Jarasandha?" Bheeshmaka asked, suddenly worried. "How did he react?"
"Jarasandha, Kamsa's father-in-law, is the emperor of Magadha—the most powerful kingdom of our days," Rukmi explained. "The emperor is furious. Both his daughters have been widowed in one stroke. He vows to take revenge and has sent for me. I must go and see about the chariots."

As Rukmi stormed out, Rukmini slipped away, her mind racing. *I must know more about Krishna,* she thought.

Left alone, Bheeshmaka and his wife shared a quiet moment. "The boy seems to be virtuous," the queen said softly. "I cannot think of a more suitable husband for Rukmini."
"The same thought occurred to me," Bheeshmaka agreed. "He refused the crown of Mathura. How it must have pleased poor, old Ugrasena."

Meanwhile, with the help of Rukmi and other allies, Jarasandha made several massive attempts to avenge the death of his son-in-law. Eighteen times he attacked Mathura, and eighteen times he failed. Finally, frustrated and exhausted, the emperor declared, "The vile Yadava and his brother have dodged us again. We will have to bide our time. Let my friends return to their capitals for the present."

Far away in Dwaraka, Krishna's new, impregnable island city, the Yadava princes were relaxing. "Our spies say that Jarasandha has decided to lie low for a while," Krishna informed his brother.
Balarama looked at Krishna with a merry twinkle in his eye. "Now you'll have all the leisure to dream of the beautiful princess of Vidarbha—the most sought-after princess on earth."
"Ah, Balarama! My mind is set on marrying her," Krishna sighed. "But her father is a vassal of Jarasandha, and her brother, Rukmi, is his staunch ally."

***

Back in Vidarbha, in the lush gardens of Bheeshmaka's palace, Rukmini was lost in her own dreams of the Yadava hero who had completely won her heart. *Even the mighty emperor with all his allies has not been able to vanquish him,* she thought fondly. *He and only he shall be my lord.*

Suddenly, she heard voices nearby. *My father! Rukmi! They're coming this way.* She quickly hid behind a leafy trellis.
They were discussing her marriage.
"Your mother and I have decided to give her to Krishna," Bheeshmaka said.
Rukmini's heart soared. *How fortunate am I!*

But her joy was short-lived. "How could you, father?" Rukmi demanded angrily. "Don't forget, he murdered my dear friend, the emperor's son-in-law. Surely, we cannot afford to displease the mighty emperor!"
Rukmini gripped the vines in terror. *Please Rukmi! Don't make him change his mind!*

Bheeshmaka was silent for a while. Then he sighed. "But we cannot think of a more suitable husband for her. After all, Jarasandha has not yet been able to avenge Kamsa's death..."
"But he certainly will!" Rukmi insisted. "It is only a matter of time."
"Then whom do you suggest?"
"The emperor's favourite and my dear friend—Shishupala, the crown prince of Chedi. He is enamoured of Rukmini."

*That jackal!* Rukmini thought, horrified. *Never! I shall never marry him. O Rukmi, how could you sell your own sister to win the emperor's favour? Don't agree, father, please don't agree!*

But King Bheeshmaka invariably permitted his aggressive eldest son to make all major decisions, even when they went against his own wishes. He heaved a defeated sigh. "All right, Rukmi. Do as you will."
"Then I shall send a formal proposal to Chedi and invite Shishupala to come and marry Rukmini," Rukmi declared triumphantly.

As they walked away, Rukmini buried her face in her hands. *So they don't even plan to hold a Swayamvara. To whom shall I speak? What shall I do?*

Suddenly, a bold idea occurred to her. *Why not! Why shouldn't I? After all, my parents are for it.* 

She rushed into the palace and secretly sent for Sunanda, an old, trusted Brahman who was devoted to her. When he arrived, he was alarmed. "Why do you look so pale, my little one? What's the matter?"
Rukmini told him about the devastating conversation she had overheard. Then, looking him in the eye, she asked, "O venerable one, would it be wrong on my part to send a secret message to the Yadava hero?"

The old Brahman smiled gently. "No, little one, never. It would be wrong to marry Shishupala when your heart is set on another. And your parents have in their hearts approved."
"But with whom can I entrust such a dangerous message?"
"I will go to Dwaraka, Rukmini. I shall carry the message for you."

Relieved, Rukmini quickly wrote out a detailed message and handed it to the Brahman, explaining her daring plan. "...And be sure to tell him that I would not want my kith and kin to be killed on my account," she pleaded.
"Do not worry, Rukmini. I will not forget."

***

With great difficulty, Sunanda crossed the seas and reached Dwaraka, where he was given a warm welcome. After he had rested, Krishna approached him. "O venerable one, if it would not mean betraying any confidence, tell us why you have come to our inaccessible city?"

"I have come with a message from Rukmini, the princess of Vidarbha," Sunanda smiled, pulling out the scroll.
"Rukmini! My own Rukmini!" Krishna exclaimed.
Sunanda recounted the conversation Rukmini had overheard, then handed the message to Krishna. 

Krishna unrolled the scroll and read: 
*...I have chosen you as my husband. Come to Vidarbha, vanquish the armies of Jarasandha and Shishupala, and claim me...*

"If you do not come and take her away, she has decided to give up her life," Sunanda warned gravely.
"She mentions that in the message," Krishna nodded. "But little does she realise that I too have set my heart on winning her."
"Yes! I have," Krishna added. "But I know that Rukmi does not want to give her to me."

Sunanda's face fell. "Does that mean you will not come?"
"No. It does not," Krishna smiled fiercely. "Now that she has revealed her heart to me, I shall make her mine. I shall not hesitate to rout all the kings who stand in my way."

Overjoyed, Sunanda unfolded the rest of Rukmini's clever plan. "The gentle one is anxious that there should be no family bloodshed on her account."
"It is but natural for her, a veritable goddess, to feel that way," Krishna replied reverently.

Krishna immediately sent for his charioteer. "Get my chariot ready at once, Daruka! And tell Balarama that I shall be leaving for Vidarbha immediately."

Meanwhile, Rukmi's formal proposal had reached Shishupala in Chedi. He was jubilant. "Rukmi says that there is not going to be any Swayamvara. All I have to do is to go to Vidarbha and marry Rukmini! The emperor must hear the good news."

But Emperor Jarasandha was not as confident. "It's not as easy as you think. I do not trust that vile cowherd. He is sure to hear of the news and snatch the bride away. We will have to be prepared." 
Jarasandha immediately sent for all his vassals and allies. "I am bent on securing the princess of Vidarbha for Shishupala. You must be ready to confront the cowherd if he comes there and tries to take away the bride."

Back in Dwaraka, as soon as Krishna had left, one of Balarama's spies rushed to him. "Lord, the emperor has informed all his allies to march to Vidarbha. He expects trouble from us."
"And Krishna has gone all alone!" Balarama shouted. "Get my chariot ready! Rally our armies! Elephants, horses, chariots, all!"

***

At Vidarbha, Rukmini's anxiety increased with each passing moment. *The hour is drawing nearer. Why has my lord not yet come? Did my message disgust him? If so, then why hasn't Sunanda returned?*

As she looked out of the window for the hundredth time, she saw Sunanda entering the palace gates. *He looks cheerful! His gait is confident. He must bring good news. His mission has been successful!*

She turned to her maids. "You may go to your rooms. I wish to be alone." When they left, she ran to the door and waited eagerly for the Brahman.
"He has come, little one," Sunanda whispered, his eyes shining. "Everything is to go according to plan. His brother too has come with the Yadava forces."

News of Krishna's arrival soon reached King Bheeshmaka. "Krishna has arrived. He has come to witness the wedding of the princess," the king told his advisors, secretly thinking, *Dare I hope that he has come to carry away Rukmini? Dare I?*
"Have a mansion ready for him. Furnish it with every luxury," the king ordered aloud. "I shall ride out to receive him."

At the gates of the city, Bheeshmaka greeted the Yadavas. "Welcome, O Princes! We are happy to see you here. Come, let me lead you to the palace where you will stay."
But Rukmi watched from the ramparts with a scowl. *Balarama and their forces too have come. He means to take Rukmini away by force, for sure!*

As they rode into the city, they passed the magnificent shrine of Goddess Parvati, the family deity of the royal household. 
*Ah! That is the temple Rukmini wrote about,* Krishna smiled to himself. *How anxious my dear one must be.*

Balarama, however, was scanning the crowds. "The emperor and his men have flooded the city," he noted.
"Shishupala is the emperor's favourite, and the emperor has many allies," Bheeshmaka warned quietly. "This should help you plan your strategy."

Meanwhile, at the palace, the time had come. "Rukmini, your chariot has arrived. Are you ready?" a maid asked.
"I am. Let us leave."

Accompanied by her maids, Rukmini left for the shrine. When they reached the temple courtyard, she stepped out of her chariot and glanced around nervously. *I do not see Krishna among them. They say he is dark, wears a yellow robe, and sports a peacock feather in his crown.*

As she walked to the temple, a myriad of eyes followed her every movement, the rival kings drinking in her beauty. 
"Alas! She can never be mine," one king sighed.
"Shishupala is lucky. She is the very Goddess of Wealth incarnate," another muttered enviously.

Inside the temple, Rukmini knelt before the deity. "I worship thee, O faithful consort of Shiva. I bow to thee, thy lord, and thy two sons, Ganesha and Kartikeya." She washed the goddess' feet and placed offerings before her. Then, closing her eyes tightly, she poured out her heart. "O goddess, please let Krishna and only Krishna win and wed me. Do not fail me."

Her worship over, she came out of the temple. As she walked slowly towards the gate, her eyes anxiously scanned the vast assembly of kings. *I do not see him anywhere.*
Heavy was her heart, and slow her gait. Her chariot loomed before her all too soon. *Will Krishna ever become... mine?*

Suddenly, a strong hand gripped her arm. "Rukmini. It's me. Your Krishna."
Before anyone could react, he swung her up, and the next moment she was seated next to him in his chariot. 

"Stop him!" the kings shouted in disbelief. 
"Rukmini! Wait!"

But Krishna's chariot sped away, blowing dust straight into the eyes of the bemused kings. 

Jarasandha was furious. "Where is your kshatriya valour? Why do you stand there as if you've lost your senses? Pursue them!"
Jerked into action, the kings scrambled for their weapons and charged forward. 

Seeing the massive army giving chase, Balarama turned to Krishna. "They're giving chase. You ride on! We'll fall back and deal with them."
When Rukmini looked back in terror, Krishna laughed heartily and pulled her close. "Don't be alarmed. Our armies will soon rout the enemy. Balarama will not lose his dear sister-in-law so easily."

He was right. "Chase the jackals away, my men!" Balarama roared to the Yadava forces. "Shoot their horses! Destroy their chariots!" 

While Balarama was busy tackling Jarasandha, Shishupala, and their hordes, Rukmi managed to slip past the blockade, charging furiously after Krishna's vanishing chariot. "I will not re-enter the capital without Rukmini!" he vowed.
Balarama saw him but did not try to stop him. *Krishna can deal with him, even with Rukmini in his chariot,* he smirked.

Back at the battlefield, Balarama's onslaught was so fierce that the enemy lines quickly broke.
"Let us retreat, Shishupala," Jarasandha advised, pulling his battered chariot back. "There is no hope left. The cowherds are determined. Rukmini is lost to you."
"Alas!" Shishupala wept. "I feel as if my wedded wife has been abducted. So sure was I that she was mine alone."
"Don't grieve," Jarasandha tried to console him. "Fortune favoured them and they won. We shall certainly defeat them when our luck turns." Full of hatred, the disappointed suitor and his well-wishers retreated to their capitals.

Meanwhile, Rukmi had almost caught up with Krishna. "Today I shall humble the pride of that cowherd who dared abduct my sister!" 
Soon, his chariot was but a few feet away. "Stop! Stop! Wait, you disgrace to the race of Yadu! How dare you kidnap my sister, even as a crow steals a sacred offering! Faster... faster!"

Krishna ignored him, urging his horses on. 
"You wily rascal! You have proved your cunning, now prove your valour!" Rukmi taunted, firing a sharp arrow. 

The arrow struck Krishna's back but did not even make a dent in his divine armour. Krishna calmly pulled out an arrow of his own, lifted his bow, and turned around. First, he precisely shot the horses pulling Rukmi's chariot, killing them instantly. Then, with another powerful shot, he shattered Rukmi's chariot wheel, sending it crashing to the ground.

Rukmi took cover behind the debris and lifted his bow to fire again, only to have it expertly sniped and broken to bits by Krishna's arrows. Mad with rage, Rukmi drew his heavy broadsword and rushed towards Krishna on foot. But before he could even swing it, a glowing arrow from Krishna struck the blade, shattering it, leaving Rukmi holding nothing but the hilt. 

As Rukmi threw the hilt away in disgust, Krishna picked up his own sword and leaped from his chariot, ready to rush towards the arrogant prince and end his life. 

But Rukmini threw herself at Krishna's feet, tears streaming down her face. "O virtuous lord, please spare my brother! Please don't kill him!"
Krishna stopped, his anger melting as he looked at her. "Arise, Rukmini. For your sake, Rukmi shall live."

He went up to the defeated Rukmi and bound him tightly with his own sash. "I shall shave off half his hair and half his moustache with the blade of my sword," Krishna decided. "There could be no greater punishment for a proud warrior." 

When Balarama arrived on the scene, he was horrified to see Rukmi tied up and humiliated. "Krishna! What are you doing? You have performed an impious act; an act detested by our race. To do what you have done to a relative is worse than killing him." 
Balarama quickly untied Rukmi, then turned to a mortified Rukmini. "Please do not take offence, dear sister. Your brother unfortunately has reaped the fruit of his own actions."
He then told Rukmi, "You are free to go, Rukmi."

As the humiliated prince slowly walked away, he thought bitterly, *Alas! He has only added insult to injury. It were better that they had killed me. But where shall I go? Bound by my own vow, I cannot return to my capital. I will have to build a new city for myself.*

"Come into the chariot, Rukmini," Krishna said softly, taking her hand. "We must hurry to Dwaraka and have our wedding solemnised by rituals."

A few days later, in the great city of Dwaraka, surrounded by joy and celebration, Krishna was married to his beloved Rukmini with great pomp and ceremony.
