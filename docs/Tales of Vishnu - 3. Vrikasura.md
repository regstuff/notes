# Vrikasura

The foolish but mighty Asura, Vrika, once decided to propitiate Lord Shiva to gain ultimate power. Retreating to a desolate forest, he lit a sacrificial fire and began a gruesome penance. Day after day, he cut off pieces of his own flesh and offered them to the fire. 

"Lord, with this offering, may you appear before me," he chanted, throwing bloody chunks into the flames. 

But Lord Shiva did not appear. Growing impatient and desperate, the Asura concluded, "He does not appear. Perhaps it is only the offering of my head that will propitiate the Lord."

Drawing his heavy sword, Vrikasura prepared to decapitate himself. "When he was about to sever his head from the body," Lord Shiva finally materialized from the flames, his hand raised in blessing.

"Stop, Vrika!" Shiva commanded. "There is no need to kill yourself to please one who is satisfied with a mere offering of water. What is the boon you seek?"

The ambitious Asura smirked. "I wish to conquer the world. May anyone on whose head I lay my palm, die."

"So be it," Lord Shiva granted the strange request, bound by his divine promise.

But the very next moment, the treacherous Asura's eyes gleamed with malice. Deciding to test his newfound power on the very god who had granted it, Vrika reached out his hand towards Lord Shiva's head!

"No!" Shiva cried out, dodging the treacherous blow. Realizing the danger he had put himself in, the great god broke into a run. 

Vrikasura chased him relentlessly across mountains and valleys. Desperate, Lord Shiva sought out Lord Vishnu, who was resting on the cosmic serpent amidst the ocean. "Protect me from the wicked Asura," Shiva urged. 

"Leave him to me," Vishnu smiled, instantly devising a plan. 

Assuming the form of a young, innocent-looking Brahmachari (celibate student), Lord Vishnu intercepted the running Asura. 

"Where are you running to? Whom are you seeking?" the Brahmachari asked kindly. "Rest for a while. You seem tired."

Panting, Vrika stopped and boasted of the deadly boon he had just received from Lord Shiva, and how he intended to use it on the granter himself.

The Brahmachari laughed out loud, a sound of pure disbelief. "What! Shiva duping a devotee! I can't believe it."

"What do you mean?" Vrika asked, confused.

"That's because the moment you placed your palm on his head, you would realize that he had duped you," the young boy explained innocently. "He's just tricking you. Then why don't you place your palm on your own head and see?"

The dull-witted Asura pondered for a moment. "A good idea!" he agreed.

Without stopping to think, the foolish Asura placed his heavy palm firmly on his own head. The next moment, the deadly boon activated, and Vrikasura fell dead to the ground, a victim of his own treacherous ambition. 

Just then, Lord Shiva arrived, relieved to see the threat neutralized. The clever Brahmachari transformed back into the majestic Lord Vishnu. 

"I am sorry that the unfortunate Asura had to die," Lord Shiva sighed. 

"It was inevitable," Lord Vishnu replied calmly. "He who secures a boon to destroy others seeks his own destruction."
