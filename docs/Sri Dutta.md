# Shridatta

In the great ancient city of Pataliputra lived a man named Kalanemi and his wife. They were very poor, and they had no children.
"Everybody seems to have lots of money to spend," Kalanemi sighed one day, watching the bustling market.
"And children to love," his wife added sadly. "Let's pray to Shridevi to give us children and money."
"A good idea!" Kalanemi agreed. 
So they performed a fervent sacrifice to Shridevi, the Goddess of Wealth.

The goddess appeared before them in a blaze of light. "I am pleased with you. Your desires shall be fulfilled."
Soon after, a beautiful son was born to them. 
"Isn't he a fine baby?" Kalanemi smiled.
"Let us call him Shridatta as he is a gift of Shridevi," his wife decided.

Soon after that, Kalanemi's fortunes completely turned around. "I have made a big profit this month," he marvelled. "It is all the gift of Shridevi. I shall now be able to engage the best teachers for you, my son."
As he grew, Shridatta was taught the scriptures, the fine arts, and the rigorous art of war. He grew up as good as he was talented. 

"Kalanemi is fortunate. Shridatta has brought him immense luck. He is a good and affectionate boy," the townsfolk praised him.
Eventually, the King of Pataliputra heard of the brilliant young Shridatta. Kalanemi and his son were officially invited to the royal court. 
"I want your son to live in the palace as Prince Vikramashakti's companion," the king decreed.
"We are highly honoured, your majesty," Kalanemi bowed.

The king soon became very fond of Shridatta's wisdom and gentle nature. "Learn from Shridatta, son. Be studious and humble like him," the king would often scold the arrogant prince.
Prince Vikramashakti resented this deeply. *Everyone praises Shridatta. Father likes him more than me,* the prince seethed.

Shridatta naturally made many friends at court. "I wish Shridatta were our prince," the nobles whispered. "He is as courteous as Vikramashakti is rude. I would give my life for Shridatta."
All this made Vikramashakti furiously jealous of Shridatta. *One of these days I'll get even with him,* the prince vowed.

***

A few days later, Shridatta and his friends were resting near the river Ganga. Suddenly, they heard a cry from the water.
"Help! Help!" a beautiful woman was struggling in the current.
"I must help her," Shridatta yelled, diving into the river. "Take care, Shridatta. We'll wait till you come back," his friends called out.

Shridatta swam frantically, but the woman had disappeared under the surface. As he dove deep underwater to find her, he was stunned. *She seems to have vanished!*
But instead of a riverbed, he found himself staring at a massive, ancient temple hidden beneath the waves. 

He swam into the magical structure and emerged into an air-filled chamber. "Ah! A temple. I'll rest here till morning," he decided, exhausted.
The next morning, he explored the vast underwater palace. "There she is!" he spotted the woman from the river. "I'll follow her."

He followed her into a grand hall. Inside, a strange sight met his eyes—several beautiful women were weeping bitterly. 
"Why are they weeping?" he asked the woman he had followed.
"Sit down and I will tell you everything," she sighed. "I am an Asura princess—the granddaughter of the mighty King Bali. When Lord Vishnu defeated my grandfather, he drove us out of our kingdom of Tripura. A fearsome lion guards the gate to our hidden city. That lion can be killed only by a strong human being. That is why I brought you here!"
"Please let me try," Shridatta offered bravely.

The very next day, Shridatta bravely fought the monstrous lion. With a mighty swing of his sword, he dealt a fatal blow. "That should kill him."
To his complete surprise, the dead lion magically turned into a majestic Yaksha. 

"I am grateful to you, Shridatta. You have released me from a terrible curse," the Yaksha smiled. "This divine sword is called Mrigankaka. Take it. With it, you will always win."

Then it was the turn of the Asura princess to express her deep gratitude. "Take this magic ring!" she smiled, handing him a jewel. "It has the power to wipe out the effects of any poison."
"Thank you. Now I really must get back to my world," Shridatta bowed.
"Enter this well," she pointed. "You will find yourself back in the Ganga, at the exact place where you jumped in. Farewell!"

***

Meanwhile, on the riverbank, Shridatta's friends were worried sick. "Shridatta has been away for so long! What could have happened to him?" "He may be in danger... or even dead."
Just then, a heavenly voice was heard from the sky. "Shridatta is alive! He will soon be with you."

Anxious to give Shridatta's parents the good news, his friends rushed towards the city of Pataliputra. But on their way, they were intercepted by other loyal friends from the court.
"Don't go to Pataliputra!" they warned urgently. "The king died yesterday. Prince Vikramashakti has seized the throne, killed Shridatta's parents, and is now actively looking for you to kill you too!"
"Go hide in Ujjain," one friend advised. "I'll hide near the river and bring Shridatta to you when he returns."

When Shridatta finally swam out of the Ganga, his friend was waiting. "Where are the others?" Shridatta asked.
"Shh! Come quietly into the woods and I'll tell you. It's a terrible, long story."

Shridatta was horrified and heartbroken when he heard the news of his parents' brutal murder. "I'll avenge my parents with Mrigankaka, my divine sword!" he swore angrily.
"Let's first go to Ujjain where the others are waiting safely," his friend urged.

At Ujjain, Shridatta remained deeply depressed. "You are always moody, Shridatta," his friends noted.
"I have reason enough. My parents have been killed and I have not yet avenged their murder."
"Let's go to the great fair in the city," they suggested. "It will cheer you up."

***

At the crowded fair, Shridatta noticed a beautiful woman. "Who is that lovely girl?"
"She is Princess Mrigankavati, the only child of the King of Ujjain. He wants her to marry a rich and powerful prince. One fit to become his heir."

Suddenly, there was a commotion. "What's the matter? The princess has been bitten by a snake!"
The princess was quickly carried away to a nearby hut, pale and lifeless. 
"I can save her life," Shridatta yelled, pushing through the crowd. "What are we waiting for then?" his friends urged.

They hurried to the hut where the princess was laid out. "Alas! My mistress is cold and still," her maid wept.
"Let me in! I can revive her," Shridatta commanded. 
"Make way for him."
"Let me put this magic ring on her finger," Shridatta said.
"Who is he?" the royal guards muttered. "There is no hope. But let him try."

As Shridatta slipped the Asura princess's magic ring onto her finger, Princess Mrigankavati slowly regained consciousness. "What is the matter? Who is this stranger?"
"He saved your life. You were bitten by a deadly snake."
Shridatta and the princess gazed long at each other. 
"My saviour! I owe my life to you," she whispered.
*How beautiful she is! I wish she were mine,* Shridatta thought.

Just then, the King of Ujjain entered frantically. "What is happening here? How is Mrigankavati?"
"I'm all right now, father. She was bitten by a snake. This man has just revived her."
"I'll see that you are properly rewarded, my son!" the king beamed.
"I don't want a reward. I am glad I was there in time," Shridatta bowed humbly.

As Shridatta and his friends walked away, Shridatta deliberately slipped the ring off. "Mind your step. What are you thinking of?" a friend asked.
"I just remembered... I have accidentally forgotten my ring there."
"Shall I fetch it?"
"Don't," another friend smiled knowingly. "It will give Shridatta an excuse to meet her again."

Later, Mrigankavati's maid came to their hut. "Sir, you left your precious ring behind. My mistress would like to return it personally. I will wait for her this evening in the old temple outside the city."

That evening, they met in secret. "I love you, Mrigankavati. Will you marry me?" Shridatta asked.
"My father will never agree. He wants me to marry a rich and powerful prince," she replied sadly.
"Then come away with me. I can't live without you."
"I will."

That night, Shridatta's friends planned their escape. "Shridatta, you must be seen in the city tomorrow so that people won't connect you with Mrigankavati's sudden disappearance."
"You are right. I'll join you later. Take care of her."
"Don't worry."

***

Mrigankavati's disappearance created a massive stir the next morning. "She can't go far. The king has ordered his soldiers to find her."
The king's guards questioned Shridatta. "You are the man who saved her life, aren't you?"
"Yes! I came on a visit to see the place and was here in time to be of service."
"I shall return to Pataliputra tomorrow," Shridatta announced publicly to throw off suspicion.
"Come back some time. We'll tell you what happened," the guards said.

Shridatta secretly set out to join his friends in the forest. *Everyone thinks I have gone to Pataliputra... but I'll soon be with Mrigankavati.*
But when he rode up to the rendezvous point, he found his friends bleeding on the ground. "What's the matter? Where is Mrigankavati?"
"Some heavily armed horsemen attacked us. They took our horses... and Mrigankavati too."
"Go after them! Rescue her," they groaned.
"What about you?" Shridatta worried.
"Don't worry about us. Go. Hurry!"

Shridatta rode fiercely and soon caught up with the kidnappers. "Ah! There go the villains. Stop! Stop!"
He attacked them fearlessly. "My divine sword will never fail me." In minutes, he defeated the bandits. 
"I thought you would never come," Mrigankavati wept.
"I'll set you free."

On the long return journey, they had to share a single horse. "Now to help my friends," Shridatta said. "They tried so hard to protect me."
After they had covered some distance, Shridatta noticed the animal flagging. "Our horse is dangerously tired. We must dismount and let him rest."
As they dismounted, the exhausted horse collapsed and died. "Poor fellow! He did his best. We must walk now."

After walking for miles, Mrigankavati stumbled. "I cannot walk. I am tired and so thirsty."
"It has been too much for you. Rest here while I find water."
"Do not go far. I am frightened," she pleaded.
"I'll soon be back."

Shridatta ventured deep into the woods. "I must mark the way carefully. The forest is getting denser and I might get lost."
Shridatta at last found a stream and collected water. On his way back, the sun began to set. "Did I come by this tree or by that? Both look exactly alike. The light has changed, and I can't distinguish them."

"It's getting dark. I'll climb this tree. Maybe I can see her from up there," he thought desperately. But in the fading light, he couldn't recognise a thing. "I will have to wait here till morning. May God protect my poor Mrigankavati!"

Meanwhile, Mrigankavati waited in the dark. "What could have happened to him? Why isn't he back? If someone comes what shall I do?"
Suddenly, she heard a rustling. "What is that noise? Is it a lion? GRRR! KRRK!"
She screamed, but it was only an old hunter. "EEEEE!"
"Don't be afraid, lady!" the hunter soothed. "Good sir, please take me away from here!" she cried.
"Who are you? Why are you here alone?"
"M...my husband went to fetch water. He must have lost his way. He is not back yet."
"Come! I'll take you to Mathura. It is not far from here. It's not safe in the forest at night."

Outside the city of Mathura, the hunter said, "The Prime Minister is a good, honorable man. We'll go to his house. They will take care of you safely."
"But how will my Shridatta find me? Perhaps I shouldn't have come away," she worried.
"I'll go back to the exact spot tomorrow and tell him you are here," the hunter promised.
"You are very kind, sir."

The hunter left Mrigankavati safely in the Prime Minister's house and returned to the forest at dawn. "This is where I found her. I will wait here for Shridatta."
Shridatta too, was frantically on his way back. "By daylight, the way is so clear. This is the place."
When he reached the spot, the hunter approached him. "Are you Shridatta?"
"I am. Who are you? How do you know my name?"
"I'm a hunter. I found your wife here, alone at night. Shivering with fright. I took her to the house of the Prime Minister of Mathura."
"Thank God she is safe. I'll go to Mathura immediately."

***

When Shridatta reached Mathura, he marvelled, "What a beautiful city!"
Suddenly, someone called out to him. "Shridatta! I was so frightened. Whatever happened to you?"
"At last, I have found you!" he embraced Mrigankavati.

"Come. I must tell the Prime Minister and his wife that you've returned. They have been so incredibly kind to me. They deserve my undying gratitude and respect."
When they entered the house, the Prime Minister's wife smiled. "Mother! This is my lord," Mrigankavati introduced him.
"I am Shridatta, the son of Kalanemi of Pataliputra," Shridatta bowed respectfully.

The Prime Minister dropped his cup in shock. "Kalanemi! Your father is my brother! We have not seen each other since I left home as a young man to seek my fortune. How is he?"
"Alas! Both mother and he were killed some time ago by the cruel King Vikramashakti."

Shridatta told his uncle the whole tragic story. When he had finished, his uncle wiped away tears. "God brought you here to me. We had no children. Now I have two—a son and a daughter. You shall be my heir and marry Mrigankavati properly."

Shridatta and Mrigankavati were married with great joy. A few days later, Shridatta said, "Now I must go to find my good friends in the forest, and then to Pataliputra."
"And I shall take Mrigankavati back to her father in Ujjain to make peace. He must be so unhappy," his uncle agreed.

Mrigankavati went with her uncle and aunt to Ujjain. 
"I am the Prime Minister of Mathura. Mrigankavati is my daughter-in-law," the uncle introduced himself to the King of Ujjain.
The king hugged his daughter. "I am so relieved to see you. Why did you run away? Forgive me, father."

Shridatta found his friends in the forest and brought them to Ujjain. 
"You must challenge Vikramashakti," the King of Ujjain declared. "We'll make you the rightful King of Pataliputra."
"The King of Mathura will be on our side. You can depend on that," his uncle added.
"I'll collect a massive army and we will attack Pataliputra," the King of Ujjain made a bold decision.

A terrible, earth-shaking battle was fought outside Pataliputra. Shridatta, wielding his divine sword Mrigankaka, fought his way to Vikramashakti and killed him in single combat. 
"I have avenged my parents' death at last," Shridatta declared, lowering his sword.

The people of Pataliputra cheered. Shridatta became the beloved, just King of Pataliputra and reigned happily there with Queen Mrigankavati for many years.
