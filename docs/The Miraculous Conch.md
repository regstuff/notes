# The Miraculous Conch

Once there was a man who was poor but generous. His only companions were a cat and a dog. One day a weary traveller knocked at his door, and the man welcomed him in, gave him water to wash with, and served him all the rice he had cooked for himself and his companions. The cat, the dog, and their master went hungry that night as a result.

The next morning, the refreshed guest thanked the old man for his hospitality, and before leaving, gave him a small conch. "Drop that conch into the pot the next time you cook rice," he said, and departed. "What's the use of a conch?" the cat wondered, while the dog grumbled that money would have been more useful, since their master could have bought food with that instead.

That afternoon, cooking a handful of borrowed rice, the old man decided to try dropping the conch in — "it may improve the taste." The moment he did, a delicious aroma rose from the pot, drawing the cat and dog, and soon two curious neighbours as well, drawn by the smell. The generous old man couldn't bring himself to turn any of them away, and by the time the rice was ready, quite a crowd had gathered — yet however many people he served, the pot never seemed to run low. "It could only be due to the conch. It must be a magical one," he realized.

Word spread, and every day more people flocked to his house for the delicious rice. Eventually he began charging a small price, and made good money doing no work at all, while the cat and dog grew fat on the leftovers. One morning, some travellers stopped by for rice, bought a large quantity, and went on their way — and as the old man kept ladling out portions to his other customers afterward, he began to notice the rice in the pot actually decreasing for the first time. Digging to the bottom, he realized in horror: the conch was gone. It must have gone out in the rice he'd doled out to the travellers.

He and the dog rushed after them, searching frantically, but by evening they'd found no trace — "they seem to have vanished into thin air." Believing he'd lost the conch forever, the old man returned home dejected, only for the cat to snap at the dog for not chasing down "a few coins," provoking a fight when the dog pointed out the real loss was the conch itself.

Without his magic rice to sell, the villagers began to think the old man had lost his senses, muttering about a conch that had nothing to do with rice, and avoided his hut. His savings ran out, and the cat and dog grew hungrier by the day, until the dog decided enough was enough. "I am going to search for the conch. You may come if you want to," he told the cat, who reluctantly followed. Sensing the travellers must have crossed the river — which was why they'd never found them that day — the dog carried the cat across on his back, and the two began sniffing out every hut in the village on the other side.

Eventually they caught a faint smell of the rice the conch used to produce, coming from a locked wooden trunk in one house. Unable to open it, the dog enlisted a captured rat to gnaw a hole through the trunk and push the conch out — "we won't harm you if you do as you are told" — and the plan worked, but the household's owner caught them in the act and chased them out with a broom, shouting for help.

The cat and dog fled, dodging the pursuing villagers, and reached the river with the conch at last. But as the dog swam across with the cat riding on his back holding the conch in her mouth, passing villagers laughed at the "funny" sight of the pair — and hearing children's laughter, the cat couldn't resist laughing along too, dropping the conch into the river with a *plop*. "Oh, you stupid cat!" the dog snapped, abandoning her to dive after the conch, leaving the cat — who couldn't swim — to thrash wildly and just barely make it to shore on her own. "This is what happens when you trust a dog," she muttered bitterly.

Unable to retrieve the conch, and too afraid to face their master's anger over its loss a second time, the cat hid in a tree hollow while the dog swam home alone. When the old man asked where the cat was, the dog could only think, "has she found another master? Perhaps she has done the right thing," and the old man and his dog settled back into their old, poorer life.

Several days later, with no food left in the house, the dog went down to the river hoping a fisherman might spare a fish. One of the fishermen, having caught plenty that day, gave him a large fish to take home. The old man, delighted, began cleaning it to cook — and cutting it open, found a conch inside. "I think it's the same conch which we lost. We have got our conch back!" he cried, hugging the dog joyfully.

The next day, the old man resumed cooking his special rice, and once again people began flocking to buy it. Meanwhile the cat, having grown homesick in her hiding place, and drawn back by the smell of the familiar rice, returned. "Welcome home," the old man greeted her warmly, though the dog couldn't resist a jab: "Don't tell me that! You must have smelt the rice." From then on, the cat, the dog, and their master lived happily ever after.

**A Game of Chess.** Once there was a despotic king with a strange method of acquiring slaves: anyone who came to ask a favour of him first had to play a game of chess against him — but the outcome, unbeknownst to his opponents, was decided not by skill but by a trained cat placed on the board. If the cat moved and knocked over even one of seven lamps balanced on her tail, the challenger's wish would be granted. If she sat perfectly still until the game ended, the challenger became the king's slave instead. Since the well-trained cat never moved, the king never lost, and enslaved everyone unlucky enough to need his favour.

One day, a merchant from a distant city came to ask permission to trade there, and the king's minister explained the strange chess rule he'd first have to satisfy. Confident in his skill, the merchant agreed, and did everything possible to prolong the game once he realized what he was up against — but ultimately lost anyway, becoming the king's slave, and could only think to send word to his wife about his plight.

His wife was brave and clever. Furious that the king let a cat decide her husband's fate, and determined to free him and teach the king a lesson, she dressed as a man and went to the palace with a servant. Warned of the same chess condition, she accepted readily — "that cat will collapse before the game ends!" — and was led in to play.

As the game dragged on, she had her waiting servant release a mouse into the room. The cat's ears pricked up at the sight, but a fierce glare from the king kept her frozen. A second mouse produced the same tense reaction. Growing anxious now — "something is wrong with that cat today" — the king rushed to finish the game as his wife-in-disguise released a third mouse. This time the cat couldn't restrain herself any longer: she leapt forward, sending all seven lamps crashing down.

"I have won. The cat has moved and the game has not ended," the merchant's wife declared. When the humiliated king asked what she wanted, she didn't ask for herself: "I want you to release all the men you have enslaved." The king, bound by his own rule, had no choice but to grant it — and the merchant's wife was soon reunited with her husband, along with every other man the king's rigged game had ever trapped.
