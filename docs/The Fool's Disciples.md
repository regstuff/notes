# The Fool's Disciples - 1. The Fool's Disciples

Long ago, in a village in South India, lived an aged simpleton named Paramartha. 

One day, five foolish men met him. "Bless us, O Mahatma," they said. Paramartha blessed them. The men argued over who got the blessing, so Paramartha said, "All men are foolish. Therefore I have blessed all of you."

The simpletons were amazed and became his disciples. They named themselves Maddi, Madhaya, Milecha, Moodha and Pedda. The six fools built an ashram. 

One evening they had to cross a river. The Guru told them they must wait until the river was asleep to cross it. A disciple poked the river with a lit torch. The water hissed. "The river is awake and angry!" he cried. 

Later, another disciple poked it with a dry stick. It made no sound. "It's asleep! Let's cross," he said. 

On the other side, a disciple counted the group to make sure everyone made it across. He counted the other five, but forgot to count himself. "One of us is missing! The river claimed one of us," they wailed. 

A passing magician saw they were fools. He offered to "bring back" their missing friend for forty pieces of silver. He told them to close their eyes, and he hit each of them with a stick, making them call out their names. When they heard all six names, they rejoiced and paid the magician. 

Later, the group decided they needed a horse. They saw some pumpkins in a field near some horses and thought the pumpkins were "horses' eggs." They bought a pumpkin from the farmers. 

The Guru carried the pumpkin. He tripped and dropped it. A rabbit hopped out from the bushes near the broken pumpkin. The fools thought it was a baby horse running away. 

They lost the rabbit. The Guru told them it was a good thing, because a horse might have thrown him off and killed him. 

Later, they saw the reflection of a horse statue in a pond. They thought it was a real horse hiding in the water. They tried to fish it out with a sickle tied to a turban. 

A stranger saw them struggling and told them it was just a shadow. Realizing they were simpletons, he took pity on them and gave them an old, tired horse. 

The Guru rode the horse. He told his disciples to pick up anything that fell from the horse. The horse dropped dung, and a disciple picked it up and gave it to the Guru. 

The Guru was angry. "Only pick up what is on this list!" he ordered, giving them a list of his belongings. 

Later, the horse threw the Guru into a ditch. "Pull me out!" the Guru yelled. 

The disciples checked the list. "Your name is not on the list, Guru," they said. "We cannot pull you out."

The Guru had to ask for the list and write his own name on it before the foolish disciples would pull him out. 

Eventually, the old Guru fell ill. The disciples brought him no food or water, waiting for his end. He lost consciousness. They thought he was dead and put him on a funeral pyre. 

When they started chanting loudly, the Guru woke up and asked for water. The disciples rejoiced, thinking he had returned from the dead. 

"It's a miracle! The dead saint has come alive. He is truly a Mahatma!" they cheered. They lived happily in their ashram for a long time.
