# Narmada

Taraka was a menacing asura whom the three worlds feared. He had three ambitious sons who were determined to exceed their father's dark legacy. 

"We must become more powerful than our father," the first son declared.

"How can that be possible?" another asked.

"Let us pray to Brahma," the third suggested. "He will help us."

The brothers plunged themselves into deep meditation for many years. Finally, Brahma appeared before them in all his divine radiance. "Open your eyes. What is it that you want?"

"O Brahma, we want to be the greatest in the entire universe. Grant that we may never be killed!" they chorused.

"I cannot give you such a boon," Brahma replied gently. "All that is created must come to an end some day."

"In that case, give each one of us a city to rule over," the first son demanded. "One of gold, another of silver, and the third of iron."

"They must float in the skies, not pausing even for a single moment," added the second.

"Grant that they may be destroyed only once in a thousand years, when they align with each other for a few brief moments," the third son smirked.

"So be it," Brahma conceded.

"Ha! We are truly invincible!" the brothers rejoiced. "Nobody will be able to kill us in the few moments that our cities align! We are now greater than our father himself! Ha ha!"

Full of arrogance and evil, the brothers created chaos in Indraloka, Prithviloka, and Pataaloka—heaven, earth, and the underworld. The gods, desperate for salvation, rushed to Mount Kailasha.

"Tarakasura's sons need to be stopped," they pleaded. "You are our only hope, Shiva."

"I will put an end to their tyranny," Shiva promised.

Using their immense powers, Brahma and Vishnu forged divine weapons. "Here, take this bow," Brahma offered. 

"This fire-tipped arrow, when used along with the bow, will definitely pierce the target," Vishnu assured him.

Shiva accepted the weapons and waited patiently. When the three floating cities finally aligned in a perfect row, he let loose the fiery arrow with a mighty *WHOOSH!* The three cities were instantly burned to ashes. The sons of Taraka had finally met their end.

After the ashes had cooled, Shiva took some and applied three lines on his forehead, symbolising the burning of the three cities. To this day, these three lines of ash are considered a sacred symbol by the worshippers of Shiva.

The burning arrow had produced immense heat in Shiva's body. To cool it, he sat down in deep meditation. So great was the heat that perspiration poured down his body, forming a large pool at his feet. Filled with Shiva's divine energy, the pool miraculously gave birth to a little girl.

"He must be my father," the little girl thought, looking at Shiva. "The one who created me." She quietly sat beside him and joined in his meditation. 

After a long time, Shiva opened his eyes. *A gentle child, yet she sits with such determination,* he thought. "Open your eyes, Narmada," he said softly, naming her for her delicate and demure nature. 

"Greetings, Father!" Narmada smiled, pressing her hands together.

The site where Narmada was born is known as Amarkantak, in the Maikali mountains of Madhya Pradesh. It was named after Shiva, the eternal one who trapped the poison in his throat during the churning of the ocean.

Shiva then called upon King Mekel, a devout ruler of the region. "King Mekel, I entrust my daughter Narmada to your care. Raise her well."

"It would be an honour, Lord," King Mekel bowed.

Narmada brought great joy to her adoptive father. "Look, Father! This little bird's wing has healed. She is all set to fly!" she would exclaim, tending to the forest creatures. 

"You have taken good care of her, my child," Mekel smiled.

Years rolled by, and Narmada grew into a stunning young woman. According to the custom of the land, a royal messenger made a grand proclamation: "Hear! Hear! King Mekel is looking for a groom for the Princess Narmada. Any young man who brings to the king a flower that surpasses the princess's beauty, will be her groom."

Suitors from across the land arrived, bearing the most exquisite flowers. But the king was unhappy with all of them. "No, not good enough!" he dismissed suitor after suitor. 

Then, a handsome prince approached the throne. "Greetings, O King! I am Sonbhadra, and I have with me the Gulbakawali flower. It is a rare beauty and is used in the healing of illnesses too."

King Mekel was impressed. "Sonbhadra, you have proven to be a fine match for Narmada! Let the wedding preparations begin!"

"The honour is mine, O King," Sonbhadra bowed.

When the news reached Narmada in her chambers, she grew curious. "I wonder what kind of a man Sonbhadra is. How do I find out?" She called for her trusted attendant, Johila. "Johila, I'm forbidden from going outside or meeting Sonbhadra before the wedding. Will you find out if he is a kind and caring person?"

"As you wish, Princess Narmada," Johila agreed.

"Wonderful! Here, wear my clothes. Come back soon, I'll be waiting for you," Narmada said, handing over her royal garments.

Johila set out to the guest palace. "Excuse me, sir. Do you know Sonbhadra? The man who is to marry King Mekel's daughter?" she asked a man in the courtyard.

"I am Sonbhadra," he smiled. 

*How handsome!* Johila thought, instantly infatuated. 

"And you are?" Sonbhadra asked.

Johila hesitated. "I am.... er... yes...." 

"You must be the princess herself!" Sonbhadra assumed, seeing the royal clothes. "Come, Narmada. Let me show you around the place."

Johila, blinded by her sudden affection, did not reveal her true identity and spent her time with the prince. 

Back at the palace, days passed. "It has been many days. Why hasn't Johila returned yet? I must look for her," Narmada worried. She set out secretly and reached Sonbhadra's palace, only to find them sitting happily on a swing.

"Our wedding day is drawing closer, Narmada!" Sonbhadra was saying.

"I am so happy!" Johila replied.

"Huh!" Narmada gasped in shock. She stepped forward. "Johila! How could you betray me?"

"Johila?" Sonbhadra looked confused. "She is Narmada, the princess."

Johila's lies were exposed instantly. She fell to her knees. "Forgive me, princess!"

"It is too late for forgiveness," Narmada said coldly. Heartbroken and betrayed, Narmada prayed fervently to Shiva. "I cannot bear to stay here anymore. Father, let me return to water, the element I was born from. Turn me into a river that flows away from this place."

Shiva granted her wish. The river Narmada began to flow from Amarkantak, where she was born, all the way to the Arabian Sea.

Miserable and filled with regret, Sonbhadra also prayed to Shiva. "Lord, I have made a grave error in judgement. Grant that like Narmada I may also turn into a river." His wish was granted too, and the Sonbhadra (or Son river) began to flow from Amarkantak, eventually joining the Ganga. 

Johila, guilty for her actions, begged to be transformed into a river as well. Though her wish was granted, her river in Madhya Pradesh is not worshipped like most other Indian rivers, as she was unfaithful.

The Narmada remains a deeply sacred river. For her spirited flow, she is also known as Rewa, the agile one. The resounding rush of her waters is said to be the princess's distressed cry, continuing to nourish the lands and communities along her path.


# Tamraparni

The Malaya mountains, covered with lush, green forests, were home to diverse beasts and birds. Nestled deeply within these mountains was the peaceful hermitage of sage Atri, where he lived with his wife Anasuya and their many devoted disciples.

One day, a disciple approached the sage. "Maharshi, with your permission, I would like to visit the holy city of Kashi and take a bath in the Ganga."

Atri looked at the young man. *Hmm... he is not ready yet for such a long journey,* the sage thought. *But I must not discourage him. I need to find a middle path.* 

"I will call for you in a while and tell you what can be done," Atri promised. He chose an auspicious hour, found a suitable spot in the hermitage, and summoned his student. "I am ready, Maharshi," the student said eagerly.

Atri tapped his wooden staff on the ground twice. *TAP! TAP!* Suddenly, water burst forth from the earth with a loud *SPLASH!* and *GURGLE!*

"Huh!?" the student gasped, jumping back.

"Here, you can take a dip in this river," Atri smiled.

"But... It is not the Ganga. And we are not in Kashi," the student said, confused.

"You are right, son," Atri nodded. "I invoked the Ganga to flow here and she has accepted my prayer."

The overjoyed student thanked his teacher and bathed in the fresh waters. "This river shall hereafter be known as Ghatana, the one who has united with the Ganga. She will also be known as Atreyi, the daughter of Atri," the sage declared.

Ghatana gushed through the mountainous landscape, forming smaller rivulets and mighty waterfalls. But on her banks, the sage Putrayagneshwara was sitting in deep meditation. The loud, rushing roar of the new river completely broke his concentration. He opened his eyes, raised his hand, and chanted, "HUMMMMM."

Instantly, the river stopped flowing.

When Atri heard of this, he immediately went to meet Putrayagneshwara. "Please let Ghatana go back to flowing through these lands. She will not disturb you anymore," Atri pleaded.

"Atri, I cannot undo what I have done," Putrayagneshwara replied solemnly. "But there will come a day when the sage Agastya will arrive here with Tamraparni, who will take the form of a river. Ghatana will merge with her and then resume her flow."

Resigned, Ghatana seeped into the ground and waited for the ordained day.

Far away, in Mount Kailasha, all the gods and sages had gathered to witness the grand wedding of the divine couple, Shiva and Parvati. However, the sheer number of guests weighed heavily on the land. 

"Oh, the land is sinking!" the guests cried in panic.

Shiva turned to the wise sage Agastya. "Agastya, head southwards to balance the landmass. The weight of your knowledge will help restore the balance here."

"As you wish, Lord Shiva," Agastya bowed. 

Before he departed, Shiva filled Agastya's kamandalu (small water pot) with the icy, sacred waters of Kailasha, and Parvati blessed him by giving him the wedding garland from Shiva's neck. 

Just as Agastya was about to leave the region, a miracle occurred. The garland suddenly transformed into a breathtakingly beautiful young woman. 

Agastya's wife, Lopamudra, smiled warmly at the girl. "We will name her Tamraparni, the one whose skin shines like copper."

Agastya, Lopamudra, and Tamraparni left Kailasha and journeyed down south. Soon, they reached the Malaya mountains. Malayeshwara, the god of the mountains, welcomed them warmly. "Great souls like you bless the land you walk on. I am fortunate to have you visit these mountains," he greeted them.

"It is our pleasure, Lord Malayeshwara," Agastya replied.

At that moment, the celestial singer Tumburu and the sage Parvata descended from the skies. "We bring a message from Kailasha, from the great Lord Shiva," Tumburu announced. "He would like you, Lord Malayeshwara, to adopt Tamraparni and care for her like your own child."

"I will do so," Malayeshwara agreed happily. The skies immediately resounded with divine music, and the heavens showered flowers on the earth. On the exact spot where they stood, a sacred linga appeared, and from it emerged Shiva himself. 

"Agastya, you will continue residing in these mountains," Shiva instructed. 

After Shiva disappeared, Malayeshwara built a grand temple for the linga and welcomed Tamraparni into his royal family with a massive celebration. She was given all the luxuries a princess could ever imagine, but she was not interested in any of it. Instead, she spent her days visiting Agastya's new hermitage to listen to his teachings, worshipping at the Shiva temple, or peacefully exploring the Malaya forests.

Time passed. One day, Lopamudra suggested, "We have lived in these beautiful mountains for so long. Why don't we visit the holy places around the region?"

"A wonderful idea! We can start tomorrow," Agastya agreed. 

"I would like to join the two of you as well. May I?" Tamraparni asked eagerly.

"Of course, Tamraparni, but you must take your father's permission," Lopamudra advised. Tamraparni rushed home, and Malayeshwara happily granted her request. 

The three of them set off on their pilgrimage, winding their way through dense forests. Suddenly, a piercing scream shattered the silence. 

"NOOOOO!" 

"What is that sound?!" Agastya frowned. 

"It sounds like someone in distress," Lopamudra said. 

"Let us go see!" Tamraparni urged, running ahead. 

They followed the cries and found a young woman sitting on the forest floor, weeping bitterly over a motionless man. "What is the matter? What happened to him?" Lopamudra asked gently.

"My husband Sumedha and I were attacked by a rakshasa," the woman sobbed. "He killed my husband but failed to kill me. As soon as he touched my hair, he turned to ash."

"Who are you, young woman?" Agastya asked.

"I am Kuntala, daughter of sage Kashyapa."

Tamraparni knelt beside the inconsolable Kuntala and embraced her warmly. "It is indeed a cruel fate, Kuntala. Try to be strong," she whispered comfortingly. 

Just then, a weak groan came from the ground. "Uh..."

"This cannot be true! Kuntala! I have regained life," Sumedha gasped, opening his eyes. 

"It is your presence that has given him life, sister," Kuntala said, looking at Tamraparni in awe.

"Mine?" Tamraparni asked, astonished.

"Kuntala is right, Tamraparni. Your voice has woken Sumedha from his death," Agastya confirmed. "The grace of Shiva is on you, child."

"Then I will use this grace to help whoever I can," Tamraparni vowed. After Kuntala and Sumedha paid their respects and left, Tamraparni turned to Agastya. "Great sage, transform me into a river so my waters may give life to all the plants, animals, and people I meet in my course."

"It is not just Shiva's blessing, but also your noble intentions that make you special, Tamraparni," Agastya smiled. "I will pray to Mahadeva that he may grant your wish. Once he fulfils your wish, the river Ghatana will surface from underground. Merge with her and continue flowing."

"As you say," Tamraparni bowed. She instantly transformed into a beautiful, copper-hued river. As promised, she joined the waiting Ghatana, and both rivers flowed as one.

In due course, Tamraparni expressed her desire to marry Samudraraja, the king of the ocean. Her father Malayeshwara gifted her precious pearls, which she wore on the day of her wedding. Today, it is believed that closer to the ocean, the Tamraparni river bed is still filled with pearls, and her copper color comes from the red sand and the shedding leaves of the surrounding forests.

Later, Lord Shiva and Parvati visited the river. Shiva instructed the hot-tempered sage Durvasa to bathe in the waters and compose a prayer. Thus, the famous 'Tamraparni Stotram' was born. To this day, the majestic river brings life and blessings to all who dwell on her banks.
