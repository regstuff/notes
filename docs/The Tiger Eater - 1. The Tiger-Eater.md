# The Tiger-Eater

One bright morning, a farmer named Shersingh was peacefully ploughing his field when a large, hungry tiger quietly walked up to him. 

"Good morning, friend," the tiger purred smoothly. "Have you had your breakfast?"

Shersingh, trembling in his sandals, stammered, "Y... es!"

"Then I will have mine," the tiger declared, eyeing the oxen.

"Please unyoke those bullocks," the tiger commanded.

"No! No!" Shersingh protested, throwing his hands up. "You can't eat my bullocks!"

"And why not?" the tiger growled, baring his fangs. "You've had a good breakfast, and you expect me to starve? Unyoke those bullocks!"

Shersingh's mind raced. *I must save my bullocks at any cost!* "My Lord! Spare the bullocks," he begged on his knees. "You can have the fat cow my wife looks after at home."

The tiger licked his lips. "Oh, well. One animal is as good as another. All right, I'll settle for the cow."

"I wouldn't dare trick you, my Lord. I'll send my wife with the cow," Shersingh promised, scrambling away. 

When Shersingh reached home, he hurriedly explained the terrifying encounter to his formidable wife. "Yes! We're in trouble! Real trouble!"

His wife grabbed her broom in outrage. "And you want to save your bullocks at the cost of my cow! We could live without milk but not without bread. And where will the bread come from without corn? How can I grow corn without ploughing the fields? And how can I plough without my bullocks?"

"You see I have reasoned it out and... yet couldn't outsmart a stupid tiger," Shersingh admitted sheepishly. 

"You need a brain for that," his wife scoffed, pointing a finger at him. "Go to the tiger and tell him I am bringing the cow."

Shersingh didn't dare argue. "Can't you give a simple message to a stupid animal?" she yelled after him.

When Shersingh gave the message, the tiger sat down lazily. "All right, I'll wait. But if she doesn't turn up with the cow... I'll eat you instead."

"WHAT!" Shersingh gulped.

Fortunately for Shersingh, his wife did turn up soon after. But she wasn't leading a cow. She came galloping on a horse, dressed fiercely as a man, and brandishing a sharp sickle!

"I HOPE I FIND A TIGER TODAY TOO!" she roared in a booming, terrifying voice as she approached the field. The tiger's ears perked up in alarm. 

"MM... THE TIGER I HAD FOR BREAKFAST YESTERDAY WAS SO TASTY!" she bellowed. Spotting the big cat, she pointed her sickle and yelled gleefully, "AH! I'M LUCKY! A TIGER!"

The tiger's eyes bulged in pure terror. "NO!" he screeched. "HELP!" Without looking back, the beast turned and fled into the jungle for its life.

Deep in the forest, the tiger collapsed, gasping for breath as he told a passing jackal about the terrifying 'tiger-eater'.

The jackal chuckled. "There's no need to be frightened, my Lord. For you, maybe. I wonder why he is so particular about the animal! Why can't he eat jackals?" 

"That monster eats only tigers, not jackals!" the tiger insisted, still shaking. 

"My Lord, please listen to me," the jackal said reasonably. "That monster could only be the farmer's wife. Are you sure? Yes. I saw her from up here on the hill. It was a woman dressed like a man."

"Don't go without your breakfast because of a woman," the jackal urged.

"I don't believe you," the tiger growled suspiciously. "That monster might have bribed you to lure me to her."

"Let's go together then," the jackal offered. "You might take me there and then run away," the tiger countered.

"If that's what you fear, let's tie our tails together. Then I can't run away," the jackal proposed. 

The tiger agreed. With their tails tied tightly together, the two animals set off back towards the field. 

Meanwhile, at the field, Shersingh was roaring with laughter. "HA HA! Every time I think of that stupid animal I feel like laughing." 

Suddenly, his wife pointed towards the tree line. "HO... AH... AH... NOW WHAT'S THE MATTER?" The tiger was back, and the jackal was with him! "THE TIGER! THE TIGER IS BACK! LET'S RUN AWAY!" Shersingh panicked. 

"What's that jackal doing with him? Wait!" his brave wife ordered, standing her ground with her sickle raised. 

As the tied animals approached, she walked boldly towards them and yelled in her booming, 'tiger-eater' voice: "THANK YOU, FRIEND JACKAL. IT WAS KIND OF YOU TO BRING ME SUCH A FINE, FAT TIGER!"

The tiger didn't wait to hear another word. "MY LORD... MY LORD!" the jackal cried in warning, but it was too late. 

The tiger turned and fled with such blinding speed that he could hardly hear the jackal's pleas. He ran for miles through the thorny jungle, dragging the poor, unfortunate jackal helplessly behind him. 

Watching the dust settle, Shersingh's wife smirked. "That tiger won't ever come here again!" And he never did.
