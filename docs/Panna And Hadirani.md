# Panna and Hadi Rani: Two Tales of Self-Sacrifice

## Panna Dhay

In the year 1531, Vikramaditya ascended the legendary throne of Chittor as the Rana of Mewar. He belonged to the illustrious line of Sisodia Rajputs, renowned for their valor and strength. However, the young Vikramaditya was shockingly arrogant, insolent, and utterly incompetent. Disgusted by his insulting behavior, many self-respecting nobles simply left his court. 

His mother, Rani Karmavati, was deeply distressed. "I feel sorry for his mother, the Queen," sighed Panna, her most trusted maid, nurse, and companion. Panna had always been fiercely loyal to the royal family. 

When Bahadur Shah of Gujarat saw that the nobles of Mewar were alienated from their king, he invaded the kingdom. Initially, the nobles refused to lift a finger to defend the arrogant Rana. But Rani Karmavati cleverly appealed to their pride, writing to them that Chittor had always belonged to the glorious Sisodias and must not be allowed to fall to outsiders. Her emotional plea worked; the nobles returned and fought bravely, successfully defending the fort. 

During the siege, for their own safety, Rana Vikramaditya and his younger brother, Prince Uday Singh, had been sent away to the allied state of Bundi. Panna Dhay was entrusted with the crucial task of accompanying them and ensuring the safety of the young heir, Uday Singh. 

Unfortunately, while they were away, the siege at Chittor worsened. To protect her honor from the advancing enemy, Rani Karmavati led the Rajput women in committing the ultimate sacrifice of *Jauhar* (self-immolation). 

When it was safe again, Vikramaditya returned to Chittor. But tragedy had taught him nothing. He once again surrounded himself with rogues and sycophants. Worst of all, he reinstated Banvir, a cruel and ambitious scoundrel whom his late father had specifically banished from Mewar for life. 

Panna's sharp intuition warned her of impending disaster. "Banvir is waiting for an opportunity to strike," she fretted. Her fears were entirely justified. Banvir secretly plotted to kill both the foolish Rana and his younger brother, Uday Singh, to seize the throne for himself. 

One fateful evening, Uday Singh and Panna's own son, Chandan, returned exhausted from a long day of hunting. Panna fed both the young boys and put them to sleep in the royal nursery. She had barely closed her eyes when a terrified maid burst into the room. 

"Panna! Banvir has murdered the Rana!" the maid gasped. "I saw him myself. His sword was dripping with blood, and he is coming this way. I am sure he wants to harm Uday Singh!"

Panna’s heart pounded, but she acted with lightning speed. "Bring me the biggest fruit basket you can find," she commanded a servant. 
She gently lifted the sleeping Prince Uday Singh and placed him carefully into the large basket, covering him completely with fresh fruits and leaves. "Smuggle this basket out of the palace immediately," she ordered her most trusted guards. "Keep Uday safe, and meet me at the river outside the city."

With Uday safely spirited away, Panna was left with a horrifying, agonizing choice. Banvir was fast approaching, and if he found the prince's bed empty, he would undoubtedly raise an alarm, hunt down the basket, and murder the true heir of Mewar. The dynasty had to be saved at all costs. 

With tears streaming down her face, Panna lifted her own sleeping son, Chandan, and gently laid him in the royal bed, covering him with Uday's silk blankets. 

Moments later, the murderous Banvir kicked open the nursery door, his sword raised high. "Where is Uday Singh?" he growled. 
Unable to trust herself to speak without breaking down, Panna simply pointed a trembling finger toward the bed. Without a moment's hesitation, Banvir brought his heavy sword down on the sleeping figure, instantly killing Panna's innocent child. 

In a staggering display of superhuman restraint, Panna watched her own son die without letting out a single cry of anguish, knowing that any display of grief would reveal her ruse. Banvir left the room, triumphantly believing he had eliminated the final obstacle to the throne. 

As soon as he was gone, Panna quietly slipped out of the palace in the dead of night, leaving behind the body of her beloved son. She secretly reunited with the guards holding the basket and fled to Kumbhalmer. There, she presented the true heir, Uday Singh, to the loyal chieftain Rao Asa Sah, who sheltered the prince in secret. 

Four years later, Uday Singh came of age and was married to a Rajput princess. Panna then contacted all the loyal chieftains of Mewar, dramatically revealing the truth of Uday Singh's escape and her own shattering sacrifice.

Deeply moved and enraged, the chieftains rose in massive revolt against the usurper Banvir. In a decisive battle near Maholi, Banvir's forces were utterly routed. Uday Singh marched into Chittor and was officially crowned the Maharana of Mewar. 

"Panna, I owe you a debt which I can never repay," the new King told her with tears of immense gratitude in his eyes. 
Panna smiled through her own tears. "Don't talk of debts, my son. Seeing you on the throne today, I have forgotten all my own sorrows."

## Hadi Rani

In the year 1660, Rao Ratna Singh of Salumber proudly brought his incredibly beautiful bride, Princess Hadi of Bundi, to his grand palace. Their joy, however, was destined to be painfully short-lived. 

The couple had barely settled into their chambers when a frantic messenger arrived with an urgent, royal summons. 
"Salutations, Raoji," the messenger gasped. "The Mughal Emperor, Aurangzeb, is on his way to Roop Nagar to forcibly marry Princess Prabhavati against her will. She has desperately appealed to our Maharana Raj Singh of Chittor for help. The Maharana is marching to rescue her, but he needs you to immediately intercept and stall the Mughal forces before they reach Roop Nagar."

Ratna Singh read the letter and turned visibly pale. As a valiant Rajput, he knew his duty was to immediately ride into battle. But as a deeply infatuated newlywed, the thought of leaving his beautiful bride of merely a few days was agonizing. 

"You must leave immediately," Hadi Rani told him firmly when she learned of the crisis. 
"I'll... go tomorrow," Ratna Singh hesitated, his eyes lingering on her face. "No, you will not. She was prompted by jealousy. It was her possessive love for you that made her do it," Hadi insisted. "The honor of a Rajput is at stake! Tomorrow may be too late."

Ashamed but still deeply reluctant, Ratna Singh agreed. He dressed for battle, mounted his horse, and led his troops toward the valley of Gogunde to intercept the Mughal army commanded by Dilawar Khan. 

However, as he rode away from the palace, his heart constantly looked back. He could not stop thinking about Hadi. Halting his horse, he summoned his most trusted messenger. "Go back to the Rani," he ordered secretly. "Tell her I am thinking of her. Ask her to send me a memento to keep her close to my heart during the battle."

The messenger hurried back and conveyed the message to Hadi Rani. 

Hadi listened in growing horror and profound despair. "If he is asking for a memento now, right before a battle, his heart is not fully in the fight," she realized with chilling clarity. "It is my physical form that makes him waver. He is distracted by his love for me. If his mind is divided, he will surely fall in battle, and the honor of Mewar will be lost forever."

Knowing there was only one way to completely sever his earthly attachments and instantly focus his mind on his sacred duty, the brave queen made a terrifying, ultimate decision. 

"I will give you a memento to take to my lord," she told the messenger calmly. "Tell him that he shouldn't turn away from his duty now. His loved one will meet him in the hereafter."

Hadi Rani walked into her private chamber, drew a heavy, sharp sword from the wall, and prayed to Goddess Durga for courage. Before anyone could realize what was happening, with one powerful, decisive stroke, she completely severed her own head!

Her horrified maids wrapped the queen's severed head in a clean cloth and handed the gruesome bundle to the waiting messenger, tears streaming down their faces. 

Unaware of the tragedy, Ratna Singh was waiting impatiently on the road. When the messenger arrived and silently presented the cloth bundle, Ratna Singh hurriedly pulled it open, expecting a bracelet or a ring. 

As he stared down at the beautiful, lifeless face of his newlywed bride, the sheer magnitude of her sacrifice struck him like a physical blow. In that agonizing moment, all his earthly desires, fears, and hesitations instantly evaporated. 

"Brave Hadi!" he whispered, his face hardening into a mask of pure, unstoppable resolve. "You upheld the Rajput tradition, and you paid for my default with your own life!"

Tying the cloth containing her head securely around his neck, Ratna Singh drew his sword and turned to his troops. "Forward, my soldiers! We will show them our mettle today!"

Driven by a supernatural fury and an absolute lack of fear, Ratna Singh slammed into the advancing Mughal troops like a hurricane. He fought with the terrifying relentlessness of a man who possessed absolutely nothing left to lose. 

Though heavily outnumbered and soon suffering grievous, fatal wounds, Ratna Singh refused to fall. His terrifying assault forced the mighty Dilawar Khan to order a full retreat in sheer panic. 

Ratna Singh won a spectacular victory, successfully stalling the Mughal army and allowing Maharana Raj Singh to safely rescue Princess Prabhavati. His duty fulfilled and his honor restored, the valiant Rao Ratna Singh finally succumbed to his wounds on the battlefield, peacefully joining his beloved Hadi Rani in eternal sleep.
