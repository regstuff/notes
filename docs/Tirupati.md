# Tirupati

Many, many years ago, a humble farmer was walking through the forest when he came across an enormous anthill. 

"What an enormous anthill! Anyway, I'd better hurry if I am to get home before dusk," he muttered to himself. 

Suddenly, a booming, divine voice echoed from within the mound. "Halt, peasant! Feed my ants. They are hungry."

Scared and bewildered, the farmer poured all his fresh milk onto the anthill. He ran back to the city and told the King about the strange incident. 

"It is a divine command," the King declared. "I must continue giving milk to the anthill. I am sure it will result in good for my people."

So, the King began offering milk to the anthill every morning. Soon, the heavy rains washed away the mud of the anthill, revealing a magnificent black idol hidden within. 

"What is this? An idol hidden within the anthill!" the King gasped. "I was right. This was a divine bidding. I will now build a temple for this idol, matching its serenity and beauty."

And so begins the story of the richest temple on earth. 

***

The story of Tirupati truly began long before the temple was built. Lord Vishnu had returned to Vaikuntha, his heavenly abode, after completing his Krishna avatar on Earth. 

But unrest was still rampant on Earth. Many great sages had come together to perform a massive yagna (sacrificial offering) under the guidance of Sage Kashyapa. 

The wily wandering sage, Narada, approached the sages at the yagna. "Greetings, O revered sages! Have you decided who amongst Shiva, Brahma, and Vishnu will receive the final offering of the yagna?"

The sages were confused. "It should be Shiva," one argued. 

"No, Vishnu," another countered. 

"Brahma. He is the creator of the three worlds," said a third. 

Sage Bhrigu, a temperamental and arrogant sage with an all-knowing third eye embedded in the sole of his foot, stepped forward. "There is a simple way to decide who amongst the three is worthy of your offering," Bhrigu declared proudly. "We just have to ask the worthy sage, Bhrigu, to tell us. I will undertake a journey to all three to decide who is worthy."

Bhrigu first visited Brahma in Brahmaloka. However, Brahma was deep in thought and did not even look in his direction. 

"He has not even looked in my direction. He is making me wait!" Bhrigu fumed. Brahma's lack of response enraged the arrogant sage. "O Brahma, you are arrogant and do not deserve to be worshipped! I curse you that from this day, you will not be worshipped in any form on earth."

Bhrigu then approached Kailasha, the abode of Shiva. But the guards stopped him. "Halt, O sage, you cannot go in! We have been told not to let anybody enter."

Bhrigu angrily pushed his way in and found Shiva deep in conversation with his wife, Parvati. Shiva did not notice the sage immediately. 

"What is the meaning of this?" Bhrigu shouted, insulted once more. "As you are so busy, I don't think it will be worthwhile making that offering to you. I curse you. Henceforth you will only be worshipped as a Lingam, by all on earth."

Finally, Bhrigu proceeded to Vaikuntha, the abode of Vishnu, expecting more disrespect. He found Vishnu resting on the great serpent Adisesha, with Goddess Lakshmi at his feet. 

Enraged by what he perceived as Vishnu ignoring him, Bhrigu marched right up to the Lord... and struck Vishnu violently on his chest with his foot! 

With his divine intuition, Vishnu immediately understood the reason for Bhrigu's anger. Instead of retaliating, Vishnu proceeded to wash Bhrigu's feet. 

"O great sage, welcome! Let me have the honour of serving you," Vishnu said humbly. And as he massaged Bhrigu's feet, he secretly pinched the extra third eye in Bhrigu's sole shut, thus destroying the source of the sage's arrogance. 

Bhrigu immediately realized his terrible mistake. "Vishnu, my arrogance blinded me. You are the worthiest of all. Please accept the sacrificial offering made by the sages on earth," Bhrigu bowed, his arrogance completely gone. 

But when Bhrigu left, Lakshmi was furious. "Sage Bhrigu kicked you on your chest, on the very place where I reside! He has insulted me... and you did not even reprimand him for his insolence!"

"Lakshmi..." Vishnu tried to calm her. 

"I cannot stay here any longer!" Lakshmi declared. She descended to earth and began to live in Karvipur, leaving a desolate Vishnu behind. 

Searching for his beloved Lakshmi, Vishnu came down to earth and stepped into the Seshachalam hills. Missing her terribly, he sat in an anthill and meditated, going without food or water. 

Brahma and Shiva, seeing his plight from the heavens, decided to help. They took the form of a calf and a cow and went to Lakshmi, who told them of her plan. Lakshmi took the divine cow and calf and gifted them to the local Chola King. 

The King, in turn, presented the cow and calf to his Queen. "You will take the cow and calf to graze every day," the Queen instructed her royal cowherd. 

But soon, the Queen noticed something strange. "Why is the cow yielding no milk? The cowherd is cheating us! I must get to the bottom of this."

The Queen confronted the cowherd. "I am innocent. Give me a chance to prove it," he pleaded. 

The cowherd followed the cow secretly into the forest to see what happened to her milk. To his absolute surprise, the cow walked up to a large anthill... and voluntarily emptied all her milk right into it! 

"In his anger at the animal, the cowherd hurled his heavy axe at the cow. But to his horror... it hit Lord Vishnu, who stepped out of the anthill to protect the cow. 

*THWAACK!!!* The axe struck Vishnu's head, inflicting a deep, bleeding wound. 

Shocked at what he had done to the Lord, the cowherd fell to the ground, unconscious. 

The terrified cow returned bellowing to the palace. "What is this? The cow and the calf are so terrified. What could have happened?" the King wondered, following the cow into the forest. 

The cow led the King to where the unconscious cowherd lay, with an angry, bleeding Vishnu standing over him. 

"You fool! Because of your ignorance, this cowherd lies unconscious and I am hurt," Vishnu thundered at the King. "I curse you that you will have no heir."

"My Lord, forgive me, for I did not know. Give me a chance to atone for my sins," the King wept, falling at Vishnu's feet. 

Seeing the King's genuine regret, Vishnu forgave him. "In your next birth, you will be born as King Akasa Raja. You will then have a daughter whom I shall marry."

Meanwhile, Vishnu settled down in Varahakshetra in the form of a hermit named Shrinivasa. His deep wound left a perceptible bald spot on his head. While he brooded over his reflection in the water, a Gandharva princess named Neela Devi watched him. Moved by his misery, she cut off her own lustrous hair and pressed it onto Vishnu's bald spot, miraculously healing it. Overwhelmed by her selfless act, Vishnu decreed that whoever came to worship him at this hill would sacrifice their hair to her. 

As foretold by Vishnu, the Chola King was reborn as King Akasa Raja, the prosperous ruler of Thondamandalam. 

"If only my wish for an heir was fulfilled," Akasa Raja sighed. 

The King went to the sages for advice. "We will need to perform a special yagna. We'll make the preparations immediately," they advised. "O King, as part of the yagna, you and your Queen must till this fertile piece of land."

As Akasa Raja began to till the land, his plough struck something hard. *THUNKKKK!*

He dug into the soil and found a golden lotus. Resting atop the lotus was a beautiful baby girl! 

"A baby girl! Our daughter!" the overjoyed King and Queen cried. As she was found resting atop a golden lotus, they named her Padmavati. 

Suddenly, Lord Agni, the God of Fire, appeared from the yagna fire. "O King, take good care of this child. She is the reincarnation of Vedavati, a devout maiden who sacrificed herself to escape the demon Ravana. She is destined to marry Lord Vishnu himself in this birth."

The years went by, and Padmavati grew into a beautiful young woman. 

One day, Shrinivasa was out hunting in the forest when he heard cries for help. "Help! Help!"

It was a rogue elephant chasing Princess Padmavati and her maids! Shrinivasa bravely subdued the elephant and chased it away. 

"He saved our lives," Padmavati breathed, gazing at the handsome hermit. "Whoever he may be, I vow to marry only him."

Shrinivasa was equally captivated. He returned to his foster mother, Vakula Devi. "...Her name is Padmavati and she is the daughter of Akasa Raja. Mother, you must go to the King and ask for her hand in marriage to me. She has been destined to marry me." And Shrinivasa revealed Padmavati's divine story and his true identity as Lord Vishnu to Vakula Devi. 

"My son is adamant about marrying Padmavati. But will the King believe me when I tell him about Shrinivasa's true identity?" Vakula Devi worried. 

To help his mother, Shrinivasa disguised himself as a fortune-teller and went to the palace. He predicted to the Queen, "The Princess is destined to marry Lord Vishnu himself. A proposal will soon come that you must not refuse."

Soon after, Vakula Devi reached the palace and explained her errand. "...And hence, I have come here, O King, to ask for your daughter's hand in marriage to my son, Shrinivasa."

The Queen quickly informed the King about the fortune-teller's prediction. The King consulted his advisor, Brihaspati. "O King, the Lord himself has come seeking your daughter in marriage. Do not hesitate, all will be well," Brihaspati smiled. 

Preparations for the grand wedding began in earnest. However, Shrinivasa was worried. "I have no money, nothing... how do I bring my bride to a home befitting a princess?"

To find a solution, Shrinivasa approached Kubera, the Lord of Wealth. "O Kubera, please loan me the money I need, to marry and bring my wife home."

Kubera gladly loaned Shrinivasa the massive wealth. With this wealth, Shrinivasa created a magnificent home for Padmavati and himself, which was like Vaikuntha itself. 

The day of the wedding arrived. In the presence of Brahma, Shiva, and all the gods, Shrinivasa married Padmavati in a glorious ceremony. After their marriage, they lived happily, spending time in each other's company. 

But their peace was soon shattered. 

Meanwhile, in Karvipur, sage Narada visited Lakshmi. "Narayana, Narayana! O Mother Lakshmi, have you heard the good news? Lord Vishnu has married Princess Padmavati. What pomp, what gaiety..."

Seething with rage and jealousy, Lakshmi rushed to Varahakshetra. At the terrible sight of Lakshmi's anger, Vishnu simply froze in fright. 

Then began a great fight between Lakshmi and Padmavati. 

"I have resided in his heart since the beginning of time, and you, an upstart, claim him from me!" Lakshmi yelled. 

"He was destined to be my Lord!" Padmavati retorted. 

In his utter panic and fear, caught between his two furious wives, Lord Vishnu miraculously turned into a magnificent stone idol! 

Their terrible fight brought Brahma and Shiva rushing to Earth. 

"O Brahma! O Shiva! You know that I have always resided in the Lord. And now she wants to take him away from me," Lakshmi wept. 

Brahma gently narrated the story of Vedavati's sacrifice and Padmavati's destiny to Lakshmi. "Vishnu is only fulfilling a divine promise," Brahma explained. 

"I understand," Lakshmi sighed, her anger melting away. 

She chose to stay near her Lord, settling down at Tiruchanur as Alarmel Mangai, offering boons to her devotees. Padmavati settled down as well. 

And the magnificent stone idol of Vishnu remained on the hills, worshipped to this day as Lord Venkateshwara, or one who washes away all sins. A visit to him is considered incomplete without a visit to the Padmavati temple.

## Seshachalam

The origin of the Tirumala hills lies in an ancient contest of power between Vayu, the God of Wind, and Adisesha, the King of Serpents. 
Once, Vishnu wanted to spend some quiet time with his consort, Lakshmi, and asked Adisesha to ensure that they were not disturbed. The dutiful Adisesha refused to let even Vayu enter Vaikuntha. Incensed by what he perceived as a slight, Vayu threatened to blow Adisesha off Mount Meru. But the serpent king did not budge. 
The terrible contest that followed lasted for many days and wreaked utter havoc in the heavens. Finally, a shaken Brahma approached Adisesha and begged him to relent to save the universe. The serpent king acceded to Brahma's request and relaxed his grip... and was immediately blown away to earth by the waiting Vayu. 
However, in return for his graciousness, Brahma granted Adisesha a boon - he would be with his Lord forever and worshipped as such. Adisesha was thus transformed into a massive range of hills, with his hood intact. The hill range is what we know today as Seshachalam - the home of Lord Balaji of Tirupati. 

## Neela Devi

Why do people make an offering of their hair at Tirupati? The answer lies in the story of Lord Vishnu's debt to a kind-hearted Gandharva princess, Neela Devi. 
The wound that the cowherd's axe had inflicted on Lord Vishnu did not heal completely and the handsome god was left with a perceptible bald spot. While he brooded over his reflection in the water, a Gandharva princess named Neela Devi watched him with great interest from behind a tree. 
Moved by his misery, she decided to help him. Approaching him, she said, "My Lord, such an attractive face should be without a flaw." She cut off her own lustrous hair and pressed it on Vishnu's bald spot. 
Overwhelmed by her selfless act, Vishnu decreed that whoever came to worship him would sacrifice their hair to her. Even now, thousands of Vishnu's devotees flock to Tirupati and offer this highly unusual gift, to clear his debt of gratitude to Neela Devi. 

## Rangadasa

Rangadasa was a staunch devotee of Lord Vishnu. He worshipped the Lord daily, standing in front of a makeshift temple. The temple had an idol of Lord Balaji enclosed by rough stones, at the base of a tamarind tree. 
One day, distracted by a few Gandharvas, Rangadasa forgot his worship and sat in front of the temple, his puja tray and other things in disarray. Suddenly, the idol cracked open and an angry Lord Vishnu emerged from within. He admonished his devotee for failing in his worship. 
Rangadasa fell at his feet and begged for forgiveness. Vishnu relented and blessed Rangadasa for his devotion, saying that he would be reborn as a great ruler and construct a beautiful temple with a Vimana and high surrounding walls. The temple, dedicated to Vishnu, would bring eternal glory to Rangadasa as well as to his descendants. 
Thus, the humble devotee was reborn as Thondaman, King of Thondamandalam, who commissioned the grand temple for Shrinivasa. It stands even today, as Tirupati, on the seventh hill of Tirumala.
