# Arjuna in Indraloka

Having acquired celestial weapons from Lord Shiva and the Lokapalas, Arjuna waited on the mountain peaks for the great vehicle of Lord Indra. Soon, a spectacular golden chariot guided by Matali descended through the clouds, illuminating the heavens. 

"O fortunate son of Indra," Matali addressed him, "ascend the vehicle sent for you by your father. He and the celestials are waiting to behold you."

Arjuna purified himself by bathing in the Ganga and offered prayers to his ancestors. Blazing with a radiant aura, he ascended the celestial vehicle. The chariot coursed through the skies, becoming invisible to the mortals on the earth, passing thousands of extraordinary flying chariots and celestial beings. 

Finally, Arjuna arrived at Amaravati, the resplendent city of Indra. He was guided to the palace, where he paid his respects to the great Lord of the Devas. Indra affectionately took Arjuna by the hand and made him sit beside him on his own divine throne. Arjuna spent the next five years in heaven, living in absolute comfort and luxury. 

One day, Indra addressed his son. "O son of Kunti, learn the art of music and dancing from Chitrasena. This music of the celestials does not exist in the world of men, but you will benefit from it."

Under the expert guidance of Chitrasena, Arjuna mastered the celestial arts. The two became great friends, but the thought of his brothers and mother Kunti always troubled Arjuna. 

To honor Arjuna's visit, Indra convened a large assembly. The Gandharvas played their veenas, and the Apsaras, including the incomparably beautiful Urvashi, danced. As Arjuna watched the performance, his eyes lingered on Urvashi's beauty. 

Noticing this, Indra secretly commanded Chitrasena to approach Urvashi. "Through my grace, he has learnt about weapons and other arts," Indra said. "But now he should be conversant with the art of conducting himself in female company. Tell Urvashi to wait upon Arjuna."

Chitrasena delivered the message. Urvashi, pleased and eager, prepared herself and visited Arjuna's mansion that night. However, upon seeing her, Arjuna closed his eyes out of modesty and offered his worship as if to an elder. 

"O foremost of Apsaras," Arjuna said, bowing in reverence. "I command me and I shall wait upon you as a servant."

Urvashi explained that she had come impelled by Kamadeva and the command of his father. But Arjuna was overcome with embarrassment. Shutting his ears, he replied, "O blessed lady, it causes me great unhappiness to hear such words from you. You are worthy of being the wife of someone superior. I respect you like my mother Kunti or Shachi, the Queen of Indra."

"O son of Indra, we Apsaras are appointed to give pleasure to all," Urvashi argued. "Do not elevate me to the status of a superior. The sons and grandsons of Puru's race have accepted our services without incurring any sin."

Arjuna remained firm. "I bow my head and prostrate myself at your feet. For you deserve to be worshipped as my mother, and it is your duty to protect me as your son."

Urvashi trembled with rage at this rejection. Contracting her brows, she cursed him. "Since you have scorned a woman who came to your mansion at the command of your father and of her own free will, I curse you! You shall have to bide your time among women, unrespected and as a dancer. You shall be regarded as one destitute of manliness and disdained as a eunuch!"

Seething with anger, Urvashi returned to her abode. A distressed Arjuna sought out Chitrasena, who in turn informed Indra of the happenings. 

Indra smiled and consoled his son. "My mighty son, you have surpassed even great rishis by the patience and self-control you have shown. The curse that Urvashi has inflicted upon you will actually be to your benefit."

"How?" Arjuna asked. 

"You have to pass the thirteenth year of your exile incognito," Indra explained. "That is the time when the spell cast on you by Urvashi will work. At the end of that year, you will regain your manhood."

Relieved, Arjuna ceased to worry about the curse and spent the rest of his days in heaven happily. 

Sometime later, the great Rishi Lomasha arrived in Indraloka during his wanderings. To his great surprise, he saw Arjuna seated with Lord Indra on his throne. 

*What acts of merit has he performed to occupy the seat that is worshipped by the gods?* the Rishi wondered. 

Perceiving the Rishi's thoughts, Indra clarified, "O Brahmarshi, Arjuna is no mortal, though he has been born among men. He is born of Kunti with my grace and has come here to acquire weapons to cleanse the earth of all evil. Arjuna and Krishna are none other than Nara and Narayana, who have been born on earth to ensure the prevalence of Dharma."

Indra then gave Lomasha a mission. "Go to the earth and protect the sons of Pandu. Tell Yudhishthira that Arjuna has acquired the celestial weapons and will return when the time is right. But in the meantime, advise Yudhishthira to visit the various places of pilgrimage and distribute charity to Brahmanas."

And so, Sage Lomasha departed for the earth to bring hope and comfort to the exiled Pandavas.
