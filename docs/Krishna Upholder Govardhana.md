# Krishna: The Upholder of Govardhana

Shukadeva continued his narration of young Krishna's exploits to King Pareekshit. 

When winter set in, the maidens of Vraja observed their customary prayers to Goddess Durga, praying to have Krishna as their husband. One day, while they were bathing in the Yamuna, Krishna playfully took their clothes and climbed a Kadamba tree. "Come and collect your belongings," he called out. 

"Come, Krishna, we know you are Nanda's darling son... We too are ever willing to do your bidding," the maidens pleaded from the water. 

"In that case, you must come here, bow to me, and take your belongings," Krishna smiled, testing their devotion and shedding their lingering embarrassments. He then blessed them, promising their desires would be fulfilled. 

On another day, while grazing cows, the cowherd boys felt hungry and thirsty. Krishna sent them to a group of Brahmans performing a Yajna nearby. But the Brahmans, proud of their rituals, ignored the young boys. 

Krishna laughed softly. "Go and meet their wives this time. They are devoted to me and will certainly give you food." 

When the boys mentioned Krishna's name to the wives, the women eagerly carried vessels filled with delicious food to the forest. "Welcome, dear ladies. Your love for me is indeed pure and selfless. But now you must return to complete the Yajna being performed by your husbands," Krishna told them gently. 

The women returned, and their husbands felt deeply remorseful. "Fie on us! We ignored the request of Lord Krishna himself. Look at the devotion of our wives... How lucky we are to have such wives!" 

Sometime later, Krishna noticed Nanda and the cowherds preparing for a grand annual Yajna for Lord Indra. 

"Father, what is the merit of this Yajna? A living being is born and dies according to his Karma. Why then do we need Indra?" Krishna questioned. "A man should worship whatever he lives by. For forest dwellers like us, we should worship our cows, the forests, and Mount Govardhana." 

Nanda and the cowherds were convinced. They offered the feast and prayers to Mount Govardhana instead of Indra. Suddenly, Krishna assumed an enormous, radiant form on top of Mount Govardhana. "I am Govardhana!" he declared, consuming the offerings. 

Indra was furious at being denied his sacrifice. He summoned the destructive clouds, Samvartaka, and commanded them to unleash a deluge. "Attack them! Kill their cattle!" 

Lightning struck, hail rained down, and huge columns of water flooded Vraja. Frightened, the Gopas and Gopikas ran to Krishna. "Krishna! Save us from Indra's wrath!" 

"With the ease of a child plucking a mushroom, Krishna reached for Govardhana." Playfully, he lifted the entire mountain on the tip of his little finger! "Come, bring all your cattle and belongings. Take shelter under this mountain. Rest assured, Govardhana will not fall from my hands." 

For seven days and seven nights, Krishna held Mount Govardhana aloft without food or drink. Indra was amazed, and his pride vanished. He ordered the clouds to stop, and the floodwaters receded. Krishna carefully placed the mountain back in its place. 

The people of Vraja embraced Krishna with tears of gratitude. They marveled at his feats, recalling how as an infant he killed Pootana, overturned a heavy cart, choked the demon Trinavarta, uprooted the Arjuna trees, and conquered the serpent Kaliya. Nanda reminded them of Gargacharya's prophecy that the boy was equal to Vishnu. 

Later, when Nanda was dragged into the water by a demon while bathing at an inauspicious hour, Krishna dived into the river and went to the palace of Varuna, Lord of the Netherworld. Varuna bowed down in worship to Krishna and released Nanda. When Nanda described Varuna's reverence, Krishna revealed his cosmic Vishwaroopa form to the people of Vraja, filling them with bliss. 

On a full moon night in autumn, Krishna played an enchanting tune on his flute. Drawn by the divine melody, the Gopikas dropped all their household chores and ran into the forest to meet him. 

Krishna welcomed them with a disarming smile and tested their resolve, telling them to return to their husbands. But the Gopikas wept, "Accept our devotion, O Krishna... Allow us to serve you." 

Krishna agreed, and the grand Ras-Lila began. "Miraculously, there appeared one Krishna by the side of each Gopika." Each maiden was elated, thinking Krishna danced with her alone. 

Noting their pride, Krishna suddenly vanished from their midst. Filled with despair, the Gopikas searched the forest, asking the trees and plants if they had seen Krishna, and playfully enacting his past deeds. 

Realizing their mistake, they wept and prayed on the banks of the Yamuna. Krishna reappeared, reassuring them: "If I appear and disappear from amidst you, it is only to test your devotion." 

They resumed their joyful Ras-Lila, dancing under the moonlight and sporting in the cool waters of the Yamuna until dawn.
