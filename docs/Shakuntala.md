# Shakuntala: The Forgotten Wife

Long ago, Menaka, a beautiful heavenly nymph, left her newborn baby girl near the peaceful hermitage of Sage Kanva. Surrounded by fluttering Shakuntal birds, the infant's cries caught the attention of the meditating sage. 

"I shall adopt the baby girl and call her Shakuntala," Sage Kanva smiled, gently picking her up. 

Shakuntala grew up to be a maiden of incomparable beauty and gentle character. She lived a simple and joyful life in the forest, deeply befriending the animals and spending her days with her devoted friends, Priyamvada and Anasuya.

One day, the young and handsome King Dushyant of Hastinapur came to the forest on a hunting expedition. Chasing a fine stag, he was suddenly stopped by the hermits of the ashram. 

"Stop! The fawn belongs to Sage Kanva," they pleaded.

Respecting the sanctity of the ashram, Dushyant lowered his bow. "I shall hunt no more, but go for the blessing of the sage." Leaving his chariot and royal robes behind, he walked into the hermitage dressed in simple clothes.

As he wandered, he heard sweet voices and hid behind a tree. There, he saw Shakuntala desperately trying to swat away a buzzing bee. "Oh Priya, help me! There is a bee worrying me!" she cried.

Her friends teased her, "Call the king to help you, Shakuntala. It is his duty to help his subjects."

Watching her, Dushyant thought, *Lucky bee! To touch her lovely face.* Unable to resist, he stepped forward. "May I help you? There, it won't trouble you anymore."

Shakuntala blushed, instantly captivated. "Who are you, sir? What are you doing here?" 

"I have been sent by the King to see what is going on in his kingdom," Dushyant lied smoothly. 

"The sage has gone on a journey, but won't you be our guest?" the friends offered. As Shakuntala shyly tried to slip away, her friends teasingly demanded a reward for their chores. Dushyant offered his signet ring. Seeing the royal seal, the girls realized their guest was the King himself.

As days passed, Dushyant stayed at the ashram, promising to protect the hermits from forest demons. Meanwhile, Shakuntala fell deeply in love with him. Encouraged by her friends, she etched a love letter onto a lotus leaf with her nail: *"I do not know what you think of me, but I think you are the most wonderful man on earth."*

When Dushyant received the message, he confessed his own love. "I love you very much, Shakuntala. Let us exchange garlands and marry as forest people do." And so, they were secretly married in a simple Gandharva ceremony.

A few days later, an urgent message summoned the king back to Hastinapur. "I must go now, dearest," Dushyant promised, placing his royal signet ring on her finger. "Wear this ring always. I shall soon send my ministers to bring you to my palace."

Sometime later, the short-tempered Sage Durvasa visited the hermitage. Lost in thoughts of her husband, Shakuntala completely failed to notice the guest. Insulted, Durvasa cursed her fiercely: "You have forgotten your duty to a guest, so the person you're thinking of will also forget you!"

Horrified, her friends begged for mercy. Relenting slightly, the sage amended, "I cannot take back my curse... but I can change it a little. That person will remember Shakuntala when he sees an object he has given to her." The friends breathed a sigh of relief, knowing she wore the king's ring.

When Sage Kanva finally returned, a heavenly voice informed him of Shakuntala's secret marriage. Overjoyed, he declared, "We must send you to your husband!"

As Shakuntala prepared to leave, the entire forest mourned. "I will never forget you. I will come to meet you often," she wept, hugging her pet fawn. The trees shed their leaves like tears, and the peacocks stopped their dancing.

During the journey to Hastinapur, the group stopped to bathe in the holy river Ganga. As Shakuntala dipped her hands into the cool water, the king's heavy ring quietly slipped off her finger and sank, unnoticed.

When they finally arrived at the royal court, Dushyant, under the spell of Durvasa's curse, stared at her blankly. "I don't know anyone by that name. You must be mistaken."

Desperate, Shakuntala reached for the ring, only to find her finger bare. "Here is the ring you gave me—Oh! Where is it? I had never removed it!" 

"Your face is indeed lovely, but I have never seen you before," Dushyant replied coldly, dismissing her heartfelt memories as mere chatter.

Heartbroken and disowned, Shakuntala was left in tears. Suddenly, a blinding light filled the sky. Her mother, the heavenly nymph Menaka, descended from the heavens and carried her grieving daughter away into the clouds.

A few days later, a commotion broke out in the city bazaar. Guards arrested a poor fisherman trying to sell a ring bearing the royal seal. Dragged before the king, the terrified man pleaded, "Oh King, do not kill me! I found this ring in the belly of a fish I caught today."

The moment Dushyant laid eyes on the ring, the curse shattered. "Oh, now I remember everything!" he cried out in agony. "This is the ring I had given to Shakuntala, my wife!" 

Though he launched a frantic search, Shakuntala was nowhere to be found. For years, the grieving king lived a quiet, sorrowful life, painting portraits of his lost love to ease his pain. 

One day, Matali, the charioteer of God Indra, arrived with a divine request. "The Gods want your help in a war against the demons." Seeking purpose, Dushyant fought valiantly, helping the Gods secure a great victory. 

On his journey back to Earth in the flying chariot, they passed by the tranquil hermitage of Sage Maricha. There, Dushyant was astonished to see a young boy fearlessly prying open a lion cub's jaws. "Open your mouth wide and let me count your teeth!" the brave child ordered the beast.

When the boy's magical protective amulet slipped from his arm, Dushyant rushed to pick it up, ignoring the terrified warnings of the caretakers that it would turn into a poisonous snake for anyone but the child's parents. But the amulet remained perfectly safe in his hands.

Realizing the truth, Dushyant fell to his knees. "Oh, my son! My son!"

"Don't call me your son," the fierce little boy retorted. "The great King Dushyant is my father, not you."

Just then, Shakuntala emerged. Seeing his long-lost wife, Dushyant wept, "Dear Shakuntala! Forgive me for not remembering you at first. When I found your ring, I remembered everything."

With tears of joy, Shakuntala turned to her son. "He is indeed your father, little one."

Reunited at last, King Dushyant brought his family back to Hastinapur in triumph. The fearless boy, Bharat, would eventually grow up to become one of the greatest emperors in history, and the entire country would proudly bear his name—Bharat.
