# Rani Shantala: The Queen Who Loved Peace

In the year 1108 A.D., in the grand Hoysala capital of Dorasamudra, King Ballala I lay on his deathbed, burning with a severe fever. He called for his valiant younger brother, Prince Bittideva. 

"Bittiga, I know that my end is near," the dying king whispered weakly. "I have not been blessed with a son. You, my valiant brother, shall be my heir. I leave the kingdom in your hands." 
As Bittideva wept, the king made one final, passionate request. "Though I will be gone, Bittiga, you must fight the cruel Chalukya Emperor and make our dream of absolute independence come true!"

Soon after, King Ballala died. Prince Bittideva was officially proclaimed the new king, taking the legendary, historic title of **Vishnu Vardhana Hoysala**. 

Upon ascending the throne, Vishnu Vardhana had one single, burning ambition: to completely avenge his elder brother's humiliating defeats and permanently free the Hoysala kingdom from the oppressive rule of the mighty Chalukya Empire. "Every kingdom, from beyond the Tungabhadra to the Kaveri, will bow to me!" he declared fiercely. 

However, his beautiful and deeply beloved queen, **Shantala**, held a completely different view of the world. Brought up as a devout Jain, Queen Shantala profoundly believed in the supreme principle of *Ahimsa* (absolute non-violence). 

"War is inevitable, my beautiful queen," Vishnu Vardhana tried to explain to her. "What do you know of the glory of war?"
"Only that it wounds, maims, and kills," Shantala replied softly but firmly, her eyes filled with sorrow. "Only a deep respect for all life, be it in the smallest form, can ensure any true, lasting glory. Not war."

But the king was bound by his sacred oath to his dying brother. He immediately launched a series of massive, incredibly bloody military campaigns, successfully attacking the Chola kingdom and decisively defeating the vassals of the Chalukyas. In glorious triumph, he returned to Dorasamudra, heavily laden with war booty and celebrating his spectacular victories. 

Yet, amidst the grand royal celebrations, Queen Shantala remained completely aloof, shedding bitter, silent tears for the thousands of innocent lives brutally destroyed in the name of political ambition. 

To bridge the growing emotional gap between them and to permanently commemorate his spectacular military victories, Vishnu Vardhana generously gifted Shantala a massive piece of land in Belur. "To commemorate my victory, my Lord," Shantala smiled gratefully, "the victory of our union... I could not have been given a greater gift."

Shantala passionately plunged herself into the profound delights of art and architecture. She commissioned the brilliant, immensely talented sculptor Dasoja from Balipura to build the magnificent **Vijaya Narayana Temple** (now famous as the Chennakeshava Temple at Belur). 

Under Shantala's deeply inspired, devoted supervision, the brilliant sculptors carved impossibly intricate, breathtakingly beautiful friezes into the hard stone. Shantala herself, being a highly accomplished classical dancer, personally inspired the incredibly ornate, incredibly graceful figures of dancers (*Madanikas*) that adorned the temple pillars. The temple became a sublime, immortal masterpiece of Hoysala architecture, vibrating with the pure, peaceful rhythm of her art. 

While Shantala was thus beautifully engaged at home, building magnificent temples and peaceful pilgrim shelters, Vishnu Vardhana was far away, brutally warring with the Chalukya Emperor, Vikramaditya. 

For years, Vishnu Vardhana tasted spectacular success after success. But finally, a desperate Emperor Vikramaditya threw all his massive imperial might into completely crushing the 'insolent' Hoysala king. In a devastating, decisive battle at Halasuru, Vishnu Vardhana suffered a crushing, humiliating defeat and was forced to retreat in absolute haste. 

He returned to his capital, deeply dejected, utterly broken, and severely ill from the humiliating defeat. 

Queen Shantala sat by his bedside, tenderly and lovingly nursing him back to perfect health. "Shantala, my defeat fills me with deep shame," the heartbroken king confessed. 
"My Lord," Shantala replied gently, "when you are able to walk, we will go to our temple. There is a magnificent sculpture by Dasoja that I must show you."

When he had fully recovered, Shantala took him to the newly completed temple. Gazing at the breathtaking, perfectly serene beauty of the intricately carved stones, the warlike king drew a deep, shuddering breath. "My Queen," he admitted softly, "I lost that war when I was so absolutely sure of victory. I want another chance."

Shantala was heartbroken. "But I beg of you," the king pleaded desperately, "let me go on one absolutely last campaign, and fulfill Anna's (my brother's) last command."
Knowing she could not forcibly bind his soul, Shantala tearfully agreed. 

Vishnu Vardhana marched out to war once again, leaving his capable young son, Prince Ballala, to efficiently manage the administrative affairs of the kingdom. While the king fought brutally on the borders, slowly gaining ground against the Chalukyas, a sudden, unimaginable tragedy struck the capital. 

In the year 1131, the young, beloved Prince Ballala suddenly fell terminally ill and died. 

The devastated King immediately returned to Dorasamudra in absolute, frantic haste, plunging the entire palace and city into deep, inconsolable mourning. 

For Queen Shantala, the tragic, untimely death of her only son was the final, absolutely unbearable blow. The endless, bloody cycles of war, death, and destruction had utterly broken her gentle, peace-loving spirit. 

"Oh, Ballala, how much I had depended on you..." she wept bitterly over his lifeless body. 

Some time later, a pale and completely withdrawn Shantala approached her grieving husband. "My Lord, I plan to go away... to Shivaganga... on a holy pilgrimage," she announced quietly. 
Hoping the spiritual journey would soothe her shattered heart, the king agreed. "It will be a change for you... I sincerely hope you find peace."

But Shantala had no intention of ever returning. Months later, while Vishnu Vardhana was desperately fighting the Kadambas at Bankapura, a frantic messenger arrived with absolutely horrifying news. 

"Threw herself over a cliff? No! No! Oh, my Queen, why did you do it?" the King screamed in absolute, agonizing despair, collapsing in tears. 

In a sudden, blinding flash of painful realization, the King finally understood. Shantala had willfully sacrificed her own precious life. She had given up her life because the sheer, overwhelming weight of the violence and endless bloodshed her husband continuously unleashed upon the world had simply become too agonizing for her pure, non-violent soul to bear. 

Deeply haunted by her tragic, ultimate sacrifice, Vishnu Vardhana's heart was permanently changed. He lost his burning, lifelong appetite for war and brutal conquest. He began to spend much of his remaining time wandering silently among the incredibly beautiful, peaceful temples his beloved queen had built. 

Looking up at a breathtakingly beautiful stone sculpture of a dancer that perfectly resembled his lost Shantala, the grieving king found his only true solace. 
"Isn't it possible," he whispered softly to himself, "that this beautiful shrine will live on, spreading the true, peaceful glory of the Hoysalas, when most else—including my bloody wars—is completely lost and forgotten?"

And history proved him absolutely right. Today, the bloody wars of Vishnu Vardhana are largely forgotten by the world, but the immortal, breathtakingly beautiful temples built by the peace-loving Queen Shantala still stand proudly in Belur and Halebid, continuing to mesmerize the entire world with their eternal, unparalleled beauty.
