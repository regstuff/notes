# Raman, the Matchless Wit

In the glorious court of Emperor Krishnadevaraya of Vijayanagara, there lived a legendary Telugu poet and jester named Tenali Raman. Known as the "Birbal of the South," Raman used his nimble, unparalleled wit to outsmart arrogant scholars, greedy courtiers, and even the Emperor himself. 

Here are some of the most famous, hilarious tales of his matchless wit:

### The Undefeated Scholar

One day, a highly renowned, extremely arrogant scholar from Varanasi visited the royal court. He proudly challenged all the court scholars to a grand debate in any branch of knowledge of their choice, boldly claiming that no one in the land could defeat him. 

Panditraj, the learned court scholar, panicked. Knowing the man's immense reputation, Panditraj secretly told the Emperor, "Maharaj, we dare not accept the challenge. I don't stand a chance." 
To Panditraj's immense despair, Emperor Krishnadevaraya turned to his favorite jester. "Raman, I trust you know what you are taking on. Accept his challenge."

That evening, the grand debate was arranged. The arrogant scholar sat proudly on a high seat. Raman arrived with great pomp, followed by his disciples carrying a massive bundle respectfully wrapped in fine silk cloth. 

"I am ready," the arrogant scholar smirked. "Which great work have you chosen to debate on?"
Raman pointed confidently at his silk-wrapped bundle. "I expect you are intimately familiar with the *Tilakashtha Mahisha Bandhana*..."

The great scholar froze. His eyes widened in absolute panic. *Tilakashtha Mahisha Bandhana?* he thought wildly. *I have read every scripture in existence, but I have never even heard of that work! How much more intelligent must this local scholar be?*

Sweating profusely, the arrogant scholar asked for a night to prepare. But instead of returning the next morning, he secretly fled the city in utter disgrace! 

When the Emperor heard the news, he was highly amused but deeply curious. "Raman, what is this great, mysterious work you spoke about? I must confess, I too haven't heard of it!"
Smiling mischievously, Raman unwrapped his silk bundle. Inside was a bundle of dry stalks tied with a thick, coarse rope. 

"These are the *Tilakashthas* (stalks of the sesame plant)," Raman grinned, pointing to the sticks. "And this is the *Mahisha Bandhana* (the rope with which buffaloes are tethered). Simple, isn't it?"

The Emperor burst into roaring laughter, realizing Raman had brilliantly defeated the arrogant scholar using nothing but pure bluff and matchless wit! 

### The Golden Mangoes

When Emperor Krishnadevaraya's elderly mother passed away, her unfulfilled last wish was to eat a ripe mango. Unfortunately, it was not the mango season. Deeply saddened, the Emperor asked the court Brahmanas how he could bring peace to his mother's restless soul. 

Seeing a golden opportunity to exploit the grieving king, the greedy Brahmanas declared, "Maharaj, her soul will remain restless unless you invite a few virtuous Brahmanas and present each one of them with a solid golden mango!" 
The gullible Emperor immediately agreed, richly rewarding the greedy men with heavy, solid gold mangoes. 

Raman watched the entire spectacle with deep disgust. Determined to teach the greedy men a lesson, he invited the exact same group of Brahmanas to his own house the very next day, claiming his own mother had recently died with an unfulfilled wish. 

Eager for more rich gifts, the greedy Brahmanas arrived at Raman's house. To their utter horror, they found Raman fiercely heating long iron rods in a blazing fire. 

"My poor mother died of terrible rheumatism," Raman sighed sadly, holding up a glowing, red-hot iron rod. "Her absolute last wish was to have her painful legs branded with hot irons. Alas, she died before I could get the irons ready! Her soul will know no peace until I brand the legs of learned and deserving Brahmanas. Shall we begin?"

"You are mad!" the terrified Brahmanas screamed, scrambling toward the door. 
"But I must fulfill her last wish!" Raman protested mockingly as the men fled for their lives. 

When the furious Brahmanas complained to the Emperor, Krishnadevaraya summoned Raman. "What a pity we had to part with our mangoes!" Raman sighed innocently. The Emperor instantly realized he had been played for a fool by the greedy priests and burst into laughter, thanking Raman for opening his eyes. 

### The Execution

However, the orthodox courtiers were deeply insulted. They pressured the Emperor to punish Raman severely. Yielding to their demands, the angry Emperor ordered his royal guards to take Raman to the banks of the Tungabhadra river and completely sever his head with a single sweep of a sword. 

As the two executioners prepared to strike, Raman made a final, pious request. "I want you to cut off my neck the exact moment I call out to Mother Kali," he prayed. "And please, strike at the exact same time so I don't suffer."

The guards agreed. Raman knelt and loudly screamed, "JAI KALI!" 
Instantly, Raman ducked his head down. The two guards swung their heavy swords with all their might, entirely missing Raman and nearly chopping off each other's heads! 

"He tricked us!" the guards gasped. Bound by their strict orders to execute him with *one* sweep, they couldn't try again without the Emperor's explicit permission. They dragged Raman back to the court. 

Furious that Raman had outsmarted his executioners, the Emperor devised a more foolproof method. "Bury him up to his neck in the earth, and have a wild elephant trample his head!" 

The guards took Raman to the outskirts of the city, buried him tightly up to his neck in a deep pit, and went to fetch the mad elephant. While Raman was hopelessly stuck, an elderly, severely hunchbacked washerman walked by, carrying a heavy load of clothes. 

"Guru Maharaj! Why have you buried yourself like this?" the washerman asked in shock. 
"My friend," Raman lied smoothly, "I am actually under treatment. I used to be a terrible hunchback just like you! A learned physician suggested this miracle cure. You just bury yourself in this pit for an hour, and you will walk perfectly erect!"

Desperate to be cured of his painful deformity, the gullible washerman eagerly dug Raman out and enthusiastically took his place in the pit, allowing Raman to pack the mud tightly around his neck. 

Grabbing the washerman's bundle of clothes, Raman quickly hurried back toward the city. Minutes later, the royal guards arrived with the raging elephant. Just as the beast was about to crush the buried man's head, the terrified washerman began screaming for help in his distinct, squeaky voice. Recognizing the washerman, the panicked guards stopped the elephant just in time and dug him out, realizing Raman had brilliantly escaped yet again! 

When the thoroughly defeated guards reported the hilarious escape to the Emperor, Krishnadevaraya laughed so hard tears rolled down his cheeks. Fully pardoning his favorite jester, he warmly welcomed Raman back to the court. 

### The Arabian Horse

One day, an Arabian merchant arrived at the court with a herd of magnificent, swift-footed Arabian horses. The Emperor bought the entire lot. Deciding to test his courtiers' skills, he allotted one horse to each prominent noble. "Take good care of them," the Emperor ordered. "One month from today, a grand race will be held. The master of the winning horse will be handsomely rewarded." 

Wanting to challenge his witty jester, the Emperor deliberately gave a horse to Raman as well. 

While the other courtiers spent a fortune feeding their horses rich diets to make them plump and shiny, Raman had a completely different, highly unconventional plan. He built a tiny, perfectly enclosed stone stable with only one incredibly small window. Every day, he simply thrust a tiny, meager handful of hay through the window, keeping the poor beast absolutely ravenous. 

A month later, all the courtiers proudly presented their fat, well-fed horses at the racing ground. But Raman was nowhere to be seen. 
"Where is Raman's horse?" the angry Emperor asked. 

"I don't believe he even trained it, Maharaj," Panditraj sneered. "He's probably too ashamed to bring it."
"Go, Panditraj," the Emperor ordered impatiently. "Bring the horse here immediately."

Panditraj proudly marched to Raman's house and haughtily peered through the tiny window of the stone stable. Inside, the half-starved, desperate horse saw a face appear in the window. Mistaking Panditraj's long, bushy, flowing beard for a delicious bundle of fresh hay, the starving beast violently lunged forward and grabbed the screaming scholar tightly by his beard! 

Hearing Panditraj's muffled, agonizing screams, a huge crowd quickly gathered. Because the window was too small to pull the horse away, they literally had to hack down the thick stone walls with pickaxes to free the deeply traumatized scholar! 

When they finally dragged the starving horse and the weeping Panditraj before the Emperor, Krishnadevaraya was utterly baffled. "What on earth is this, Raman?" 

"Maharaj," Raman bowed with a perfectly straight face, "you ordered us to train our horses well. Look at this magnificent beast! He is so incredibly well-trained that he can flawlessly catch a grown man entirely by his beard!"

The Emperor exploded into uncontrollable laughter, officially declaring Raman the ultimate, undisputed master of matchless wit! 

### The Ultimate Command

Though he loved Raman dearly, Emperor Krishnadevaraya had a fiery temper. One day, during a heated argument, the deeply annoyed Emperor lost his patience. 
"Raman! Enough of your insolent pranks!" the Emperor bellowed furiously. "You have gone too far this time! Get out of my sight, and **don't ever show me your face again!**"

Raman silently bowed and left the court. 

The next morning, as the Emperor woke up and opened his eyes, he was greeted by a highly disturbing, bizarre sight. A man was standing quietly in his royal bedchamber, his entire head and face completely covered by a large, perfectly round clay mud-pot with two tiny eye-holes poked through it! 

"Who are you?!" the startled Emperor demanded. 
"It is me, Raman, Maharaj," the muffled voice echoed from inside the pot. 
"What is the meaning of this ridiculous mockery?" the Emperor asked, struggling not to smile. 

"Maharaj, I am merely obeying your strict, royal command," Raman replied innocently. "You explicitly ordered me never to show you my face again. That is why I have so carefully covered it!"

The Emperor completely broke down, laughing until his sides ached. "Ha! Ha! Raman, it's absolutely impossible to be angry with you," the Emperor chuckled, personally lifting the clay pot off his beloved jester's head. "The gods themselves cannot outwit you! May you live long to make my people laugh!"
