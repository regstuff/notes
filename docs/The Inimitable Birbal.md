# The Inimitable Birbal - 1. Pandit Gangaram

Birbal was known for solving problems. A Brahmin named Gangaram came to him one day. 

"I am a Brahmin, but I have no wealth or learning. I just want people to call me Pandit," Gangaram said. 

Birbal told him he could do it in two days if Gangaram followed his instructions. "Whenever anyone calls you Pandit, shout and yell at him angrily," Birbal said. 

The next day, Birbal went to Gangaram's street and told the boys playing there to call Gangaram 'Panditji' to tease him. 

When the boys called him Panditji, Gangaram chased them with a stick, pretending to be angry. The boys found this very funny and started doing it all the time. 

Soon, everyone in the street was calling him Panditji just to tease him. 

After a few days, the boys got bored of the game because Gangaram stopped chasing them. But the name stuck, and from then on, everyone called him Panditji.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 2. And Curry Of Course

One day, Emperor Akbar called for Birbal. 

"Birbal, you went to a wedding yesterday. What did you eat?" Akbar asked. 

"I had laddus, phirni, pulao..." Birbal listed. 
"What else?" Akbar asked. 
"Kheer and biryani," Birbal added. 
"What else, Birbal?" Akbar asked again. 
"There were so many things like..." Birbal started to say. 

Just then, a courtier interrupted with an important message. The conversation was left unfinished. 

The next day in the durbar, Akbar wanted to test Birbal's famous memory. He turned to Birbal suddenly. 

"What else, Birbal?" the Emperor asked. 

Birbal immediately realized Akbar was referring to the interrupted conversation from the previous day. 

"And curry, of course," Birbal replied promptly. 

Akbar was immensely pleased and gave Birbal a pearl necklace. The other courtiers were perplexed as to why Birbal got a reward for just saying the word "curry". 

They discussed it and decided to bring curry to the Emperor the next day, hoping for a reward. 

The next day, they all arrived with servants carrying huge urns of curry. 

"What's all this?" Akbar asked. 
"We brought curry for you, Jahanpanah, since you like it so much," they said. 

Akbar understood at once. "You fools! What Birbal said yesterday was in another context. As a punishment for your stupidity, I will make you eat all this curry just now," he ordered. 

Birbal had a good laugh at their expense.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 3. The Only Rooster

Emperor Akbar loved to play tricks on Birbal, but Birbal always outsmarted him. Akbar was determined to get even. 

One day, before Birbal arrived at court, Akbar gave each of the other courtiers an egg. He told them to hide it in their clothes. 

"Later, when I ask you to dive into the pool in the garden, you must pretend you found it there. Understood?" Akbar instructed them. 

When Birbal arrived, Akbar announced that he had had a dream. In his dream, he learned a way to test the loyalty of his men. 

"All of you must dive into the pond in the garden. The genuine men will find an egg in the water. If you come out without an egg, it means you are not reliable," Akbar declared. 

Birbal saw the courtiers whispering and suspected a trick. 

One by one, the courtiers dived into the pool and came out holding an egg. 

"Now it's your turn, Birbal," Akbar said, smiling. 

Birbal dived into the pool and searched the bottom, but there were no eggs. He knew the Emperor was playing a trick on him. 

He came out of the water empty-handed. He stood up, shook the water off himself, and loudly crowed, "COCK-A-DOODLE-DOO! COCK-A-DOODLE-DOO!"

Akbar was surprised. "Where's your egg, Birbal? What on earth are you crowing for?"

Birbal replied calmly, "Surely you know that only hens lay eggs, not cocks. They all laid eggs, but I am the only rooster!"

The Emperor and the courtiers burst out laughing. Once again, Birbal had gotten the better of them.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 4. Parting Of Friends

While Birbal was away on a mission, Emperor Akbar called his courtiers together to discuss a problem. 

"I am worried about Prince Salim. He is a good boy, but lately, he has fallen into bad company," Akbar explained. "He spends all his time with that boy Yasin. All they do is laze around, play cards, and go hunting. I want to separate them, but it is difficult to separate a sixteen-year-old from his best friend."

The courtiers tried to think of a plan, but a whole month passed, and no one had a good idea. Sending Yasin away would only make Salim angry with his father. 

When Birbal returned, Akbar told him the problem. "You want to separate the two young men? Give me just two days," Birbal promised. 

The next day at court, Birbal called Yasin over. He leaned in and whispered some absolute nonsense into Yasin's ear. 

Then, Birbal said aloud so everyone could hear, "Now, don't breathe a word of this to anyone."

Prince Salim was very curious. As soon as the court dispersed, Salim rushed to Yasin. 

"What was it? What secret did Birbal tell you?" Salim demanded. 

"Nothing. He just whispered some nonsense. He said, 'just one seed in every mango'," Yasin replied truthfully. 

But Salim didn't believe him. "You are hiding something from me, Yasin. I thought you were my friend."

"Of course I am! I am telling you the truth. Maybe Birbal is going crazy," Yasin argued. 

Salim was convinced Yasin was keeping a secret from him. "I don't believe you," Salim said coldly. 

"If you don't trust me, I too don't want you as a friend," Yasin retorted angrily. 

And just like that, the two boys parted ways, and Salim went back to his royal duties. Birbal had successfully separated them without anyone being sent away.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 5. The Phases Of The Moon

Emperor Akbar sent Birbal to Kabul on a secret royal mission. 

While in Kabul, Birbal tried to mingle with the local crowd, but the people were suspicious of him. "He doesn't look like an ordinary person. He must be a spy," they thought, and reported him to their king. 

The King of Kabul had Birbal brought before him. "Tell me truly. Who are you? Why are you here?" the king demanded. 

"I am just a traveller. I have travelled through many kingdoms and arrived here," Birbal replied. 

"Is that so?" the king said. "Well, since you have travelled so much and seen so much of the world, tell me, what do you think of my rule?"

Birbal paused to think. "You are like the full moon. No phase of the moon can compare with it for glory," he praised. 

The king looked very pleased. Then, as an afterthought, he asked, "And what of your own king? What do you think of Emperor Akbar?"

"Oh him!" Birbal chuckled. "He is like the crescent moon— thin and weak."

The King of Kabul was delighted to hear this and gave Birbal a bag of gold coins as a gift. 

Birbal returned to Delhi. But soon, news of his conversation with the King of Kabul reached Akbar's court. A courtier named Hussain Khan eagerly told the Emperor what Birbal had said. 

"He pretends to be your loyal aide," Hussain Khan sneered. "But he declared that the king of Kabul was like a full moon, while you were just a crescent moon."

Akbar was furious. He immediately summoned Birbal. "Birbal, I have a serious charge against you. Tell me on oath, did you not compare me to a crescent moon, and the king of Kabul to a full moon?"

"That I did, sire," Birbal admitted calmly. 
"How dare you, Birbal! You are supposed to be my most loyal aide," Akbar thundered. 

"I did make that comparison, sire," Birbal explained gently. "But don't you realise that the full moon is destined to decrease in glory and size? It is the crescent moon that is full of promise for the future. It will grow in glory day by day. Don't the Muslims and Hindus venerate the moon of the second lunar day?"

Akbar's anger vanished, replaced by a smile. "I should have known better than to suspect you. As usual, Birbal, you win."


# The Inimitable Birbal - 6. Value Of Waste

One day in the court, Emperor Akbar proudly showed a new vase to Birbal. 

"See this excellent vase!" Akbar said. 
Birbal looked at it closely. "No, it's a little chipped. Never show me anything broken."

"Why, Huzoor?" Birbal asked, pretending to be confused. 

"Surely, Birbal, you know that anything that is broken, crushed or rotten is of absolutely no use to anyone," Akbar declared. 

"Sometimes maybe, but that is not always true," Birbal disagreed. 
"Prove it to me, Birbal," Akbar challenged. 

"Think of sugarcane," Birbal began. "The juice we get from sugarcane by breaking and crushing gives sugar, jaggery and delicious sweets, fit to be a divine offering."

"Furthermore, the cotton pod bursts forth to yield the cotton string. Clothes made from its spinning and weaving are fit for even a king," Birbal continued. "And the rotten decaying rags, old jute and other wastes yield paper for the sacred Quran as well as our Puranas."

Akbar nodded, impressed by Birbal's wisdom. 

"Indeed that's true, Birbal. Everything has its use, even the broken, crushed and rotten stuff," Akbar agreed.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 7. The True Owner

One day in the Diwan-i-Am, a trader from Afghanistan came to Emperor Akbar for help. 

"Help me, Huzoor," the trader pleaded. "I hired a boat to bring my goods here. Now the boatman claims that he is the owner of my things."

Akbar asked Birbal to find the facts. Birbal sent for the boatman and his crew. 

"Why are you harassing this trader? Give him back his goods," Birbal said. 
"Huzoor, they are mine," the boatman lied. "He just took a ride on my boat. Ask all my oarsmen."

"Yes, the goods belong to our sahib," the oarsmen echoed in agreement. 

"We'll settle the matter here in the court tomorrow," Birbal declared. 

That afternoon, Birbal changed into a munim's clothes and went to the riverbank with a friend. They approached the boatman's boat. 

"What goods have you to sell?" Birbal's friend asked the boatman. 
"Oh! The finest Persian carpets. I have goods worth ten thousand rupees," the boatman replied. 

"Well, the market for carpets is rather low in Delhi these days. So, if you want to sell your stuff, I'll take it for five thousand," the friend offered. 
"Wait! Wait! I'll sell it for five thousand," the boatman agreed quickly. 

Birbal examined the carpets. "Hmm, not very good I'm afraid. Even five thousand is too much for these inferior quality carpets. We shouldn't pay more than three thousand."

"Well, if that's the maximum you are ready to pay, I'll have to accept it," the greedy boatman agreed. They promised to return the next day. 

Then, Birbal and his friend went to the inn where the Afghan trader was staying. 

"I heard you have brought goods worth 10,000 for sale," the friend said. "Will you sell for five thousand?"
"WHAAAT?!" the trader gasped. 

"Well, say seven thousand! Come, it's a good price," the friend offered. 
"No, never. No one here will give you more than that? Never mind," the trader said firmly. "I have bought the goods for ten thousand. I will sell them only at a profit, not otherwise."

The next day at court, Birbal knew exactly who the true owner was. He called the oarsmen aside privately. 

"I know your secret. Admit, or else..." Birbal threatened. 
"Yes sir, the goods belong to the trader," the terrified oarsmen confessed. "Our master gave us 25 rupees each to say they were his. Spare us!"

Now that his crime was out, the boatman quickly confessed. He was fined 500 rupees, and the goods were returned to the trader.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 8. Limits Of Loyalty

There was nothing Akbar loved more than challenging Birbal. 

One day in the court, Akbar said, "Birbal, I want you to find the most loyal and the most ungrateful creature on earth. You must present them in the durbar tomorrow, or else..."

"As you please, Huzoor," Birbal agreed. 

Birbal went home and told his daughter about the problem. 
"He wants me to bring him the most loyal and the most ungrateful creature by tomorrow morning. How can I locate them so fast?" Birbal worried. 

"Don't worry, father. Sleep peacefully. I'll give you both these in the morning," his daughter reassured him. 

The next morning, she told him, "Get ready for court, father. All you have to do is take with you, your son-in-law and our dog, Moti."

Birbal arrived in the durbar with his son-in-law and his pet dog. 

"What is this, Birbal? Why have you brought your dog? Don't you have any respect for the durbar?" Akbar asked angrily. 

"On your instructions, Huzoor. You had asked me to bring you the most loyal creature," Birbal replied smoothly. "If you give a stray dog even a morsel of food, he will be ever so grateful. He will probably follow you everywhere like a shadow."

"Hmm. Perhaps you are right. But the other one...?" Akbar asked. 

"You mean the ungrateful wretch. Here he is," Birbal pointed to his son-in-law. 
"But that is your son-in-law," Akbar said. 

"Yes, he represents all sons-in-law. No matter how much you give them, they are never satisfied," Birbal smiled. 

"That's true, Birbal," Akbar laughed. "But we must then reward the dog and punish the son-in-law. Let the dog be fed sumptuously. As for the son-in-law, let him be hanged."

"But... but... Huzoor," Birbal stammered. 
"No buts, Birbal. I believe in justice," Akbar insisted. 

"If you decide to award a punishment, all of them must be hanged," Birbal declared. 
"What do you mean?" Akbar asked. 

"I mean, you will have to hang all sons-in-law... including me and your royal highness. Everyone of us is somebody's son-in-law," Birbal pointed out. 

Akbar realized the truth in Birbal's words. "In that case, let him go!" he laughed.


# The Inimitable Birbal - 9. And Off It Flew

Emperor Akbar was fond of stories. He could not sleep unless he listened to a new tale every night. He would have his courtiers summoned one by one to tell him stories. 

One evening, it was Birbal's turn. Birbal spun a long yarn. Each time he paused for breath, Akbar would immediately ask, "And then?"

"All he has to say is 'And then?' It's my poor jaw that gets weary, talking," Birbal thought to himself. "I must cure him of this habit of his. How can we find endless new stories every day?"

Birbal waited patiently till his turn came again. 

"Come Birbal, I am feeling restless. Tell me a really long story tonight," Akbar requested. 

Birbal settled down comfortably and began. "One day, a rich farmer ordered a granary to be made. He wanted the granary to be absolutely airtight, without any window or ventilation."

"Hmmm," Akbar murmured. 

"When the wheat crop was harvested, it was stored in the new granary. But there was one little problem," Birbal continued. 

"What?" Akbar asked. 

"High up on one of the walls of the granary, a small opening remained. One day, a sparrow came, took some grain and flew off."

"Then?" Akbar asked eagerly. 

"Then another sparrow came, filled her beak with grain, and off it flew."
"Then what happened?"
"One more sparrow managed to get in and off it flew with two grains of wheat."

Birbal slowly described how fifty individual birds had gotten into the granary and flown off, one by one. 

"Oh come on! Enough of the sparrows picking grain!" Akbar groaned, losing his patience. 

"But, Jahanpanah, thousands of birds came to that granary. I have mentioned only a few. Only when the granary is empty will the story move on," Birbal explained smoothly. "Why, it might take months or even years to complete the story."

Akbar sighed. "Forget it! I don't want to hear any stories."


# The Inimitable Birbal - 10. A Shared Dream

One day in the Durbar, Emperor Akbar decided to tease Birbal. 

"Birbal, I had the strangest dream last night," Akbar announced loudly. 
"What was it, Jahanpanah?" Birbal asked. 

"We were both floating in the sky like clouds. Then suddenly we both fell down with a bang," Akbar narrated. "I fell into a big pit filled with honey. But you, Birbal, fell into a gutter."

The courtiers snickered. "Huh!" Birbal exclaimed in surprise. 

"Oh, how strange, Jahanpanah! How very odd," Birbal smiled. 
"Isn't it?" Akbar laughed. 

"No, I mean I too had exactly the same dream," Birbal explained calmly. 
"Really?" Akbar asked. 

"Yes, but I think you awoke before the dream was over," Birbal said. 
"Why, Birbal?"

"Because, in my dream, with great effort you got out of the honey pit. I too managed to clamber out of the gutter," Birbal smiled. "And, then, Jahanpanah! We both began to lick one another clean."

Akbar gasped in shock as the courtiers struggled to hide their amusement. Birbal had turned the joke back on him!
