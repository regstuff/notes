# The Learned Liars

Once upon a time, a wealthy King invited two renowned scholars to his palace. One of them was called Pandit Gyanraj, and the other was called Pandit Vidyaraj. 

They were learned men indeed, and they had something new and fascinating to tell the King and his courtiers every single day. 

One day, the King thought to himself, *Such great men should be given rich rewards.* He decided to hold a grand ceremony in their honor. 

The King invited several hundred people to his palace. 

"We are here today to honour and reward two of the most learned men in our land," the King announced proudly to the gathered crowd. 

But as he looked at the two scholars, the King suddenly realized that he did not know much about either man personally. *Pandit Gyanraj is... er...* he thought, struggling to find the right words of praise. *Pandit Vidyaraj will be able to help me out.*

He called Pandit Vidyaraj aside privately. "Pandit Vidyaraj, please tell me all about your friend."

"Well... I... er..." Vidyaraj stammered. *I must be careful,* he thought greedily. *If I praise Gyanraj, the King might give him a better reward than the one he gives me.*

"Your Majesty, I do not like to say it, but Pandit Gyanraj is an... an ass!" Vidyaraj lied smoothly. "He doesn't know anything. He just repeats what he hears from me."

*That's a lie!* the King thought, hiding his surprise. *I have heard Pandit Gyanraj speak and I know he is learned.*

"Anyway," the King said aloud, "let's see what Gyanraj has to say about his friend."

The King dismissed Vidyaraj and called for Pandit Gyanraj. "Pandit Gyanraj, please tell me all about your friend."

"He is... er..." Gyanraj hesitated. He had the exact same greedy thought as his companion. *I must be careful. If I praise Vidyaraj, the King might give him a better reward than the one he gives me.*

"Your Majesty, since you ask me, I must tell you that Pandit Vidyaraj is a... a donkey!" Gyanraj declared. "He doesn't know anything. He just repeats what he hears from me."

The King frowned. *These scholars have read many books, but what is the use of all their learning? They are no better than any of us here. How can I honour such greedy, deceitful men?*

"Listen carefully and do as I say," the King whispered a command to his royal servants. 

Sometime later, two servants walked into the grand hall carrying large silver plates covered with beautiful silk cloths. They placed one plate respectfully before each pandit. 

The two scholars rubbed their hands in glee, expecting gold and jewels. They eagerly pulled away the silk cloths...

Both pandits stared in sheer confusion. 

"What is this, Your Majesty?" Pandit Gyanraj asked, horrified. 

"Fresh, green grass!" the King replied cheerfully. 

"You yourselves have told me that one of you is an ass and the other a donkey," the King announced loudly for the entire court to hear. "So I have given you the reward asses and donkeys would like best—the finest grass that could be found in the kingdom!"

The two learned liars felt deeply ashamed of their greed and jealousy. They left the palace in disgrace, and no one ever heard of them again.
