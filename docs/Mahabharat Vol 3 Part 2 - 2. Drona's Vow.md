# Drona's Vow

The fall of Bheeshma plunged the Kaurava army into profound grief. The hapless warriors turned to Karna to save them from what seemed like certain doom. Karna, having received Bheeshma's blessing, finally entered the battlefield, filling the Kaurava ranks with renewed hope. 

Duryodhana approached Karna for advice on who should be the next Commander-in-Chief. "I regard you as the man who can save us," Duryodhana said. 

"I would advise you to appoint the venerable warrior Drona as our next Commander-in-Chief," Karna replied. "He is the seniormost among us and has been a teacher to most of us."

Duryodhana formally requested Drona to lead the forces. Drona accepted the honor, but laid down a chilling condition. "I shall fight unto death all the Pandava warriors, except Dhrishtadyumna, for he is destined to kill me."

The next morning, Duryodhana came to Drona with a specific, strategic request. "O King! By giving me the command of the forces, you have bestowed an honor on me," Drona said. "Tell me, what may I do to please you?"

"O revered one! If you wish to please me, seize Yudhishthira alive and bring him before me," Duryodhana asked. "Because if Yudhishthira is killed, the rest of the Pandavas will never spare our lives. Instead, if Yudhishthira is captured, he will be persuaded to play dice again, and the Pandavas will once again be banished into the forest."

Drona was thoughtful. *I should have suspected his wicked motive,* he thought. Aloud, he laid down a counter-condition. "So long as Yudhishthira is under the protection of Arjuna, I cannot capture him. You must separate Arjuna from Yudhishthira."

Duryodhana agreed, and Drona made a public announcement of his vow to capture Yudhishthira, ensuring he could not go back on his word. 

When Yudhishthira learned of Drona's resolve, he spoke to Arjuna. "Think of a strategy that will ensure that I will be under your immediate protection," he asked. 

"O King, it is as reprehensible for me to kill my teacher as it is to abandon you," Arjuna replied. "Hence I shall not let either happen. The sky may fall to pieces, but Drona will not take you captive as long as I am alive."

The war resumed with explosive fury. Drona attacked and scattered Dhrishtadyumna's forces. Abhimanyu fought brilliantly, engaging in a spectacular duel with King Paurava, breaking his bow and sending him flying from his chariot. Jayadratha also fought fiercely, clashing with Abhimanyu and enduring the young prince's relentless assault. 

The next day, Duryodhana put his plan into action. The Samshaptakas (the Trigarta warriors led by King Susharma) challenged Arjuna to battle at the southern end of the battlefield, deliberately drawing him away from Yudhishthira. Bound by his Kshatriya honor to answer a challenge, Arjuna had no choice but to follow them. 

With Arjuna drawn away, Drona unleashed a ferocious attack on the center of the Pandava army, aiming straight for Yudhishthira. Dhrishtadyumna, Bheema, and Shikhandi fought desperately to hold him back, but Drona was an unstoppable force. 

Suddenly, a massive threat appeared from another quarter. King Bhagadatta, riding his gargantuan and invincible elephant Suprateeka, charged into the Pandava lines. The beast trampled chariots, horses, and men underfoot. 

Bheema rushed to confront the monstrous elephant. As the beast charged menacingly, Bheema ducked under its belly, a known strategy to bring raging elephants under control. He stroked its belly, briefly quieting the beast, before slipping out. However, the elephant quickly resumed its rampage, catching Satyaki's chariot in its trunk and hurling it far away. 

Far to the south, Arjuna, having devastated the Samshaptakas, heard the terrifying roar of Bhagadatta's elephant. "O Krishna, Bhagadatta's elephant is playing havoc with our army," Arjuna said, alarmed. "Take the chariot to him. I shall kill him."

As Arjuna arrived, Bhagadatta hurled his deadliest weapon at him—the Vaishnava *Astra*, an all-destroying, unconquerable missile. 

Arjuna prepared to counter it, but Lord Krishna suddenly stood up in the chariot, shielding Arjuna with his own body. The terrifying weapon struck Krishna squarely on the chest... and instantly transformed into a resplendent garland of flowers adorning the Lord's bosom. 

Arjuna was pained and confused. "O Krishna! It is not proper that you break your vow of not taking part in the war. You should let me do the fighting while I can."

Krishna smiled gently. "Many years ago, Prithvi, the Goddess Earth, approached me when I was in the form of Vishnu. She asked for my most powerful weapon to protect her son, Narakasura. I gave her this Vaishnava weapon, making Narakasura unconquerable. Bhagadatta obtained it from him. No one can endure the terrible impact of this weapon but me. O Arjuna, I have protected you by depriving him of the weapon. Proceed now to kill him."

With the deadly threat neutralized, Arjuna did not hesitate. With one shot, he sent Bhagadatta's mighty elephant to its death. With his next arrow, he pierced Bhagadatta's chest, ending the life of the great warrior. 

The day ended with Drona's vow unfulfilled. Yudhishthira was safe, but the cost had been immense, and the Kauravas, desperate for victory, were preparing even more treacherous strategies for the days to come.
