# Matsya Avatar

Exhausted, Brahma stretched out and yawned. His day's work was over. The end of the current Kalpa was imminent. As his eyelids drooped with sleep, without his knowledge the Vedas slipped out of his mouth. 

The asura, Hayagriva, was alert. "This is my chance!" he thought. "I must now concentrate with all my mind and the Vedas falling out of the creator's mouth will enter mine."

But Vishnu, the preserver, caught Hayagriva in the act. "The asura is absorbing the Vedas," Vishnu realized. "They will be lost to the next Kalpa. I must retrieve them. There are but a few days left for Pralaya (the great dissolution)."
As he wondered what to do, he saw the royal sage, Satyavrata, a staunch devotee, offering water to the manes in the river. 
"I will take the form of a fish and retrieve the Vedas while doing a good turn to my devotee," Vishnu decided. "He shall live through Pralaya to become the Manu of the seventh Manvantara of the next Kalpa."

As Satyavrata scooped up the next handful of water, he noticed something. "What's this? A wee fish. Poor creature. How frightened it must be! I'll drop it back into the river."
He was about to do so when the tiny fish spoke. "O kind king, do not throw me back into the water. Helpless as I am, other creatures of the river will soon eat me up."
Satyavrata was deeply moved. "Don't fear little one. I will certainly protect you." He gently put the fish into his kamandalu (water pot) and took it to his hermitage. 

That night, however, the fish grew and grew till it filled the whole kamandalu. 
In the morning when Satyavrata went to see if it was all right, the fish said, "O king, this kamandalu is too small. Please find a more spacious vessel for me."
Removing it from the kamandalu, Satyavrata carefully placed it in a large vessel of water. 
But within barely an hour, the fish had outgrown it. "O king, even this vessel is too small. I have sought your protection. You must find the living space for me."

Satyavrata pushed the heavy vessel to a large pond near the hermitage and tipped the fish into it. But in no time, the fish grew till it filled the whole pond!
"O king, lead me to a lake - a deep, large one. I will die if I remain here."
Satyavrata took the fish to a number of lakes, each larger than the previous one. But the fish kept growing. "I need more water, O king. You are bound to provide it."

Exasperated, Satyavrata decided to lead it to the ocean. But when they got there, the fish pleaded, "O valiant king, do not leave me here and go. The gigantic creatures of the sea may eat me."
Satyavrata now became suspicious. "Who are you? I have neither seen nor heard of a wonderful creature like you. In one day you have grown big enough to cover the largest of lakes."
Then it dawned on him. "You are! Lord Narayana, forgive me!"

Satyavrata prostrated himself before the fish. "Hail to you, supreme one. I know you assume different forms and come to earth for the good of all but... why have you come in this lowly form?"
The Lord looked lovingly at his devotee. "Seven days from now, all the three worlds will be submerged by the ocean which will rise for the dissolution of creation. At that time a spacious boat sent by me will approach you. Meanwhile, you must collect all the herbs and seeds and animals you want for the next Kalpa. Then, along with the Sapta Rishis (seven sages), get into the boat and await me. Don't forget to bring Vasuki, king of the serpents, with you."

And the fish set off on its other mission. Deep in the ocean, when Hayagriva saw the gigantic fish approach, he was filled with terror. "But I must not open my mouth lest I lose the Vedas," he thought desperately. 
That was easier said than done. The divine fish soon put an end to him and recovered the sacred texts. 
"There! The Vedas are safe," Vishnu thought. "I will restore them to Brahma as he wakes up from his slumber immediately before the next Kalpa."

Seven days later, huge dark clouds gathered over the ocean. The rain poured down in torrents. The ocean began rising and huge tidal waves began swallowing the earth. 
As advised by the fish, Satyavrata and the Sapta Rishis waited on the shores. "Meditate on the Lord. He has promised us deliverance," Satyavrata told them. 

Suddenly, through the pouring rain, Satyavrata pointed. "Ah! There comes the boat as the Lord had promised. Be prepared to go on board."
As soon as the vessel touched the shores, Satyavrata, along with the Sapta Rishis and the select living creatures, boarded it. As their boat was tossed about by the turbulent ocean, they suddenly saw the magnificent fish approach them. It had a large horn now and was completely golden in colour. It lit up the darkening skies, reassuring the inmates of the boat. 

"Fasten the vessel to my horn, using Vasuki as a rope," the golden fish instructed. "Do not fear. Not one of you shall come to any harm."
While they sailed securely on that vast expanse of water, throughout that long Night of Brahma, Vishnu taught Satyavrata and the Sapta Rishis the highest form of truth which has come down to us as a collection of Puranic lore, called the Matsya Purana. 
Thus did Vishnu save his true devotees from dissolution so that they might carry divine knowledge to the next Kalpa.
