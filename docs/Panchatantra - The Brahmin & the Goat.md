# Panchatantra: The Brahmin and the Goat

### The Brahmin and the Goat

One day, a simple-minded Brahmin named Mitra Sharma was returning to his home from a neighboring village. He carried a plump, healthy goat slung across his shoulders, intending to offer it for a religious sacrifice. 

As he walked down the lonely dirt road, three hungry crooks spotted him. Their mouths watered at the sight of the plump goat. "What a fine dinner that would make!" the first crook exclaimed. "Let's trick him out of it."

The three crooks quickly hatched a plan and positioned themselves at intervals along the Brahmin's path. 

As Mitra Sharma approached the first crook, the rogue gasped in mock horror. "O Brahmin! How can you defile yourself by carrying a filthy dog on your shoulder?"
The Brahmin was furious. "You blind fool! Don't you know a goat from a dog?" he shouted.
"Now, now! Keep your temper," the crook replied smoothly, stepping aside. "You are welcome to carry the dog if you wish."

Muttering angrily, the Brahmin continued on his way. A little further down the road, the second crook stopped him, looking deeply sympathetic. 
"Why, holy sir! This dead calf may have been very dear to you... but must you carry it on your shoulders? Have you forgotten that you are a Brahmin?"
Mitra Sharma stopped in his tracks, his anger replaced by a flicker of doubt. "Are you blind? Can't you see that this is a live goat and not a dead calf?"
The second crook quickly apologized. "Please don't be angry, holy sir. I'm sorry. My mistake, perhaps," he said, walking away. 

The Brahmin resumed his journey, but now his mind was churning with confusion. "Am I mad, or are they?" he wondered, constantly looking back at the goat on his shoulders. 

Hardly had he walked a few more yards when the third crook approached him, shaking his head in disgust. "O Brahmin, drop that donkey before anyone sees you! People will talk and mock your foolishness."

This was the final blow to the Brahmin's confidence. "No! Three of them cannot be wrong!" he panicked. Convinced that the creature he was carrying was not a goat at all, but some terrifying, shape-shifting goblin trying to trick him, the Brahmin frantically threw the poor goat off his shoulders. 

Without stopping to look back, Mitra Sharma ran away as fast as his legs could carry him. As soon as he was out of sight, the three laughing crooks gathered the goat, collected some firewood, and enjoyed a magnificent feast, having successfully preyed on the Brahmin's lack of self-trust. 

**Moral: Trust yourself before you trust others.**

### The King Elephant and the Mice

During a particularly harsh summer, all the rivers and watering holes in a vast forest completely dried up. The King Elephant, leading a massive herd, was deeply worried that his subjects would soon die of thirst. Fortunately, a scout reported finding a large, hidden lake just a few miles away. 

Relieved, the King Elephant ordered the herd to march. When the thirsty elephants finally spotted the sparkling water, they broke into a desperate, joyous stampede. Blinded by their thirst, they charged towards the lake, completely unaware that they were trampling straight through a massive colony of mice. 

Hundreds of mice were crushed to death beneath the massive feet of the elephants. When the elephants finally left, the surviving mice held an emergency meeting. They realized that if the herd took the same route back, their entire colony would be wiped out. 

Gathering their courage, a delegation of mice approached the King Elephant. "O King, we have been living near this lake for generations," they pleaded. "While your herd came charging this way, it trampled to death nearly half our clan. We beg of you, please have mercy and find some other path to and from the lake. Small though we are, some day we may prove useful to you."

The King Elephant, being truly noble and kind-hearted, felt deep remorse for the accidental destruction. "Set your minds at rest. I shall tell my herd to take another path," he promised gently. The mice thanked him profusely and scampered away. 

A few days later, a band of cruel trappers arrived at the lake. They set strong, heavy rope snares along the elephant trails. When the herd returned for water, they were instantly caught. The trappers tied the struggling elephants to heavy trees and left, planning to return the next morning with tamed elephants to lead the captives away. 

As the King Elephant struggled helplessly against his thick rope bindings, he suddenly remembered the mice. He managed to trumpet a distress signal to a free elephant, instructing him to fetch the mice immediately. 

Upon hearing the news, the entire mouse colony rushed to the lake. Without wasting a second, thousands of tiny mice swarmed over the thick ropes, gnawing at them furiously with their sharp teeth. Working tirelessly through the night, the mice completely severed the heavy ropes just before dawn. When the trappers returned, they found nothing but chewed ropes and empty forest. 

The King Elephant humbly thanked the tiny creatures, deeply realizing the profound truth of their earlier promise. 

**Moral: Never underestimate anybody, however small he may be.**

### The Brahman, the Thief, and the Rakshasa

In a quiet village, there lived an ascetic Brahman named Drona, who had renounced all worldly luxuries. Seeing his lean and hungry state, a devout follower gifted him two healthy calves. Drona gratefully accepted the gift and began feeding the calves with the best butter, oil, and grain he could find. Soon, the calves grew exceedingly fat and beautiful. 

A local thief noticed the plump calves and greedily decided to steal them. One dark night, armed with a thick rope, he set out towards the Brahman's house. On his way, he suddenly encountered a terrifying, ghastly figure with sharp fangs and glowing eyes. 

"I am a Rakshasa (demon) named Satyavachana. Who are you?" the monster demanded. 
Trembling, the thief introduced himself and confessed his plan to steal the Brahman's calves. 
The Rakshasa laughed. "Excellent! I am very hungry. I am going to the same house. I will eat the Brahman while you take the calves. Let us go together as friends."

When they reached Drona's house, they peered through the window and saw the Brahman sleeping soundly. The Rakshasa immediately stepped forward to enter. 
"Wait!" the thief whispered urgently, grabbing the demon's arm. "Let me take the calves first. If he wakes up while you are eating him, he might make a noise, and the calves will run away!"
"No!" the Rakshasa argued fiercely. "I am starving. If the calves make a noise while you steal them, the Brahman will wake up and escape. I must eat him first!"

Their whispered argument quickly escalated into a furious, shouting match. The loud voices immediately woke the sleeping Brahman. He sat up, bewildered by the bizarre sight at his window. 

Realizing they had been discovered, the criminals turned on each other. 
"O Brahman, this Rakshasa wants to eat you!" the thief yelled. 
"O Brahman, this thief wants to steal your calves!" the Rakshasa roared back. 

Terrified but quick-witted, the Brahman immediately began chanting powerful, sacred mantras to his protective deity. The divine vibrations instantly overwhelmed the Rakshasa, and with a howl of pain, the demon vanished into thin air. 

With the monster gone, the Brahman boldly grabbed a heavy wooden staff and chased the cowardly thief far away from his home, saving both his own life and his precious calves. 

**Moral: When your enemies quarrel amongst themselves, you stand to gain.**

### The Lion, the Jackal, and the Cave

Once, a ferocious lion named Kharanakhara was roaming his territory. He had hunted all day but hadn't found a single morsel to eat. Exhausted and starving, he stumbled upon a large, dark cave at sunset. 

"Some animal must be living in this cave," the lion thought to himself. "I will go in and quietly await its return." He crept deep into the shadows and waited. 

A little while later, the true owner of the cave—a cunning jackal—returned home. As he approached the entrance, his sharp eyes noticed something alarming. There were clear, heavy lion footprints leading directly into his cave, but absolutely no footprints leading out!

The jackal realized a lion was undoubtedly hiding inside, waiting to ambush him. But he needed to be absolutely sure before running away. Thinking quickly, the jackal stood at a safe distance and shouted loudly, "Hello, cave!"

He waited a moment, then shouted again, sounding annoyed. "Why don't you answer me? You always greet me when I return home. If you don't answer me today, I'll just go sleep in another cave!"

Inside the dark cave, the starving lion heard this and was fooled. "Ah, this cave must usually speak to welcome the jackal," the lion reasoned. "But today, because I am inside, it is afraid and silent. I'd better answer for the cave, or my dinner will run away!"

Taking a deep breath, the lion let out a massive, echoing roar. "Hello, Jackal! It's all right for you to come in. Please do!"

The thunderous roar echoed off the cave walls, confirming the jackal's worst fears. Grinning at the lion's sheer stupidity, the jackal immediately turned tail and ran to safety, leaving the foolish lion to starve in the dark. 

**Moral: To be forewarned is to be forearmed.**

### The Mouse and the Sage

Long, long ago, the great Sage Yajnavalkya was offering his morning prayers waist-deep in the sacred river Ganga. Suddenly, a hawk flying overhead accidentally dropped its prey—a tiny, frightened mouse—right into the sage's open hands. 

Feeling deep compassion for the shivering creature, the sage decided not to leave it on the bank where the hawk might return. Instead, using his immense yogic powers, he transformed the tiny mouse into a beautiful human baby girl. 

He took the infant home to his wife, who had always desperately wanted a child. "Take her, dear wife, and rear her as our own daughter," the sage smiled. 

The girl grew up in the peaceful ashrama, loved and cherished. When she turned twelve, the sage decided it was time to find her a suitable, exceptionally worthy husband. Using his powers, he summoned the blazing Sun god. 

"Will you marry the Sun, who gives light and warmth to all the three worlds?" the sage asked his daughter. 
"No, father," she replied, shielding her eyes. "I do not like him because he is far too hot. Isn't there someone greater than him?"
The Sun smiled. "The Cloud is greater than I am, for he can blot me out whenever he pleases."

So, the sage summoned the mighty Cloud. 
"Will you marry him, little girl?"
"No, father. He is dark and cold. Please find me someone better."
The Cloud rumbled. "The Wind is greater than me, for he can push me about as he pleases."

The sage summoned the roaring Wind. 
"I'm sorry, father," the girl shook her head. "How can I marry him? He is always so restless."
The Wind howled. "The Mountain is greater than I am, for try as I might, I cannot push him around."

The sage took his daughter to the towering Mountain. "Will you accept my daughter for a wife?" the sage asked. 
"Gladly, O Sage," the Mountain rumbled happily. 
But the daughter burst into tears. "O father, please spare me! He is so incredibly coarse, heavy, and lifeless. Please, please find someone more lively."

The sage looked up at the Mountain in dismay. The Mountain chuckled wisely. "Of course I can. The best mate for your daughter would be the King of the Mice. He is the liveliest, friskiest creature I've seen in my lifetime. And what's more, he burrows massive holes right into me, and I cannot stop him!"

The sage immediately summoned the King of the Mice. The moment the girl laid eyes on the scampering, squeaking mouse, she quivered with overwhelming joy. "Father, he's the one I'll marry!" she cried happily. "Please turn me back into a mouse so that I can keep house for him as a good wife should."

Smiling at the strange ways of nature, the sage used his yogic powers to turn her back into a little mouse. He gladly gave her in marriage to the King of the Mice, realizing that despite all his efforts to elevate her, her true nature could never be completely erased. 

**Moral: Let a mouse be a mouse.**

### The Bird Who Shed Golden Droppings

Deep in a massive jungle, there lived a very peculiar bird named Sindhuka. This extraordinary bird possessed a magical quality: whenever it passed its droppings, the droppings instantly turned into pure, solid gold!

One day, a poor hunter was resting under Sindhuka's tree. Suddenly, a dropping fell on a rock near him. As he disgustedly bent down to brush it away, he gasped in sheer shock—it had turned into a solid nugget of gold! Realizing the bird's incredible value, the hunter quickly set a clever snare on the bird's favorite branch. 

That evening, when the unwary bird returned to roost, it was instantly caught in the net. The hunter happily shoved the magical bird into a cage, dreaming of a life free from poverty. 

However, on his way home, the hunter began to panic. "If I suddenly become incredibly rich, the King's spies will surely notice," he worried. "They will inform the King, and he might accuse me of theft and behead me to take the bird for himself! My life is worth far more than all the gold in the world."

Deciding it was better to be safe than sorry, the hunter took the caged bird directly to the royal palace and presented it to the King as a rare, magical gift. 

The King was absolutely delighted. He ordered his attendants to guard the bird with the utmost care, feeding it the finest grains and water. 

However, the King's arrogant ministers were deeply jealous of the attention the poor hunter was receiving. They approached the King with sneering smiles. "O King, it is not wise to trust the words of a mere, uneducated hunter," they advised smoothly. "How can a simple bird's droppings possibly turn into gold? It defies all logic! It is cruel to cage this poor creature based on a foolish lie. It should be flying freely in the forest."

Blinded by his trust in his "wise" ministers, the foolish King agreed without bothering to test the hunter's claim for himself. He ordered the cage to be opened immediately. 

The moment the cage door swung open, Sindhuka flew out joyfully. But instead of flying back to the forest, the bird perched itself proudly on the very top of the grand palace gates. Right before the eyes of the King, the ministers, and the hunter, the bird deliberately let fall a large dropping. It hit the stone courtyard with a heavy *clink* and instantly turned into a gleaming nugget of pure gold!

As the King and his ministers gasped in utter shock and embarrassment, the bird laughed from the rooftop. 
"We have all been a pack of fools!" Sindhuka chirped loudly. "First I, for not looking out for the hunter's snare. Then the cowardly hunter, for giving away such a treasure. Then your arrogant ministers, for giving foolish advice. And finally you, O King, for believing them without testing the truth! No, I will not be so foolish as to be caught again!"

With a triumphant flap of its wings, the magical bird flew away forever into the clouds, leaving the King and his ministers to bitterly regret their foolishness. 

**Moral: Ascertain a fact before you accept another's word for it.**
