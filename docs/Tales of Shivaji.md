# Tales of Shivaji - 1. Hira the Milkmaid

Raigadh, the grand capital fort of the great Maratha king Chhatrapati Shivaji, was situated securely on the very top of a steep, rocky hill.

At the foot of the hill, there was a small, quiet village... where Hira, a young, hardworking milkmaid, lived with her little child, Baalya, and her old mother-in-law.

Leaving her child safely with her mother-in-law, Hira would carry heavy pots and climb up the steep hill to Raigadh twice a day—early in the morning and again in the late evening—to sell fresh milk to the soldiers and people safely inside the mighty fort. 

By sunset every day, she would normally be back home, free to play with her beloved child.

But once, during the grand Kojagiri festival, Hira stayed late and stopped to happily watch the joyous celebrations after finishing her afternoon round of selling milk.

Carried away by the loud music and merry-making, she completely lost track of the hours.

Suddenly— *GONG! GONG! GONG!* 

The heavy fort bells rang loudly. 

"Oh no!" Hira gasped in panic. "It is six in the evening! The massive main gate of the fort will soon be securely closed for the night!"

Hira grabbed her empty milk pots and ran frantically towards the main gate... but it was sadly too late. The heavy wooden doors were already shut and bolted tight.

Hira ran up to the guards on duty. "Please open the gate," she begged them desperately. 

"Open the gate? What for?" the guard captain frowned. 

"I desperately want to go home. My little child will be anxiously waiting for me! I simply have to safely go back down to him!" Hira pleaded, tears in her eyes.

"Don't you know the strict rules?" the guard sighed. "Once the heavy gate is closed for the night, it absolutely must not be opened again till the morning. It is the Chhatrapati's direct orders."

"Please, just open it just a little bit," Hira begged, pressing her hands together. "Just enough for me to slip out. Please."

"It can't be done," the guard said firmly. "Orders are strict orders!"

Another guard felt sorry for her. "You can safely spend the night with some friendly family here in the fort," he offered kindly.

"Spend the whole night up here? Away from my crying child?" Hira said, shaking her head furiously. "No, no, absolutely never!"

She walked away from the gates, wiping her tears. *It's absolutely no use. They're definitely not going to open it,* she thought to herself. *But what shall I securely do? Baalya must be crying desperately for me.*

Hira looked over the incredibly high, thick stone wall that surrounded the fort. *I absolutely must get out of this fort... even if I have to forcefully climb over the steep wall to freely do it!*

She peered over the edge. *It's far too dangerously high here. Perhaps further down the wall it might be safely possible.*

She kept walking carefully alongside the massive wall, looking hopefully up at it from time to time in the darkening evening. 

When she finally reached the quiet eastern side of the fort, she suddenly stopped. The wall here was slightly lower, though the hill below was incredibly sheer and steep. 

She quickly hid her empty, clanking metal vessel safely in the dark bushes. Then, taking a deep breath, she clambered up the rough stone wall... and slowly slithered down the other side. 

"Out at last!" Hira sighed in relief, her hands scraped and bleeding.

But the hardest part was yet to come. She started carefully climbing down the dark, jagged side of the hill. It was incredibly steep, and she had to be terribly careful in the dim moonlight. 

Suddenly, her foot slipped on a loose rock! She completely lost her foothold and fell backwards into the dark abyss.

"Baalya!" she screamed, reaching out blindly. 

Fortunately for her, there was a strong, thorny bush growing out of the rock face lower down. She desperately grabbed it and hung on tightly to it... slowly steadying herself against the cliff. 

*I'm coming, Baalya,* she thought fiercely, catching her breath. 

At last, exhausted and bruised, she safely reached the flat base of the hill... and ran desperately through the dark fields towards her quiet village. 

The following morning, when the guards proudly opened the main gate... they completely could not believe their eyes. 

Hira was walking up the path, carrying her milk pots as usual. 

"You!" the guard captain gasped in shock. "But... you were safely locked inside the fort last night!"

Hira calmly set down her pot. "I wasn't," she said simply. "I bravely got out from the steep eastern side."

"Impossible!" the guard yelled. "This main gate is the absolute only way in or out of the fort, unless..."

The guards looked at each other nervously. "...Unless there is a secret, hidden passage we don't know about."

"And the Maharaj would definitely like to hear immediately about that!" the captain decided firmly. 

The guards quickly marched Hira off directly to King Shivaji's royal court and told him absolutely everything about the impossible escape.

Shivaji listened carefully. He looked at the simple milkmaid. "Can you clearly show me the exact spot from where you successfully went down the steep hill?" he asked. 

"I can, Maharaj," Hira nodded respectfully.

Hira led the large royal party across the fort to the eastern side. She reached into the bushes. "Here's the heavy metal vessel I successfully left behind before I climbed, Maharaj," she showed him.

Shivaji walked up to the edge of the wall and looked down the terrifying, sheer cliff face. 

"Can you safely climb down it right now using the exact same route?" Shivaji asked her. 

Hira clambered up the wall with practiced ease. But when she stood on the top and looked down the sheer, terrifying drop in the bright daylight... she was absolutely terrified. 

She hurriedly climbed back down onto the safe grass inside the fort, shaking. 

"No! No! I definitely can't!" she gasped in fear.

Shivaji frowned gently. "Why not? You clearly did it just last night in the absolute dark, didn't you?"

"Maharaj, the rocky hill is so incredibly steep!" Hira cried, looking over the edge. "I could easily slip and fall to my terrible death!"

"That obviously didn't stop you last night!" Shivaji pointed out. 

"Because... because... last night I was desperately thinking *only* of my young child, Maharaj," Hira explained softly, tears in her eyes. "He would be anxiously crying and waiting for me... I had no room for fear."

Shivaji smiled warmly, deeply moved. "I completely believe you, Hira. You are a truly brave woman! A remarkably brave mother! You definitely deserve a grand reward."

Shivaji then turned seriously to his chief minister. "Hira has unintentionally done us a very great service today," he said. "If a mother could bravely get out, armed enemies could definitely get in exactly the same way."

"Immediately raise the height of the stone wall here, and build a strong watch-tower right on this very spot," Shivaji commanded. 

Shivaji and Hira are no longer alive today. But 'Hira Burz'—the massive watch-tower built by Shivaji and respectfully named after Hira the brave milkmaid—can still be proudly seen in Raigadh fort to this very day.


# Tales of Shivaji - 2. The Rani of Belavadi

In the year 1677, a proud Maratha envoy came with a strict message to Isha Prabhu, the strong Desai (Feudal Chieftain) of the fortress of Belavadi in the Belgaum district.

The heavy, sealed message was from Dadaji Raghunath Nedkar, one of Chhatrapati Shivaji's most capable and ruthless commanders. 

Isha Prabhu angrily read the scroll. *"Swear complete allegiance to Shivaji in exchange for his protection?"* Isha Prabhu scoffed. "Absolutely never!"

Isha Prabhu turned to the waiting envoy. "I have incredibly great respect for Shivaji Maharaj," Isha Prabhu explained calmly. "I deeply admire his legendary courage and his fierce love of freedom."

Isha Prabhu stood up, hand on his sword. "But I also proudly share his exact same love of absolute freedom."

"Does it mean..." the envoy asked cautiously.

"Yes. It clearly means I fiercely want to remain free," Isha Prabhu stated. 

The envoy left for the Maratha camp to deliver the bad news.

Later, when Isha Prabhu anxiously discussed the day's heavy happenings with his brave wife, Mallamma (also known respectfully as Savitribai)...

"That incredibly ambitious Maratha commander, Dadaji, is definitively not going to respectfully like my defiant reply," Isha Prabhu sighed heavily. 

"But I am absolutely sure the great Shivaji Maharaj will proudly respect it," Mallamma reassured him. 

"I'm completely not so perfectly sure," Isha Prabhu frowned. "To him, I am just another one of the many small Desais he aggressively wants to forcefully bring under his growing sway."

Mallamma looked worried. "Would that definitely mean terrible war?"

"Yes," Isha Prabhu nodded sadly. "Brutal war and bloodshed."

Exactly as expected, Commander Dadaji quickly laid heavy siege to Belavadi. Thousands of strong Maratha soldiers completely surrounded the thick stone walls of the fort.

"When they hopelessly run completely out of food and supplies, they will definitely have to painfully surrender," Dadaji smiled to his officers.

But Isha Prabhu was thoroughly prepared for a long siege. His granaries were completely full. 

For weeks, absolutely every desperate attempt of the enemy to aggressively scale the steep fort walls was brilliantly foiled. 

"Ahh..." a Maratha soldier screamed as he was violently pushed off a long ladder.

Dadaji was growing increasingly worried and frustrated. "We have actively been completely stuck here for over a long month and the stubborn fort is perfectly not yet ours!" he yelled. "We'll definitely have to completely change our battle tactics!"

That very night, under the cover of thick darkness, a single, agile Maratha soldier stealthily scaled the high stone wall of the fort completely unseen... 

He ran quietly down the dark stone steps of the watch-tower and confidently made his secret way... 

...to the dry place exactly where all the heavy hay for the horses was safely stacked right next to the fort's main food granary. 

He quietly struck a flint and set the massive pile of hay completely on blazing fire. 

The first to frantically notice the massive, bright fire were the sleepy guards stationed on the high rampart. 

"FIRE! TERRIBLE FIRE!" a guard screamed. 

As the guards frantically ran down to desperately help... the Maratha spy ran swiftly towards the unguarded main heavy wooden gates of the fort and quickly threw them wide open.

Soon, the Desai arrived desperately on the chaotic scene. "What on earth are you soldiers doing down here?" Isha Prabhu yelled over the roaring flames.

"The entire main granary is completely ablaze, sir!" a guard cried, throwing a small bucket of water. 

"And the terrible fire is rapidly spreading to the houses! We're desperately trying to put it out," another yelled. 

Isha Prabhu drew his sword. "You heavily armed men quickly get back to your defensive posts! Quick!" he commanded. "The brave women and older children will bravely fight the fire!"

But it was already tragically too late. The heavy main gates were open, and the roaring Marathas had already flooded directly into the fort!

"We will fiercely drive them completely out—every single one of them!" Isha Prabhu yelled.

He leaped onto his horse. "Follow me! Every one of you brave men!"

Isha Prabhu and his loyal men fought so incredibly fiercely and violently in the smoky darkness... that the surprised Marathas were completely compelled to actively retreat back towards the gates.

In the brutal chaos, Isha Prabhu was severely and terribly wounded, bleeding heavily... yet he bravely kept desperately fighting...

...till the very last of the Marathas were finally violently driven completely out of the fort. 

Even exactly as the heavy gates were securely closed and locked again... Isha Prabhu finally collapsed and heavily fell from his tired horse. 

His devastated men rushed over and tragically found him completely dead.

When Rani Mallamma sadly came to the bloody spot... she didn't completely break down crying. Instead, she respectfully touched the feet of her brave, dead husband... 

...and fiercely picked up the heavy, bloody sword lying exactly beside him. 

"We will strongly continue the brave fight," Rani Mallamma declared firmly, raising the sword high. 

But the following difficult day... 

"Rani Saheba," a guard reported sadly. "The terrible fire last night has completely destroyed absolutely all the fort's food grains."

"And absolutely no fresh supplies can possibly reach us from outside until the massive siege is finally withdrawn," another guard added. 

"Commander Dadaji will definitely not politely withdraw the siege," the Rani sighed.

"What shall we desperately do then, Rani Saheba? Starve?" the guard asked. 

The Rani's eyes flashed fiercely. "We'll actively break the siege ourselves."

So the heavy main gates were unexpectedly thrown wide open. The brave Rani proudly rode directly out on her war horse, leading her desperate men. 

"ATTACK!" she roared, swinging her sword. 

Incredibly inspired by her fearless courage, her men fought absolutely fiercely. The Rani moved with terrifying lightning speed, cutting down soldiers left and right. 

Dadaji's confident men completely reeled under the surprisingly brutal attack. 

*We'll definitely tragically lose the battle completely unless the fierce Rani is successfully contained,* Commander Dadaji thought in pure panic. 

"Quickly surround the fierce Rani! Actively disarm her at once!" Dadaji screamed to his men. 

As the massive group of heavily armed men closed safely in on her, the Rani bravely fought even more vigorously, slashing at them. 

"Get her strong horse!" Dadaji ordered brutally. 

Cruel swords flashed, and the strong legs of the Rani's proud horse were viciously hacked completely off. 

But the Rani was incredibly quick. As the horse screamed and fell, she nimbly jumped off the dying animal and... seamlessly continued the fierce fight on foot.

But suddenly, she was violently struck hard from safely behind... and her heavy sword violently flew completely out of her hand. 

She stood proudly disarmed, panting heavily. No one dared to actively move towards her. All the battle stood completely still. 

Then the brave Rani spoke clearly. "I officially accept defeat. Please politely ensure that my loyal people come to absolutely no further harm."

Dadaji nodded respectfully. "They will be perfectly safe, Rani Saheba."

Later, when Chhatrapati Shivaji was formally informed of the bloody fall of Belavadi, he was not happy. 

"Dadaji," Shivaji said strictly. "The brave Rani of Belavadi is an incredibly great, proud woman. Immediately bring her safely here to me with the absolute respect properly due to a royal sovereign."

When the captive Rani was respectfully brought into his grand royal tent, the great Shivaji immediately stood respectfully up to warmly receive her. 

"Mother, I deeply salute you for your incredible bravery," Shivaji said, bowing low.

The Rani looked at him, confused. "I completely do not precisely know who is the true victor here," she said softly. "I only perfectly know you are obviously not the vanquished. But you shall incredibly ever remain free in spirit."

Shivaji looked at her sadly. "I sadly can't possibly give you back your brave, dead husband. I surprisingly cannot completely give you back your absolute freedom because your fierce spirit clearly never truly lost it. I don't really have absolutely anything to generously give." 

Shivaji bowed his head deeply. "But I definitely have something incredibly important to desperately ask of you."

"What is it?" the Rani asked. 

"Your complete forgiveness, Mother," Shivaji said sincerely, for the pain his army had caused her. 

The Rani's hard expression finally softened. "I angrily entered your enemy camp completely filled with pure hatred—hatred for him whom I had once deeply admired."

She smiled softly. "But your deeply respectful words have completely dispelled my heavy hatred. I no longer feel bitter."

Shivaji stepped forward. "Bless me, Mother. I deeply need your holy blessings."

The Rani raised her hand gently. "I sincerely wish you perfectly well, Maharaj. May Almighty God be strongly with you always!"


# Tales of Shivaji - 3. The Gift

Following a massive, victorious battle, Chhatrapati Shivaji held a grand, special royal durbar (court) to proudly mark the successful conquest of the great city of Kalyan.

One by one, the wealthy spoils of the long military campaign were formally presented to the victorious king by his generals. Heavy chests of gold, fine silks, and captured weapons were laid at his feet.

Finally, Abaji Mahadev, the trusted Maratha commander-in-charge of the Kalyan camp, confidently stepped forward. He had an incredibly unique, highly unusual gift to personally offer his king. 

"Maharaj!" Abaji announced loudly, gesturing behind him. "I excitedly have a very special, beautiful gift specifically for you!"

Two guards escorted a terrified, incredibly beautiful young woman into the middle of the crowded military court. She was covered modestly in a fine veil, trembling slightly. 

"She is the beautiful daughter-in-law of Mulla Ahmed, a cowardly high official of Kalyan who desperately ran away to save himself when we successfully took the fort," Abaji explained proudly to the court. 

Abaji turned to Shivaji with a wide, knowing grin. "Isn't she absolutely beautiful, Maharaj? Wouldn't you simply love to completely own her?"

The entire court fell completely silent, waiting for the king's reaction. 

Shivaji's face hardened. He silently stood up from his grand throne. 

He slowly walked completely past the smiling Abaji... walked gently up to the terrified young girl... 

...and softly placed his hand respectfully on her head. 

"Yes," Shivaji said softly, looking kindly into her frightened eyes. "I'd definitely love to happily have you..."

He turned and glared sharply at Abaji. "...As my very own respected sister!"

Abaji gasped in absolute, complete shock. "Maharaj!"

Shivaji looked back down at the young girl and smiled warmly. "If my own beloved *Ma Saheb* (Mother) were truly as incredibly beautiful as you are, then I too would have certainly been born a very handsome man."

Abaji hung his head in absolute, burning shame. *How incredibly foolish! How could I possibly have so terribly misunderstood my noble master so completely!* Abaji thought, his face completely red.

Shivaji turned back to the shamed commander. "Abaji, I actually must sincerely thank you for finding me such an incredibly beautiful sister today!" Shivaji said, his voice dripping with heavy sarcasm. 

Shivaji's tone turned strictly serious. "Immediately have her safely and respectfully escorted directly back to her own people. This rough military durbar is absolutely no proper place for her."

Abaji bowed incredibly low, completely humiliated. "Yes, Maharaj. Exactly as you strictly command."

And the beautiful young girl was safely sent directly back to her relieved husband's house, completely unharmed, and escorted with many rich, respectful royal gifts from the great Shivaji.
