# Bappa Rawal: A Great Rajput Hero

In the 8th century, while foreign enemies mounted invasion after invasion on the western parts of India, the Indian rulers continued to waste away their strength in fighting each other for selfish ends. 

In the kingdom of Idar, the ruler Nagaditya was killed by his own Bhil subjects. His widow, the queen mother, fled into hiding with her only son, the three-year-old Prince Shailadhish, and a few loyal followers. A few years later, finding the hard life unbearable, she complained to the royal priest, the Rajguru. 
"You will have to bear it for the sake of the prince," the Rajguru advised. "We plan to give him another name. Since the destiny of Mewar lies in his hands, his name will now be Bappa Rawal. We will take him to Parashar village in the Trikoot range, where nobody will recognize him."

The Rajguru took the young boy to a Brahman's house in Nagda. "Bappa is hardworking and honest," the Rajguru told the Brahman, who agreed to take the boy in. 
Every morning, Bappa faithfully took his master's cattle to the hills to graze. While the cattle grazed, Bappa befriended two local boys, Baliya and Dev. The boys taught him archery, sword-fighting, and wrestling. Bappa's natural royal blood showed; he quickly mastered all the skills, and the boys happily accepted him as their leader. 

As Bappa grew into a hefty, strong young man, the Brahman's wife grew suspicious of him, especially when their grey cow's milk yield decreased. She accused Bappa of stealing the milk. To prove his honesty, Bappa and his friends followed the grey cow. They discovered the cow standing over a natural Shiva lingam in the forest, its milk flowing out by itself!
The boys also discovered a fearsome-looking saint, Mahatma Harit, meditating nearby. From that day on, the boys made it a point to secretly clean the grove where the saint meditated. When Mahatma Harit finally called them, he was immensely pleased with their devotion. He took Bappa under his wing, instructing him in sacred lore and martial arts. 

During the autumn festival, Bappa and his friends went to the local 'Jhool Jhoolni' fair. The beautiful Solanki princess of Nagda was also there to visit the temple. When the princess and her maids wanted to play on the swings, they realized they had forgotten their ropes. They asked Bappa and his friends for help.
Bappa, feeling mischievous, agreed on one condition: "You will have to marry me!" 
Though angry at his insolence, the princess's friends persuaded her it was just a mock ceremony, like playing with dolls. Bappa and the princess playfully went through a mock wedding. 
"You will soon forget me, Rajkumari," Bappa teased. 
"Never!" she replied seriously. "I will remember this mock marriage all my life."

Months later, the King of Nagda arranged a marriage for the princess. But when the royal astrologers compared the horoscopes, they were shocked. "She is already married!" they declared. Remembering the mock wedding, the princess drew a perfect portrait of Bappa from memory. The king, enraged that his daughter had "married" a commoner, ordered his guards to find the boy and bring him in. 

Hearing that the king's men were hunting for him, Bappa realized it was no longer safe in Nagda. He fled to Mahatma Harit for guidance. The old saint blessed him and gifted him a magical sword. "Brilliant young man, a time will come when you will unite Rajasthan and lead her to victory!"
Around the same time, the Queen Mother (who was secretly the sister of Raja Mori of Chittor) finally revealed Bappa's true royal identity to him. Bappa swore an oath: "I promise that I will win back the territories of my forefathers and restore honor to them."

The fifteen-year-old Bappa traveled to the court of his uncle, Raja Mori of Chittor. Impressed by the boy's sharp intellect and polite conduct, Raja Mori appointed him to his council of ministers and eventually made him the commander of his forces. The older ministers were fiercely jealous of the young upstart. 

Meanwhile, the Solanki King of Nagda, having learned of Bappa's true royal lineage and his rising fame in Chittor, proudly sent a formal wedding proposal to Chittor. Bappa accepted, and he was finally officially united in marriage to the princess who had waited for him. 

Soon after Bappa's return to Chittor, a massive Arab army invaded the kingdom. The jealous old ministers cowardly refused to fight, taunting Bappa to take on the enemy alone. 
"Most certainly, I will!" Bappa roared. "Let the aged go home and rest. Bappa will take care of the enemy, unaided."

Bappa led his forces to the battlefield. The Solanki ruler of Nagda sent his own army to attack the invaders from the rear. Caught in a devastating double-pronged attack, the enemy was completely routed. Ashamed of their cowardice, the old Chittor ministers tried to join the battle, but it was already too late—Bappa had won a glorious victory. 

Not stopping at Chittor's borders, Bappa marched his army all the way to Ghazni. He defeated the foreign ruler Salim, liberating the ancestral territories of his forefathers. 

When Bappa returned to Chittor, he was accorded a hero's welcome. The aging Raja Mori willingly stepped down. "Chittor needs a brave and efficient ruler like you. Councillors, of my free will I appoint Bappa Rawal my successor!"

Bappa Rawal was crowned King of Chittor. He immediately invited the chieftains of all the neighboring Rajput states. "Friends, if only we were united, no one from across the borders would dare to attack us," he addressed them. Inspired by his leadership, the chieftains willingly accepted him as their supreme king, finally uniting Rajasthan into one powerful kingdom. 

Bappa returned to his mother, his promise fulfilled. "Mother, the states of Rajasthan have merged to form one united whole."
"And Bappa is their chosen king," she smiled proudly. "I am proud to be your mother."

Under Bappa Rawal's united banner, the Rajput armies became an unstoppable force. They invaded and conquered various foreign strongholds, including Ispahan, Kandahar, Iran, Turan, and Khorasan, bringing them under Indian rule and securing the western borders for generations. 
After ruling Chittor wisely and peacefully for many years, Bappa Rawal handed over the kingdom to his son and retired to the tranquility of the Himalayas, where he died at the ripe old age of a hundred, forever remembered as a great Rajput hero.
