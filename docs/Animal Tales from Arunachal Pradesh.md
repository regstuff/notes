# Animal Tales from Arunachal Pradesh

## 1. The Pig and the Dog

A man had a pig and a dog. One day, he suddenly realized that the two animals did no work at all. Angry, he kicked them awake. "Get up, you lazy creatures! Go and do something useful! Whichever of you does some work might find a place in my house."

The pig and the dog went to their master's field. There, the hardworking pig ploughed the field the whole day long, while the lazy dog slept under a tree. 
In the evening, the dog woke up and asked the tired pig, "Have you finished?"
"Yes... and I am exhausted," the pig sighed. 
As the pig wearily set off for home, the cunning dog jumped into the freshly ploughed field, ran up and down several times to leave his paw prints everywhere, and then followed the pig home. 

That night, the master asked, "What work did you do today?"
"I ploughed the field, master," the pig answered honestly. 
"And you?" he asked the dog. 
"Master... it was I who ploughed the field," the dog lied smoothly. 

"I will have to find out the truth for myself," the man thought. He got up early the next morning and went to inspect the field. Seeing only the dog's footprints all over the freshly turned soil, he was convinced. 
When he returned, he asked the pig, "Are you satisfied with my work, master?"
"What work?" the man yelled. "I saw only the dog's footprints in the soil. It was he who ploughed the field."
"But... But... I ploughed your field," the poor pig stammered.
"You liar!" the master shouted. "Go away, you lazy creature." He then turned to the dog. "Come, dog. You have done some hard work. You may come into the house with me."

And that is why, ever since that day, dogs have stayed in their masters' houses, and pigs have not been allowed inside.

## 2. The Owl and the Tiger

One day, the birds and animals of the earth held a great meeting to choose their leaders. They chose the strong tiger to be the king of the jungle, and the wise owl to be his minister. 
But the king and his minister had quite different natures. The tiger was rash and hot-tempered, while the owl was proud and stubborn. 

One day, they had an argument which quickly turned into a violent quarrel. The angry tiger lost his temper and slapped the owl so hard right in the face... that the owl's face became completely flat! 
Furious, the injured bird picked up a burning, charred log from a fire and struck the tiger with it, leaving long black scorch stripes all over the tiger's golden body. 

Since that day, owls have had flat faces, and tigers have had black stripes on their bodies.

## 3. Why Crabs Have Flat Bodies

There once lived a crab near a river. One day, as he was looking longingly at the juicy fruit on a tall tree, his friend the mouse came along. 
"Would you like to eat some of those fruit?" the mouse asked. "I'll climb up and throw some down for you."

The mouse climbed the tree and began throwing the fruit down to the crab. Unfortunately, one of the fruits hit a nest of fierce red ants. 
The ants got furious and rushed out of their nest, ready to bite the culprit. Looking around, they saw a wild boar nearby. "Who did that? It must be that boar! The scoundrel! Let's teach him a lesson," the ants declared, swarming and biting the poor boar. 

"Aaah! Oooooh!" the boar squealed in pain. He ran blindly through the forest and accidentally crashed into a tree where a bat was sleeping. 
The startled bat thought, "That boar is after me!" and flew away in panic. Looking for a place to hide, the bat flew straight into an elephant's ear! 

"Help! Help! A bat has got into my ear," the elephant trumpeted in terror. Thrashing about wildly, the massive elephant ran into a village and accidentally knocked down an old woman's hut. 
"Come back, you brute! How dare you knock my hut down," the angry old woman scolded. 

The elephant apologized, "I am sorry... but there's a bat in my ear. See! There it is!" As the bat flew out, it explained, "I was trying to get away from a boar. He knocked down the tree in which I was sleeping."
The woman tracked down the boar, who blamed the ants. "It's not my fault. Some ants attacked me and I was trying to get away from them."
When she questioned the ants, they blamed the mouse and the crab. "We had good reason to attack him. He broke our nest," the ants said, pointing to the crab. "A mouse was throwing that crab some fruit and one of them hit the nest."

When the crab was questioned, he stammered, "I... er... yes. One of the fruit did hit the nest."
The angry old woman crossed her arms. "So you are to be blamed for the destruction of my hut. You'll have to give me something to make up for it." 
"She is right," the elephant trumpeted menacingly. 

"All right. I'll get you something from my house," the terrified crab squeaked. He quickly crept under a heavy stone... and stayed there, refusing to come out. 
"Come out! Come out!" the woman demanded. Finally, the woman and the animals got very angry. "Let's press the rock down on him," the old woman ordered. The elephant pushed the heavy rock down on the hiding crab until they heard a muffled "Help!"
Thinking that was enough punishment, the old woman and the animals went away. 

Sometime later, the crab crawled out from under the rock, but the heavy weight had completely crushed him; he had become as flat as a pancake! Crabs ever since have had flat bodies.

## 4. The Otter and the Wildcat

An otter and a wildcat had become good friends. One day, the otter accompanied the cat to a human village. 
"I'll steal a chicken for you and one for me..." the wildcat whispered. "...But keep an eye out for men. Shout a warning if you see any of them coming towards me."

The cat crept into a hut and successfully caught a fowl. But the bird squawked so loudly that the inmates of the house awoke and began to chase the cat with sticks. 
The otter, who was watching from the bushes, was absolutely terrified. "After those people catch him, they'll come after me. I'd better get out of here," the cowardly otter thought, and ran away as fast as he could without warning his friend. 

Despite the chase, the agile cat got away from his pursuers and eventually caught up with the panting otter. 
"You coward!" the wildcat hissed angrily. 
"I... I am sorry. I am not used to this sort of thing," the otter apologized weakly. 
The cat ignored him, ate the entire fowl himself, and threw only the bare bones to the otter. "There, that's your share," the cat mocked. The otter felt deeply hurt but did not say anything. 

Some days later, as they were passing by a deep pond, the otter said, "Let's catch some fish."
"But I can't swim," the wildcat pointed out. 
"You go into the pond, catch the fish and throw them to me," the cat ordered arrogantly. 

The otter dived into the pond, gracefully caught several large, juicy fish, and ate them all himself! Then, remembering the cat's insult, he threw only the bare fish bones onto the bank to the cat. "There! That's your share," the otter laughed. 

"You rascal! I'll tear you apart," the cat roared. In his blind anger, the cat completely forgot that he did not know how to swim. He jumped furiously into the deep water to catch the otter... and was drowned.

## 5. The Porcupine and the Elephants

A herd of elephants used to bathe in a particular, pristine pool in the jungle. One day, they found the water incredibly dirty. 
"Why is our pool so muddy today?" the leader asked. "Somebody has been playing in the water."
They asked a passing dragonfly if he knew who had dirtied their pool. "If you find the culprit, let us know," they told him. 

Sometime later, the dragonfly saw a small porcupine. "Did you by any chance bathe in the pool the elephants use? They are annoyed because you made it so muddy," the dragonfly informed him. 
"I did," the porcupine replied casually. "I can't help it if there's so much mud in that pool. Anyway, that pool doesn't belong to them. I'll use it whenever I want to."

The dragonfly flew back and reported this to the elephants. "He is not at all repentant. He says he will use this pool again," the dragonfly said. 
"He doesn't know who he is dealing with," the massive leader of the herd grunted angrily. He plucked a thick, coarse hair from his own head and gave it to the dragonfly. "Show this to that stupid creature. Tell him he is picking a quarrel with one whose hair is so thick."

The dragonfly took the elephant's hair to the porcupine and delivered the warning. The porcupine looked at the hair and laughed. "Each hair of theirs is so thick? You call that thick? Here, take one of mine to them." He plucked out one of his sharp, thick, stiff quills and handed it to the insect. 

When the dragonfly presented the porcupine's quill to the elephants, they were stunned. "He has sent you a hair of his own," the dragonfly announced. 
The elephants examined the thick, sharp quill in absolute horror. "What! Is each hair of his so thick?" the leader gasped. "He must be a giant of a creature!"
"Let's not quarrel with him, brothers. We'll find another pool tomorrow," another elephant panicked. 
Terrified of the imaginary giant, the massive elephants scrambled out of the pool and ran away forever, leaving the pool to the clever little porcupine.

## 6. The Frog and the Monkey

Long ago, there was a strict Raja who had a monkey and a frog as his servants. One day, he ordered them: "Go to the forest and bring me some fruit. And you get me some fish from the stream."

The frog and the monkey set out. But when the lazy monkey reached the forest, he greedily ate every single fruit he picked. When his stomach was full, he curled up in a tree and went to sleep. 
He awoke only towards evening. Realizing he had nothing to show for his day, he was terrified. "The raja will beat me for not bringing him fruits. Now what should I do?" he panicked. Finally, he got an idea. He climbed down the tree, rolled in the thick mud several times, and set off for home, looking exhausted and filthy. 

On the way, he met the frog. The hardworking frog had caught a huge pile of fish. 
"This creature will get me into trouble," the monkey thought enviously. "Why did he have to catch so many fish? And I don't have so much as a berry to give the raja. This won't do at all!"
"Hey! What are you doing?" the frog cried out as the monkey suddenly grabbed his fish and threw them all back into the rushing river! 
"See what you've done! I've lost all the fish!" the frog wept.
"It doesn't matter," the monkey sneered. 

"Why are you so afraid of the raja?" the frog demanded. "He won't remain our master much longer you know. I'm going to rebel against him. And when I become the new raja, I'll make you my minister."
The monkey pretended to be impressed. "Now come, let's go home. Tell him you didn't catch any fish."

When they arrived, the Raja was furious to see them returning empty-handed. 
"What could I do, master?" the monkey lied flawlessly. "I scoured the whole jungle for fruit-bearing trees... But I could not find any. See how dirty and disheveled I am. Does that not show that I have been working hard? He, on the other hand, looks so clean and fresh. He must have spent the whole day playing in the water."

"You know that's not true!" the frog protested, but the Raja wouldn't listen. 
"You useless creature!" the Raja yelled, hitting the frog so hard on the head that the top of his head became completely flat! 
"I sent you to catch fish, not to play in the water!"
"I did catch fish. But he threw all of them into the stream!" the frog cried, pointing at the smirking monkey. "I can't stay here anymore. There's no justice here." 
The frog left the palace and went to live in the river forever. And that is why frogs, since then, have flat heads. 

The monkey was glad to get rid of the frog. A few days later, hoping to fulfill the frog's idea, the monkey secretly rebelled against the Raja. He gathered his monkey relatives in the forest to launch an attack. 
When the Raja heard the traitorous monkey was coming, he thought, "I must act fast if I am to save myself." The clever Raja made a thick black paste and covered his entire face with it, making himself look fierce and strange. He then sat on his throne and waited. 

When the monkey arrived alone to scout the palace, he was startled by the Raja's appearance. "Where did you get that paste, master? I would like to put some on my face too."
The Raja smiled inwardly. "My servants put me in the hollow of a large tree and lit a fire around it. The smoke made my face beautifully black."
"It looks beautiful now," the foolish monkey admired. "Please ask your servants to do the same thing for me, and my friends."
"Gladly," the Raja agreed. 

The Raja's servants led the monkey and his entire rebel army into the deep hollow of a large, dry tree... and set fire to it! 
The trapped monkeys perished in the flames. Only one of the monkeys escaped—a female whose face had been permanently scorched black by the thick smoke. Her descendants are the black-faced monkeys we see today.

## 7. The Frog and the Tiger

A frog and a tiger were unusual friends. The frog was a frequent visitor to the tiger's den, and whenever he came, the generous tiger always gave him fresh meat to eat. 
One day, the tiger said, "I have come to your house so often. But you have never come to mine. Please come to my house tomorrow."
"I would be delighted to come," the frog agreed hesitantly. "But... I am a meat-eater."
"As if there's no meat in my house! Please do come," the tiger insisted. 
"Oh, all right," the frog sighed. 

The frog hopped home feeling rather worried. "What have I gone and done! From where shall I get meat for a tiger?" he panicked. 
Suddenly, he saw a wild horse grazing near the river. "Aha! A horse! I'll bite off some of his flesh," the foolish frog decided. 

He leaped high onto the horse's back and bit down hard. Startled and annoyed, the horse neighed loudly, bucked, and delivered a swift, powerful kick that sent the tiny frog flying through the air. 
With a heavy *THUMP*, the frog landed hard on the ground. "Ouch! Both my legs must be broken," he groaned, dragging his battered body back home. 

The next day, when the tiger arrived for the feast, the frog greeted him from the floor. "Welcome, my friend, welcome! Please make yourself at home while I get you something to eat."
The frog painfully hopped up to his food loft. After waiting for some time, the hungry tiger grew impatient and peeked up into the loft. "Ooooooh! What is he doing?" the tiger gasped in horror. 

The frog was desperately trying to bite off pieces of his own injured leg! 
"What are you doing, my friend? Why are you biting off flesh from your leg?" the tiger asked, appalled. 
"I... uh... I had no meat to offer you..." the frog stammered in shame. 
"...So you wanted to give me your own flesh?" the tiger asked gently. "Do you think I would have eaten it? Why did you do such a thing?" 

The frog felt incredibly ashamed of his foolish pride. When the tiger left, the humiliated frog hopped to the river and jumped into the deep water. "I could never show my face to the tiger again," he wept. "I shall stay in the water for the rest of my life."
And so, out of shame, frogs to this day live in the water.
