# The Syamantaka Gem

Prince Satrajit of Dwaraka was an ardent and devoted worshipper of Surya, the Sun God. Every day, he would pray by the riverbanks, his eyes fixed on the brilliant sun. 

One day, as Satrajit was riding his chariot through a dense forest, he was suddenly blinded by a terrible glare. The trees parted, and the radiant Sun God materialized before him. 

"Who are you, my Lord?" Satrajit asked, shielding his eyes.

"He whom you worship," Surya replied with a warm smile. "Your devotion merits a gift. The Syamantaka gem is yours. Take it." 

Surya handed him a dazzling, golden jewel and disappeared. The Syamantaka gem shone so brightly that it literally lit Satrajit's path through the dark forest. 

When Satrajit rode back into Dwaraka wearing the radiant jewel, the citizens were bewildered. "What has happened to our prince?" they whispered. "He shines with a strange light."

Wonderstruck, the people went to Lord Krishna. "Lord Krishna, behold! The sun has come to earth," they cried.

Krishna, the all-knowing one, smiled gently. "Surya has given Satrajit the Syamantaka jewel."

This was no ordinary gem. The Syamantaka jewel had the miraculous power to prevent famine, disease, and war. More wonderfully, it yielded eight measures of solid gold every single day. 

Satrajit installed the gem in a specially built temple, and it brought immense peace and prosperity to his household. However, the immense wealth also brought arrogance.

One day, Lord Krishna visited Satrajit. "Welcome, Shri Krishna," Satrajit greeted him. 

"Dwaraka is humming with tales of your good fortune," Krishna observed. "Now that you have enough gold, why don't you give the gem to King Ugrasena? Because he is the king, the gem will be safest with him, and its benefits can be shared by the entire kingdom."

Satrajit immediately grew defensive and suspicious. "It will be equally safe with me," he refused flatly. "I will not part with it."

A few days later, Satrajit's younger brother, Prasena, decided to go out on a hunt. Eager to show off, he wore the glowing Syamantaka jewel around his neck. "Take care, Prasena," Satrajit warned.

"I may lose my life, but I'll guard the jewel," Prasena promised boastfully. 

Deep in the forest, as Prasena dismounted and waited for game, a ferocious lion suddenly leaped from the bushes. "Aaagh!" Prasena screamed as the lion struck him down, killing him instantly. 

The beast noticed the brightly glowing gem around the dead man's neck. Snapping the chain, the lion picked up the jewel in its jaws and padded away. 

However, the lion was being watched. Jambavan, the mighty King of the Bears, saw the beast walking away with the blinding gem. "What a fine jewel!" Jambavan thought. He attacked the lion, killed it with his bare hands, and took the gem back to his dark cave, giving it to his young child as a toy.

Meanwhile, in Dwaraka, Satrajit was growing frantic. "Prasena has not returned from the hunt," he worried. Fueled by his previous paranoia, he jumped to a terrible conclusion. "I am sure Krishna must have killed my brother for the sake of the gem!"

Satrajit maliciously spread the rumor throughout the city. The whispers reached Krishna. 

"Satrajit suspects me," Krishna sighed. "I must find the jewel and establish my innocence."

Krishna, accompanied by his men, set out into the forest. A few hours later, they found Prasena's mangled body. 

"He's dead!" a soldier cried. "Red marks round his neck... A broken branch..."

"Look! Lion tracks!" another pointed out. 

"Follow me," Krishna ordered. "It looks as if he was killed by a lion."

They followed the tracks and soon stumbled upon the carcass of the lion. "Dead! I wonder who killed it," Krishna muttered. Looking closely at the ground, he spotted large, strange footprints leading away. "Ah! There lies our culprit. Bear tracks!"

The tracks led them to a massive, dark cave. Krishna ordered his men to wait outside. "I will go into the cave. If I do not return in twelve days, assume I am dead and go back."

Deep inside the cave, Krishna found a bear-child playing with the glowing Syamantaka gem. As Krishna reached out and took the gem, the child squealed in fright. A female bear rushed in and screamed, alerting the King of the Bears.

Jambavan roared into the cavern. "Who are you, stranger? He frightened my child. Then he must be punished! Prepare to fight, intruder!"

A legendary battle began in the darkness of the cave. For twenty-eight days, Krishna and Jambavan fought relentlessly. They fought with weapons, then with stones and mountain rocks, and when those were exhausted, they fought with uprooted trees and bare fists. 

Finally, battered and exhausted by Krishna's inexhaustible strength, Jambavan fell to his knees. "You are invincible. I surrender," he panted. Looking closely at his conqueror, realization dawned upon the Bear King. He recognized the divine nature of his opponent. 

"I will, but soon after the wedding we must return to Dwaraka," Krishna smiled, accepting Jambavan's surrender. To honor the victor, Jambavan offered not only the Syamantaka gem but also his beautiful daughter, Jambavati, in marriage. 

Meanwhile, outside the cave, twenty-eight days had passed. Fearing the worst, Krishna's men had returned to Dwaraka, where his family was mourning his assumed death. 

But suddenly, the palace doors opened, and Krishna walked in, radiant, with his new bride and the glowing Syamantaka jewel! 

Krishna immediately summoned Satrajit to the court. "I have found the Syamantaka gem," Krishna announced, recounting the true story of the lion, the bear, and the epic battle. 

Satrajit hung his head in profound shame. "I did you grievous wrong, Lord," he apologized profusely. "It was out of my ignorance."

Eager to make amends for his malicious rumors, Satrajit pleaded, "Keep the jewel, Krishna... and also accept my beautiful daughter, Satyabhama, in marriage."

Krishna gladly accepted Satyabhama as his wife, but he returned the jewel. "I will marry your daughter with pleasure. But the gem is rightfully yours. Keep it. You may however give me the gold it yields for the welfare of the people."

However, the trouble surrounding the gem was far from over. Satyabhama had been previously promised to several other powerful men—Akrura, Kritavarma, and Shatadhanwa. Outraged by her sudden marriage to Krishna, the disappointed suitors nursed thoughts of bitter revenge.

"Satrajit had promised his daughter to one of us," Akrura whispered to Shatadhanwa. "Why don't you kill him and make the Syamantaka jewel yours?"

Eager to possess the magical gem, the evil Shatadhanwa sneaked into Satrajit's palace in the dead of night and brutally murdered the prince while he slept. Snatching the gem, he fled.

Satyabhama was heartbroken. She rushed to Hastinapura, where Krishna was visiting, and collapsed in tears. "Lord, my father has been slain!"

Furious, Krishna and his mighty brother Balarama mounted their chariot and rode out in hot pursuit of the murderer. 

Hearing that the furious brothers were after him, the guilty Shatadhanwa panicked. He appealed to Kritavarma for shelter. "Go elsewhere," Kritavarma refused, terrified of Krishna's wrath. 

Shatadhanwa then ran to Akrura. "Akrura, please keep the gem with you then. I'm fleeing from Dwaraka!" Handing the glowing jewel to Akrura, Shatadhanwa mounted his fastest horse and rode away.

Near Mithila, Shatadhanwa's exhausted horse tripped and fell, throwing him to the ground. Krishna and Balarama caught up with him. 

"Halt! You murderer," Krishna roared. In the brief fight that followed, Shatadhanwa fell dead, struck by Krishna's divine weapons. 

But when Krishna searched the body, he frowned. "He does not have the jewel. He must have given it to Akrura."

When the cowardly Akrura heard of Shatadhanwa's death, he immediately fled Dwaraka, taking the gem with him. 

A few days later, Krishna addressed the court. "Unless the gem is found, I will remain suspect in the eyes of the people. Only Akrura can help me. I will send for him."

Assuring him of his safety, Krishna summoned Akrura back to Dwaraka. When the nervous man arrived, Krishna spoke to him gently but firmly. 

"I know Shatadhanwa gave the Syamantaka gem to you for safe-keeping," Krishna said. "Akrura, you may keep the gem. But please admit that you are in possession of it. This will clear all doubts in the mind of the people."

Realizing Krishna's magnanimity, Akrura produced the dazzling jewel and publicly admitted he had it. 

"Perhaps he has his reasons to act the way he did!" Akrura thought, immensely relieved. "Anyway I will not speculate idly. The jewel is mine and with it, prosperity."

Having finally set the minds of the citizens at rest and permanently cleared his name of all suspicion, Lord Krishna was able to relax in peace, the chaotic saga of the Syamantaka gem finally drawn to a close.
