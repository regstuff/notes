# The Golden Crab

To the east of Rajagriha, there was a peaceful village named Salindiya. There lived in that village a brahmin who made his living by farming. He was a happy, contented man and spent his days carefully looking after his fields. 

One day, while working in the hot sun, he said to himself, "It's such a terribly hot day. I will go and refresh myself at the pond." 
As he splashed the cool water over himself, his eye fell on a surprising sight—a beautiful, tiny golden baby crab. 
He carefully lifted the crab and placed it gently in his shawl. 

When he returned to his workers, he showed them his discovery. "Look what I found in the pond. A completely golden crab!"
"Oh! How lovely!" they marvelled.
"He will make a good companion for me," the brahmin smiled.
"How can a crab be your friend?" a worker chuckled. "Are you going to keep him in your house?"
"No! He will be much happier in his own home in the pond," the brahmin decided.

In the evening, as he headed home, he gently lowered his new friend back into the cool pond. "There you go!" 
Everyday on his way to work, the brahmin would stop at the pond. "Ah! You are becoming clever," he smiled, finding the crab waiting for him. "You wait for me right on the edge of the water."

During the day, he carried the golden crab with him safely tucked in his shawl as he worked in the fields. 
"Our master has really become deeply attached to that strange crab," his workers gossiped.
"Yes! He cannot do without him."

Atop a tall palm tree at the very edge of the brahmin's fields, there dwelt a pair of wicked crows. 
"Have you closely seen the eyes of that man?" the she-crow asked her husband greedily. "I feel an irresistible desire to eat his eyes."
"Forget it!" the male crow scoffed. "It is not possible to do so. He is a strong man."
"But you must get them for me," she insisted stubbornly. "I insist."
"How can I possibly attack him? He'll easily drive me away or kill me," the husband argued.

The she-crow hatched a devious plan. "A deadly snake lives in the bamboo grove just ahead. Make friends with him. Persuade him to bite that man when he isn't looking. Then, when he dies from the snake bite, you can safely get his eyes out for me."
"All right, I will try," the crow agreed.

The crow began to actively befriend the venomous snake, bringing him gifts. "My wife sent this choice, fresh meat just for you," he would say.
"Oh, how delicious!" the snake hissed happily. 

As the seasons passed, the crops grew. "The crop is ready for harvesting, master," the workers reported.
"Yes. How time has flown!" the brahmin smiled.
"Your pet crab has also grown so incredibly big meanwhile," a worker pointed out. Indeed, the golden crab was now large and strong.

The false friendship between the crow and the snake had also grown over the months. 
"You do so much for me," the snake offered one day. "I would like to do you a great favour in return. Will you name it?"
"Surely," the crow smiled wickedly. "My wife yearns for the eyes of the master of these fields. If you bite him, I could pluck out his eyes without danger."
"That is easy," the snake agreed. "I will do it tomorrow morning. He comes here early before any of his men."

As usual, early the next morning, the brahmin picked up his pet crab from the pond, tucked it in his shawl, and came to the field alone. 
"I will hide here in the bushes," the snake whispered to the crow. "The brahmin always comes exactly this way after refreshing himself in the pond."

As the brahmin walked past the bushes, the snake struck. "AHH!" the brahmin screamed, collapsing to the ground as the venom rushed through his veins.
"Now I will get his eyes at last!" the crow cawed happily, flying down to the paralyzed man.

Meanwhile, the golden crab felt his master fall and crept out from the folds of the shawl. "My friend is in terrible danger," the crab realized.
As the crow landed on the brahmin's chest, preparing to peck at his eyes, the crab lunged forward with lightning speed and snapped its powerful pincers around the crow's neck.
"Ahh! My friend, the snake!" the crow choked in terror. "Where are you? Save me from this monstrous crab!"

The snake turned back on hearing his friend's desperate plea. He slithered back to attack the crab.
But the golden crab was much quicker and extended its other massive pincer, brutally grabbing the snake by the throat too. 

"You don't eat either crow meat or snake meat!" the suffocating snake gasped. "Then why did you catch us both?"
"To prevent you from harming this man, who is my greatest friend and protector," the crab replied coldly. "Everyday he brings me from the pond and looks after me with love. Should he die of snake-bite, my life too will eventually be in danger."
"So my enemies will try to kill me for my tasty and tender flesh," the crab continued, tightening its grip.

The snake, feeling its life slip away, tried to fool the crab. "If you are truly concerned about this man, free me immediately. I'll suck the venom out from his body."
"Come on! Hurry up and free us," the crow choked. "Don't you know how fast the venom spreads? He will die!"

*He is trying to trick me,* the crab thought intelligently. 
"Okay. I will carefully loosen my hold on you so that you can move a little to reach the wound," the crab told the snake. "But I shall absolutely not let the crow go till you completely cure my friend."
The crab adjusted its grip, holding the snake tightly by its tail, allowing him just enough freedom of movement to reach the bite mark.

Trapped, the snake had no choice. He placed his fangs over the bite and sucked back all the deadly venom from the brahmin's body. 
"Ah!" the brahmin gasped, suddenly recovering his senses as the poison was withdrawn. "The poison is out."

The crab watched carefully. *My friend has recovered. But I cannot trust these two wicked creatures. They may attack him again the moment I let go.*
With a swift, powerful squeeze of both its pincers, the golden crab instantly killed both the snake and the crow. 

The she-crow, watching in horror from the palm tree, immediately flew off, abandoning her nest. "The place is no longer safe!" she shrieked.

By then, the farm helpers arrived at the field and saw the dead snake and crow. "What happened, master?" they asked in shock.
"The snake bit me and the crow was about to take out my eyes while I was paralyzed," the brahmin explained, gently petting his golden crab. "But my loyal friend the crab, saved my life. He is truly a friend in need."
