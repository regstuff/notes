# Ram Shastri: Crusader for Justice and Truth

In the small, devout village of Mahuli Kshetra in the Satara district of Maharashtra, the annual temple festival was in full swing. According to the proud local tradition, rich donations and generous gifts were distributed to all qualified, learned Brahman students and scholars at the end of the grand *puja*. 

Among the crowd was a playful, carefree young boy named Ramya (Ram). Orphaned and living under the strict care of his uncle, Ram had utterly neglected his studies, preferring to frolic in the river with his friends. When the time came to collect the gifts, Ram eagerly queued up with the other students. 

"Which school do you go to?" the presiding Shastri asked him sternly. 
Ram hung his head in deep shame. "I have no right..." he whispered, refusing to lie. Utterly miserable and humiliated by his own ignorance, he walked away empty-handed. 

"I wash my hands off you!" his embarrassed uncle scolded him fiercely. "I forbid you to enter my home. You are your own master now!"

Completely at peace with himself and driven by a sudden, fierce determination, the young Ram left Mahuli and walked to the city of Satara. There, he managed to secure a humble job as a lowly menial servant in the wealthy household of Anagal Saheb. For months, Ram washed heavy dishes, drew water, and ran tiring errands without a single word of complaint. 

One day, while quietly assisting the household *pujari* (priest) during prayers, Ram politely asked the priest to recite the complex Sanskrit hymns a little slower so he could learn and repeat them. Amused by the servant boy's earnest desire to learn, the kind priest happily obliged. 

Shortly after, Anagal Saheb returned home and noticed Ram wearing a pair of beautiful, traditional gold earrings—the mark of a learned scholar. 
"Fool! What are you thinking of?" Anagal Saheb scolded him angrily. "Your earrings, sir! Why do you feel like wearing such earrings when you are a mere menial?"

Ram stood tall and looked his master squarely in the eye. "You will first have to become worthy of them," Anagal mocked. 
"I shall!" Ram replied with quiet, unshakeable confidence. "I am no longer your menial. I will go to Varanasi, find a master, learn at his feet, and become a true Shastri. Till then, I will not set foot on this land!"

True to his word, the resolute sixteen-year-old boy undertook the long, incredibly arduous trek across India to the holy city of Varanasi. There, he humbly approached the revered Guru Paigunde, begging to be accepted as a pupil. 

Though his much younger, privileged classmates relentlessly mocked and teased him for starting his education so late in life, Ram took it all good-naturedly. He poured his entire soul into his studies. Twelve disciplined years passed in this rigorous manner, and Ram finally emerged as a brilliant, absolute master of the Vedas, Vedangas, and Shastras. 

Having been formally ordained as a Shastri, Ram prepared to return to his homeland. "Don't sell your knowledge," his wise Guru advised him gently at their final parting. "That is the greatest *Dakshina* (fee) you could offer me. Let justice, the king, and society directly benefit by your profound learning."

Ram Shastri kept that sacred promise. When he returned to Maharashtra, his immense reputation as an unparalleled, fiercely honest Sanskrit scholar quickly caught the attention of Peshwa Madhav Rao in Poona. The Peshwa respectfully invited Ram Shastri to his royal court and appointed him as a senior judge. 

Madhav Rao was a genuinely good, capable ruler, but being a deeply orthodox Brahman, he often spent far too much time on elaborate religious rituals, severely neglecting his pressing state duties. 

One afternoon, after waiting for hours outside the Durbar hall while the Peshwa conducted a lengthy *puja*, Ram Shastri made a bold, shocking decision. When the Peshwa finally emerged, Ram Shastri handed in his immediate resignation. 
"The ruler neglects his vital duties," Ram Shastri stated fearlessly. "But how can I let him know without offending him? My duties as a Brahman go ill with my duties as a judge. So I have decided to completely give up one of them. I am leaving for Kashi."

The wise Peshwa instantly understood the profound, unspoken reprimand. Deeply humbled, he immediately apologized, prioritizing his administrative duties from that day onward. His immense respect and absolute confidence in Ram Shastri grew exponentially. When the Chief Justice eventually passed away, Madhav Rao proudly appointed Ram Shastri to the highest judicial position in the Maratha Empire. 

Ram Shastri's name quickly became an absolute byword for fearless, impartial justice. Once, while visiting his hometown of Mahuli, he witnessed the Peshwa's own soldiers brutally laying waste to farmers' fields and forcefully dragging away young men for unpaid labor. Furious at the blatant abuse of power, Ram Shastri personally arrested the soldiers and hauled them directly before the Peshwa in Poona. 

"It is nearing harvest time," Ram Shastri argued passionately before the stunned court. "If the farmers are forcibly taken away, the land will lie fallow. Terrible famine will strike! The ruler will have failed in his most basic duty."
Realizing the grave error, the Peshwa immediately pardoned the farmers and permanently banned the cruel practice of forced labor throughout the entire state. 

However, dark days soon fell upon the Maratha Empire. When Madhav Rao's health severely declined, he formally placed his younger brother, Narayan Rao, in the care of their ambitious, unpredictable uncle, Raghunath Rao (Raghoba), appointing him as the royal regent. 

While Raghoba was initially satisfied with the regency, his wicked, intensely power-hungry wife, Anandibai, was not. She deeply craved absolute power and began plotting a sinister, bloody coup. 

When Raghoba foolishly signed a written order instructing his loyal soldiers to *kidnap* the young Peshwa Narayan Rao, Anandibai secretly intercepted the letter. With a stroke of her pen, she maliciously altered the Marathi word *'dharawe'* (to catch) to *'marawe'* (to kill). 

Unaware of the fatal alteration, the heavily armed soldiers ruthlessly stormed the palace in broad daylight and brutally hacked the young Narayan Rao to death, even as the terrified boy clung to his bewildered uncle Raghoba for protection. 

When Ram Shastri learned of the horrific, cold-blooded assassination, he was absolutely aghast. Utterly disgusted by the bloody coup, he initially planned to leave Poona forever. But when he saw the weeping, desperate widows of the slain guards begging for justice, his absolute commitment to the law roared back to life. 

A few days later, a grand, pompous ceremony was organized in the Durbar hall to formally crown Raghoba as the new Peshwa. Just as Raghoba confidently stepped toward the sacred throne, a thundering voice stopped him dead in his tracks. 

"Wait!" 

It was Ram Shastri, standing tall and utterly fearless in the center of the stunned court. 
"As Chief Justice, it is my absolute privilege to lead the Peshwa to the throne," Ram Shastri declared, his voice ringing with terrifying authority. "But so also, in the cause of ultimate justice, I shall deprive you of the sword, if need be!"

The entire court gasped in absolute shock. 
"Ram Shastri, beware! You are going too far!" the guilty Raghoba stammered nervously. 
"You are a coward!" Ram Shastri roared fearlessly at the most powerful man in the empire. "You had your own innocent nephew murdered to illegally usurp this sacred throne!"

Furious, the wicked Anandibai ordered her heavily armed soldiers to instantly execute the insolent Chief Justice. But before anyone could move, Ram Shastri calmly reached into his cummerbund and pulled out a piece of parchment. It was the original, blood-stained assassination order, bearing Raghoba's own unmistakable royal seal! 

Anandibai turned completely pale. Even Raghoba’s own subedars (commanders) stepped forward, drawing their swords to protect the fiercely honest judge. 

"You are the Shastri," Raghoba whispered, finally breaking down in overwhelming terror and guilt. "You know the penalty for political murders. Speak out. I appeal to you. And I shall atone."

"For a cold-blooded murderer, there is absolutely no compromise where true justice is concerned," Ram Shastri declared with chilling finality. "There is only one penalty. **Death.**"

Knowing he could not legally execute the usurper with the current army surrounding him, Ram Shastri turned his back on the blood-stained throne. "It is utterly futile," he announced. "As long as this sinner and murderer sits on the sacred throne, Ram Shastri shall be an absolute alien in Poona!"

True to his unbreakable, fiercely honorable word, Ram Shastri walked out of the capital that very day. He only returned to Poona much later, when the murdered Narayan Rao's posthumous infant son—Sawai Madhav Rao—was joyously born and officially recognized as the true, rightful Peshwa, completely overthrowing the usurper Raghoba. 

From that triumphant day onwards, Ram Shastri served as the universally revered Chief Justice of the Maratha Empire until the very end of his noble days, his name forever immortalized in Indian history as the ultimate, unyielding symbol of absolute truth and fearless justice.
