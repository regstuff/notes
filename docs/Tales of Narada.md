# Tales of Narada - 1. Narada Conquers Temptation

Narada was a great Devarshi, a celestial sage who was always travelling round the world, singing praises to Lord Vishnu and offering spiritual guidance to devotees. After having gained true, deep knowledge from his father, Brahma the Creator, Narada took a strict vow of celibacy.

"I shall not marry," Narada declared. "I will dedicate my entire life to serving Lord Narayana (Vishnu)."

Narada then travelled to the cold, barren peaks of the Himalayas, where he sat down and practised severe, unbreaking austerities for many years. 

Up in heaven, Indra, the King of the Devas, grew nervous. He became deeply suspicious of Narada's intense penance. 

"I wonder what he is truly after," Indra worried, speaking to his royal preceptor, Brihaspati. "He is building up immense spiritual power. It could be he wishes to overthrow me and take my heavenly throne."

"What shall I do?" Indra paced back and forth. "Ah! I know! I could tempt him with irresistible worldly pleasures and forcibly make him swerve from his righteous path."

With this wicked plan in mind, Indra immediately sent for Kamadeva, the handsome God of Love. 

"Kama, I urgently need your help," Indra commanded. "You must go down to earth and make Narada completely give up his austerities."

"I'll certainly try, my Lord," Kama smiled confidently, stringing his flower-bow.

Kama descended to the cold, icy, barren spot where Narada was sitting completely still, deep in meditation. Kama raised his bow and shot his first magical arrow of love...

Instantly, the harsh scene suddenly changed. The ice melted away, replaced by a lush, fragrant, blooming spring forest. A breathtakingly beautiful celestial apsara magically appeared before Narada.

She began to dance gracefully before him, the bells on her ankles jingling sweetly. "O great Sage, open your eyes and behold your willing slave," she whispered softly.

But Narada's eyes remained firmly closed to her charms, and his mind was so focused on God that he hardly even heard her sweet voice. 

Realising that she would never succeed in distracting the supreme sage, the beautiful apsara finally gave up and left for her heavenly abode. 

Kama had to humbly acknowledge absolute defeat. He appeared before the sage and bowed deeply. "You are a truly great ascetic, O supreme sage!" Kama admitted. "I have entirely failed. I beg to be forgiven for my sheer audacity in trying to tempt you."

Narada finally opened his eyes and smiled. "Oh, Kama! Who sent you here to tempt me?"

"Who but Lord Indra!" Kama confessed.

Narada puffed out his chest. "Go back and plainly tell Indra that Narada has successfully conquered all worldly desires, that he is completely above temptation!"

As Kama left, Narada gloated over his incredible achievement. *I have actually defeated Kama!* he thought proudly. *Lord Shiva is no longer the only conqueror of this invincible god of love. I must go and tell Shiva about it immediately. He must now accept me as his absolute equal!*

Narada flew straight to Mount Kailas, the snowy abode of Lord Shiva.

"Salutations to Lord Shiva!" Narada said, bowing.

"Come, Narada! You seem incredibly pleased today. What's the happy reason?" Shiva asked kindly.

"I have single-handedly conquered Kamadeva!" Narada boasted proudly. "Indra sent him to tempt me with apsaras, but Kama failed completely!"

Shiva smiled gently, sensing the sage's growing ego. "I am very glad to know that! But take my advice, Narada—keep the matter to yourself. In any case, never brag about this victory to Lord Vishnu!"

Narada left Kailas feeling slightly insulted. *Shiva is obviously just jealous of me,* Narada thought arrogantly. *Why shouldn't I speak about my great success to Vishnu? Vishnu, who loves me so dearly! I am perfectly sure he will be incredibly proud to hear of my victory over Kama.*

He went straight to Vishnu's heavenly abode.

"May I pay my respects to the great Lord?" Narada asked, entering the grand hall.

Lord Vishnu smiled warmly from his serpent bed. "Come, Narada! I am always so glad to see you."

"You will be much more so, my Lord, when you learn that I, your greatest devotee, have completely conquered Kamadeva," Narada bragged.

"Is that so?" Vishnu asked calmly, not looking very surprised.

"Yes, my Lord!" Narada boasted. "Shiva is no longer the only conqueror of Kama. I, your faithful devotee, am absolutely above all temptation too."

Vishnu looked at him with serious eyes. "That is good, Narada. But never cease to be on your guard. You never truly know when temptation might strike..."

Narada bowed and left, feeling very disappointed. *Huh! Vishnu didn't seem too pleased about my amazing achievement either,* Narada fumed as he flew away. *I am no weakling! Doesn't he know that? Why should he warn me to be on my guard?*

As Narada moved on, his attention was suddenly caught by something below on earth. 

*What's that?* Narada thought in amazement. *What a wonderfully beautiful city! I've never seen one like it before! I absolutely must visit it.*

When he reached the sparkling city, he asked a passing citizen, "Who is the ruler of this big and charming city?"

"Don't you know?" the man replied. "It belongs to the glorious King Sheelanidhi. You'll find him in his grand palace."

Hearing that the famous Sage Narada had come to visit him, King Sheelanidhi came rushing out to respectfully receive him. "Welcome, O Sage! You do us great honour."

Just then, the King's daughter, Princess Shrimati, came gracefully onto the scene. 

"This is my daughter, Shrimati," the King introduced her.

Narada stared at her, his jaw dropping. *What a breathtakingly beautiful maiden she is!* he thought, his heart suddenly beating wildly. 

As was the custom, Shrimati bowed deeply to the sage. 

"Arise, dear child," Narada said softly, completely smitten. "May you ever be happy."

"She is ready for marriage," the King announced proudly. "I plan to hold a grand swayamvara (a ceremony to choose a husband) for her very soon."

Narada gazed longingly at the princess. *Your daughter is Goddess Laxmi incarnate,* he thought, entirely overcome with sudden, intense desire. *One no less than Hari (Vishnu) in glory and power, shall be her husband... and that man should be me!*

Later, the King smiled at Narada. "Your kind words fill our hearts with joy, O Sage. I cannot wait to announce the exact date of her swayamvara."

Narada left the palace, his mind racing. *If I can somehow get this beautiful princess for a wife, there will be none more fortunate and powerful in all the universe,* he plotted. *But how shall I definitely win her over all the other handsome princes?*

Desperate, Narada prayed fervently to Lord Vishnu for help. 

At last, Lord Vishnu appeared before him. "What do you desire, Narada?"

"Lord, I am deeply in love. Please, let my face perfectly resemble Hari's!" Narada begged, using one of Vishnu's many names.

Vishnu hid a small smile. "You shall certainly have the exact face of Hari," Vishnu promised. "But perhaps not the Hari you're currently thinking of."

When Narada made his eager request, he had completely forgotten that in the ancient Sanskrit language, the word *Hari* also meant 'a monkey'! And because he could not see his own face, he didn't realize the terrible change that had just happened.

Narada, sporting a very hairy, ugly monkey face, went to the grand swayamvara hall full of absolute confidence, completely sure of his victory. 

As he confidently took a seat among the handsome princes, the crowd began to point and whisper. 

"Who could that strange, ugly creature be?" a prince laughed. "A man with a monkey's face! Ha! Ha!"

"And what does he possibly want here?" another mocked. "The heart of Shrimati, the most beautiful princess in the world?"

King Sheelanidhi stood up. "I have the great honour of welcoming you, our royal guests, to the swayamvara of my daughter Shrimati. She shall choose one among you as her husband. Come my daughter, make your choice!"

Shrimati walked slowly into the hall, carrying a beautiful flower garland. 

*What a divine beauty!* Narada thought, his heart soaring. *Ah! She's coming straight towards me!*

But hardly noticing him, Shrimati walked right past. 

*You must be looking for me, beautiful one. Here I am,* Narada thought, standing up and trying to catch her eye.

"For you? Ha! Ha! She is, monkey-face! Ha! Ha!" the other princes roared with laughter at him.

*Somehow she didn't clearly see me,* Narada reasoned foolishly. *I'll quickly cross over to the other side of the hall. She definitely won't miss me then.*

He scurried across the room, annoying the other guests. "Who allowed him in? He is making a terrible nuisance of himself!" the King frowned.

Shrimati continued walking, her eyes scanning the crowd. *Lord Vishnu, my beloved, why haven't you come?* she thought sadly. *You know you are my chosen one.*

Meanwhile, Narada heard the princes mocking him. *"A very handsome one. No doubt!" "But still a monkey-face."*

*Monkey-face?* Narada thought, suddenly panicked. *Do I really have the ugly face of a monkey? All of them can't be wrong. Vishnu has cruelly duped me! I wish he were here right now. I could have angrily asked him why.*

The next moment, a blinding light filled the hall. Lord Vishnu himself appeared in all his divine glory.

"Oh! My Lord!" Shrimati gasped happily.

"Ah! There he is!" Narada thought. "Lord, why did you..."

But before Narada could complain, Shrimati ran forward and joyfully placed the wedding garland around Lord Vishnu's neck. 

"So that was it!" Narada screamed, absolutely furious. "He tricked me because he wanted her for himself. The traitor!"

Narada charged forward in a blind rage. "You promised to give me your beautiful face and gave me a monkey's instead! Why did you betray me?"

Lord Vishnu smiled calmly. "My dear Narada, you are a great scholar of Sanskrit. Don't you know, *Hari* also means monkey? You asked for the face of Hari, but you didn't specifically specify which Hari you meant."

Narada touched his hairy face in horror. "Am I stuck with this awful face for ever?"

"Calm down, Narada, and simply look around you," Vishnu commanded gently.

Narada looked around... and gasped. 

"I don't understand!" Narada cried. "King Sheela Nidhi, Shrimati, the handsome princes, the grand palace—where are they? They've all completely vanished!"

They were standing alone in a barren wasteland. 

"So has your monkey-face, Narada," Vishnu pointed out.

Narada touched his face and felt his own smooth skin again. 

"There was no beautiful city, no King, no princess, and no swayamvara," Vishnu explained softly. "It was all an illusory creation of mine, designed simply to humble your massive pride... because you foolishly boasted that you were entirely above temptation!"

Narada fell to his knees, tears of realization in his eyes. "I am deeply grateful to you, my Lord! You have truly opened my eyes to my own dangerous arrogance."


# Tales of Narada - 2. Who is the Greater Devotee?

Sage Narada, proud of his constant chanting, was sitting under a tree one day playing his veena. 

*I constantly sing the glory of Lord Vishnu day and night,* Narada thought to himself. *Who can possibly be a greater devotee than me? I genuinely wonder if the Lord himself is of the exact same opinion.*

Determined to find out, he went directly to Lord Vishnu in Vaikuntha.

"Salutations to you, my Lord!" Narada bowed.

"Welcome, O Narada! What brings you here today?" Vishnu asked warmly.

"I have come to ask you a specific question," Narada replied.

Vishnu smiled knowingly. "I already know what it is. Anyway, let me clearly hear it from you."

Narada puffed out his chest. "I have faithfully been singing your glory all my life, every single minute of it. Can there be anyone in the entire universe more devoted to you than me?"

Vishnu stood up. "If that question is to be truthfully answered, you must come down to earth with me right now."

Disguised simply as two poor, wandering farmers, Vishnu and Narada magically came to earth at dusk and walked towards a small, rundown hut near a dense forest.

"Over there, in that little hut lives one of my absolute greatest devotees!" Vishnu told Narada, pointing.

Narada frowned. *Whose devotion could possibly be deeper than mine? I wonder!* he thought suspiciously. "Does he constantly think of you all the time as I do?" Narada asked aloud.

"Wait and see for yourself," Vishnu replied softly.

As they approached the hut, a poor farmer was outside tying up his bullock. "Hari, Hari, Govinda!" the farmer prayed softly as he worked. 

When the farmer saw the two strangers, he respectfully folded his hands. "Narayana, Narayana! Where do you come from, brothers? What can I kindly do for you?"

"We were on our weary way to the city," Vishnu lied smoothly. "It'll soon be perfectly dark and the forest here is heavily infested with dangerous wild animals. We humbly seek shelter for a single night."

"You are very welcome to safely stay with me, my friends, and partake of what meagre fare I can offer you," the farmer said warmly, leading them inside.

Once inside the small, dark hut, the farmer smiled. "I am deeply grateful to God for giving me this unexpected opportunity to serve him by serving guests. Praised be his holy name!" 

He turned to his tired wife in the small kitchen area. "There are two hungry guests in the house. Please kindly make some extra *chapatis* (unleavened bread) for them."

The wife looked worried. "This is absolutely all the flour I have," she whispered, "and the children are already clamouring for more food."

"Never mind!" the farmer insisted softly. "The guests must definitely have their fill first. Make some watery gruel for the children."

Soon after they had finished eagerly eating the chapatis, Vishnu patted his stomach and complained loudly, "I am still very hungry."

Narada watched the poor farmer's reaction. *How can this simple, poor house-holder possibly be such a great devotee?* Narada thought.

The farmer immediately went to the kitchen. "Is there any food left?"

"Nothing," his wife sighed, "except the thin gruel I just cooked for the children."

The farmer took the pot of gruel without hesitation and served it to the guests. Vishnu and Narada finished that too, leaving absolutely nothing left. The poor farmer and his entire family had to go to bed completely hungry.

That night, lying on the floor, the farmer's young daughter whispered, "I can't sleep. I am so terribly hungry. Why did you freely give them our gruel, father?"

"Because by feeding a hungry guest, I am feeding Vishnu himself," the farmer explained softly.

Vishnu nudged Narada in the dark. "See, Narada, the farmer and his poor family have eaten nothing at all this evening; and yet he still peacefully sings my glory."

"That's nothing!" Narada whispered back defensively. "I have remained without food for many days on end and still remembered you constantly!"

The next morning, the farmer woke up early. "Govinda, Hari, Hari! Lord, may I always have unshakeable faith in you. I seek absolutely nothing else," he prayed. 

He came out to greet his guests. "Lord be praised! Friends, I sincerely hope you slept well last night. You may safely stay here as long as you please. But I must now be off to my fields to work."

"We'll happily come with you, if you don't mind," Vishnu said.

Narada and Vishnu accompanied the farmer to his small field. 

"Ha! Here we are!" the farmer smiled, picking up his heavy plough. "I must now quickly get down to hard work. Govinda, Hari, Hari."

Narada stepped closer to the farmer. "What a very pious man you are! Are you a great devotee of the Lord? You keep constantly repeating his name."

"I try to faithfully remember his glorious name as often as my daily work permits," the farmer said humbly, wiping sweat from his brow.

"How often would that be?" Narada pressed.

"Well, I think of Hari the moment I get up, right before I go to bed, and as many times in between as my heavy work permits," the farmer answered honestly.

"I see," Narada sniffed, looking unimpressed.

After they had taken leave of the farmer and were walking away, Narada scoffed. "He thinks of you at night, in the morning and only a few brief times in between. While I think of you absolutely *all the time*. And yet you confidently call him one of your greatest devotees!"

Vishnu smiled mysteriously. *You'll soon clearly see why, dear Narada,* he thought.

Vishnu magically produced a large brass vessel, filled to the very brim with slippery oil, and gave it to Narada.

"Balance this heavy vessel exactly on your head, walk completely round that steep hill over there, and come safely back here without spilling a single drop of the oil," Vishnu commanded.

Narada looked at the brimful vessel. "It won't be very easy, but with your divine grace is there absolutely anything that cannot be accomplished?"

And Narada confidently set off.

*I must be incredibly cautious lest the oil should spill,* Narada thought, balancing the heavy vessel on his head and taking very small, careful steps. 

Suddenly, he tripped on a loose rock. "Ah! Ah! That was a terribly close call!" he gasped as the oil swayed. "Had I slipped, the oil would have definitely spilt. I must be much more careful."

His mind was completely focused on nothing but the slippery oil.

When Narada successfully completed the entire round and returned, he smiled proudly. "I did it!"

"You're back. Good!" Vishnu said, taking the vessel. "But tell me honestly... exactly how many times did you remember me during the walk?"

Narada froze. "Not once, I'm afraid," he admitted quietly. "How could I? All my attention was completely fixed on the oil and the vessel!"

Vishnu looked at him pointedly. "That poor farmer has incredibly hard work to do all day just to survive. Yet he still remembers me—at least a few times. While you, with just a simple vessel of oil, could not remember me even once!"

Narada finally understood the great lesson. He bowed deeply. "I humbly concede it, my Lord. Those who manage to remember you amidst their heavy, daily worldly cares are without any doubt your absolute greatest devotees."


# Tales of Narada - 3. Narada Enlightened

The celestial sage, Narada, once came down to the beautiful city of Dwaraka, to see Lord Krishna (an avatar of Lord Vishnu).

"Welcome, Narada. What brings you here today?" Krishna asked warmly.

Narada looked troubled. "Krishna, I want to fully know what *Maya* (Illusion) is? Can you please clearly explain it to me?"

Krishna smiled mysteriously. "Narada, Maya simply can't be explained with just words. It absolutely has to be deeply experienced to be completely understood. Come with me."

Both Krishna and Narada left the city of Dwaraka... and kept walking for many days till they came to a hot, barren desert.

"Where exactly are we going?" Narada complained, wiping sweat from his brow. "How can I possibly experience Maya in an empty desert, Krishna?"

"Be patient, Narada," Krishna said simply.

After they had walked a very good distance in the scorching heat, Krishna suddenly stopped, clutching his throat. 

"I can't walk any further, Narada," Krishna gasped weakly. "My throat is completely parched. Take this pot... and please fetch me... some... water."

"Hold on, Krishna," Narada said, immediately taking the pot. "I'll be back as soon as I can."

Narada went in desperate search of water. Finally, in the distance, he spotted some small huts. "It looks like a human settlement over there," he said, hurrying towards it.

When he reached the village, he saw a deep stone well. "Ah! A well!" 

But as he approached, a young village maiden came to the well to draw water. Narada stopped dead in his tracks. 

*Oh! What a divine beauty!* Narada thought, instantly captivated by her big eyes and graceful movements. *She absolutely must be a goddess!*

He completely forgot about his thirst, and he completely forgot about Krishna waiting in the desert. 

"Lady, will you kindly give me some water to quench my thirst?" Narada asked smoothly.

"By all means, sir," she smiled shyly, pouring water into his pot. 

Completely enchanted, Narada followed the beautiful damsel all the way back to her house. An old, strong-looking man was sitting on the porch. 

"Are you the master of this house?" Narada asked respectfully.

"Not only of this house but also the headman of the entire village," the old man replied proudly. "What do you actively want, stranger?"

"I respectfully seek the hand of your beautiful daughter in marriage," Narada declared boldly.

The old man looked at Narada, sizing him up. "Why not? You look young, healthy, and very strong. But... the man who marries my daughter must permanently stay in this village, living in this house to help me."

"Is that all?" Narada agreed eagerly. "I'm more than willing to strictly abide by your condition." *Anything to securely make that girl my wife,* he thought desperately. 

The happy marriage soon took place. Narada took off his sage's robes, put on farmer's clothes, and settled happily into village life. 

Soon after the marriage, the old chieftain sadly died. Narada had to assume his father-in-law's title and all his heavy responsibilities. He became a wealthy, busy landowner. 

"I want the harvest work successfully finished by evening," Narada would command his workers in the fields.

"It will be done, master," the servants would bow. 

Over the years, Narada was happily blessed with four beautiful children. His life was perfectly full of love, wealth, and deep worldly attachment. "Father, put him down and excitedly carry me!" his youngest child would beg, tugging at his dhoti. 

But when Narada was at the absolute peak of his worldly success and happiness, sudden disaster came in the terrible form of cyclonic winds, freezing rain, and massive, rushing floods.

The village was completely inundated. 

"The house will soon be submerged! What shall we desperately do?" Narada panicked, holding his terrified wife and children as the water rapidly rose.

"Father!" the children cried in fear. 

Narada quickly put his beloved family into a small wooden boat and tried desperately to keep them safely afloat on the dark, swirling, violent waters. 

But the current was too strong. A massive wave hit them, and the boat tragically capsized. Narada tried desperately in vain to save his drowning wife and children.

"Father!" they screamed, disappearing under the dark waves. 

"Where are you! Don't panic! I am coming!" Narada screamed back, diving into the freezing water. But it was completely useless. They were gone.

A huge, powerful wave violently threw a sobbing, exhausted Narada onto the muddy shore. 

"My beautiful wife gone! My precious children drowned!" Narada wailed, beating his fists on the ground in absolute agony. "How can I possibly live without them?"

Suddenly, he heard a calm, familiar voice from right behind him.

"Narada, I am very thirsty. Where is the water?"

Narada gasped and quickly turned around. He beheld Lord Krishna, standing exactly where he had left him, pointing at the empty water pot in Narada's hand. 

Narada stared in absolute shock. "Krishna! My wife! My children! Please, use your divine power to bring them back to life!" he begged, still sobbing.

Krishna smiled gently and put a hand on Narada's shaking shoulder. "Come to your true senses, Narada. There never was any beautiful wife, there were never any children, and there was no terrible flood. It was all simply Maya."

Narada blinked, the memory of his entire lifetime fading like a dream. He looked around. The raging floodwaters were completely gone. They were standing alone in the dry, hot desert. 

Realizing the profound depth of the illusion he had just lived through, Narada fell to his knees and bowed deeply to Krishna. 

"I am deeply grateful to you for enlightening me, Krishna," Narada said, finally understanding. "Worldly life itself is a powerful illusion that is incredibly difficult to escape. Only with your divine grace can this terrible illusion be finally conquered!"
