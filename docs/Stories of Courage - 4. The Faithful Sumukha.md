# The Faithful Sumukha

Long ago, while King Sakula ruled in the kingdom of Mahinsak, there dwelt a large, magnificent herd of swans in a massive cave on Mount Chitrakoot. 

One day, some young swans ventured far away in search of food. When they returned, they were excited. 
"Oh! We have found a very big, beautiful lotus pond near a town," they told the herd. "It is called Manusiya."
"It is lovely. Five different types of lotus grow in it," another added.
"It would be an ideal place for us to feed ourselves. The food is plentiful."

Dhritarashtra, the wise old swan king, was hesitant. "No! We should not leave our safe territory here at Chitrakoot. It is too risky."
His trusted minister, Sumukha, agreed completely. "Yes, the king is right. Besides, it is absolutely not safe for us to go to a place so frequented by human beings. We will be easy targets."

"But at least let us see the place once," the younger swans insisted stubbornly. 
When the birds of the flock kept insisting and begging, the king finally gave in. "All right. If it makes all of you happy, we will go there just once."

The entire flock flew together towards the Manusiya pond. "Come on! It is just ahead," the scouts led the way.

Since many birds used to frequent the lake, it was naturally a favourite spot for human bird hunters too. As soon as the flock flew down to the lake and landed in the water, the swan king felt a sudden, sharp pull on his leg. He found his feet tightly caught in a cleverly hidden hunter's net.
"Ahh! I'm trapped!" he realized in horror. 

Quietly, he tried to free his foot, but the ropes were incredibly strong. *The more I pull, the deeper the cruel net cuts into my flesh,* he thought, feeling the blood flow. 
He looked at his happily feeding flock. *If I call out now, my companions will fly away in a panic without feeding. Poor things! They have flown a very long way to reach here.*

Calmly and silently, he waited in pain, enduring the trap. *Ah! Now they are playfully jumping about in the water. They must have eaten their fill,* he finally judged after a long time. 
Only then did he let out a loud, piercing call of alarm. "AHH!"

"A call of danger! Hurry, let's go back to Chitrakoota!" the flock panicked instantly. 
"But who called out?" someone asked.
"No time to find out. We must fly off immediately and save our lives!" 
The entire flock took off in a massive flurry of wings. 

Only one of the flock was reluctant to blindly fly off—Sumukha the minister. Deeply worried, he returned to the pond and searched the water. He soon saw the wounded and bleeding king trapped in the reeds. 
"I could not see our king Dhritarashtra in the sky. Is he the one who is wounded?" Sumukha landed beside him.
"Don't worry, O king," Sumukha promised. "I will free you even at the cost of my own life."

"But why do you bother?" Dhritarashtra asked sadly. "See, all the rest have flown off to safety. What hope is there for an ensnared bird like me? You should leave too."
"I would rather die with you here than abandon you like this," Sumukha replied firmly.
"But how will your useless sacrifice help either of us or anyone else?" the king argued.
"O foremost among birds, it is my sacred duty to be with you now, in this hour of peril. I will not leave."

Just then, the king spotted a man approaching. "Indeed, it is noble of you to do so, Sumukha. I deeply appreciate your devotion to me. Yet I give you my royal permission to go. Flee! See! The hunter is coming this way."

The hunter waded into the water, grinning broadly. "What luck! Two massive swans are caught in my net today. That's a great catch!"
But when he went closer, he realized something strange. "You are completely free," he said, looking at Sumukha. "You are not caught in any net. Why don't you fly off like the other swans did?"

"I cannot desert him," Sumukha replied in a clear human voice. "He is my king as well as a dear friend. Set him free and allow us to go."
"But you are free to go," the baffled hunter pointed out.
"No, I don't desire freedom for myself alone," Sumukha said bravely. "In fact, I beseech you to spare the king's life and simply capture me in his stead. I am as big and fat as he is. It will not matter to your profits if you catch me instead of him. Please, take me."

The hunter stood completely still. Thus, with his gentle, incredibly persuasive talk and selfless offer of his own life, Sumukha brought about a profound change of heart in the rough hunter. 
"I am deeply touched by your devotion," the hunter said, wiping a tear. "Go. I will not take you. I will set your king also free."
"Thank you! May you also be happy," Sumukha smiled.

Tenderly, the hunter freed the ensnared swan's bleeding feet and gently washed and cleaned his wound with fresh water. 
"O king, we owe the hunter a great favour for sparing us," Sumukha whispered to Dhritarashtra. "He could have sold us to the king as exotic show-birds or sold our flesh for a lot of money. Why don't you speak to the king on his behalf to get him a reward?"
"Take us to your king!" Dhritarashtra told the hunter.

"But that is risky!" the hunter warned. "The king may select you as a permanent pet or may just have you killed for his table."
"You had a miraculous change of heart," Sumukha reasoned. "The king too may not be hard-hearted when he hears our story."

The hunter carefully carried the birds to King Sakula's palace. "Here are two exquisite swans, your majesty. He is the king of swans and the other is his loyal commander."
"They are beautiful. How did you get them?" the king asked in wonder.

"O king, the hunter was kind enough to free us from his trap at the cost of his own livelihood," Sumukha spoke up. "We willingly came to you to ask you to get a reward for him."
"Let them be seated on golden seats!" the astonished king ordered his servants. "Bring fresh honey and lump sugar for them to eat."

When the hunter honestly related the entire incredible story of Sumukha's sacrifice and devotion, the king was deeply moved. 
The king gladly agreed to their request to reward the hunter. "I grant you a massive house, a fine chariot, gold, and an annual income of one lakh coins," he declared to the lucky hunter.

With the gracious permission of King Sakula, the swan king Dhritarashtra and his minister Sumukha safely returned to their herd at Mount Chitrakoot. 
"How did you get free?" the flock asked in awe.
"It was entirely because of the wisdom, love, and immense loyalty of my minister Sumukha," King Dhritarashtra announced proudly. "He risked his own life to save me."
