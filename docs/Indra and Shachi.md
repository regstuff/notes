# Indra and Shachi: The Lord of Heaven and His Devoted Wife

Indra, the king of the devas, lived happily with his devoted queen, Shachi, in his celestial city, Amaravati. However, their happiness was soon threatened. A son named Trishiras was born to Tvashta, an enemy of Indra. Trishiras had three heads: one to recite the Vedas, one to consume divine sura, and one to watch the world. He grew up fast, becoming strong-willed and powerful. Determined to avenge his father, Trishiras performed severe penances to destroy Indra. 

Fearing for his throne, Indra sent beautiful Apsaras to distract Trishiras and break his penance with their songs and dances. But their heavenly charms failed; Trishiras remained unmoved. Driven by insecurity and urged by Shachi to be careful, Indra threw his thunderbolt at Trishiras while he was praying, killing him instantly. However, Trishiras's eyes remained unshut and staring, haunting Indra. To close the eyes, Indra had a passing woodcutter cut off the heads, sharpening his axe with the thunderbolt. 

Having murdered a Brahman, Indra had to atone for his sin. He did penance for a long time until a divine voice declared him free, and he returned to Amaravati victorious. 

Meanwhile, deeply grieved by his son's death, Tvashta performed a sacrifice and created a fearsome asura named Vritra to kill Indra. When Indra heard of Vritra, he prepared for battle, regretting his hasty action. A terrible battle ensued, and Vritra caught Indra between his teeth! The devas quickly used the Jrimbhaka Astra, an arrow that causes uncontrollable yawning. As Vritra yawned, Indra made himself tiny and escaped. 

Seeking a permanent solution, Indra and the devas consulted Lord Vishnu. Vishnu advised Indra to make peace with Vritra and wait for a chance to kill him, promising his help. Vritra agreed to peace but on strict conditions: Indra could not kill him with any weapon that was wet or dry, made of wood or stone, or during the day or night. 

One evening, at twilight (neither day nor night), they were on the seashore. Indra saw sea foam (neither wet nor dry) and realised this was his chance. He prayed to Vishnu, who entered the foam. Indra threw the foam at Vritra, and the power of Vishnu within it killed the asura instantly. 

However, Indra had betrayed a friend and was deeply ashamed. "I have killed Vritra. But I cannot show my face to anyone. I shall hide where none can find me," he declared, and ran away. 

With Indra gone, darkness and chaos fell upon the earth and heaven. The devas met in a council and decided they needed a new king to prevent anarchy. They chose King Nahusha, a good and pious mortal. They invested him with celestial powers and strength, and made him the king of heaven. 

Nahusha enjoyed the celestial pleasures, riding Indra's white elephant and using the wish-fulfilling Kalpataru tree. But soon, power corrupted him, and he desired Shachi to be his queen. Prompted by Brihaspati, Shachi agreed on the condition that they first search for Indra. If he could not be found, she would marry Nahusha. 

The devas sought Vishnu's advice again. Vishnu told them to find Indra and have him perform an Ashwamedha sacrifice to atone for the sin of killing Vritra by deceit. Agni, the fire-god, led the search and finally found Indra hiding. Indra performed the Ashwamedha and, cleansed of his sin, set out to challenge Nahusha. 

But when Indra confronted Nahusha, he found the new king too powerful to face, as Nahusha had the combined strength of the devas. Indra lost his courage and ran away again. Shachi wept in despair. 

Determined to save her husband, Shachi prayed to Upashruti, a goddess of the night. The goddess appeared and led Shachi through the dark night, over mountains and across the ocean, to a lake. Upashruti slit the stem of a large lotus, revealing a tiny Indra cowering inside! The goddess made Shachi tiny too, so she could speak to him. 

After hearing Shachi's plight, Indra devised a clever plan. Shachi returned to Amaravati and told Nahusha that she was ready to be his queen. But she made a special request: "Indra used to come to my palace on his white elephant, a horse, or a chariot. I want you to come to me in a palanquin borne by the Seven Sages." 

Arrogant and eager, Nahusha agreed. He ordered the Seven Sages to carry his palanquin. As they carried him, Nahusha grew impatient with their slow pace, especially with the short-legged sage Agastya. "Lazy fellows! How slow they are! Sarpa! Sarpa! (Move on!)," Nahusha yelled, kicking Agastya to goad him on. 

Furious at this insult, Agastya cursed him: "How dare you! Sarpo bhava (Become a snake)! My curse will turn you into a snake." Instantly, Nahusha was transformed into a snake and fell from heaven. 

Meanwhile, Brihaspati and Agni had forced Indra to return. As they arrived in Amaravati, Agastya hurried up to them, announcing that Nahusha was now a mere snake and they were free. 

Indra was restored as the king of heaven, realising he owed his throne to the unwavering loyalty, intelligence, and devotion of his queen, Shachi.
