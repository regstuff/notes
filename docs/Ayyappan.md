# Ayyappan: The Legend of Shabari Malai

Long ago, the powerful demon Mahishasura obtained a formidable boon from Lord Brahma: he could only be killed by a woman. Emboldened by the boon, Mahishasura began brutally harassing all on earth. 

"The evil one is here! Run!" the people screamed as he and his army destroyed villages. 
The terrified Devas fled to heaven. "O Devas, save us!" the people prayed. 
The Devas held council. "This Asura must be destroyed," one said. "Because of Brahma's boon, only a woman would be able to do it."
"Then let us evoke one with our combined powers," another suggested. 

They focused their divine energies, and a magnificent, multi-armed goddess appeared. 
"We will call her Chandika Devi," the Devas declared. 
"Ha! Ha! Ha!" the fierce goddess laughed. Laughing all the while, Chandika Devi descended on earth. The whole world shook with the immense force of her arrival. 

"Who is that senseless woman?" Mahishasura scoffed when he felt the tremors. "Go bring her to me."
He sent his vast army, but Chandika Devi vanquished them in no time. The survivors returned to Mahishasura. "Lord! We had to retreat," they reported in fear. 
"Take the whole army if necessary; but bring her here!" the furious Asura king commanded. However, the entire army was no match for Chandika Devi and was soon completely routed. 

Mad with anger, Mahishasura approached Chandika Devi fiercely. "That woman has gone too far. I shall destroy her," he roared, and attacked. 
"Ha! Ha! Ha!" she laughed, easily parrying his blows. Even Mahishasura was no match for her divine power and was soon slain. Her mission accomplished, Chandika Devi peacefully ascended back to heaven. 

But the demon threat was not over. When Mahishi, the fearsome wife of Mahishasura, learnt of her husband's death, she swore revenge. "I shall teach the Devas a lesson. It was they who invoked the Devi," she hissed. 
Mahishi undertook a deep, grueling penance to propitiate Lord Brahma. Pleased, Brahma appeared before her. "Mahishi! What do you desire?"
"That I should not meet my death at the hands of either Vishnu or Shiva!" she demanded. 
Brahma granted her the boon and vanished. 

Armed with this invincibility, Mahishi began taunting and torturing the Devas. "To whom will you turn now?" she mocked. "Shiva and Vishnu are powerless against me!"

Lord Shiva watched from Kailasa and heard it all. "I must put an end to Mahishi's arrogance," he decided. He went to Lord Vishnu, who had taken the beautiful form of Mohini, the enchantress. 
"We must create a son who will destroy Mahishi," Shiva told Mohini. (Since the child would be born of both Shiva and Vishnu, Mahishi's boon would not protect her). 
"Who will rear the baby?" Mohini asked. 
"My devotees—King Rajashekhara and his queen. They long for a child. I will take him there," Shiva replied. 

As soon as the divine infant was created, Shiva placed a golden bell on a chain. "I will tie this gold bell around his neck before I take him to earth," Shiva said. 

Meanwhile, on earth, the childless King Rajashekhara was out hunting with his retinue near the banks of the river Pampa. After a tiring day, while the party relaxed, Rajashekhara wandered off alone. "How peaceful and quiet it is here," he thought. 
Suddenly, he heard a feeble wail. *Wah! Wah... Wah!*
"A baby's cry? Here?" he wondered. Rajashekhara walked in the direction of the sound and found a radiant, helpless baby boy lying all alone on a rock. 

"Poor baby! Don't cry!" the king said tenderly, picking him up. He looked around. "Not a soul in sight. To whom could it belong?"
He debated with himself. "What should I do? Should I take him home? Or should I leave him here?" 
Just then, a mysterious Brahman appeared before him. "Do not hesitate, O King. Take him home."
"But who is he?" Rajashekhara asked in awe. 
"You will know when he is twelve years old," the Brahman smiled. As the Brahman turned to leave, the king called out, "Wait! What shall I call him?"
"Call him Manikanthan, after the gold bell which hangs round his neck."

Rajashekhara joyfully took Manikanthan home to his queen. "A child at last! How happy I am," she wept with joy. 
"He is God's own gift," the king smiled. 

But not everyone was happy. Rajashekhara's wicked Diwan (chief minister) secretly fumed. "The accursed child! It has upset all my plans to seize the throne," he thought bitterly. 

Manikanthan soon grew up into a strong, brilliant young lad. He was placed under the guidance of an excellent guru and mastered all there was to learn in record time. "Manikanthan is not an ordinary child," his guru realized. By the time his studies were over, the guru knew he was teaching a divine incarnation. 
"Sir, please accept my *guru-dakshina* (teacher's fee)," Manikanthan offered respectfully. 
"From you I want compassion. Not these," the guru said softly. He brought his blind and mute son before Manikanthan. "Manikanthan, please restore his sight and speech."
Manikanthan smiled and blessed the boy. Instantly, the guru's son saw light and spoke! "Father! I have been given a new life!"

After this first miracle, Manikanthan performed many more. The news of his divine abilities spread rapidly. "I have never seen such a gifted boy," the citizens marveled. "Our future is assured. He will succeed the king."
The wicked Diwan, who overheard these comments, got really worried. "I must get rid of the boy somehow," he plotted. 

Then, the unforeseen happened. The queen miraculously gave birth to a son of her own! 
"Alas!" the queen thought, looking at her newborn. "Why were you not born earlier? Now you will never become king. Unless..."
The Diwan was jubilant. "The throne shall yet be mine!" he thought. A few days later, he went to the queen's chambers and poisoned her mind. "Manikanthan will soon be twelve years old. He will then be crowned heir apparent. With that, your own son will lose all chances of ever becoming the ruler."
"What can I do? I am helpless," the queen cried. 
"Will you do as I tell you? For the sake of your own son?"
"I will. For my son I will do anything," she agreed blindly. 
"Then pretend to have a severe headache. Leave the rest to me," the Diwan instructed. 

The Diwan then went to the corrupt court physician. "Do as I tell you and you will be well rewarded. The queen has a severe headache. You will prescribe tigress's milk as the only remedy." The physician greedily agreed. 

Soon, a messenger rushed to the physician. "The queen is in agony. The king has asked you to examine her immediately."
After examining the moaning queen, the physician turned to the frantic king. "What is the quickest remedy? I cannot bear to see my queen in pain," Rajashekhara pleaded. 
"The only remedy for this ailment is tigress's milk," the physician lied smoothly. 

The king was aghast. "Tigress's milk? But how are we to get it?" 
He summoned the Diwan. "Send as many men as possible into the forest in search of tigress's milk. The one who brings it shall have half my kingdom."
Many brave men went, but all returned unsuccessful, terrified of the wild beasts. "We saw a number of tigers, but..." they stammered. 

Then, exactly as the wicked Diwan had foreseen, young Manikanthan stepped forward. "Father, let me go on this errand!"
"Manikanthan, no! You are too young. How can I let you go on such a dangerous venture," the king refused. 
"For my mother's sake let me go, father," the boy pleaded. 
Manikanthan persisted until Rajashekhara had to yield. "All right, my son. Go if you must. But take care."

Armed with his bow, Manikanthan set out for the deep forest. As he wandered deeper and deeper, he entered the domain of the demoness Mahishi. 
One of her Asura guards spotted him. "Who is this creature who dares to trespass on our forest? I must tell my mistress." He ran to Mahishi. "There is a boy roaming in our forests."
"How dare he, without my permission! I'll destroy him," she roared. 

Mahishi marched up to Manikanthan and attacked him fiercely. "Come, Mahishi," Manikanthan said calmly. "My earthly mission is about to be fulfilled."
A fierce battle ensued. But Mahishi's boon was useless against the son of Shiva and Vishnu. Manikanthan easily overpowered and killed the demoness, freeing the forest from her terror. 

As Manikanthan continued his search for a tigress, Lord Indra, King of the Devas, appeared before him in gratitude. "I shall take the form of a tigress and come with you into the city as your mount," Indra offered. The other Devas who had accompanied Indra also turned themselves into tigresses. "We will follow you into the city," they declared. 

As Manikanthan rode into the city on his terrifying divine mount, followed by a horde of roaring tigresses, the people fled in panic. "Tigers! Help! Run!" 
A terrified citizen ran to the palace. "Manikanthan has returned... with a horde of tigresses!"
The king rushed out to see this unbelievable sight and was absolutely stunned. He remembered the Brahman's words from twelve years ago. "Manikanthan is twelve today. Today I will know who he is," the king realized. 

Just then, a booming divine voice echoed from the heavens. "MANIKANTHAN IS NONE BUT GOD HIMSELF."
Rajashekhara ran forward and fell weeping at Manikanthan's feet. "Please send the tigresses away, Manikanthan. My people are terror-stricken."
Manikanthan smiled, and the tigresses vanished into thin air. 

"As soon as you left, the queen miraculously recovered," Rajashekhara confessed bitterly. "I became suspicious. My suspicions were confirmed when I overheard a conversation between her and my Diwan. I shall punish them for this treachery."
"Spare them," Manikanthan instructed gently. "They hastened my mission of slaying Mahishi. And now, my work here is done. I must leave this world."
"Lord, a last request," the heartbroken king begged. "Show me the place in my kingdom where I may build a temple for you."
"I am pleased. Build it where this arrow I shoot falls," Manikanthan said. 

He drew his bow and fired a glowing arrow into the sky. "You may observe the path of the arrow." The arrow soared over the forests and finally fell at the high summit of the distant Shabari hills. 
With a final smile, Manikanthan vanished. 

The king immediately began the grueling trek to the mountain top. When he finally reached the peak, he looked around, dazed. "It is so high... impossible. How will I ever achieve my task? Where do I begin?" 
"Let there be just eighteen steps leading to the temple at the eastern gate," a divine voice guided him. "I shall send Vishwakarma, the divine architect, to help you out."

Under the magical guidance of Vishwakarma, a magnificent, imposing temple was soon completed on the hill. 
"Through the Lord's grace it has risen," the king marveled. "But will I find a befitting idol now?"
Lord Vishnu divined the king's anxiety and sent for Lord Parashurama. "Go down to earth as a stone-carver and help Rajashekhara install the idol."

Parashurama arrived at the king's court in the garb of a sculptor. "I have heard of your problem. I may be able to help," he offered. 
"Show me your wares before you take up the challenging task," the king requested. Parashurama revealed his exquisite carvings. "They are flawless, each one of them," the king gasped. "You are no ordinary sculptor. Tell me the truth. Who are you?"
Parashurama assumed his true, glorious form. "I am Parashurama. I have already fashioned the idol. Today, the day of Makara Sankranti, is highly auspicious. I shall install the idol straightaway."
And thus it was that the beautiful stone image of Lord Ayyappa (as Manikanthan came to be popularly known) was sanctified in the Shabari Malai temple. 

Meanwhile, back in the capital, the wicked Diwan had been afflicted by an agonizing, incurable disease. "I have sinned," he wept in pain. "Will I ever be cleansed?"
One night, he had a vision of Lord Ayyappan in a dream. "Come to my temple on the Shabari hill. But enter only after cleansing yourself in the river Pampa." (The Pampa was said to be enlivened by the spirit of Shabari, the great devotee of Lord Rama). 

The Diwan wasted no time. The next morning, he dragged himself to the river Pampa. "I have come to wash away my sins in remorse, holy mother. Save me," he prayed, wading into the waters. 
When the Diwan emerged, he was glowing with health, his disease completely cured! "I am cleansed. The holy mother has been merciful," he cried. 

Without pausing for breath, he ran all the way up the eighteen steps to the temple. "Ayyappa! Ayyappa!"
When he reached the *sanctum sanctorum*, overcome by pure emotion and devotion, he fell weeping at the feet of the beautiful idol. "Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa! (Lord Ayyappa, I seek refuge in you!)"

And to this day, as millions of pilgrims make their arduous trek through the dense forests to the sacred hill temple of Lord Ayyappa at Shabari Malai, their faces shining with devotion, they call out with all their hearts: *"Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa!"*
