# The Reunion

Without Arjuna, the Kamyaka forest felt devoid of joy for the remaining Pandavas. Yudhishthira and his brothers were filled with profound melancholy. "In Arjuna's absence the earth seems to be forlorn," Draupadi lamented. "Even this forest with its blossoming trees does not seem as delightful as before. I cannot feel any happiness, O King!"

Bheema, Nakula, and Sahadeva echoed her sentiments, their hearts heavy with the absence of their valiant brother. Seeing their grief, Yudhishthira became sadder still. 

Just then, the celestial Rishi Narada appeared. Seeking a distraction from their sorrow, Yudhishthira asked the sage about the merits of pilgrimage. Narada described the various holy Teerthas and the spiritual merit obtained by visiting them. 

Shortly after Narada departed, the great Rishi Lomasha arrived, bearing news directly from Indraloka. "I have seen Arjuna," Lomasha announced to the overjoyed Pandavas. "He has acquired celestial weapons and has mastered the arts of dancing, music, singing, and recitation. He will join you soon, but meanwhile, you must devote yourselves to austerities at the various Teerthas."

"In obedience to Indra and at the request of Arjuna, I will accompany you on your pilgrimage," Lomasha promised. 

The Pandavas, accompanied by Lomasha, Dhaumya, and other ascetics, set out on their grand pilgrimage. They traveled far and wide, bathing in sacred waters, performing rites, and giving away wealth and cows in charity. 

As they marched into the Gandhamadana forests, Draupadi, unaccustomed to such rugged travel on foot, faltered and collapsed in exhaustion. Yudhishthira placed her head in his lap and wept bitterly, cursing his addiction to dice that had brought such suffering to his delicate wife. 

Bheema immediately took charge. "Ghatotkacha!" he called out to his mighty Rakshasa son. 

Ghatotkacha appeared instantly. "Father, here I am, ready to do your bidding."

"My child, carry Draupadi and follow us," Bheema commanded. 

"I have only to think of him and he will be here," Ghatotkacha said, calling upon other benevolent Rakshasas. In moments, strong Rakshasas carried not only Draupadi but also the weary Pandavas, swiftly covering the treacherous terrain. 

They reached the sacred hermitage of Nara and Narayana at Badari. While dwelling there, a strong wind blew a beautiful, incredibly fragrant flower into their camp. 

"Bheema, look at this exquisite flower!" Draupadi exclaimed. "I must have a few of these. If you love me, dear Bheema, I must have these flowers."

Eager to please her, Bheema armed himself with his mace and bow and rushed into the forest in search of the flowers. As he hurried through the thick jungle, animals fled in terror from his thunderous path. 

Suddenly, he was brought to a halt. A monkey with a long tail was lying lazily on the ground, completely blocking the path. 

"I am ill," the monkey said lazily. "I was asleep and you woke me up. Being a man, you are wiser than I. You ought to be more considerate. Who are you?"

"I am Bheema, the son of Pandu, born to Kunti through the grace of Vayu!" Bheema announced proudly. "Now let me pass or I will give you a thrashing."

"O mighty Bheema, I am old and weak," the monkey sighed. "Have pity on me. Lift my tail and place it aside and go across."

Bheema grabbed the monkey's tail with his left hand, intending to casually toss it aside. To his utter shock, he couldn't move it an inch. Grunting with effort, he used both hands, straining until his muscles bulged and sweat poured down his face, but the tail remained immovable as a mountain. 

Defeated and humbled, Bheema folded his hands. "What's the matter with me? I, Bheema am unable to lift the tail of a mere monkey! But is it a mere monkey? If not, who is he?"

The monkey smiled. "O Bheema, I am your brother Hanuman!"

Bheema fell to his knees and embraced his elder brother, drawing immense strength and courage from him. Hanuman then pointed to a hidden path that would lead Bheema to the Saugandhika forest, the favorite playground of Kubera, the God of Wealth. 

Bheema reached the lake in the forest and began plucking the celestial flowers. The fierce Rakshasas and Yakshas guarding the lake attacked him for intruding without Kubera's permission, but Bheema easily defeated them, killing over a hundred. When the surviving guards reported the incident to Kubera, the Lord of Wealth merely smiled. "I know why Bheema is here! Return to your posts. Let him take the flowers."

Sometime later, a demon named Jatasura, disguised as a Brahmana, infiltrated the Pandavas' camp. Finding Bheema absent, the demon abducted Yudhishthira, Draupadi, and the twins, carrying them off. Bheema returned just in time, chased down the demon, and slew him in a brutal wrestling match, rescuing his family. 

After five long years of separation, as the Pandavas waited at their camp, the heavens suddenly lit up. The earth trembled, and a glorious chariot driven by Matali descended from the sky. 

Arjuna had returned. 

Adorned in celestial garlands, wearing a divine diadem, and carrying the formidable Gandeeva bow, Arjuna stepped down and bowed to his elders. The brothers rushed to embrace him, weeping tears of joy. Even Lord Indra descended briefly from heaven to bless Yudhishthira. 

"You will rule the earth," Indra promised before departing. 

That night, Arjuna recounted his incredible adventures. He told them of his battles with the Nivatakavachas, the fierce demons of the ocean whom he had annihilated single-handedly with Lord Indra's weapons. He spoke of destroying the flying city of Hiranyapura and its demonic inhabitants. 

"Now even the celestials cannot conquer you, let alone mortals," Yudhishthira said, his heart swelling with pride. "There is no strength in the world greater than yours, and there is no one to compare with you in valour."

United once more, the invincible brothers felt a renewed surge of hope. With Arjuna's celestial arsenal and Bheema's unmatched strength, the ultimate victory over the wicked Kauravas was now a certainty.
