# The Pig And The Dog - 1. The Pig And The Dog

Here is a collection of folk tales from Arunachal Pradesh that have ingenious and hilarious explanations for all kinds of phenomena, from why langurs have black faces, to why tigers have stripes. 

## The Pig and the Dog
A man had a pig and a dog. One day he suddenly realized that the two animals did no work at all. "Get up, you lazy creatures!" he shouted. "Go and do something useful! Whichever of you does some work might find a place in my house."

The pig and the dog went to their master's field. The pig plowed the field the whole day long with its snout, while the dog simply slept under a tree. 

In the evening, the pig wearily set off for home. The dog, well-rested, quickly jumped up, ran up and down the freshly plowed field several times, and then followed the pig. 

"I will have to find out the truth for myself," the master thought. He got up early the next morning and went to the field. 

"Are you satisfied with my work, master?" the dog asked when the man returned. 
"What work?" the pig squealed in protest. 
"I saw only the dog's footprints in the soil," the master told the pig. "It was he who plowed the field. Go away, you lazy creature!"

He then turned to the dog. "Come, dog. You have done some hard work. You may come into the house with me."

And ever since, dogs have stayed in their masters' houses, and pigs have not been allowed inside.

## The Owl and the Tiger
One day, the birds and animals of the earth held a meeting and chose the tiger to be king of the jungle, and the owl to be his minister. 

But the king and his minister had quite different natures. The tiger was rash, and the owl was proud. 

One day they had a heated argument which turned into a terrible quarrel. The angry tiger slapped the owl so hard that the owl's face became flat! 

The furious owl grabbed a burning log from a nearby fire and struck the tiger with it, leaving long black stripes on its body. 

Since that day, owls have flat faces and tigers have black stripes on their bodies.

## Why Crabs Have Flat Bodies
There once lived a crab near a river. One day, his friend the mouse offered to climb a tall tree and throw down some delicious fruit for him. 

The mouse began throwing the fruit down, but one of them accidentally hit a nest of red ants. The furious ants rushed out, ready to attack. 

"Who did that? It must be that boar!" they wrongly concluded, and swarmed the passing boar, biting him fiercely. 

"AAAH! OOOOH!" the boar squealed in pain and ran blindly, crashing into a bat hiding in a tree. 

"A bat in my ear! HELP!" an elephant trumpeted, startled by the flying bat. The terrified elephant panicked and knocked down an old woman's hut. 

When the angry woman demanded an explanation, the chain of blame led back from the elephant, to the bat, to the boar, to the ants, and finally to the mouse and the crab. 

"So you are to be blamed for the destruction of my hut," the woman told the crab. "You'll have to give me something to make up for it."

"All right. I'll get you something from my house," the crab promised, and quickly crept under a heavy stone, refusing to come out. 

Finally, the angry woman and the animals lost their patience. "Let's press the rock down on him!" they yelled, pushing the heavy stone down hard before walking away. 

Sometime later, the crab crawled out from under the rock, but he had been crushed as flat as a pancake. And crabs ever since have had flat bodies.

## The Otter and the Wildcat
An otter and a wildcat were friends. One day, the cat offered to steal a chicken from a village hut, asking the otter to keep watch. 

The cat caught a fowl, but it squawked loudly, waking the house's inmates. The terrified otter immediately ran away to save himself. The cat managed to escape his pursuers and caught up with the otter, angry at his cowardice. 

The cat ate the fowl and insultingly threw the bare bones to the otter. "There, that's your share," he sneered. The otter felt deeply hurt but said nothing. 

Some days later, they passed by a pond. The cat, who couldn't swim, asked the otter to dive in and catch some fish for them. 

The otter gladly obliged. He caught and ate several delicious fish, and then, remembering the cat's insult, threw the bare bones to the cat. "There! That's your share," the otter mocked. 

"You rascal! I'll tear you apart!" the furious cat roared. In his blind anger, he forgot he didn't know how to swim. He jumped into the deep water to catch the otter... and was drowned.

## The Bulbul and the Hornbill
The hornbill was once the king of the birds, but he was a cruel ruler who killed smaller birds for the slightest mistakes. The birds decided to choose a new king: the gentle bulbul. 

But how could they dethrone the fierce hornbill? The bulbul had a clever idea and asked the woodpecker for help. 

They challenged the hornbill to a test of worthiness: whoever could break a thick branch by sitting on it would be king. The woodpecker secretly bored almost entirely through a specific thick branch beforehand. 

The hornbill confidently flew up and landed with all his might on a different branch, but it didn't break. 

Then it was the bulbul's turn. He lightly landed on the specially prepared, even thicker branch, and *CRACK!* it broke instantly. 

The hornbill, acknowledging defeat, flew away in shame, and the bulbul became the new king of the birds.

## The Porcupine and the Elephants
A herd of elephants was angry because their favorite bathing pool had become incredibly muddy. A dragonfly discovered that a porcupine had been playing in the water. 

When confronted, the porcupine was completely unrepentant. The furious elephant leader plucked a thick hair from his own head and sent it with the dragonfly to the porcupine, warning, "Tell him he is picking a quarrel with one whose hair is so thick!"

The porcupine laughed. "You call that thick? Here, take one of mine to them." He sent back one of his sharp, thick quills. 

When the elephants saw the massive quill, they gasped in terror. "Is each hair of his so thick? He must be a giant of a creature!" 

Terrified of this "giant," the elephants scrambled out of the pool and ran away forever.

## The Frog and the Monkey
A raja had two servants: a monkey and a frog. One day, he sent them to the forest for fruit and to the stream for fish. 

The lazy monkey ate all the fruit he found and went to sleep. The hardworking frog caught a huge pile of fish. When they met on the way home, the monkey realized he would be punished while the frog would be praised. 

To save himself, the monkey secretly grabbed the frog's catch and threw all the fish back into the stream! 

When they returned empty-handed, the raja was furious. The monkey claimed he had searched the whole jungle, pointing to his dirty, disheveled appearance as "proof" of his hard work. He then pointed at the clean, wet frog. "He must have spent the whole day playing in the water!"

Believing the lie, the angry raja hit the frog so hard on the head that it became flat! The poor, wronged frog left the palace to live in the river forever. And frogs since then have flat heads. 

Later, the treacherous monkey rebelled against the raja. The terrified raja created a thick black paste and covered his face with it, hoping to hide. 

"Where did you get that paste, master?" the monkey asked when he found him. "I would like to put some on my face too."
"My servants put me in the hollow of a large tree and lit a fire around it," the clever raja lied. "The smoke made my face black."

Eager to look beautiful, the monkey and his rebel friends ordered the servants to do the same to them. The servants trapped the monkeys in a hollow tree and set fire to it! Only one female monkey escaped, her face permanently blackened by the smoke. Her descendants are the black-faced langur monkeys we see today.

## The Frog and the Tiger
A frog and a tiger were friends. The tiger frequently invited the frog over and served him meat. Feeling obligated, the frog invited the tiger to his home for dinner. 

"I would be delighted," the tiger said, "but I am a meat-eater."
"As if there's no meat in my house!" the frog boasted proudly. 

But as the day approached, the frog worried. How could he get meat for a tiger? He spotted a horse and bravely tried to bite a chunk of flesh from its leg. The horse simply kicked him away with a massive *THUMP*, breaking both the frog's legs! 

The injured frog dragged himself home. When the tiger arrived, the frog hid in the loft. 

"Welcome, my friend!" the frog called down. 
"What are you doing up there?" the tiger asked. 

The tiger watched in horror as the frog began painfully biting his own injured legs. "What are you doing? Why are you biting off flesh from your leg?"
"I... uh... I had no meat to offer you... so I wanted to give you my own flesh," the frog lied, deeply ashamed. 

"Do you think I would have eaten it? Why did you do such a thing?" the disgusted tiger scolded him before leaving. 

The frog felt so embarrassed and humiliated that he hopped to the river and jumped in. "I could never show my face to the tiger again. I shall stay in the water for the rest of my life!" 

And frogs to this day live in the water.
