# Rama Avatar

Ravana, the arrogant rakshasa king of the island of Lanka, had performed severe penances and had secured boons which made him all but immortal. Perturbed by the atrocities committed by the rakshasa, the devas approached Lord Vishnu for protection. 
"He now plans to invade our very regions," Indra worried. 
Vishnu reassured them. "Do not worry. To slay him I shall manifest myself on earth as the four sons of Dasharatha - wholly in Rama, the eldest, and partially in his brothers."

Accordingly, four sons were born to the three queens of Dasharatha, the heirless king of Ayodhya. Rama, the eldest, to Kausalya; Bharata, to Kaikeyi; and Laxmana and Shatrughna, to Sumitra. 
"Rama will make an ideal king. He is virtuous and valiant," King Dasharatha thought proudly. 

But, a few years later, instead of crowning him Yuvaraja (crown prince), a heartbroken Dasharatha, bound by a promise to Kaikeyi, was compelled to banish Rama to the forest for fourteen years. Sita, Rama's young wife, and Laxmana insisted on going with him. 
"Forgive me, Rama! Forgive me, Sita," Dasharatha wept as they left. 

While they were in the forest, a demoness approached them. 
"Noble sir, I, Shoorpanakha, King Ravana's sister, wish to make you my own," she told Rama. 
Though amused, Rama kept a straight face. "But I am not free. I belong to Sita."
His words transformed Shoorpanakha's lust into fury. She charged menacingly towards Sita. But Laxmana was too quick for her. He sprang forward and hacked off her nose and ears with his sword. 

A tearful, disfigured Shoorpanakha stood before Ravana in Lanka. 
"Since their love for Sita made them do this to you, I shall punish them by abducting her for myself," Ravana promised. 
Ravana took the help of his uncle Mareecha. "I will not risk an open encounter with the brothers. Transform yourself into a golden deer and lure them away. In their absence I will carry Sita away."

Ravana's ruse worked. As Mareecha lured Rama and Laxmana away, Ravana kidnapped Sita. 
"O Lord! Save me!" Sita cried out as Ravana's chariot flew across the sky. 

In the days that followed, while Rama and Laxmana wandered in the forest not knowing where to look for Sita, they won the affection of Sugreeva, the king of the monkeys. 
"We shall help you find Sita, wherever she may be," Sugreeva promised. 
Hanuman, his great minister, went on the errand and came back with the news that Sita was captive at Lanka. 
"Poor Sita! Let us march to the sea-shore with your forces, Sugreeva," Hanuman urged. 

At the seashore, Rama wondered, "How shall we cross this mighty ocean?"
"The Lord of the Ocean will help us," Laxmana said. 
Rama waited for three days on the shore without having any food or water. At last, Varuna appeared and immediately knew him to be Vishnu. 
"O Lord, pardon my ignorance. You can march across my domain at will but... enhance your own fame by building a bridge across me for which posterity will sing your glory."

Assisted by the monkeys and other creatures of the forest, a bridge was soon laid by Rama and Laxmana, across the seas to the shore of Lanka. 
As Ravana saw the two brothers and their hordes approach, he sent for his son, his brother and his foremost warriors. 
"Go! Meet the invading army and defeat it!" Ravana ordered. 

But Rama, Sugreeva, Laxmana, Hanuman and their wild hordes effortlessly destroyed Ravana's huge army of elephants, cavalry, infantry and chariots, in a single encounter!
Ravana was furious. He rushed towards Rama in his chariot. 
But Rama stood ready. "O vile rakshasa, reap today the fruits of your villainy."

And with one twang of his mighty bow... his arrow pierced Ravana's chest. Vishnu, as Rama, had completed the divine mission for which he had taken his birth on earth.
