# Vasantasena

Palaka, the King of Ujjaini, was a ruthless and unpopular tyrant who had only come to the throne because his brother Gopala had abdicated in his favour. Most of his subjects hated him, especially because he had given a free hand to his wicked brother-in-law, Samsthanaka.

"Palaka is becoming more ruthless," whispered Sharvilaka, a brave young man and a leader of the opposition. "But be patient. It is prophesied that Aryaka, Gopala's son, will soon become King. We must see that the prophecy comes true."

In that same bustling town lived Vasantasena, a breathtakingly beautiful and wealthy dancer. She was pursued by many, including the evil Samsthanaka, but her heart belonged to a noble but impoverished merchant named Charudatta. Charudatta was once a very rich man with many friends, but his generosity had left him penniless, with only his loyal friend Maitreya remaining by his side.

One evening, as Vasantasena was returning home from the temple of Kamadeva, she was suddenly accosted by the wicked Samsthanaka. "Hey! Wait! Now where did she vanish?" he roared as Vasantasena fled in terror. 

Spotting the open gate of Charudatta's humble house, she darted inside, finding refuge. When she explained her plight to Charudatta, he kindly offered her shelter. Not wanting to carry her heavy gold jewels home through the dangerous streets, she requested, "Sir, a certain scoundrel chased me for my jewels. Will you keep them safe for me?" 

Charudatta agreed and entrusted the jewels to Maitreya, who then escorted Vasantasena safely home. 

Vasantasena had a faithful and clever maid named Madanika. Madanika was deeply in love with the rebel leader, Sharvilaka. Sharvilaka loved her too, but as a poor man, he had no money to buy her freedom from her mistress. 

Desperate, Sharvilaka decided to resort to thievery. That very night, he crept into Charudatta's garden. Finding the walls old and weak, he easily broke a hole through the bricks and entered the room where Charudatta and Maitreya were sleeping. 

Maitreya, tossing in his sleep and dreaming of thieves, mumbled, "Here, take these jewels!" He unknowingly thrust Vasantasena's gold ornaments right into Sharvilaka's hands! "The thought of being able to buy Madanika's freedom stilled his uneasy conscience," Sharvilaka thought as he grabbed the jewels and fled.

The next morning, the theft was discovered. Charudatta was devastated, not for the wealth, but for his honour. Charudatta's devoted wife, seeing her husband's distress, quietly gave her own invaluable pearl necklace—a gift from her mother—to Maitreya. "Go and give this necklace to Vasantasena in place of her stolen jewels," she instructed. 

Meanwhile, Sharvilaka proudly presented the stolen gold to Madanika, hoping to buy her freedom. But Madanika instantly recognized her mistress's ornaments. Horrified, she warned him of the danger. "Pretend that Charudatta has sent you to my mistress to return the jewels," she advised quickly. 

Sharvilaka did exactly as she said. Vasantasena, who had secretly overheard their conversation, saw through the ruse but admired their true love. Smiling, she gracefully accepted the jewels and then generously declared, "Go, Madanika. You are a free woman. Heaven bless you." 

That evening, Vasantasena visited Charudatta's house. There, she saw his young son crying. "He wants a golden cart to play with," the maid explained sadly. Deeply moved by Charudatta's poverty and nobility, Vasantasena took off her remaining gold ornaments and filled the child's little clay cart with them. 

Just then, Charudatta returned, accompanied by a heavy storm. He confessed that Maitreya was bringing a pearl necklace to replace her 'lost' jewels. Touched by his absolute integrity, Vasantasena revealed that she had already recovered her jewels. The two lovers finally confessed their feelings, and as the storm raged outside, Vasantasena spent the night safely in Charudatta's home. 

The next morning, Charudatta left early for the public park, leaving his carriage behind for Vasantasena to follow. When Vasantasena emerged, she saw a carriage waiting and hurriedly got in. 

To her absolute horror, it was not Charudatta's carriage, but the carriage of the evil Samsthanaka! 

"Vasantasena! You! So you did come to me," Samsthanaka gloated as the carriage rolled into a secluded grove. 

"Let me go, you evil one! I seek only the noble Charudatta," she spat. 

Furious at being rejected for a poor merchant, Samsthanaka dragged her out of the carriage. In a fit of violent rage, he strangled her and threw her limp body under a pile of dry leaves, leaving her for dead. 

Meanwhile, back at the town, the prophecy was unfolding. Prince Aryaka had escaped from King Palaka's prison! Running through the streets with his shackles still on, he spotted Charudatta's empty carriage waiting for Vasantasena and jumped in to hide. 

The driver, mistaking the clinking of Aryaka's chains for Vasantasena's anklets, drove off to the park. When the carriage arrived, Charudatta stepped forward to welcome his love, only to find the escaped Prince Aryaka! 

Realizing the Prince's danger, the noble Charudatta immediately removed Aryaka's chains and offered him the carriage to escape. "Use my carriage till you reach your friends and safety," Charudatta offered bravely, knowingly risking his own life for the rightful heir. 

Hours later, a wandering Buddhist monk passing through the grove stumbled upon the pile of leaves. To his shock, he found Vasantasena underneath, barely alive! He quickly carried her to his monastery to nurse her back to health. 

Back in the city, the cowardly Samsthanaka panicked. "To save my skin, I'd better go to the court and present a charge, accusing Charudatta of the murder!" he plotted. 

He rushed to the King's court and formally accused Charudatta, claiming the impoverished merchant had murdered the wealthy dancer for her jewels. Charudatta was summoned. To make matters worse, Maitreya arrived at the court to defend his friend, and in a scuffle with Samsthanaka, Vasantasena's jewels—the very ones she had left in the clay cart—spilled out of Maitreya's robes!

"The woman's jewels! The motive for the murder! There is no doubt about the guilt of the accused!" the judge declared, shocked.

King Palaka swiftly sentenced the innocent Charudatta to death. He was led through the streets towards the execution grounds, accompanied by a mourning crowd. 

Just as the executioner raised his sword, a cry rang out. "Stop! Stop! I am alive! Charudatta must not be executed! He is innocent!" 

It was Vasantasena, fully recovered, rushing through the crowd with the monk! Charudatta could not believe his eyes. Vasantasena turned to the stunned crowd and pointed furiously at Samsthanaka. "Hear, all of you! It was the evil Samsthanaka who tried to murder me!"

The angry mob instantly turned on Samsthanaka, ready to tear him apart. But the ever-merciful Charudatta intervened. "No, I beg you, please spare his life. The punishment he needs is kindness."

Suddenly, a loud cheer erupted from the back of the crowd. Sharvilaka, the rebel leader, pushed his way through. "I have slain Palaka! Aryaka is now our King!" he announced triumphantly. 

He turned to the overjoyed Charudatta. "King Aryaka has named you Viceroy of Kushavati! He has also conferred upon noble Vasantasena the official status of 'second wife to Charudatta'."

Charudatta lovingly led Vasantasena back to his home, where his first wife welcomed the talented dancer with open arms. After enduring so much suffering, theft, false accusations, and near-death, the noble merchant, his loyal friends, and his beloved Vasantasena were finally united in peace and prosperity.
