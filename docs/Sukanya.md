# Sukanya

Long, long ago, there lived a deeply devoted sage named Chyavana. He performed his penances and austerities, meditating near a serene lake in a dense jungle on the outskirts of King Sharyaati's kingdom. 

Days turned into months, and months into years. Chyavana continued to meditate, sitting perfectly still. Eventually, he was completely covered by an anthill. Being deeply immersed in meditation, he was not aware of anything happening around him. 

King Sharyaati had many beautiful daughters, but he loved Princess Sukanya the most. "Sukanya shall marry only the most handsome, accomplished prince in the land," the King would proudly declare. 

One day, the King and his royal entourage, including the beautiful princesses, visited the lake in the forest. While the others rested, Sukanya happily wandered off to her favorite grove. 

Suddenly, something in the distance attracted her attention. "What is that? A heap of mud?" she wondered, walking closer to the large anthill covering Sage Chyavana. 

Peering closely, she saw two bright sparks glowing inside the dark holes of the anthill. "Could they be fireflies?" she thought playfully. "I'll drive them out and catch them. The others would be delighted to see them."

Picking up two sharp twigs, the innocent princess poked firmly at the two bright objects. 

Suddenly from the anthill came a terrible cry of pain: "E... E... EAAAH!"

Sukanya was terrified. She dropped the twigs, ran for her life, and did not stop until she reached her father. 

At that exact moment, a strange and terrible sight met her eyes. The King's mighty guards and attendants were suddenly writhing on the ground in agonizing pain. 

"Could this have anything to do with the strange sound that came from the anthill?" Sukanya wondered, trembling. 

The King was perplexed by this sudden scourge that had hit all his men. "Could this be a curse? I wonder... perhaps," he deduced. He immediately commanded his minister, "We were near the hermitage of Sage Chyavana. Has anyone offended the great sage? Find out immediately."

Hearing this, Sukanya stepped forward, tears streaming down her face. "Father, I have to tell you something. Near the lake, I saw an anthill. Embedded in it were two bright objects which looked like fireflies. I... I was curious. I poked at them with twigs. Suddenly from the anthill came a cry of pain."

The King was horrified. "I'll go to the lake and see," he said, rushing to the anthill. 

When the King saw the old sage with portions of the anthill still sticking to his body, bleeding from his blinded eyes, he fell to his knees. "Pardon me! O great one, pardon me! I am King Sharyaati. My daughter mistook your eyes for fireflies and blinded you."

"Pray forgive us. Please have mercy on us and lift your curse!" the King pleaded. 

"I have not cursed anybody," the blind sage replied sternly. "You are reaping the fruits of your daughter's actions. She has disturbed my meditation and blinded me."

"How can we make amends for this great sin? Command me! Anything! Anything!" the King offered desperately.

"I will need someone to look after me, blinded and helpless as I am," Sage Chyavana stated. "I will not have servants. I want your daughter to serve me. Think about it and let me know."

King Sharyaati returned to the palace, his heart heavy. "How can I send my favourite daughter to serve this old sage? But would it be fair to allow my subjects to suffer for the sins of my daughter?" he agonized. 

Hearing her father's distress, Sukanya made a brave and selfless decision. She walked into the royal court and took a solemn oath before everyone. "In the name of Goddess Durga, I vow that I shall marry only Sage Chyavana. If my words be true, let all be relieved of the scourge."

Instantly, the agonizing pain left the King's men. "A miracle! We are cured!" they rejoiced. 

The next day, King Sharyaati, Sukanya, and the minister proceeded to the lake. The King offered rich robes and jewels to the sage, but Chyavana refused them all. "Take away all my jewels and rich robes," Sukanya told her father gently. "I should wear something that befits my status as a hermit's wife."

Leaving Sukanya to the care of the blind sage, the King returned to his palace with a heavy heart. 

Sukanya completely dedicated herself to the service of her husband. She picked flowers for his rituals, helped him with his yagnas, fed him, washed his hands, and only slept when she was sure he was deep in slumber. "I never thought that there would be so much pleasure in serving another," she realized happily. 

And so the years passed. One day, the Ashwini twins, the handsome celestial physicians to the gods, happened to pass by the lake. They saw Sukanya, still young and beautiful, returning from a bath. 

"Fair maiden, tell us who you are. Are you a goddess?" they asked. "Surely your father must be a cruel man to have given you in marriage to a blind, old sage. Marry one of us and be happy!"

"I am Sukanya, the chaste wife of Sage Chyavana," she replied firmly, offended by their proposal. "How dare you speak these words to a chaste woman! Gods you claim to be. Go away from here before I curse you!"

"Pray, wait!" the twins quickly apologized. "As a reward for your loyalty, we are eager to do you a good turn. We shall restore your husband's vision... and give him back his youth, too."

Sukanya's eyes lit up with hope. "But...?" she asked, sensing a catch. 

"...After giving him back his vision and youth, we shall make him look exactly like us. And you must spot him. If you fail, you must agree to this test," the twins challenged. 

Sukanya rushed back to the ashram and told her husband. "Advise me, my lord. Shall I submit myself to this test of the gods?"

"Sukanya! You have all my blessings," Sage Chyavana agreed immediately. "Lead me to the place."

The blind sage and the two golden gods waded into the lake and immersed themselves in the water. "Wait till we come up again," they instructed her. 

As soon as they disappeared under the water, Sukanya prayed fervently. "O Divine Mother, help me." 

The Goddess answered her prayer in a vision. "Sukanya, I am pleased with your devotion. Look carefully. Only the eyes of mortals blink."

After a while, the three men came up again. They stepped onto the bank, looking absolutely identical—three handsome, radiant, youthful men. 

"Can't you spot me?" they challenged in unison. 

Sukanya looked carefully at them for a while. Remembering the Goddess's words, she watched their faces closely. Suddenly, she saw that the eyes of the man standing in the centre blinked!

"This is my husband," she smiled, confidently taking Chyavana's hand. 

Her guess was right. The Ashwini twins were thoroughly impressed. "Sukanya, you, a chaste woman, have humbled the very gods! Your glory shall live for ever." With a respectful bow, the twins departed to the heavens.

Later, at the ashram, a youthful, seeing Chyavana smiled at his devoted wife. "I have everything a wife could wish for," he said lovingly. "Is there nothing left that I could do for my beloved wife?"

"There is something, my lord," Sukanya admitted softly. "I long to see my father. How happy he would be to see us now!"

"He is already on his way!" Chyavana smiled, using his foresight. 

When King Sharyaati arrived and saw his daughter glowing with happiness next to a handsome, youthful sage, he was overwhelmed with joy and pride. 

"Sukanya, you have made me proud! Nay! You have made all womankind proud!!" the King declared. "As long as the sun and the moon exist, let her be known and revered as *Sati Sukanya*—the perfectly chaste wife."
