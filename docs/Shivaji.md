# Shivaji

It was the 19th day of February in the year 1630. The sun was about to set when the heavy drums in the fortress of Shivneri, in Maharashtra, began to beat triumphantly. They proclaimed the joyous news that a son was born to Jijabai. 

Her husband, the powerful nobleman Shahaji, was far away, fighting battles for the Sultan of Bijapur. For hundreds of years, the Marathas had lived under the suppression of foreign rulers. At the time of Shivaji's birth, the region was dominated by the Sultan, the great Mughal Emperor of Delhi, and the coastal king, Siddhi Johar.

Inside the fortress, the women gathered around the newborn. "Glowing eyes and a beak-like nose!" one marvelled.
"We shall name him after the local goddess, Shivai," Jijabai decided fondly. 

When Shiva grew to be a little boy, Shahaji took him to Bijapur and placed him under the guidance of a great teacher, Dadaji Kond-deo. 
"From now on, he will be your guardian," Shahaji told his son.
"May God bless you, my boy! May you rise to great heights of glory!" Dadaji blessed him.

Dadaji taught him how to read, write, and shoot. "Well hit this time!" Dadaji would praise him during archery practice. He also taught the young boy to ride horses like a true warrior. 

Shiva had a sharp and inquisitive mind. "Sir, why does my father stay away from us?" he asked his guardian one day.
"You know he is a great warrior. He is always on the battlefield."
"Fighting other people's battles? Why has he to do that? Why?" the young boy pondered thoughtfully.

His mother, Jijabai, greatly influenced his growing mind. She told him inspiring stories from the great epics, the Ramayana and the Mahabharata. "And Lord Krishna told Arjuna—even death in the cause of one's duty should be dear to a hero's heart," she taught him.
"Mother, what is my duty?" Shiva asked earnestly. "To fight for a foreign king by the side of my father? Or to fight for my people against the king?"
"Your duty lies in fighting for your people," Jijabai answered firmly.
"Against the Sultan?"
"Yes, if necessary!"

"I must tell him what I think is right!" Shiva decided.
Jijabai saw that her son was troubled. "Your father loves freedom no less than any other person," she comforted him. "But he has been in the Sultan's service for so long that he hasn't the heart to go against him."

***

One day, Dadaji announced, "Dadaji, I made the Sultan appoint you the collector of Poona! You shall take Shiva and his mother with you."
As Shiva travelled through the Sahyadri mountains towards Poona, he looked out at the majestic landscape. *This beautiful land is ours!* he thought fiercely. *These great forts should rightly belong to us!*

In Poona, Shiva made friends with the simple, sturdy boys of the surrounding mountains. They loved to stage mock battles and train together. "We swear," they pledged to one another, "we shall not rest till we have won our freedom."

Though appointed to collect revenue, Dadaji became the virtual ruler of Poona. Right under his nose, young Shiva made bold plans with his friends. 
"We'll start capturing forts," Shiva declared.
"There will be no resistance. There are many officers on our side!" his friends agreed. 
When Dadaji found out, he was proud but cautious. *I will not come in their way,* he decided silently.

The first important fort Shivaji conquered was Torna. As his men celebrated the victory, Shivaji held up a hand. "No, my men! It is not yet time to make merry. The struggle has just begun."

Jijabai proudly blessed his efforts. "Congratulations, Shiva! They tell me you captured a fort today."
"It was easy, mother! The Sultan's defences were poor and the officers sided with us!"

The news of Shivaji's daring adventures deeply annoyed the Sultan of Bijapur. 
Soon, shocking news arrived at Poona. "They say the Sultan has arrested your father—to teach you a lesson."
"Afzul Khan, the wicked commander, put handcuffs on Shahaji's hands! And the Sultan has sent an army to attack us!"
"My father a prisoner!" Shivaji raged. "It's the Sultan who needs to be taught a lesson!"

When the Sultan's army approached, Shivaji was stationed at Fort Purandar. "Go and meet the enemy," he commanded his generals. 
Some of the enemy troops had encamped in a small fortress nearby. Shivaji's forces decisively attacked and defeated them. And when the main enemy force tried to attack the fort at Purandar, it was driven away with every means at hand.

The Sultan had learnt his lesson. Defeated, he ordered, "Release Shahaji! Treat him with honour!"

But vengeance was in the air. Soon, Afzul Khan, Bijapur's mighty soldier, started with a massive army against Shivaji. "I will catch that mountain rat—dead or alive!" the giant Khan boasted.

Afzul Khan swooped on towns and villages, deliberately spreading terror and ruin to draw Shivaji out. "Goddess Bhavani! Shiva's favourite temple! Pull it down!" he ordered his men. His army then camped in the mountainous district of Wai, plundering the surrounding villages. 

In the neighbourhood of Wai was Pratapgadh, where Shivaji was stationed.
"Afzul Khan! You mean the man who made my father a prisoner?" Shivaji's eyes flashed.
"Yes! The very man, Maharaj!"

Soon, an arrogant envoy from Afzul Khan arrived. "Surrender or die—eh?" the envoy sneered.
Shivaji acted tactfully, hiding his anger. "The Khan is like a father to me. Why does he frighten me with his might?"
"Will you come to his camp?"
"No, sir! Let him come to Pratapgadh. I'll step down the fort halfway to meet him."

Meanwhile, Shivaji's spies brought him disturbing news. "You say the Khan wants to kill me?" Shivaji nodded grimly. "I will be prepared."
Shivaji wore a chainmail armour hidden inside his dress and slipped a deadly, concealed weapon called 'tiger claws' (Bagh nakh) onto his fingers. He prayed to Goddess Bhavani before stepping out.

The great Khan arrived in his palanquin, but brought an army with him despite the agreement. "Don't bring your army, said the rat! Coward!" the Khan laughed to himself.

Shivaji went down to meet the Khan with only two guards. 
"Your end is near!" the Khan thought as he saw Shivaji approaching. "Come, my son," he said aloud, opening his massive arms.

But as the giant Khan embraced Shivaji with a great show of love, he suddenly pulled a dagger and lunged. *He's trying to stab me!* Shivaji realized. 
Shivaji quickly seized the Khan's knife arm with his left hand, and raised his right hand. With a swift, deadly motion, the iron tiger claws on Shivaji's fingers pierced deep into the Khan's stomach. 

"Ya Allah!" the Khan screamed. As the mortally wounded Khan staggered out, he collapsed. "The Khan is dead! Long live Shivaji Maharaj!" Shivaji's guards shouted.

Suddenly, out of the dense jungle that surrounded the fort, wave after wave of hidden Maratha soldiers poured out, raising their terrifying war cry: "Har Har Mahadeo!"
The fierce battle that followed lasted till sunset. The Bijapur army, leaderless and panicked, was routed. "They are running away! Victory is ours!"

There was jubilation all around. 
"What do we do with the Khan's head?" a soldier asked.
"Bury it along with his body with full honours," Shivaji commanded respectfully.
"What a noble man! He doesn't wish to dishonour a fallen enemy," his men marvelled.

***

"That was a near escape, my dear son. The battle has ended, hasn't it?" Jijabai asked when he returned.
"Yes, mother. And much wealth too has fallen in our hands."

It was Shivaji's hour of victory, and he took full advantage of it. "We shall undertake an immediate campaign."
It was a brilliant whirlwind campaign. In thirteen days, he rode over a thousand miles, everywhere defeating the enemy and rapidly enlarging his kingdom. Finally, he took Panhala, a strategic fort of tremendous value. 

But as they secured Panhala, his commander Baji Prabhu pointed to the horizon. "A sea of soldiers heading towards us."
"Maharaj, it is Siddhi Johar, our enemy from the sea-coast," another warned. "And the sea-faring Englishmen are helping him!"

The Siddhi's massive troops completely surrounded Panhala. All exits were blocked. The siege went on for agonizing months, but the Marathas defended the fort bravely. 
"It will rain any moment now!" Shivaji hoped, looking at the dark clouds. The monsoon broke with full fury, making conditions miserable for the besiegers.
"How long can we go on like this?" Baji asked.
"I must be tactful now," Shivaji decided. 

He had his men spread rumors. In the evening, the enemy soldiers heard: "Shivaji is thinking of giving up the fort!" 
"Good!" Siddhi Johar laughed. "After this we will turn to Vishalgadh!"
But that night, through the heavy rain and thunder, Shivaji quietly slipped out of the fort with a thousand select soldiers, making his way to Vishalgadh.

When the enemy realized he was gone, they gave chase. 
As Shivaji's men reached a narrow, highly defensible mountain pass, they heard the enemy horses. "Are they on the alert? Yes sir, we are being chased!"
"A narrow pass! But once we are through, we reach Vishalgadh!" Shivaji yelled.
Baji Prabhu drew his swords. "Go ahead, master. We will remain here to defend the pass and hold them off."
Shivaji touched his shoulder. "You are a great soldier, Baji!"

With hardly six hundred Marathas under him, Baji fought the massive enemy force fiercely to buy Shivaji time. Despite fighting bravely, Baji was fatally wounded. As he lay dying on the battlefield, he heard a distant sound.
"The cannon booms from a distance! That means the master has safely reached Vishalgadh!" Baji smiled. "I may now die in peace!"

At Panhala, Siddhi Johar's anger knew no bounds when he realized he had been duped. "And see what the Sultan writes! He says I deliberately let Shivaji escape."
Though the Siddhi did not know it, there were very few guards left at Panhala. Shivaji did not want them to die unnecessarily against an overwhelming force, so he had ordered the fort to be handed over. 
"An empty fort! What can I do with it?" the Siddhi roared in frustration.

***

By the time Shivaji returned from Vishalgadh, a still more powerful enemy was occupying his beloved Poona—Shayista Khan, the uncle of the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb of Delhi.
"Shayista Khan is residing in Lal Mahal, where our beloved Shivaji lived as a boy!" the locals whispered bitterly. "How we wish that our Shivaji were here!"

Shayista Khan was paranoid and had posted heavy armed guards on the outskirts of the city. One night, a loud, musical procession approached the gates.
"Oh! A marriage procession? Let it pass!" the guards waved them through.
But as soon as the 'marriage procession' reached the gates of Lal Mahal, Shivaji drew his sword. "Attack!" 

Shivaji and his men stormed the palace. Room after room was searched. 
"There goes the Khan!" 
"Heavens! It's Shivaji!" Shayista Khan screamed in terror as he tried to jump out of a window. Shivaji's sword slashed down, and Shayista Khan escaped into the night—but minus three of his fingers!

The humiliating incident shook Shayista Khan terribly. "That Shivaji—comes and goes like a ghost!" he muttered, hastily retreating from Poona.

Shivaji was left in peace for some time, but his kingdom needed resources. "We can have no peace till complete freedom is ours!" he told his ministers. "What we need is money, Maharaj. Wars are costly."
"My spies tell me, there is money in Surat. The Englishmen who are looting our country have amassed great wealth there. It is also Aurangzeb's prestige port."
"To Surat we go!" Shivaji declared.

Shivaji's attack on Surat was as swift as lightning. For four days, he methodically sacked the wealthy city. 
"Don't rob the poor!" he strictly ordered his men. "And above all, don't harass the women!"
Having secured immense wealth to fund his armies, they returned as quickly as they had come. 

Emperor Aurangzeb in Delhi was alarmed. "He must be crushed! Raja Jai Singh, you are our most powerful noble. Go and defeat Shivaji."

Raja Jai Singh moved south with his new, powerful Italian-trained artillery. He systematically bombarded and took fort after fort. 
"This is the heroic Fort Purandar... Attack!" Jai Singh ordered. The bombardment was devastating, till Shivaji finally had to make temporary peace with Jai Singh to save his people.

"Shivaji, you are a brave man," Jai Singh admitted during their peace talks.
"Sir, you are braver. But you've sold your soul to the Mughal," Shivaji replied bluntly.
"The emperor is in Agra," Jai Singh offered. "He cordially invites you to meet him. Bring along your young son Sambhaji too!"
"What guarantee is there of my safety?"
"Leave that to me. I look upon you as my son! And the emperor means well."

Shivaji's ministers were deeply worried. "We don't like this, Maharaj. How could you rely on Jai Singh's word?"
"We have no choice now," Shivaji sighed. "We are in a weak position. We cannot refuse."

For two months, Shivaji and his son traveled to Agra. Wherever they went, people looked on in wonder. "There he is! The great Shivaji. Hope they've not set a trap for him there!"

At last, they reached Agra. "Welcome to Agra! This is your guest house," Jai Singh's son, Ram Singh, greeted them. 
"Why the heavy guard?" Shivaji asked suspiciously.
"In your honour and for your safety, sir!"

The next day, father and son left for the grand imperial durbar with mixed feelings. 
In the opulent palace, the great Mughal emperor deliberately did not so much as notice them. Instead of a seat of honour, they were rudely asked to stand at the back with a group of low-ranking officers. 

"Did you notice that, father?" young Sambhaji whispered. "The emperor has honoured others with presents. He is ignoring us!"
Shivaji flushed with intense anger. "This is a deliberate insult! He is honouring the men whom I defeated!"

He rushed out of the durbar in a rage. When an official tried to calm him, Shivaji snapped, "Not a word!"
Shivaji's fiery reaction surprised the arrogant emperor. "Strange man! Call him tomorrow. I'll honour him and his son too."
But it was too late. "Tell the emperor, I have a fever. I can't come," Shivaji sent word. 
So young Sambhaji went alone to the court and received robes of honour and a sword. 
"You didn't make a proper bow, my lad," the emperor noted.
"I only bow before God and my mother, sir!" the fearless boy replied.

Shivaji doubted the emperor's sincerity completely. "I know now that this is a trap. The emperor wants to lock us in."
"Will he kill us, father?" Sambhaji asked.
Shivaji's feigned illness gave him time to plan a daring escape. "We'll send out large baskets of sweets every day. Tell the guards they are to be given to the poor for my recovery!"

For weeks, the heavy baskets were carried out daily. The guards diligently checked them at first. "Checked. Let it pass!" 
But soon, they grew bored. "We need not check these. They are the usual baskets of sweets." The watchmen eventually did not even glance at the baskets.

One morning, the baskets contained something else entirely. "Get into it, my son! It's quite roomy," Shivaji whispered, climbing into a second basket himself. The porters smoothly carried them right past the oblivious guards.

Later that day, an officer checked the guest house. "Have you seen Shivaji and Sambhaji?"
"I saw them a moment ago. They are fast asleep," a guard replied.
The officer checked the beds. "My God! There's nobody there! Just pillows!"

Aurangzeb flared up in terrible anger when he heard the news. "You have allowed Shivaji to escape! You shall be beheaded for this!"
Horsemen were desperately dispatched in all directions, but Shivaji was long gone.

Meanwhile, in a lonely spot outside the city, loyal Marathas waited. "Horses in readiness for you, Maharaj!"
"I thank you!" 
Changing his disguise every now and then, Shivaji managed the long, dangerous journey to the south. Everywhere along the way, common people secretly helped him. To keep his son safe, he left him at a trusted hermitage. "Sambhaji will stay with you for a while."
"I am honoured, Maharaj!" the priest bowed.

Finally, Shivaji reached home safely. "Our beloved Shivaji has come back! Safe and sound!" his mother wept with joy. "Glory be to God!"

A few years after his daring escape from Agra, having rebuilt and strengthened his forces, Shivaji was finally crowned king at a grand, historic ceremony at Raigadh. 
"My son, this is the day I've been waiting for!" Jijabai said, her eyes shining with pride.
"A people's king!" the crowds cheered. "As long as freedom is cherished, his name will shine and inspire millions."

As a crowned king, he ruled only for five years before his death, but the mighty Maratha power which he had painstakingly built, flourished and protected the land for many years after him.
