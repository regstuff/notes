# Manonmani

Long ago, the Pandya kingdom was ruled by King Jeevakan, a weak but well-meaning monarch. Guided by the spiritual wisdom of his trusted advisor, Sage Sundara, the king managed to rule fairly, and his subjects lived in peace. He had only one child, the beautiful and virtuous Princess Manonmani. 

However, beneath the surface of the peaceful kingdom, ambition was festering. The king’s chief minister, Kudilan, was a deeply cunning and treacherous man. Kudilan harbored a secret desire to usurp the throne by marrying his arrogant son, Baladevan, to Princess Manonmani. "If my plans have to succeed, I must get that interfering, old sage out of the way," Kudilan plotted.

His opportunity arose when Sage Sundara departed on a long pilgrimage to the holy river Ganga. Before leaving, the sage blessed the princess, wishing her marriage to a virtuous and valiant man. But with the wise sage gone, Kudilan quickly gained total influence over the gullible King Jeevakan. 

"The king is old and has no son," Kudilan told Baladevan. "The man who marries the princess will be the next king. To begin with, I must get the king to move the capital from Madurai to Tirunelveli, my stronghold."

By threatening to resign and preying on the king's insecurities, Kudilan successfully manipulated Jeevakan into shifting the royal capital to Tirunelveli, much to the dismay of the people and Princess Manonmani. 

When Sage Sundara returned from his pilgrimage, he went to the old capital of Madurai, only to find the palace deserted. "It is Kudilan’s work," the sage realized grimly. "He seems to have some strange power over the king. I must find a way to release the weak king from the clutches of that wicked minister."

The sage traveled to Tirunelveli. To prevent the sage from staying at the palace and regaining his influence, Kudilan cleverly suggested that a true ascetic should live in seclusion. The king agreed, building a small hermitage for the sage outside the city. 

From his hermitage, Sage Sundara advised the king to arrange Manonmani's marriage to Purushothaman, the noble and valiant King of the neighboring Chera kingdom. The sage suggested sending an ambassador named Natarajan with the proposal. 

Terrified of this alliance, Kudilan intercepted Natarajan and sent him away on a false errand. He then lied to King Jeevakan, claiming Natarajan was unavailable, and volunteered his own son, Baladevan, to act as the messenger to the Chera King instead.

Meanwhile, a mystical connection had blossomed. Princess Manonmani confided to her friend that she had been visited in a dream by a handsome prince who garlanded her. "I don't know who he is, but I will marry him and no one else," she declared. Miles away, the Chera King, Purushothaman, woke from a similar dream, his heart stolen by a vision of a beautiful maiden.

When Baladevan arrived at the Chera court, he deliberately sabotaged the peace. Instead of respectfully proposing marriage, he insolently demanded unpaid tributes from King Purushothaman. "If you don't want to pay the tribute, marry our princess," Baladevan sneered.

Outraged by the insult, King Purushothaman declared war on the Pandya kingdom. Baladevan returned to Tirunelveli and falsely reported to King Jeevakan that the Chera king had aggressively rejected the marriage proposal with a call for war. 

Deceived by his minister, King Jeevakan led his army into battle against the Chera forces. The old king fought bravely on the back of his royal elephant, earning the admiration of King Purushothaman himself. 

Seeing an opportunity to eliminate the king, Baladevan treacherously split the Pandya army, leaving the king vulnerable. At that crucial moment, a stray arrow struck King Jeevakan. Gravely wounded, the king slumped onto his elephant. 

Sensing victory, Kudilan followed the elephant as it carried the bleeding king back toward the safety of the Tirunelveli fort. The moment the elephant entered the gates, Kudilan ordered the heavy doors shut, trapping the rest of the Pandya army outside to be slaughtered. 

Inside the fort, the loyal elephant carried its master to Sage Sundara's hermitage. Under the sage's expert care, King Jeevakan regained consciousness. "How bitter is the taste of defeat," the broken king wept. 

Seizing the moment of despair, Kudilan approached the king. "Your majesty, why don't you marry her to my son, Baladevan?" he urged. 
Believing Kudilan to be his only remaining loyal friend, Jeevakan told his daughter, "Manonmani, I want you to marry Baladevan."
"Oh, no!" Manonmani cried. "I would rather fall into the hands of the enemy than marry him!"

Realizing the fort was about to fall, Sage Sundara revealed a secret escape tunnel he had dug in anticipation of such a crisis. "It will take you out of the city. Escape now. Collect your men and fight later," the sage advised.

But Kudilan’s treachery knew no bounds. Pretending to scout ahead, he slipped through the tunnel and went straight to the enemy camp. Presenting himself to the Chera King, Kudilan offered a vile deal. "I am Kudilan, once Jeevakan's minister, now your admirer and slave. Kill Jeevakan and make me the king. I'll be your vassal all your life."

Disgusted by the betrayal, King Purushothaman spat, "You treacherous rat!"

Back at the hermitage, King Jeevakan, still blind to the betrayal, insisted the sage prepare the wedding ceremony for Manonmani and Baladevan. Sage Sundara calmly agreed, secretly noting, "If my calculations are correct, Baladevan will not be the groom."

Suddenly, the doors burst open. It was King Purushothaman, holding Kudilan and Baladevan at sword point. 
"There he is! That's Jeevakan. Kill him!" Baladevan shouted desperately. 

But King Purushothaman turned his sword on the traitors. "You worm! You traitor!" the Chera king roared. "Did you believe that I would kill the valiant king? It's you who shall die!" 
Moved by King Jeevakan's nobility, Purushothaman spared their lives but banished Kudilan and his son from the kingdom forever. 

It was only then, as the dust settled, that Princess Manonmani and King Purushothaman finally saw each other clearly. They instantly recognized the faces from their dreams. 

Walking as if in a beautiful trance, Manonmani took the wedding garland and placed it around Purushothaman's neck. King Jeevakan, his eyes finally opened to the truth, happily blessed their union, bringing a lasting peace and an end to the treacherous shadows that had plagued the Pandya kingdom.
