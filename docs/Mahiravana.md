# Mahiravana: A Magician Outwitted

During the great war in Lanka, Ravana, the demon king, grew increasingly desperate. Prince Rama of Ayodhya and his mighty monkey army had already slain Ravana’s greatest warriors, including the fearsome Kumbhakarna and the powerful Indrajit. In his desperation, Ravana thought of his son, Mahiravana, the powerful magician-king who ruled the nether world. 

Deep in his subterranean palace, Mahiravana felt a supernatural tug. "Ah! It's my father!" he realized. "He is in danger! I must go to him immediately." Chanting a powerful spell, he glided upwards through the earth and emerged in the desolate city of Lanka.

"You have come, my son!" Ravana greeted him impatiently. He explained how a banished prince and a monkey-king had decimated his forces and burned his city. "You must help us to vanquish the enemy, son."
"If only you had called me earlier!" Mahiravana replied, immediately setting to work on a cunning plan.

However, in Rama's camp, the wise Vibheeshana—Ravana's righteous brother who had defected to Rama's side—suspected a trap. Taking the form of a bird, he flew to Ravana’s palace and overheard the plot. He rushed back to warn the camp. 

"O Rama! Ravana has summoned Mahiravana," Vibheeshana warned urgently. "It is his magical powers which are to be feared most. He is a wily person. We must be very careful tonight."

"We will keep awake and foil Mahiravana," Hanuman promised. To ensure absolute safety, Hanuman used his incredible powers to extend his tail, winding it round and round the entire army until it formed an impenetrable, sky-high fortress. Rama and Lakshmana sat safely in the center, while the divine discus, Sudarshan, guarded the fortress's summit. Vibheeshana stood guard at the only entrance.

When Mahiravana arrived at the camp, he was astounded by the impenetrable tail-fortress. Realizing brute force would fail, he resorted to his dark magic. He first tried to enter disguised as King Dasharatha, Rama's deceased father. But Hanuman, warned by Vibheeshana, refused to let him pass. 

Later, while Hanuman was guarding the entrance alone, Mahiravana approached disguised as Vibheeshana himself. "I am going in to tie these protective strings round the wrists of Rama and Lakshmana," the disguised magician said smoothly. 
Trusting his friend, Hanuman let the fake Vibheeshana inside. 

Moments later, the real Vibheeshana returned. When Hanuman mentioned he had just let him inside, Vibheeshana gasped in horror. "You have been tricked! That was Mahiravana!"

They rushed into the center of the fortress, only to find it empty. Mahiravana had sprinkled a magical sleeping powder over Rama and Lakshmana and carried them away through a secret tunnel dug straight into the earth.

Devastated but determined, Hanuman leaped into the dark tunnel and traveled all the way down to the nether world. Emerging near a beautiful lake, he shrank himself to the size of a tiny monkey and hid in a tree. He soon overheard local women gossiping about two handsome human princes who were to be sacrificed to Goddess Durga that very night. 

"They are locked up in a secret room," the women whispered. 

Following their directions, Hanuman located Mahiravana's palace. He assumed the form of a tiny fly, slipped through the keyhole of the secret chamber, and found Rama and Lakshmana lying unconscious on the floor. Resuming his normal form, he woke the bewildered princes. 

"I'll fight the Rakshasas, O Rama," Hanuman vowed. "Let me destroy this magician once and for all."

Before acting, Hanuman took the form of a fly once more and visited the temple of Goddess Durga to uncover Mahiravana's exact plan. He prayed to the Goddess, asking if she truly desired this sacrifice. 
"No, no! I have not ordered any sacrifice!" the Goddess revealed. "Mahiravana wants to get rid of his enemies. He is not doing it to please me." The Goddess then whispered a clever counter-plan to Hanuman.

Later that night, the grand sacrificial ritual began. Rama and Lakshmana were brought before the altar in the grand temple. Mahiravana, brimming with arrogance, purified his massive sword and offered it to the deity. 

"Come, you mortals!" Mahiravana commanded the princes. "Consider yourselves privileged to be offered to the goddess. Come and bow to her!"

"We are princes of Ayodhya, Mahiravana," Rama replied calmly, following the Goddess's secret advice. "We have never bowed to anyone in our life. We don't know how to bow. You must show us first. Then we'll do it."

"You arrogant fools! Don't you know how to bow?" Mahiravana sneered. "All right! I'll show you!"

Mahiravana turned to the altar and bowed his head low, exposing his neck. 

In a flash of lightning speed, Hanuman dropped his invisibility, sprang forward, and snatched the heavy sacrificial sword from the altar. With one mighty, sweeping strike, Hanuman severed Mahiravana's head. The Lord of the nether world lay dead on his own altar!

With two more swift blows, Hanuman smashed the chains binding Rama and Lakshmana. The remaining demons, terrified by the sudden death of their invincible king, dropped their weapons and fled for their lives. 

"You acted promptly, brave Hanuman!" Rama praised him as they made their way back up the tunnel. "You saved our lives today."

When they emerged in the monkey camp, it was past noon. Sugreeva and the others were waiting in agonizing suspense. Seeing Hanuman return safely with Rama and Lakshmana, the camp erupted in joyous cheers. 

"Mahiravana is dead, friends," Hanuman announced triumphantly. "And Ravana has no one left to fight for him!"
