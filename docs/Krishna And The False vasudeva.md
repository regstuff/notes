# Krishna and the False Vaasudeva

Envy and flattery flourished at the court of the powerful King Vaasudeva of Pundra (modern North Bengal). It was a dangerous combination. Vaasudeva became proud and boastful—only because he shared a name with the divine Krishna, who was also known as Vaasudeva. 

When a messenger from Jarasandha arrived with news that Shishupala was to marry Rukmini and warned the King to be prepared for trouble from Krishna, Vaasudeva went to Vidarbha with his allies. However, Krishna carried off Rukmini right under their noses. 

Returned to his capital, Vaasudeva was dejected. "He was one and we were many. Yet he carried off Rukmini under our very nose!" 

His courtiers, worried by his listlessness, came up with an idea to flatter him. "Why not proclaim our King the Lord Incarnate, and Krishna an impostor?" 

The courtiers set to work, convincing the people and the King himself. "Our King is Vaasudeva, Lord Vishnu incarnate! Krishna is an impostor!" 

Soon, the King believed himself to be Vishnu. He began to dress like Krishna—wearing a crown, holding an artificial lotus, mace, and discus. 

One day, Vaasudeva sent an ambassador to Dwaraka. "Go to Dwaraka and give Krishna this message: 'Our divine King, Vishnu incarnate, says: Come to Pundra and give up my discus, you foolish man. Lay aside my insignia and my name. If you come and pay homage to me, I will vouchsafe your safety.'" 

When the messenger reached Dwaraka and read the message, Krishna was amused. "Oh! So that's it! Vaasudeva has divine designs!" 

Krishna replied calmly, "Go to Vaasudeva and tell him this from me: I will obey his command and bring the discus, his emblem, and consign it to him." 

When the messenger returned, the foolish King was overjoyed, believing Krishna was yielding easily. But his ally, the King of Kashi, warned him. "I don't trust that Yadava! There is more to this than meets the eye! I had better go with my troops." 

Meanwhile, Krishna, mounted on Garuda, set out for Pundra armed with his mace, discus, and bow. 

As he neared the city, Krishna saw the King of Pundra dressed exactly like himself. Laughing all the while, Krishna attacked, scattering the combined armies of Pundra and Kashi. 

"Vaasudeva, you wanted me to hand over my insignia! Here is my discus... and here is my mace!" Krishna called out. 

Krishna's mace hit Vaasudeva, hurling him to the ground. The next moment, the Sudarshana Chakra cut his throat, ending the false Vaasudeva's life and shattering the peacock-feathered standard on his chariot. 

The King of Kashi tried to fight on. "Why don't you fight me, you impostor?" 

Krishna merely smiled, raised his bow, and let fly an arrow. The arrow severed the King of Kashi's head, and its momentum carried the head through the air all the way back to the palace gates of Kashi! 

When Sudakshina, the son of the King of Kashi, saw his father's head, he vowed revenge. "It is the work of Krishna of Dwaraka. I will not rest till I see him dead!" 

Along with his family priest, Sudakshina performed a dark sacrificial yajna. From the blazing flames emerged a formidable, terrifying female fiend (Kritya) with fire leaping out of her hair. 

"I won't rest till Dwaraka with all its inhabitants is razed to the ground!" the fiend roared, charging through the air towards Dwaraka. 

In Dwaraka, Krishna was playing dice with Rukmini when the panicked citizens rushed in. "Lord, save us! A fiery fiend is heading towards Dwaraka!" 

Unwilling to be disturbed, Krishna turned to his discus. "Go, Sudarshana-Chakra! Kill this fierce creature with flaming tresses!" 

The blazing discus set out. As soon as the fiery fiend heard the deafening whir of the Sudarshana Chakra, she was terrified and turned to flee. But the discus pursued her relentlessly. 

Realizing that Sudakshina had brought her to this plight, the fiend turned her wrath on him! She flew back to Kashi, attacked Sudakshina, and burnt him to death. 

The blazing Sudarshana Chakra whirled into the city, setting the assembly halls, stables, houses, and granaries of Kashi on fire, till the entire city was wrapped in flames and the fiend herself was consumed. 

Having fulfilled its mission, the victorious discus whirled back through the sky to Dwaraka, returning safely to the hands of the true Vaasudeva.
