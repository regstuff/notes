# The Priceless Gem - 1. The Priceless Gem

Aushadha Kumar was a reincarnation of the Bodhisattva, born with supernatural healing powers and unsurpassed wisdom. King Vaideha of Mithila adopted him and brought him to the royal court, but the older court pandits, especially Chief Counsellor Senaka, became deeply envious of the brilliant young man. 

One morning, the King spotted a fabulous, sparkling gem in the royal tank. When the best swimmers failed to retrieve it, Senaka seized the opportunity to impress the King. He ordered the tank drained and the mud dug up, but the gem vanished! When the tank was refilled, the gem mysteriously reappeared. 

Aushadha Kumar simply took a *thali* (brass plate) filled with water. "Maharaj, the gem you see in the tank... is only a reflection," he explained, pointing up. "The real gem is in the crow's nest in that palm tree." Aushadha easily retrieved the priceless gem, earning the King's great admiration and fueling Senaka's jealousy even further. 

Aushadha's wisdom extended beyond solving riddles. When King Vaideha rescued a beautiful woman stranded in a tree by her cruel husband, he married her and made her Queen Udumbara Devi. When the Queen smiled at her former husband out of gratitude for her new life, the King misunderstood and threatened to execute her. While Senaka fueled the King's anger, Aushadha correctly explained her noble intentions, saving her life. 

To test his pandits, King Vaideha asked: "Who is superior, a man who is rich but stupid, or a man who is wise but poor?"

Senaka argued for the rich man. But Aushadha proved that a rich, stupid man inevitably loses his wealth because he lacks the wisdom to employ good advisers, while a wise man's knowledge is permanent. Aushadha's brilliant logic won him the reward of sixteen villages, further angering Senaka. 

Desperate to ruin Aushadha, Senaka stole the King's crown jewel, replaced it with a fake, and secretly had a maid hide the real jewel in a pot of curds sold to Aushadha's new wife, Amara Devi. 

Amara Devi, who was as intelligent as her husband, immediately suspected a trap when the maid refused payment. She followed the maid back to Senaka's house and discovered the jewel in the curds. 

When the King found his jewel missing, Senaka insisted everyone's house be searched. The jewel was "found" in Aushadha's house. Furious, King Vaideha refused to listen to any explanations. Aushadha fled the capital and lived incognito as a humble potter's assistant in a remote village. 

After the King calmed down, Amara Devi presented her evidence to the court. The maid was brought in and confessed that Senaka had given her the curds. Senaka's plot was exposed! The King ordered Senaka severely punished, but the benevolent Aushadha forgave him, passing it off as a mere prank. In return for his nobility, the King appointed Aushadha as Commander of the Army. 

Senaka, however, refused to admit defeat. He tried a new tactic. He asked Aushadha, "To whom would you confide your deepest secrets?"
"No one," Aushadha replied. "Not even to the King."

Senaka immediately twisted these words, warning King Vaideha that the new Commander was keeping dangerous secrets from him. "He has his eyes set on the throne!" Senaka lied. 

Aushadha noticed the King growing suspicious and fearful. Knowing Senaka was plotting again, Aushadha hid in a pile of grain near where the four pandits met secretly and overheard them conspiring to have him murdered! 

Realizing his life was in danger, Aushadha gathered his loyal troops and surrounded the palace, effectively taking control of the city. 

He then marched into the throne room. The terrified King Vaideha shouted, "You hypocrite! Why do you shout my praises when you are out to do me harm?"

"I have no intention of harming you, Maharaj," Aushadha replied calmly. "I just want to make you aware of certain facts. Senaka pandit wanted to have me murdered, so he poisoned your mind to gain your support."

Aushadha then revealed the exact details of the pandits' secret meeting. "You revealed it to me yourself by speaking of it to others," Aushadha told the shocked Senaka. "I think you will agree now that a secret should never be divulged to anyone. For... the moment it is divulged it ceases to remain a secret."

Aushadha then bowed to the King. "I also wanted to prove that I would ever remain faithful to you. It is a sin to wrong a friend or a King under whose shelter I have lived. I could never even dream of harming you, Maharaj."

King Vaideha, deeply ashamed of his misplaced suspicions, condemned the four pandits to become Aushadha's slaves. But the noble Aushadha once again forgave them, freeing them from the bonds of slavery and proving forever that true wisdom is always tempered with compassion.
