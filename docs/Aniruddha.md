# Aniruddha: Beloved Grandson of Krishna

Bana was the powerful, thousand-armed king of the Asuras. He was a great devotee of Lord Shiva. One day, with his thousand arms, he played a magnificent array of musical instruments as Shiva danced the divine Tandava. Immensely pleased with Bana's devotion, Shiva granted him a boon: "For the pleasure you have given me, I assure you of my protection at all times."

But as time passed, the invincible Bana grew vain and arrogant. He went to Shiva and complained, "My thousand arms are becoming a useless burden to me. Even without them, there is none equal to me in battle, save you."
To put an end to his arrogance, Shiva cursed him: "O vain and foolish one, in a future encounter, one equal to me will humble you." 
Instead of being frightened, the arrogant Bana welcomed the curse, eagerly looking forward to finally meeting a peer in battle. 

Bana had a beautiful daughter named Usha. One afternoon, after picking flowers in her favorite grove, she lay down to rest under the trees. As she drowsed, she had a vivid dream of a stunningly handsome, dark-blue-skinned prince wearing yellow garments. When she woke up with a start, reaching out for the prince, her friends teased her. "Who is this lucky lord who has won your heart?"

But Chitralekha, the daughter of one of Bana's ministers and Usha's closest companion, shooed the others away and consoled the heartbroken princess. "Do not grieve, my princess. If this prince exists, I can and shall bring him to you. But first, you will have to identify him for me."
Chitralekha, being a skilled artist, began painting portraits of various gods, Gandharvas, and Yakshas. Usha rejected them all. Finally, Chitralekha painted the great Yadava warriors: Krishna, and his son Pradyumna. Usha blushed, admitting they closely resembled the prince of her dreams. Finally, Chitralekha drew the portrait of Aniruddha, the son of Pradyumna and grandson of Krishna.
"This is he!" Usha cried happily. 
"I should have known!" Chitralekha thought. "Surely, among men, it could only have been one of the valiant Yadava race who could have disturbed my princess."

Using her yogic powers, Chitralekha flew to the great city of Dwaraka. She entered Aniruddha's palace and found him fast asleep on his bed. Using her magic, she transported the sleeping prince back to the kingdom of Bana, placing him right in Usha's chambers. 

When Aniruddha woke up, he was bewildered to find himself in a strange palace with a beautiful princess. "You have been brought here in secrecy," Usha confessed, "and you are free to leave if you so desire."
But Aniruddha, captivated by her beauty and charm, replied, "I cannot dream of leaving you. I would rather stay here and delight in your company." Thus imprisoned by his love for the lovely Usha, Aniruddha secretly married her and stayed hidden in her chambers.

Aniruddha lost count of the days, living happily in secret. But such stolen happiness could not last forever. Bana's palace guards noticed that the princess hadn't stepped out of her rooms for days. Suspecting something, they spied on her and were shocked to find the Yadava prince. They immediately rushed to King Bana. "She has secretly married the Yadava prince, Aniruddha!" they reported.

"Enough! Let me see this for myself," Bana thundered, bursting into Usha's room. 
Bana ordered his guards to seize Aniruddha. But Aniruddha was quick; he grabbed a heavy mace from an attendant and slew all the guards before they could even realize what was happening. Livid with rage, Bana ordered Usha out of the room and attacked Aniruddha himself. Using his thousand arms, the Asura king overpowered the young prince and bound him from head to foot using live, venomous serpents as ropes. 

Meanwhile, back in Dwaraka, four months had passed, and the Yadavas were deeply upset over Aniruddha's mysterious disappearance. Krishna sent for the all-knowing Sage Narada, who brought the grim news: "Aniruddha is in the court of Bana. Bana has bound him with live serpents and imprisoned him."

Krishna immediately called a council of war. "Get your horses, chariots, and arms ready for battle!" he commanded. Krishna, Pradyumna, and the great warrior Satyaki, leading an army of hundreds of Yadava warriors, marched on Bana's kingdom to rescue the young prince. 

When they arrived, Krishna gave the command to attack. Bana, furious, marched out with an equally large Asura army. True to his boon, Lord Shiva arrived to protect his devotee Bana, bringing along his son, Kartikeya, and his wild hordes. A battle of epic, cosmic proportions ensued. The celestials, headed by Brahma, gathered in the sky to watch. 

Krishna fought directly with Shiva, while Pradyumna fought Kartikeya, and Satyaki took on Bana. Krishna was easily the greatest warrior on the field. Using the powerful *Jrimbhanastra* (a mystical weapon of slumber), he stupefied Lord Shiva, putting him into a deep trance. Meanwhile, Pradyumna vanquished Kartikeya with ease.

Seeing his forces falling, Bana was blind with anger. He abandoned his fight with Satyaki and rushed furiously at Krishna. Using his thousand arms, Bana drew five hundred bows at the same time and set two arrows in each, unleashing a terrifying storm of arrows. 
But Krishna was too quick. With his spinning, razor-sharp discus (the *Sudarshana Chakra*), Krishna swiftly cut off nine hundred and ninety-six of Bana's thousand arms, killed his charioteer and horses, and destroyed his chariot. 

Defeated and bleeding, Banasura turned to flee back into his city gates. Krishna was about to follow and finish him when Shiva, having recovered from his trance, intervened. "Krishna, Bana is devoted to me, and I have promised him my protection. I plead with you to spare him."

Krishna smiled respectfully at Shiva. "It is only to humble him that I have cut off all except four of his arms. And to relieve the earth of her burden, I have destroyed his army. He still has four arms left. Besides, I grant him immortality and everlasting youth."

Shiva then summoned the humbled Asura king. "Krishna has pardoned as well as blessed you," Shiva told Bana. "Bring his grandson and your daughter to him."
A completely reformed Bana went into the city and soon returned, bringing Usha and the freed Aniruddha. 

"All Dwaraka is anxious for the return of Aniruddha. We shall set off immediately," Krishna announced. Placing the happily reunited couple, Usha and Aniruddha, in the front of his chariot, the victorious Krishna, along with Pradyumna and Satyaki, returned in glory to Dwaraka.
