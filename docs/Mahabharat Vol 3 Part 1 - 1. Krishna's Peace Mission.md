# Krishna's Peace Mission

Following the wedding celebrations of Abhimanyu and Uttaraa, the illustrious descendants of the Kuru race and their allies assembled at the court of King Virata. The assembly included the five Pandavas, Virata, Drupada, Lord Krishna, Balarama, and Satyaki. 

Krishna addressed the gathering, stating the facts plainly. "You are all aware of how Shakuni deceitfully defeated Yudhishthira in the game of dice, and how the Pandavas have now completed their thirteen years in exile. What is rightfully theirs should be restored. I suggest we send a wise and virtuous man as our ambassador to persuade Duryodhana to give half his kingdom to Yudhishthira."

Balarama agreed, advocating for peace. However, Satyaki and Drupada strongly believed that Duryodhana would never yield without a fight and urged the Pandavas to prepare for war. Drupada ultimately sent his own learned priest as an emissary to Hastinapura to test the waters. 

In Hastinapura, the priest delivered the message: the Pandavas demanded their rightful share but wished to avoid war. Bheeshma acknowledged the justice of their claim, but the arrogant Karna insulted the priest, claiming the Pandavas had not fulfilled the terms of their exile. Dhritarashtra, avoiding a direct answer, sent his trusted charioteer, Sanjaya, to the Pandava camp with a hollow message of peace, asking the Pandavas not to fight but offering nothing in return. 

Yudhishthira saw through the empty words, and Krishna firmly rebuked Sanjaya. "Tell Dhritarashtra that theft is despicable, whether committed openly or otherwise. The Pandavas and their allies are prepared for war if that is what Duryodhana wants. But that will be the end of the Kauravas."

Sanjaya returned to Hastinapura and severely criticized Dhritarashtra for his blind love for his son, warning him of the impending destruction. Dhritarashtra, deeply anxious, sought the counsel of Vidura, who advised him to restore the kingdom to the Pandavas. 

In the open court, Sanjaya delivered the Pandavas' final terms: give them at least five villages—Kushasthala, Vrikasthala, Makandi, Varanavata, and one other—or face war. 

Bheeshma, Drona, and Vidura pleaded with Duryodhana to accept the generous offer. But Duryodhana remained obstinate. "I will not surrender to the Pandavas even so much land as covered by the sharp point of a needle!" he declared, stalking out of the court. 

Back in Upaplavya, Draupadi, weeping bitterly over the memory of her public humiliation, demanded revenge. Krishna comforted her. "O Draupadi, soon you will see the Kaurava women crying as you do now. I will make a final effort for peace, but if war comes, not a single Kaurava prince will be left alive."

The next morning, Krishna set out for Hastinapura on a historic peace mission. Despite strange omens like thunder from a clear sky and rivers flowing upstream, Krishna arrived safely. Dhritarashtra had ordered lavish preparations to welcome him, hoping to bribe him with gifts, but Vidura and Bheeshma saw through the ruse. 

Krishna was received with great honor. He first visited Kunti, his aunt, who wept at the plight of her sons and daughter-in-law. Krishna consoled her before returning to the court. He refused Duryodhana's invitation to a royal feast, choosing instead to eat at Vidura's humble home, stating he would only accept hospitality after his mission was successful. 

The following day, Krishna addressed the Kaurava court. He made an impassioned plea to Dhritarashtra. "O King, I come here to prevent bloodshed and establish lasting peace. You keep your sons under check, and I will restrain the Pandavas."

Bheeshma, Drona, and Vidura urged Duryodhana to listen to Krishna's wise counsel. But Duryodhana was furious and unyielding. When reprimanded, he stormed out of the assembly with his brothers. 

In a desperate, foolish move, Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni, and Duhshasana conspired to arrest Krishna, hoping to break the spirit of the Pandavas. Satyaki learned of the plot and informed Krishna. 

Krishna laughed scornfully at their ignorance. As the conspirators approached, Krishna revealed his terrifying, divine universal form (*Vishwaroopa*). The court was blinded by his radiance, and the earth trembled. The kings closed their eyes in fear and awe. 

Resuming his human form, Krishna told Dhritarashtra, "You have seen the power I wield. Your sons' efforts to bring about peace are a sham." He declared his peace mission a failure and left the court. 

Before departing Hastinapura, Krishna visited Kunti one last time. She gave him a message for her sons: "Tell Yudhishthira that the kingdom that rightfully belongs to him lies in the hands of the enemy. He must try every means to recover it—if not by conciliation, then by war. Tell him that he must fight."

Krishna then met with Karna privately. He revealed the shocking truth of Karna's birth—that he was the eldest son of Kunti and Surya. Krishna offered Karna the kingdom and the position of emperor if he joined his brothers. 

Karna, though deeply moved, refused. "I cannot abandon Duryodhana, who has given me wealth, status, and friendship. My foster parents raised me with love. I know that the Kauravas are doomed to lose this war against you and Arjuna, but I must fight for my friend. Please keep my identity a secret, for if Yudhishthira knows I am his elder brother, he will never accept the kingdom."

With a heavy heart, Krishna accepted Karna's tragic choice. "If we come out of this great battle alive, then we shall meet again here on earth," Karna said. "Otherwise, certainly, we shall meet in heaven."

Krishna embraced Karna fondly and then drove his chariot back to Upaplavya, bringing the inevitable news that war could no longer be avoided.
