# The King in a Parrot's Body - 1. The King in a Parrot's Body

Long, long ago there was a noble king called Shuklapaksha who reigned over a city called Prithvibhushana. He was pure of heart, but his minister, Krishnapaksha, was a wicked man. 

One day, a merchant brought a beautiful but wild horse to the king. The wicked minister suggested the king try riding it, hoping the wild steed would kill him. 

The king mounted the horse, and it immediately bolted out of the palace and into the forest, carrying the king far away until it finally collapsed and died of exhaustion. 

The tired king walked wearily through the forest until he found a peaceful hermitage. There, he met a holy sage and a beautiful maiden named Nivritti. 

"Who is that maiden?" the king asked the sage. 
"She is Nivritti, a demigod princess. She was abducted and left here years ago. Her father went after the abductor and told me to marry her to a worthy man if he didn't return. The only condition is that the man must possess the magical power to enter a dead body," the sage explained. 

"I would gladly marry her!" the king declared. "But I do not have that power. Isn't there some way?"
"You may marry her now, but you must not take her into your inner palace until you acquire the power," the sage agreed. 

So the king married Nivritti. Soon, the king's courtiers, led by the wicked minister, found him in the forest and escorted him back to his kingdom. Nivritti came with him, living in a separate palace in the royal park. 

The king desperately wanted to learn the magical power. The minister suggested they build a resthouse for holy men, hoping to find a yogi who knew the secret. 

After months of searching, they finally learned of Yogi Sadananda, who lived on a distant mountain peak. The king and his minister travelled to the mountain and meditated before the yogi. 

Finally, the yogi spoke to the king. "I am pleased with you. I am willing to give the power to you, but not to that man. He is not fit to possess this power! He is not to be trusted."

"Holy sir, for my sake let my minister, too, be given the power," the king pleaded naively. 
"All right, but I warn you, be wary of him," the yogi sighed, and imparted the secret to them both. 

On their way back, they found a dead elephant. "Here is an opportunity to test my newly acquired power!" the king said. "I'm going to enter the body of this elephant. Please take care of my body till I come back."

The moment the king left his body, the wicked minister seized his chance. He quickly entered the king's empty body! Then, to ensure the king could never return, the minister burned his own discarded body on a pyre and rode back to the palace, pretending to be the king. 

When the king returned to his body, he found it missing, along with his minister. Realizing he had been betrayed, the king, still in the elephant's body, hurried back to his kingdom. 

Meanwhile, the minister (in the king's body) went to Nivritti. "I have the power! But alas, my noble minister lost his life saving mine from a lion," he lied. 

Nivritti didn't believe him. She suspected it was the wicked minister in her husband's body. She told him she had taken a vow and he must not visit her for a few days. 

When the minister heard a rogue elephant was approaching the city, he ordered his soldiers to hunt it down. The king (in the elephant's body) was chased by the soldiers. 

To escape, the king transferred his soul into a dead deer. But the soldiers shot the deer. As the deer died, the king transferred his soul into a dead parrot nearby. 

The parrot flew to Nivritti's palace but was caught in a hunter's net. The parrot cleverly told the hunter to sell him to the Queen's maid for a lakh of rupees. 

When the maid told Nivritti about the talking bird, Nivritti immediately gave her the money. "Buy that bird! I'm sure it will help me solve the mystery."

The minister became suspicious of the Queen's costly purchase. He went to her palace and snatched the parrot from the maid. 

"He'll kill me!" the king (in the parrot's body) thought, and quickly transferred his soul into a dead bee on the floor. 

The minister wrung the parrot's neck. Nivritti burst into tears. "You killed my bird! I'll kill myself!" she wept. 

Desperate to calm her down, the minister said, "Give me a few minutes and I'll bring your parrot back to life." He took the dead parrot into the next room. Leaving the king's body, he transferred his soul into the parrot and flew back to Nivritti to show her he had revived it. 

This was exactly what the king was waiting for! The king immediately left the bee's body and entered his own royal body, which the minister had foolishly left empty in the next room. 

The king walked out to his queen. "Nivritti! I am back!"
"My lord!" she cried happily. 

"I've been duped!" the parrot (the wicked minister) squawked in dismay. 

The king told Nivritti everything. "Give me that parrot. I'll wring its neck!" he said angrily. 
"Leave him to his own fate. We'll put him in an iron cage so he can do no more mischief," Nivritti suggested wisely. 

And so the wicked minister spent the rest of his days trapped as a parrot in an iron cage, while the noble king and his queen lived happily ever after.
