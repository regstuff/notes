# Yayati: Retold from the Mahabharata

Devayani was the only daughter of Shukracharya, the great preceptor of Vrishaparva, king of the Asuras. Since her father doted on her, she grew up to be a spoilt and wilful girl, accustomed to having her every whim and fancy indulged. 

One morning, Sharmishtha, the Asura princess, called on Shukracharya. "Holy one," she asked, "we are going to the garden of the Gandharvas in the forest to swim in the lake. May we take Devayani with us?"

"Do so by all means," Shukracharya replied. "But take good care of her." 

"I certainly will, Holy One! I know how much you love her," Sharmishtha assured him.

As the girls approached the lake, the garden resounded with their laughter. They left their clothes in separate heaps on the bank and plunged into the refreshing water. Suddenly, a storm broke out, blowing their clothes around and jumbling them up into a single pile. 

When the girls hurriedly came out of the lake, they sorted out their clothes and dressed themselves. But as they were about to set out homewards, a quarrel broke out.

"Hey! That's my upper garment!" Devayani snapped. "How dare Sharmishtha take it!"

Sharmishtha, who had mistakenly put on the garment, retorted, "How dare you take my garment? Don't you know, you should not covet what belongs to another, you thief?"

"Why don't you answer me—you common thief?" Devayani sneered.

Stunned by the harsh words, Sharmishtha thought, *How unreasonable can Devayani get! Can't she realise that it was done by mistake?* Aloud she said, "All right, I'll answer you. How could I be a thief when I've only taken what is mine?"

"How dare you call my father, the great Shukracharya, a beggar!" Devayani shrieked. 

"Yes. You have nothing to call your own," the princess shot back. "Your father gives you what he receives from my father as alms, O daughter of a beggar! He is one! He sings the praises of my father, begs, and gets alms which he passes on to you as gifts."

"No!!" Devayani cried. As Sharmishtha began to walk away, Devayani ran after her and pulled her robe. "Give me back my clothes, you thief! I won't allow you to get away with your loot."

Furious, Sharmishtha gave her a harsh push. Devayani lost her balance and tumbled down into a dry well. Sharmishtha walked away without looking back, ignoring Devayani's cries for help.

Just then, King Yayati, who had been out hunting, passed by. *My throat is parched, and there's not a pool in sight,* he thought. Spotting the well, he rode up and looked into it. 

"Help me out of this well, O stranger," Devayani called out from the bottom. 

"Who are you?" Yayati asked in surprise. 

"I am the daughter of Shukracharya who brings back to life the Asuras slain by the gods," she replied. "Noble one, hold my hand and help me to come out."

Yayati firmly grasped her hand and pulled her out. She gazed at him, thinking, *How strong and firm is his grip!*

"Go home, fair one, and convey my respects to your revered father," Yayati said gently, before going on his way. 

Meanwhile, at the ashram, Shukracharya was growing anxious. He went out searching and finally found Devayani weeping in the forest. 

"Child, where are the others? Why are you weeping?" he asked. 

Devayani poured out the story of what had happened. "Her cruel words hurt me more than her cruel act," she sobbed. "Father, she called me the daughter of a hired chanter of praises who begs and accepts alms."

"You are the daughter of one who praises none but is praised by all," Shukracharya comforted her. "Come home, Devayani."

"No. I won't! I want to go away from this kingdom," she declared. 

"One who overcomes anger even though there may be cause for it, will earn merit, nay, salvation," he reasoned. 

"No, father. Let us go away. The wise should not dwell among those who do not respect them. How can I live in this kingdom as a dependant of that arrogant princess?"

Seeing his beloved daughter's distress, Shukracharya resolved to act. "Compose yourself, my daughter. She who has hurt you will not go unpunished." Leaving Devayani in the forest, he stomped into the palace of the Asura king. 

King Vrishaparva stood up to receive him, but Shukracharya was blunt. "Vrishaparva, I have come to tell you that I am leaving your kingdom—never to return."

"Have I unwittingly hurt your feelings?" the king asked, alarmed. 

"Don't ask me. Ask my daughter," Shukracharya replied coldly, and walked out without another word.

When Vrishaparva questioned Sharmishtha, she realized the gravity of her actions. "Father, I am to be blamed for this calamity. I am sorry. I am willing to make amends." 

The king and the princess rushed to the forest to plead with the sage. "If the respected Shukracharya refuses to return, we are doomed," Vrishaparva thought. "Who else will bring the Asuras killed by the Devas back to life?"

"O Devayani, please forgive Sharmishtha," the king pleaded. "Anything you ask for will be granted."

"I will, on one condition," Devayani replied haughtily. "Sharmishtha with a thousand maids should wait on me. She must follow me wherever I go."

"I shall cheerfully do so, Devayani," Sharmishtha agreed, swallowing her pride. "You and your revered father must not leave the Asuras."

Her anger appeased, Devayani returned to the ashram. A few days later, she went to the same forest, now accompanied by Sharmishtha and the other maids. 

"Sharmishtha, I'm tired. Please press my feet," Devayani ordered. 

"I'm coming, Devayani," her new maid replied obediently.

Just then, King Yayati happened to pass by again. *Ah! Isn't that the girl I rescued from the well?* he recognized her. 

"It's him! My saviour!" Devayani whispered happily. 

Yayati approached the women. "May I know your names, fair ones? And what has brought you to these parts?"

"I am Devayani, the daughter of Shukracharya. And she is my maid, Sharmishtha," she introduced them. 

"I am King Yayati, the son of Nahusha," he replied. "Tired and thirsty after a long hunt, I have come here to quench my thirst at the well." 

"I await your command to leave this place," Yayati offered courteously.

Devayani quickly sent for her father. When Shukracharya arrived, Devayani stepped forward. "O father, he took hold of my hand when I was in distress. You will bestow me upon him, won't you?"

Turning to Yayati, she asked, "My command? How can I command you whom I look upon as my lord? Yes, O king. You took my hand in yours! Accept me as your wife."

"I will, if your father offers you to me," Yayati smiled. 

"He will do so, with pleasure," Devayani assured him. 

"How can I bestow upon another, the hand which this Rajarshi has claimed!" Shukracharya agreed warmly. 

A few days later, Yayati and Devayani were married. Yayati left for his capital with his new bride, along with Sharmishtha and the two thousand maids. When they reached his palace, Devayani said, "My lord, although Sharmishtha has been given to me as a maid, she is like a sister to me. Please have a palace built for her."

"How generous you are, Devayani!" Yayati praised her. So a separate palace was built for Sharmishtha on the outskirts of the city. 

Devayani lived happily with Yayati, and in time, two sons were born to them—Yadu and Turvasu. 

One day, while returning from a hunt, Yayati strayed into Sharmishtha's garden. 

"Welcome, O King!" Sharmishtha greeted him. 

"Sharmishtha, how are you? Are you happy?" he inquired kindly. 

"How could I be, O king, in a house where a child's laughter is not heard?" she wept, falling at his feet. "O King, I beg you, accept me as your wife and enable me to become a mother of sons as brave and accomplished as you."

Yayati was taken aback. "How can I, Sharmishtha? I am married to Devayani."

"Our scriptures allow a king to marry many times," she argued. "I am a slave of Devayani whose master you are. Therefore, I rightfully belong to you. Accept me."

Yayati reasoned with himself. "I am lonely and unprotected. You can bring joy and security into my life... It is my duty to accept her. But what will Devayani say?"

Feeling he had no choice, Yayati married Sharmishtha in secret by the Gandharva rite. In due course, Sharmishtha became the mother of three sons—Druhyu, Anu and Puru. 

Devayani remained completely unaware of this. A few years later, she accompanied Yayati to the forest and saw three young boys playing. 

"Stop the chariot! Those boys! How much they resemble you in strength and looks!" she exclaimed. She got down and approached them. "Whose children are you?"

"It's father!" one of the boys said, pointing to Yayati. 

"Father? No!" Devayani gasped in disbelief. Just then, Sharmishtha came out to greet them. 

"My husband has married Sharmishtha without my knowledge," Devayani realized furiously. "They have made a fool of me. O King, you have deceived me! Not a moment longer will I stay with you!"

"Please listen, Devayani... Devayani, come back!" Yayati called out, but she rushed straight to her father's ashram. 

"Father, I've been deceived by my husband," Devayani cried, telling Shukracharya everything. 

Yayati, who had followed closely behind, entered the ashram just as she finished. 

"O King, you had promised me you would not hurt my daughter by your words or deeds," Shukracharya thundered in anger. "You have broken your promise. Since it was the curse of youth that prompted you to do so, may you lose your youth and grow old this minute!"

"No!" Yayati cried, but the next minute, his hair turned white and his body withered. A sudden change came over him, reducing him to a frail old man. 

Trembling at his fate, Yayati fell to his knees. "Holy one, I implore you, take back your words and restore my youth. I have been reduced to a state of helplessness! I cannot hunt, I cannot play, and thousands of my desires remain unfulfilled."

"I cannot revoke a curse, Yayati," the sage relented slightly. "But if anyone is prepared to oblige you, you may exchange his youth for your old age."

"One of my dutiful sons will certainly oblige me," Yayati said hopefully. 

He rushed back to his capital and summoned Yadu, his eldest son. "O Yadu, my dutiful son, take my old age and give me your youth for a period of a thousand years."

"How can I, father?" Yadu refused. "I have my own dreams and ambitions. How can I fulfil my desires if I give up my youth and embrace old age?"

Yayati's request was turned down by his next three sons as well. Finally, he approached his youngest son, Puru. "Puru, you are my only hope. Or will you also..."

"No, father, I won't," Puru replied selflessly. "I will gladly exchange my youth for your old age. Take my youth, and retain it as long as you desire."

In a flash, Yayati became a young man again, while Puru transformed into a frail elder. 

"Son, I will give back your youth after a thousand years. And then you shall be the king," Yayati promised, before eagerly returning to his life of pleasure. 

A thousand years passed. Yayati, having lived a full life, sent for Puru. 

"Son, with your youth I've enjoyed the pleasures of life to my heart's content. You may take it back," Yayati said gently. "I shall spend the rest of my days in meditation."

The next moment, Puru regained his youth, and Yayati his old age. As promised, Yayati crowned Puru as the new king. 

"Father, would you like to retain my youth for another thousand years? Please do, if you so desire," Puru offered out of love. 

"No, Puru," Yayati smiled wisely. "I have found that desires are never gratified by indulgence. On the other hand, with indulgence, they only flare up like fire fed with libations of sacrificial ghee."

With his heart finally at peace, Yayati retired to the forest, where he spent his remaining days meditating upon Brahman, the ultimate reality.
