# Chennamma of Keladi: The Queen Who Defied Aurangzeb

The year was 1663. Somashekhara Nayak, king of Keladi (a kingdom in the Malnad area of Karnataka), was visiting the Rameshwar fair. It was there that he first saw Chennamma, an exuberant, child-like daughter of a merchant, and was captivated by her. 

Later, at the palace in Bidanur, the king held a meeting. His ministers were visibly worried. 
"Maharaj, the kingdom of Keladi needs an heir. Is it not time you took a wife?" a minister urged. 
"In fact, the neighbouring kings of Swadi and Beelagi have both offered to give you their daughters in marriage," another noted. 
"An alliance with these powerful chiefs would definitely benefit both you and Keladi."
King Somashekhara nodded slightly. "Hmm! Thank you for your suggestions, my ministers. I want to be left alone now."

All but one of the ministers walked out. 
"Veeranna, my old and trusted friend, you stay on. What is the matter?" the King asked. 
"Old and experienced as I am, I know what it is," Veeranna smiled. "You shall have the bride you desire."

Bidanur, decorated with flowers and lamps, plunged joyfully into the celebration of a much-awaited day. "Long live our king! Long live his young bride!" the people cheered as King Somashekhara married Chennamma. 
Taking a prominent part in the wedding was the man the king held as his dearest friend—Bharama Mahuta, the powerful chief of the elephant force. 

But behind his smile, Bharama seethed. "Foiled! All my plans are foiled! Now there will be children which means there will be heirs to the throne. I was gathering support to seize power. Now this royal wedding will make the wretched king popular again," he thought. 
For a split second, his mask slipped, revealing pure malice. 
"Aah!" Chennamma gasped, catching his dark look. 
"Chenna, what is the matter? Sit properly," the King whispered. 
Chennamma gazed back into the now smiling countenance of Bharama... but was not consoled. She was trembling. 
"Highness, she'll be all right. I think the excitement has been too much for her," Bharama said smoothly. 

In the following months, little Chenna, the merchant's daughter, became transformed into Rani Chennamma of Keladi, learning the ways of archery, horse-riding, and statecraft. 
"My Chenna is now ready to become the mother of the heir to Keladi's throne," the King beamed. 

Years passed. Chennamma actively supervised palace affairs and charity. 
"Where shall I put these old things, amma?" a maid asked. 
"In the shed, please. This courtyard must be cleared quickly for this afternoon. The poor will be feasted."
Bharama Mahuta walked in. "Oh, it's you! Become quite old, hasn't it, this little royal cradle?" he mocked. 
Ignoring Bharama, the queen turned to a servant, Koraga. "Take this home for your new-born son."
"Thank you, highness," Koraga bowed. 
"Amma, look, here's Gauri being taken to graze with her calf, Valli," a maid pointed. 
"Valli, little one... How endearing small creatures are!" Chennamma smiled, petting the calf. 

Bharama watched closely. "I was dreaming of an heir to the throne, Maharaj... a son as handsome and competent as his father... Don't we all wish that?" he murmured to the King later. 
"It's been some years now, Bharama. I must confess, I am disappointed," the King sighed. "Is it not said in the sacred books that the birth of a child ensures his parents a safe journey to heaven?"
"You have taken the words straight out of my mouth, highness. Which reminds me - I can suggest a remedy."
"A remedy? But Bharama, have there not been childless couples before now? And if fate has so willed, then we must bear with it. Besides, though Chenna and I have barely discussed it, I know her mind on this matter."
"Ah! There you are mistaken, highness," Bharama whispered poisonously. "The queen... is silently pining for a child. I know it. I saw her caressing an elephant calf, with tears in her eyes. And she held a cradle most tenderly."

The King was moved. "All right, Bharama. What is this remedy you have?"
"It is a secret potion. I will instruct the royal physician to have it ready by the time we return from the war against Belur. But as this remedy is a little unusual, speak of it to no one."
"I... I won't," the King promised. 

After a victorious campaign, the King returned to Bidanur. "Glory to our king Somashekhara... Glory to the conqueror of Kannatur and Belur," the crowds cheered. 
Chennamma greeted him. "Glory to my lord. Congratulations on your victory. Any good news?"
"What is it?" the King asked. 
"Father is coming to stay with us."
"Oh, I see. Yes... That's good news," the King muttered distractedly. 
"Are you feeling unwell?" she asked. 
"No... no... but Chenna, I promise you, I will acquire more and more cities for you; become a great conqueror."
Chennamma frowned. "I leave again for war in a fortnight," the King announced. 
"Should we not first consolidate our position in Kannatur and Belur, establish law and order and bring the life of their citizens to normal again?" Chennamma advised carefully. "I remember what you said - that a king must be like a father to the citizens he has subjugated, so that all can be secure and happy..."
"Father? Why do you mock me with that word, Chenna?" the King snapped, walking away. 
"What is the matter with him?" she wondered. 

Five minutes later, the King asked Bharama secretly, "Bharama, is it ready?"
"It is, highness," Bharama grinned, handing him the potion. 

That night, a horrifying scream echoed from the royal bedchamber. "Chenna, Chenna... Chenna!" the King thrashed wildly on the bed. 
Later, as the King lay unconscious, a terrified Chennamma told her father and the doctors, "He screamed like one mad... demented..."
In his delirium, the King murmured, "...Give me more... I will drink... anything... for Chenna's sake..." and "...Ah- how endearing small creatures are..."
Chennamma's eyes widened in realization. "Bharama Mahuta!"

The next day, she summoned Bharama. "Good morning! You sent for me, highness?"
"Bharama Mahuta, the king is dead."
"No... no... it cannot be," Bharama gasped in mock horror. 
"Bharama Mahuta, by poisoning one who trusted you as a friend, you have proved yourself the blackest of traitors," Chennamma declared coldly. 
"That is a lie..."
"By giving madness-inducing poison to your king, you have proved yourself the blackest of men."
"Mercy, highness, mercy..." Bharama fell to his knees. 

"The king had nominated you to be his successor. When shall we have the crowning ceremony?" she mocked him. 
"A few days must surely pass in mourning..." he stammered. 
Chennamma gave a nod to the guards. "Don't move!"
"What is this? ...What...?" Bharama panicked as the guards seized him. 
"Take him away to some place... any place where he will not thus set fire to the homes of those who were... once so happy," Chennamma ordered harshly. "That place is the land of the dead. Yes, the land of the dead where he has just sent his king. Bury this snake in a pit. Let masses of thorns be the crown over his head."
"Highness, this is..."

Later, by the King's funeral pyre, Chennamma wept. "I knew everything... long ago... I knew that you were not to be trusted. My dearest, in what moment of weakness did you allow yourself to be led astray like this! Children? Sons? Heirs? We had each other. And need our children be born of our own bodies? The citizens of Keladi - they are our children. All the children we know - they are our children. What a trivial cause for which you threw away your life! Highness..."

"Oh, father. Why didn't you let me go with him on the pyre?" Chennamma cried to her father. 
"He is gone, child. But his work remains," her father comforted her. "I swear on his ashes, I shall complete his work, I shall look after Keladi; I shall make her the epitome of justice, nobility and bravery."
Chennamma wiped her tears, her resolve hardening. "Yes, his work remains."

With the King dead, Keladi became a troubled land. Every powerful minister sponsored a different person as successor to Somashekhara. 
"Highness, Thimmanna may be the commander-in-chief, but as the councillors, we want Shivappa Nayak here to be crowned king," one faction argued. 
"No - I support Veerabhadra. He has a greater right to the throne," another countered. 
"Gentlemen, I will think the matter over and let you know," Chennamma replied diplomatically. 
But the queen's reply was mistaken for indecision, and the intrigues consequently deepened. The ministers began to plot against Chennamma herself. "We will overthrow her. A merchant's daughter with not a drop of royal blood in her veins! Even as regent, she is unsuitable."

But the very next day in court, amidst noisy arguments and angry shoutings, the biggest blow fell. 
"Quiet, everyone! Listen!" a messenger burst in. "News has just arrived that the massive Bijapur army under Jannopant is on its way to attack Keladi."
"Oh, no! What shall we do? What shall we do?" the plotting ministers panicked. 

Chennamma stood up. "You have just heard the news. Keladi is under attack. Who is to rule is not important. Our first job is to repel the invader."
Without a word, Thimmanna Nayak, the commander-in-chief, began to walk out. 
"Thimmanna, wait- we need you more than ever at such a time," she called out. 
"I cannot stay in a kingdom that does not have a ruler," Thimmanna replied stubbornly, and left. 

Undeterred, Chennamma summoned the army and the people. "Brave soldiers and citizens of Keladi! This, our land, nurtured and handed down to us by our elders is in danger. It is our duty to safeguard it. Do not think of me as a queen. Think of me as the voice of the great former kings! And the voice of the one we have just lost. It is their spirit which enjoins you to fight for Keladi."
She began removing her royal ornaments. "I shed my jewels. I present them all to the war effort. To you, o soldiers, and to you, o citizens, and to your families. Take them."
Drawing a sword, she raised it high. "And these hands that once wore gold bangles, now take up the sword to fight along with you. Will you fight?"
"We will fight! We will fight! We will fight!" the crowd roared. 

The army of Keladi marched against the far larger army of Bijapur. The soldiers held their heads high and their hearts were elated. But the massive Bijapur army forced its way through and captured the fort of Bidanur. 
However, they found an empty city. "Cleverly evacuated no doubt!" the Bijapur commander realized. "Where is the throne? Where is your woman-ruler? Where are the jewels, the money in your royal treasury?" he demanded of a captive. 
"Gone where you can never find them," the captive defied him. "We are the few who have remained. Do what you will with us."

Meanwhile, Chennamma had taken refuge in the hidden fortress of Bhuvanagiri, deep in the dense Malnad forest. 
"It pains me to have abandoned Bidanur, our beloved capital," Chennamma sighed. 
"We did right, highness. The enemy was too powerful to resist head-on," her minister advised. 
Just then, a visitor was ushered in. "You, Thimmanna?"
Thimmanna fell to his knees. "I feel ashamed even to ask for your forgiveness. The fall of Bidanur broke my heart. I have sinned in not realising that the one to whom I owe allegiance is you; only you. Highness, please accept me back into your service."

Chennamma looked at him. "Thimmanna, of what value are your words, when you have already turned your back once on the queen?" 
Thimmanna bowed his head in deep shame. 
Chennamma smiled softly. "Thimmanna, welcome back. Keladi cannot forget the service you have so often rendered. I make you my commander-in-chief once again."
"O noble highness..."

Thimmanna Nayak rapidly organised the chieftains and citizens from the surrounding parts of Bhuvanagiri and trained them into a courageous band of guerilla soldiers. 
"The Bijapur army has located our hideout. They are marching towards us," Thimmanna briefed them. "My men, you know this jungle terrain like the back of your hand. Use hit and run tactics. We will finish them in a week."
And so it happened. In many surprise manoeuvres, the Keladi army decimated the enemy forces in the unfamiliar forests. Proceeding to Bidanur, they joyfully freed their capital. 

The year was 1671. Recognizing her true leadership, Chennamma was formally crowned the Queen of Keladi. "With God as your witness, rule the kingdom justly and ably," the head priest blessed her. 

Chennamma took full control of the administration of the kingdom. "Our army must be expanded, Thimmanna. Also let our borders be further strengthened."
Then, one day, Chennamma came to the inauguration of a new settlement. With her was a young boy. 
"Basava, these are the houses that the king was once so busy constructing for wise men and teachers," Chennamma told the boy. "Now only you and I are here to see it complete. It shall be called 'Somashekhara'."
She turned to the people. "In memory of the king and with the people's heartfelt blessings, I have adopted you, Basava. You are our child - the king's and mine. Live to bring honour to his name, my son."

Chennamma gave special attention to Basava Nayaka's upbringing. "Mother, you are already so busy at court, giving in charity, doing your meditation and prayers. Why add the job of supervising my lessons to this long list of duties?" Basava asked. 
"Son, you are Keladi's future. In you lie our hopes and dreams. How can I not spare some moments for you?"

Then fell on Chennamma's shoulders, the burden of new wars. Many envied Keladi's long sea-coast that enabled her to trade profitably with the Arabs, the Portuguese, the Dutch and the English. Chikkadevaraya Wodeyar, the powerful king of Mysore, was one of these jealous rulers. He launched an attack on Keladi. 
Rebellious Keladi chieftains and neighbouring kings joined Mysore to oust the "woman in a man's world." But Chennamma, like the Goddess of War, enlisted victory on her side in the end. She treated her prisoners with utmost courtesy and won the admiration of her enemies. Keladi and Mysore signed a historic treaty of friendship, leading to a period of peace. 

One day during her daily alms-giving, Chennamma noticed a young monk. "That man - there's something about him."
"May God bless you," she said, giving him alms. "Who are you? Why have you embraced the life of a monk at such a young age?"
The monk looked around cautiously. "I am no monk, mother. Think of me as your son. I need a mother's protection more than you may know."
"May I ask why?"
"I am a fugitive, running away from a dangerous pursuer. I am Rajaram, son of Chhatrapati Shivaji."
"Ah...!" Chennamma gasped. 
"Having brutally killed my step-brother Sambhaji, the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb has sent his army to destroy me as well. I shall be temporarily safe in the fort of Jinji in the south. But every southern king, for fear of Aurangzeb, has refused me permission to pass through his kingdom."

He looked at her. "And you, mother? Will you face the wrath of Aurangzeb for granting Rajaram passage through Keladi?"
Chennamma smiled proudly. "You mock me in putting your request doubtfully as a question. Say instead, 'You will face the wrath of Aurangzeb for granting Rajaram passage through Keladi', for I will... I must... In accordance with the tradition of Keladi rulers it is my strict duty to give protection to those who ask it of me."

Just then, her commander arrived. "Highness, I do not agree with your decision. We shall never be able to stand up to the Mughal might - never! We must at least try to avoid this war."
Chennamma turned to her adopted son. "And what do you say, Basava Nayaka?"
"You have done right in giving refuge to Prince Rajaram," Basava said firmly. 
Chennamma turned to Rajaram. "You heard the prince, Rajaram. He speaks for Keladi. Now go; refresh yourself. When you feel rested enough, I shall arrange for men to safely escort you to Jinji."
She then turned to her commander. "Thimmanna, prepare for war."

Soon, the massive Mughal army under Jaan Nisar Khan got wind of Rajaram's presence in Keladi and immediately marched towards its borders. 
Even as the army approached, Chennamma received a threatening letter. *"The Emperor of Emperors, Aurangzeb, greets the Queen of Keladi. Give up my enemy, Rajaram to me, who I hear, is now in your kingdom. Only then will you avoid the holocaust my army has been instructed to release upon you."*
Chennamma wrote back fearlessly: *"Rajaram is no longer in my kingdom. But, as you know, he did pass through Keladi."*

Before Chennamma's letter could reach Aurangzeb, the Mughal army attacked, gaining entry into the kingdom. 
However, once the Mughals were in the thick mazes of the Malenadu jungles, the Keladi soldiers put into use their brilliant hit-and-run manoeuvres. They won many successes... but also lost in many encounters against the sheer numbers of the Mughals. 

Then the heavy monsoons set in. The dense forests, unfamiliar to Jaan Nisar Khan and his men, turned into soggy, impassable marshes. 
"Who is our enemy - Keladi, or this eternal sheet of rains? Or these leeches that suck a man dry? Or this ague that attacks us in epidemic form?" Khan coughed, miserable in his tent. The war proceeded, but on a slow, feeble pace. 

Then came the news that Rajaram had safely reached the impregnable fortress of Jinji. 
"The emperor's letter!" Khan announced to his officers. "He orders us to change our course and make straight for Jinji. What a relief! I am sick of this war."

Back at Bidanur, Thimmanna brought the news. "The Mughals have called for peace, highness. They leave for Jinji tomorrow."
"They are in for a long, difficult time. Jinji is too well fortified," Chennamma sighed in relief. "For us, their call for peace has come just in time. We could not have stood the strain much longer. But we tried, Thimmanna, and our conscience is clear. May God's hand be over Rajaram."

Some time later, a heartfelt letter arrived from Jinji. *"The rulers of kingdoms larger and more powerful than Keladi could not look the Mughals in the eye. But you gave me shelter, hospitality and passage. I shall never forget this kindness; shall ever stand in awe of this brand of courage. May Goddess Bhavani bless you and your people with every happiness. Your son, Rajaram."*

Chennamma ruled for 25 glorious years; years noted for efficient rule, strict justice, and immense generosity. Places of learning and temple towns flourished during her reign. 
On her deathbed, she called her son. "Basava, I think my time has come. Though not born of our bodies, the king and I could not have wished for a finer son than you. Before I go... you will have Keladi. What more precious treasures can there be? You are already an able administrator. But I have some final words of advice. Always keep your word. Beware of those who seek refuge. Never lose courage when faced with difficulties. Worship God and strive to know yourself."

When Chennamma died, Basava Nayaka came to the throne and earned a good name. Today, the fort of Chennagiri, named by Basava Nayaka after his brave mother, and the fine Kannada literary work 'Shivatattvaratnakara', written by Basava, remain as standing memorials of that glorious bygone age.
