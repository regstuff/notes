# Bhagawat: The Krishna Avatar - The Subduer of Kaliya

Shukadeva, the son of Sage Vyasa, was telling Pareekshit, the grandson of Arjuna, the continuing story of Lord Krishna. 
"You mean all the people of Gokul left the place together?" Pareekshit asked.
"Yes," Shukadeva replied. "The elders of the clan thought it was wiser to completely leave Gokul. Krishna, the beloved son of their leader Nanda, had been involved in too many terrifying mishaps in Gokul. So they packed up all their belongings and went to Vrindavan to settle there."

They found Vrindavan to be a truly beauteous spot in incredibly lush surroundings. Mount Govardhan loomed majestically at a distance, and the great river Yamuna, with its soft grassy banks, flowed gently past the forest. The new settlers first built sturdy sheds for their massive herds of cattle, and soon settled down comfortably after building their own homes.

Balarama and Krishna were absolutely delighted to be in Vrindavan. In their sweet, childish voices they would often praise their new place. 
"May I also go to graze the cows in the forest?" little Krishna asked Yashoda.
"No! You are much too small to go deep into the forest," she replied firmly.
"But I so much wish to go, Ma!" he pleaded.
"All right," Yashoda gave in. "You and Balarama may take the small calves to graze nearby. But don't go too far!"
While the calves grazed the tender grass contentedly, the boys would be deeply engrossed in their games. They played their wooden flutes and danced happily to the rhythm of their tinkling anklets, or they would playfully pretend to be angry bulls fighting each other.

One day, Krishna and his friends were herding their cattle together along the banks of the Yamuna. Suddenly, Krishna stopped to stare intensely at something he had seen. 
"Balarama! See that black calf!" he pointed. "Surely he is not one of ours."
"Yes, he looks a little odd," Balarama agreed.
"Shh! Let us approach him quietly from behind," Krishna whispered.

In reality, the calf was Vatsasura, a wicked demon in disguise sent by King Kamsa. 
Krishna stealthily approached the demon calf from behind and, holding his hind legs firmly in one hand, powerfully twirled him around and around. Then, with a casual twist of his wrist, he threw the demon calf high into the air onto the top of a massive wood-apple tree. 
The demon regained his true, enormous form as he fell to his death, crushing a number of huge trees under his immense weight. 

Krishna's other companions became incredibly excited as they saw it. 
"Well done!"
"Aha! What a feat!" they cheered.

When they were a little older, Krishna and Balarama were finally allowed to take the older cattle deep into the forest to graze. They would carry their morning meal with them, and joyfully roam all day, from forest to forest, with their vast herd. 
One hot day, Krishna said, "Come, let us drive the cows towards the lake. They must be thirsty now."

When the cows had quenched their thirst, suddenly a boy shouted, "Look there! What a huge crane!" 
Perched completely still beside the water was an unnaturally strong and massive crane. As they approached, it suddenly pounced on Krishna and aggressively picked him up in its sharp, deadly beak. 
Even as Krishna's companions stared open-mouthed with absolute shock, the massive creature swallowed Krishna in one single gulp. So terrified were the cowherds that they fainted from shock.

But as soon as Krishna reached down the dark mouth of the demon, he radiated intense, divine heat. The creature felt an unbearable burning inside his throat and quickly, agonizingly spewed out his victim. 
The crane was none other than Bakasura, a powerful demon friend of Kamsa. Enraged at being burned, he attacked Krishna violently with his sharp beak. 
Now Krishna sprang up, effortlessly grabbed the massive creature's upper and lower beak, and playfully tore it entirely apart, splitting the demon in two.

When his friends regained consciousness, the cowherds were overjoyed to have their playmate back and hugged him affectionately. "Come, gather the cattle now. Let us go back home," Krishna said calmly. 

They hurried home to eagerly relate the incredible incident to their parents. The gopas and gopikas gathered together in awe to hear the tale. "...and Krishna simply tore the giant demon apart as if he were tearing up a tiny reed," the boys exaggerated wildly.
"We were so relieved to see him safe!"
The elders were deeply amazed. "Once again this boy has miraculously escaped from the jaws of death."
"Whoever tries to harm this boy always seemingly comes to harm himself."
"Gargacharya was absolutely right. This boy is indeed exceptionally great."

Krishna and Balarama spent their days happily frolicking and playing with the other cowherds. Early at dawn, they would leave their homes with their herd and spend the day sporting safely in the forest and fields. They would creatively adorn themselves with wild flowers, fresh blossoms, and bright peacock feathers. They played long games of hide and seek, played sweet music on their flutes and pipes, or raced one another. 
"Come! Let's see who can run and touch Krishna first!" they would yell.
They would playfully imitate the sounds of the forest, buzzing like bees, cooing like cuckoos, or gracefully imitating the birds' walk and dance. 
"See! I am as graceful as a swan," Krishna would laugh. 
"I am a peacock. See my dance," another would reply. 
With the wild monkeys of the jungle, they would bravely scamper up the tall trees and playfully leap from one branch to another.

One day, when the cowherds were happily playing their simple games, Aghasura, a dreadful demon, saw them. 
"How happily they are playing here, as if they are just innocent children," the demon sneered. "But among them is that boy Krishna who brutally killed my sister Pootana and my brother Bakasura. Today I will completely kill him and all his little friends."

Aghasura, who was strong enough to terrify even the mighty devas (gods), assumed the terrifying form of an enormous, miles-long serpent. Like a huge, dark mountain, he spread himself across the path, keeping his cave-like mouth wide open. His lower lip rested flat on the earth, while his upper lip reached up to the clouds. Like a red ribbon of a road, his massive tongue emerged from within.

When the young cowherds saw the bizarre creature in their path, they were entirely amazed. 
"See this odd creature. It looks exactly like a giant python waiting to swallow us!" one boy joked.
"Ha! Ha! See how the rays of the sun are turning the clouds pink. They look like his upper jaw," another laughed.
Innocently, they continued to joke. "And the shadow of those clouds is also red. That must be his lower lip."
"See this long red-hued road. It looks just like the tongue of a serpent!"
"How uncomfortably hot the wind is! There must be a forest fire nearby!"
"And such a terrible stench too! Some poor creatures must have been burnt in the fire!"

"Should we dare to boldly enter his mouth? Supposing he actually swallows us?" one boy asked playfully.
"If he dares to do so, our Krishna will simply slay him like he did Bakasura," they agreed confidently. "It can't be a real serpent. Let's go! Let's go!"

Unaware of the true, terrible danger, the boys stepped cheerfully inside the mouth of the demon, gleefully clapping their hands. Before Krishna's very eyes, all the cowherds and their entire cattle herd had casually gone inside the asura's mouth. 
*How could they mistake this deadly serpent for an illusion?* Krishna thought in dismay. *I must save them now.*

Aghasura was patiently waiting for Krishna to enter his mouth so that he would swallow all of them at one single gulp and close his jaws. Moments later, Krishna bravely stepped in. 

Once inside Aghasura's dark, suffocating mouth, Krishna began to rapidly grow. He grew... and grew and grew. He expanded to an utterly colossal size. 
Now Aghasura could not breathe. His throat was entirely choked by Krishna's massive form. His eyeballs bulged out in agony, and soon his enormous body became completely lifeless. 

Krishna returned to his normal size. He, the cowherds, and the cattle safely stepped out entirely unscathed from the dead demon's massive mouth. 
After some time, the huge skin of Aghasura became stiff and dry, and it became a favourite, safe place for the children to play inside.

***

When Krishna and Balarama were six, they were allowed to go even further from home, into the deep forest with their cows. 
The incredibly scented flowers, the sparkling, clear waters of the beautiful lakes, the gracefully galloping deer, and the sweet sound of birdsong in the dense forests of Vrindavan never failed to completely delight Krishna. 

One day, Krishna and Balarama were resting under a tree after playing. Among their closest cowherd friends were Subala and Shridama. 
They said, "You, Balarama and Krishna, are truly unequalled in strength. Do you know there is a remarkably dense forest nearby with several palm trees heavily laden with delicious fruit?"
Shridama added nervously, "But there is a wicked, terrifying asura named Dhenuka living there in the guise of a massive donkey. He does not allow anyone, neither man nor beast, to ever encroach upon that area. Despite the lure of the sweet fruit, absolutely no one dares to go into that forest."
"But you must help us get that fruit, Krishna," Subala pleaded.

With a joyful laugh, Krishna and Balarama sprang up. "Come, show us the way."
"Yes, let's go right now."

When they went deeply into the forbidden forest, Balarama firmly caught hold of the trunk of one of the tall palm trees and shook it so vigorously that many ripe fruit fell down like rain. 
The asura Dhenuka came running angrily as he heard the loud sound. With his powerful hind legs, he gave a surprisingly swift, vicious kick to Balarama. But when he tried to do so again, Balarama simply caught hold of his hind legs, twirled him round so fast that the dizzy asura instantly died, and forcefully flung Dhenukasura up into the air. 

He fell heavily on the top of a tall palm tree. With a deafening noise, the tree crashed to the ground, felling a large number of other trees too.
Krishna and Balarama returned home incredibly happy. From that day on, the people of Vrindavan ventured fearlessly into the beautiful forest to eat the palm fruit and safely graze their cattle.

***

Balarama and Krishna were usually completely inseparable. One day, however, Krishna went with his other friends to the banks of the Yamuna, unexpectedly leaving Balarama behind in the village. 
It was a terribly hot, dry summer's day and the young cowherds were incredibly thirsty. 

"Don't drink the water from that pool!" Krishna warned them sharply. "It is completely poisoned."
"Yes, that highly venomous serpent Kaliya lives inside," a boy remembered.
"His venom is so unbelievably powerful that even the sturdy plants near the pond have entirely wilted and died," Krishna pointed out.
"But our cows are so incredibly thirsty!" the boys complained.

Unmindful of Krishna's stark warning, the desperate cowherds led the thirsty cattle to the river. No sooner had they drunk the tainted water than the cattle and the boys instantly collapsed on the river bank, lifeless. 

Krishna could not bear to see this tragic sight. Swiftly, he clambered up a tall tree overhanging the water. 
"Krishna! What are you doing?" the few remaining conscious boys yelled.
Without a word, Krishna powerfully dived from the high branches into the poisoned pond. Playfully, he began to splash about loudly in the water. 

Deep underwater, Kaliya, the massive, terrifying serpent, heard the insolent sound. Suddenly emerging violently from the depths, he appeared before Krishna. With a hundred and one forked tongues viciously lashing out of his hundred and one massive heads, he was an absolutely awesome, terrifying sight indeed.

But Krishna remained perfectly calm and simply gazed at him with a gentle, fearless smile on his lips. This blatant disrespect made Kaliya all the more violently angry. He aggressively pounced on Krishna and tightly caught the boy in his crushing coils. 

By now the cowherd boys who had watched from the bank became incredibly alarmed. 
"Krishna is caught! Krishna is caught!"
"Call Nanda! Quick!"
"Save him! Help!"

On quickly hearing the terrible news, Nanda, Yashoda, and others rushed frantically to the spot. Some of the cowherds had fainted completely out of fright. Yashoda was about to desperately jump into the deadly river, but the gopikas held her back forcibly. "Oh my son!" she wailed. 
They tried to urgently console her. "Don't worry about Krishna. Remember how bravely Krishna faced the wicked Pootana?"
Balarama, who had arrived, calmly coaxed Nanda against the dangerous idea of trying to rescue Krishna. He knew his brother's immense power.

Bound tightly as he was in the massive serpent's crushing coils, Krishna now simply made his divine body swell. He grew bigger... and bigger... till he effortlessly burst free from Kaliya's unbreakable hold. Kaliya broke away in shock, but raised his terrifying, venomous hoods ominously over Krishna. 
He blew out deadly sprays of venom through his nostrils. His burning red eyes glared hatefully at Krishna. Literal flames shot out of his many terrifying mouths. 

Krishna playfully and nimbly dodged every vicious attack of the serpent. When Kaliya finally seemed to have tired a little from the exertion, in one swift, impossible leap Krishna was incredibly on top of his massive hood. With rhythmic, heavy steps, Krishna began to dance beautifully but crushingly on Kaliya, nimbly stepping from one terrifying head to another. 

At the immense, divine touch of Krishna's feet, the snake's heads were utterly crushed. Kaliya soon entirely lost his massive strength. Blood oozed heavily from his many mouths and nostrils, and he finally fell in a weak faint. 
Regaining a little desperate strength, Kaliya would try to weakly lift one of his bruised heads. Quickly, Krishna would mercilessly jump heavily on it. 

Pareekshit urgently interrupted Shukadeva in his narration. "Did Krishna kill Kaliya?"
Shukadeva smiled gently. "Kaliya's end certainly seemed to be near. Just then, his many wives and children, who were also secretly living in that pond, appeared fearfully before Krishna, and desperately begged him to let Kaliya go. Their pitiful entreaties deeply moved Krishna's tender heart, and he mercifully decided to spare Kaliya his life."

Addressing the defeated Kaliya, Krishna said firmly, "You must not stay any longer in this pool. Your poison ruins the land. Take away all your kin and depart immediately for the deep, high seas."
As strictly commanded by Krishna, Kaliya humbly left the pool and went far away. The tainted water of the pool became miraculously, instantly pure and crystal clear, and the boys and cattle revived.

Peace and absolute tranquillity successfully returned to Vrindavan. Even in the incredibly hot summer days, the weather remained pleasantly cool there, surrounded as the place was with incredibly dense forests and sparkling, pure streams. Krishna, Balarama, and their friends revelled joyously in the beautiful surroundings, happily wrestling in the lush grass, dancing, and playing endless games of hide and seek.

***

One day when Krishna and Balarama were taking their cattle to graze peacefully by the river-side, Pralamba, a wicked asura, came there in the clever disguise of a normal cowherd. 
Krishna, however, was absolutely not fooled by the illusion. *There is something very odd about this new boy!* he thought instantly. *I will have to playfully trick him and find out his true intentions.*

He happily called all the boys. "Come! We will play an entirely new game today. We will split into two competing teams. Who will be the strong leaders?"
"You and Balarama, of course," the boys cheered. 
Krishna and Balarama quickly chose their teams and played several fun games. 

"We won! Our team has won this game," Krishna's team cheered. 
"Now for the next game," Krishna announced, "your losing team must carry our winning boys on their backs all the way up to that far tree there."
The cowherds were completely thrilled with the new, fun game. The winner would gleefully clamber onto the back of the loser. 

In the next round, Pralamba the disguised asura deliberately arranged to carry Balarama. *Krishna is far too strong for me. I will not be able to defeat him,* the demon thought cunningly. *Let me take this perfect chance and carry Balarama far away at least.*

So Pralamba smoothly hoisted Balarama onto his back and hurried to the target tree. But instead of stopping there as agreed, he ran incredibly fast further... and further into the deep woods. But soon he unexpectedly felt deeply fatigued with the immense, divine weight of Balarama. 
"Ohh! This little boy is incredibly too heavy. I c-a-n-t carry him any further," the demon panted. 
When he desperately paused for breath, panting and heavily sweating, he lost his concentration and assumed his true, terrifying asura form. The fair, shining form of Balarama shone radiantly atop the massive dark body of Pralamba, like the resplendent moon in a dark, stormy cloud. 

Now the giant asura began to magically fly upwards into the sky. 
"What's this? I am being carried up by this incredibly ugly creature," Balarama realized in shock. "Take this!" 
Balarama proved entirely true to his name, which means 'strength'. One single, incredibly powerful blow from his clenched fist directly to the demon's head was enough to instantly crush the asura's skull. Down he collapsed to the earth, dead in a massive heap. "Ahhh!"

The extremely anxious cowherds who had by now followed the pair into the woods, were incredibly relieved to see Balarama perfectly safe and sound. "Bravo, Balarama!" "Well done!" "You were really courageous, Balarama!"

Children that they were, the cowherds, happily along with Krishna and Balarama, soon went carelessly back to their fun and frolic. "Let's play hide and seek now!"
Meanwhile, completely ignored, their massive herds of cows wandered off... and strayed deeply into a dense, confusing wood nearby, in search of tender green grass. 

After a long while, the boys finally noticed their total absence. "Where are the cows? I can't even hear them lowing."
"They must have wandered completely off while we were playing."
"Let's look for them quickly. Here are some hoof tracks!"

But though they looked for a very long time, they couldn't find them. "Oh dear! I hope we find them soon."
"We absolutely cannot go back without them. And it is getting quite worryingly late."
"Here are some more clear tracks."
"There! I can see them now. Safely in that thicket there."

By the time they finally rounded up all their scattered cattle, the boys were extremely tired. "Come Rambha! Come! There, all of them are safely together now," they sighed in relief.

Just then, a terrifying forest fire unexpectedly broke out around them. Whipped up by a strong, dry wind, the massive flames spread incredibly rapidly. The panicked cowherds and terrified cattle suddenly found themselves trapped in the midst of a raging, inescapable fire. 
"Help! Krishna, Balarama!" they screamed. "Protect us, Krishna!"

Hearing the desperate, terrified cries of his companions, Krishna said perfectly calmly, "Don't be afraid. Just securely close your eyes and do not look."
"As you say..." they obeyed blindly, trusting him completely.

Krishna then opened his mouth incredibly wide, and miraculously, the massive leaping flames of the entire forest fire were powerfully drawn completely into his mouth. 

Shukadeva told Pareekshit about the incredible miracle performed by Krishna. "O Pareekshit, Lord Krishna literally swallowed the entire raging fire. When the frightened cowherds carefully opened their eyes, they were stunned. They found themselves completely safe, peacefully sitting in the cool shade of the exact tree where they had been playing earlier in the day."

At the end of this incredibly long and eventful day, Krishna and Balarama safely led their amazed friends back home, cheerfully playing their sweet flutes as if nothing at all had happened. 
"Thus ends the second session of our rendering of the tenth chapter of the Bhagawat Purana," Shukadeva concluded peacefully.
