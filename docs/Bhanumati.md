# Bhanumati: Granddaughter of Krishna

Bhanumati was the beautiful granddaughter of Lord Krishna, and the daughter of Bhanu. 

Years ago, when Bhanumati was just a young girl, Krishna and his family had gone to the forest of Raivataka to perform a grand sacrifice. The sacrifice was to celebrate the victory of Pradyumna (Krishna’s son) over the invincible Asura, Vajranabha. Pradyumna had returned to Dwaraka not only victorious but also with a bride—Prabhavati, the daughter of the slain Asura. 

While the elders were busy at the sacrificial grounds, young Bhanumati and her companions sneaked away to wander into the woods. "Come! This is our chance! No one's looking!" Bhanumati laughed. 
As she ran blindly through the trees, she accidentally bumped hard into a passing sage. 
"You ill-mannered child!" the sage roared. 

It was the formidable Sage Durvasa, known across the three worlds for his terrible temper. Enraged, Durvasa immediately cursed the girl. "You shall be abducted by a demon and carried away!"
Sage Narada, who happened to be nearby, was shocked. "O Durvasa, how can a wise soul like you curse an innocent child—and so harshly!"
Like all bad-tempered people, Durvasa quickly felt sorry for his rash words. "A curse once uttered by me cannot be revoked. It must come to pass," he sighed. "However, she will remain unsullied. She will also marry a worthy man and be a happy housewife."

Frightened, Bhanumati and her friends ran back to the enclosure. "Let's not breathe a word of this to anybody," Bhanumati told her friends. "Besides, what the sage said cannot be. Our city, guarded by my father, uncles, and grandfather, is impregnable." 
And so, Krishna and his large family returned to Dwaraka, completely unaware of the little drama in the forest.

The years went by. Bhanumati grew up to be a stunningly beautiful maiden. Her father, Bhanu, decided it was time to find her a husband. "I must soon hold a Swayamvara for her. The tests for the suitors shall be such that only the most worthy will be able to win her. I will hold it when we return from our cruise," Bhanu decided. 
Soon after, Krishna and the Yadava men, along with their wives, left on a long pleasure cruise, leaving the aged Ugrasena and Vasudeva to guard Dwaraka. 

Meanwhile, at Shatpura, a powerful Asura named Nikumbha sat brooding. He was the brother of Vajranabha. "Since that fateful day when I fled from the battle of Vajra, I've had only one thought: I must destroy the vain Yadavas and avenge my brother's death!"
Just then, a spy rushed in. "Lord! I have good news for you! Dwaraka is virtually unguarded. Krishna and the warriors have gone on a cruise."
Without wasting a moment, Nikumbha flew towards Dwaraka. 

At Dwaraka, Bhanumati and her companions were relaxing in the palace gardens. Suddenly, Nikumbha swooped down from the sky. "What better way to avenge my brother's death and dishonor!" he thought. "I will abduct Bhanumati. When they come to rescue her, I'll defeat them."
Before anyone could react, Nikumbha grabbed the screaming Bhanumati. "Help! The Asura Nikumbha!" she cried. 
Hearing the commotion, Ugrasena and Vasudeva rushed out. But Nikumbha quickly used his dark magic to make both himself and Bhanumati completely invisible, vanishing before their very eyes. 

Realizing they were helpless against the invisible sorcerer, Vasudeva sent an urgent message to Krishna. 
Krishna, along with Arjuna (the Pandava prince who was visiting) and Pradyumna, immediately returned. "Come on, Arjuna. He must have headed for his city. We'll pursue him on Garuda," Krishna declared, summoning the giant divine bird. "Pradyumna, you follow us in your chariot."

Meanwhile, flying high in the sky, Nikumbha thought he was safe. He dropped his invisibility. "Once we reach Shatpura, it is the Yadavas' lives you will have to beg for," he mocked his fainting captive. 
Suddenly, a great shadow covered him. "It's Garuda! Krishna has caught up with me!" Nikumbha panicked. "I'll use her as a shield to defend myself."

Krishna and Arjuna dared not strike Nikumbha with their heavy maces, fearing they might hurt Bhanumati. "I'll use my arrows. I'll be able to pierce him without harming her," Arjuna said, firing with unerring precision. 
Hit by the arrows, Nikumbha screamed. Realizing he could not hold onto the girl and defend himself, he suddenly vanished, turning invisible once more. 
"They will never know where I am," Nikumbha sneered. 

But he had not reckoned with Pradyumna. Arriving in his flying chariot, Pradyumna used the secret magical arts his wife Mayavati had taught him to expose the invisible. "There! There he is! Chase him!" Pradyumna shouted, revealing the Asura. 
Realizing he couldn't outrun Garuda, Nikumbha shapeshifted into a massive yellow vulture. Gripping Bhanumati tightly in his talons, he flew desperately towards Mount Gokarna. But Arjuna relentlessly pelted him with arrows, piercing his vitals. Exhausted and bleeding, the vulture took a sudden dip and plummeted toward the earth. 
Seizing the opportunity, Pradyumna swooped in with his chariot and caught the falling Bhanumati. "Leave her at Dwaraka and come back. We'll finish Nikumbha," Krishna commanded. 

As Krishna and Arjuna chased him, Nikumbha reached the narrow cave that served as the only entrance to his impregnable city of Shatpura. He dove inside and used his last ounce of strength to block the inner entrance with massive boulders. 
"Alas! He's gone. What shall we do?" Arjuna sighed. 
"We'll spend the night here. He's bound to come out, thinking we've gone away," Krishna smiled. 

Early the next morning, Pradyumna returned. "I left Bhanumati safe with Bhanu," he reported. "But where is Nikumbha?"
"The coward is hiding in his city. I'll bring him out," Pradyumna said. He raised his voice and began taunting the Asura loudly. "The Asuras are a valorous people indeed! When assailed, they hide behind women or the gates of their city."

The taunt worked. Seething with fury, Nikumbha charged out of the cave, completely invisible. "Come, taste the valour of the Asuras!" he roared, striking Arjuna down with a massive blow from his unseen mace. Pradyumna quickly used his powers to expose the Asura and attacked, but he too was struck down unconscious. 
Seeing his friends fall, Krishna raised his divine discus, the Sudarshana Chakra. Terrified by the deadly weapon, Nikumbha used his dark magic to pull his soul out of his physical body and fled, leaving his empty shell behind. 

When Arjuna and Pradyumna regained consciousness, they saw the lifeless body. "Father! The wicked Nikumbha is not dead," Pradyumna realized. "He has escaped, leaving his body here."
Hardly had he spoken when Nikumbha's empty body vanished. Suddenly, the sky and earth were filled with thousands of identical Nikumbhas! He had split his body into countless fractions and covered each with an illusory form. 
"We'll do the same," Krishna smiled. Instantly, Krishna, Arjuna, and Pradyumna split into innumerable forms to match the Asura army. 

"I must seize the forms of Arjuna. The deadliest arrows are his," the thousands of Nikumbhas thought. They swarmed and grabbed all the Arjunas. As soon as they were caught, the innumerable forms of Arjuna fused back into one. The innumerable forms of Nikumbha also fused back into one giant Asura, holding Arjuna tightly. 
"I shall carry him away, leave him inside my city, and return to tackle the other two," Nikumbha planned, flying off with Arjuna. 

Krishna and Pradyumna fired their arrows to cut him down, but to their utter amazement, every time an arrow hit, the fragment of Nikumbha sprang up as *two* Nikumbhas—each holding an Arjuna! 
"I will have to use my divine powers and discern the true form of Nikumbha," Krishna said, concentrating his cosmic vision. Spotting the true Asura glowing among the illusions, Krishna threw his discus. 
"Receive Arjuna in your chariot as he falls," Krishna ordered Pradyumna. 
The mighty Sudarshana Chakra struck the true Nikumbha, cleanly severing his head. As the evil Asura's body hurtled down, Arjuna slipped from his lifeless grasp and landed safely in Pradyumna's chariot. 

The three heroes returned to Dwaraka in triumph, but they were met by a weeping Bhanu. 
"The Asura is slain. But think of the shame!" Bhanu cried. "He abducted my daughter and tarnished her reputation. Who will marry her now?"

Just then, Sage Narada appeared and revealed the secret of Durvasa's curse and his blessing. "So, do not lament. What had to happen, happened. The sage promised she would marry a worthy man. Give your daughter to Sahadeva, for that Pandava is virtuous, heroic, and handsome."
Krishna smiled in agreement. He immediately sent an emissary to invite Sahadeva. As soon as Sahadeva reached Dwaraka, he and Bhanumati were joyfully married. 

As the happy couple left for Sahadeva's city, Krishna smiled. "The sage's curse did come true after all. But so did his blessings. Who would be a better husband than this powerful Pandava?"
