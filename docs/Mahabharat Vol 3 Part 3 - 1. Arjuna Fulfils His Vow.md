# Arjuna Fulfils His Vow

"When Arjuna took a vow to kill Jayadratha and twanged his bow, and Krishna blew lustily on his conch Panchajanya, the very earth trembled," Sanjaya narrated to King Dhritarashtra. 

In the Kaurava camp, Jayadratha was terrified. "Arjuna has sworn to kill me. I think I'll run away to my kingdom," he told Duryodhana. 

"Ah! But who can change Arjuna's oath?" Duryodhana wavered, before regaining his composure. "Fear not. Who can dare to kill you while you are amidst brave Kshatriyas? I will guard you. Besides, there are heroes like Karna, Chitrasena, Duhshasana, and several others who, along with their armies, will protect you."

Seeking ultimate assurance, they went to Drona. "I have taught you both equally well," Drona told Duryodhana. "But the possession of celestial weapons, constant practice, and his endurance gives Arjuna an edge over you. Yet there is no cause for you to fear Arjuna while I protect you. Such a formidable Vyuha (formation) shall I build that even Arjuna will not be able to break through."

That night, an anxious Arjuna drifted into a fitful sleep. In a dream, Lord Krishna appeared. "Meditate on Lord Shiva," Krishna advised. "If he grants you that weapon, you will surely overpower Jayadratha." Arjuna dreamt he and Krishna journeyed to the Himalayas and received the *Pashupata* weapon from Shiva himself. 

The dawn of the fourteenth day found the Pandavas in a determined mood. The Kauravas had arrayed their forces in a complex triple formation. Drona guarded the Lotus formation, and Durmarshana stationed in front of the Needle formation (Shakatavyuha). Deep inside, heavily guarded, stood Jayadratha. 

Arjuna, driven by his fierce vow, launched his chariot like a thunderbolt. "Lead me, O Krishna, to Durmarshana," he ordered. Like a strong gale scattering clouds, Arjuna's arrows destroyed Durmarshana's forces. He then bypassed Drona with Krishna's deft maneuvering and plunged deep into the Kaurava army. 

Furious battles raged across the field. Drona attacked Dhrishtadyumna. Bheema, Satyaki, Nakula, and Sahadeva fought desperately to support Arjuna's advance. 

As the sun began its descent, Yudhishthira grew deeply anxious, unable to hear Arjuna's conch or see his banner. He sent Satyaki, and then Bheema, to find Arjuna and assist him. Bheema smashed his way through the Kaurava ranks, even destroying Drona's chariot with a single throw of his mace, before finally reuniting with his brother. 

The sun was rapidly sinking towards the horizon. Jayadratha was still heavily protected by Karna, Ashwatthama, Kripa, Shalya, and Duryodhana. Arjuna fought fiercely, but the sheer numbers of the Kaurava heroes slowed him down. 

"I must see my brother at once. Then I will return and kill Karna," Arjuna told Krishna, feeling the pressure of time. 

Krishna realized the danger. "I will do as you bid to help Arjuna succeed," Krishna muttered. Using his immense divine powers, Lord Krishna created an illusion of darkness, making the sun disappear completely from the sky. 

Believing the sun had set and Arjuna had failed his vow, the Kaurava army rejoiced. The tension evaporated, and the warriors relaxed their guard. Even Jayadratha, curious and relieved, stretched his neck out to look at the sky. 

"Look Arjuna! How the relieved Jayadratha raises his head towards the sun!" Krishna cried out. With a wave of his hand, Krishna withdrew the illusion. The sun reappeared, shining brightly above the horizon. 

"The sun is yet in his southern voyage. I do not wish to die at this inauspicious hour," Bheeshma had said earlier, and now Jayadratha faced his doom. 

Krishna quickly cautioned Arjuna. "Hear me well, O Arjuna! Jayadratha's father has blessed his son thus: 'In battle, whoever causes the head of my only son to fall on earth, his head will immediately shatter into hundreds of fragments.' Therefore, you must shoot in such a way that his head falls onto his father's lap."

Without a moment's hesitation, Arjuna drew the Gandeeva and released a specialized, hawk-like arrow. The arrow struck Jayadratha, severing his head cleanly, and carried it skyward. Following Krishna's instructions, Arjuna shot a continuous stream of arrows, juggling the severed head through the air over a great distance. 

Far away from the battlefield, Jayadratha's father was sitting in deep meditation. Unaware of the gruesome delivery, the severed head of his son suddenly fell onto his lap. Startled, the old king stood up, and the head rolled off his lap onto the ground. Instantly, the father's own head split into a hundred pieces, fulfilling his own curse. 

As the sun finally set, Krishna blew his conch, signaling the fulfillment of the terrible vow. The Pandavas rejoiced, while the Kauravas stared in horrified silence at the sheer power and strategy of Krishna and Arjuna.
