# The Pious Cat and other tales - 1. The Pious Cat and other tales

## The Pious Cat
A hungry cat once snuck into a farmer's kitchen and began eating curds straight from a clay pot. However, the pot got stuck on her head! When it finally broke, the rim remained firmly stuck around her neck. 

Instead of being embarrassed, the clever cat decided to use it to her advantage. "I look good in it!" she thought. "A holy chain for the holy cat! And now to the forest."

In the forest, the cat approached a peacock, acting very saintly. "By this holy necklace of Lord Kedar, I renounce the way of beasts of prey," she proclaimed. "Let's go to the holy river Ganga to wash our sins away. Come, friend!"

Impressed by the "pious" cat who didn't try to attack him, the peacock joined her. Along the way, they were joined by a pigeon and a mouse, all completely fooled by the cat's holy disguise. 

At nightfall, they rested in a small forest hut. The cat offered to preach to them about dharma and karma while they sang hymns. The cat stood on a platform, clanging cymbals rhythmically while the peacock, pigeon, and mouse danced in devotion. 

However, as they danced faster and faster, the cat's true nature took over. Her eyes became bloodshot, and she began to drool. Suddenly, she jumped down, blocked the only exit, and dropped the cymbals with a loud *CLANG*. 

The quick-thinking mouse immediately began digging a hole to escape. 

The cat started taunting her victims before attacking. "You vain peacock! Whom would you woo with that display of feathers?"
"God gave me these feathers, holy one," the terrified peacock stammered. 
"And you, pigeon? Whom would you woo with your feet dyed red?"
"God gave me these red feet, holy one!"

Finally, the cat turned to the mouse. "You, my pretty one! I wish to woo *you*!" 

The cat lunged, but the mouse was too quick and slipped down his newly dug hole. The cat crashed into the wall. Taking advantage of the distraction, the peacock and pigeon quickly flew out the open door and escaped into a tree. 

Furious, the cat turned back to the mouse hole. "Come out, dear mouse," she coaxed. "I'll give you gold! Aren't you my nephew?"
"Of course I am your nephew!" the mouse squeaked from safely inside. "But I don't want your gold."

Just then, a stray dog entered the hut. The terrified cat quickly grabbed the cymbals, adjusted her "holy necklace," and tried her trick again. "By this holy necklace..."

But the dog was no fool. "Well, well! A pious cat! Marvellous!" he barked, springing on the cat and eating her in one bite. 

Later, the mouse crept out and found only the rim of the broken pot left. He dragged it out to the peacock and pigeon. "Look, Aunty has sent you this relic! A dog cut short her pilgrimage by bringing her salvation right here!"

Never again did the three friends feel the urge to go on a pilgrimage with a cat!

## The Quick-Witted Frog
A cheerful frog was exploring outside his pond when he suddenly found himself face-to-face with his worst enemy: a crow!

The crow quickly snatched the frog in his beak and flew away. The frog knew he was going to die, but he decided not to give way to despair. 

"HA! HA! Oh, brother crow, it's all so funny!" the frog suddenly laughed. 
"You fool, you are about to die and you laugh!" the crow squawked. 
"It is *you* who are in danger, my friend, not I," the frog lied cheerfully. "The snake who lives here is behind you, poised to kill."

Terrified, the crow flew to a tree. 
"Look behind you!" the frog yelled. "The cat, my foster sister, is about to pounce on you!"
The panicked crow flew to a statue of the fierce Goddess Kali. 
"HA! HA! I am a devotee of Kali, and she will not tolerate any harm done to me here!" the frog laughed. 

Exhausted, thirsty, and trembling with fear, the crow flew back to the frog's pond. 

"So you have no friend here to help you!" the crow panted, ready to finally eat the frog. 
"No, I am entirely at your mercy," the frog agreed meekly. "But will you do me one favor? Please sharpen your thick, blunt beak before you pierce me. It will make my death less painful. I warned you thrice when your life was in danger. You owe me this one last request."

The foolish crow agreed. "I'll wet my beak in the pond and then whet it on this slab till it is as sharp as an arrow-head. But don't you move from there!"

"I promise," the frog said. 

As soon as the crow turned his back to vigorously sharpen his beak on a rock, the frog silently slipped into the deep water and swam away. 

"Come, brother frog! My beak is sharp now, sharper than a sword's edge!" the crow called out proudly. 

"Is it? Good," the frog croaked from safely under a lily pad. "Now you can start sharpening your wits!"

## The Weaver Diwan (A Tale from Rajasthan)
A king once appointed a poor, brilliant weaver as his *Diwan* (Chief Minister). The powerful, high-born courtiers were deeply jealous and demanded that the low-born man prove his fitness for the post. 

Confident in his choice, the king agreed. The courtiers summoned the weaver and set an impossible task. 

"My friend, though now my Diwan, you were born a weaver," a courtier sneered. "We want you to weave us a robe of the finest gossamer. If you can't have it ready within a fortnight, you will be banished from the kingdom."

The weaver knew exactly what game they were playing. "Maharaj, weaving gossamer robes is child's play for me," he said calmly. "But you will have to provide me with one or two things before I can begin. First, I need some *ash of water*. Will you ask your courtiers to set fire to the palace tank and get me the ash?"

"Are you mad? How can we set fire to water?" cried the outraged courtiers. 

"Well," the clever weaver smiled, "if I can weave a gossamer robe out of thin air, surely you can set fire to water!"

The king burst out laughing, and the humiliated courtiers hung their heads in defeat, never challenging the clever weaver again.

## A Deer Story
A pregnant deer was resting on a forest path when a cart laden with hay came speeding towards her. The kind carter stopped just in time. 

"Brother, I am going to be a mother. Please help me," the deer pleaded. "Give me that cart-load of hay to keep my young ones warm."
Moved by her plight, the kind carter gave her the entire load. The deer built a warm, cozy hut near a fragrant saffron plant. 

Later, another cart full of wooden doors passed by. The deer asked for one, and the carter kindly gave it to her. Then came a cart full of rice and jaggery; the driver gave her enough to cleanly plaster her hut's walls. Finally, a cart carrying sugar, ghee, and special herbs passed by, and the driver generously supplied her with food. 

The deer was very happy. Soon, she gave birth to four beautiful fawns. She stayed safely inside her cozy hut for forty days, nursing them. 

When it was finally time for her to go out and graze, she warned her fawns, "Don't let anyone come in when I am away. I will sing a special song at the door, and only then must you open it."

A wicked, lame wolf overheard this. When the mother deer left, the wolf went to the door and sang the mother's song in a high voice. 

The innocent fawns were about to open the door when the fragrant saffron plant outside suddenly spoke! "Don't, children! Don't open the door! It's the lame wolf outside, not your mother!"

Furious, the wolf pulled the saffron plant out by its roots. He tried again, but the dying plant managed to croak out another warning. Seething with rage, the wolf burned the plant to ashes, mixed it with water, and drank it so it could never warn them again. 

When the wolf sang the song a third time, the fawns heard no warning from the plant. Believing it was safe, they opened the door. The wicked wolf swallowed all four fawns whole! 

When the mother deer returned, she found her babies gone and wolf tracks leading away. Crying loudly, she followed the tracks to the river, where the bloated wolf was resting. 

"Oh, cruel wolf! Give me back my children!" she cried. 
"I'll eat you too!" the wolf snarled, springing at her. 

But he was too full and heavy. As he leaped, he fell hard on his stomach. *PHUT!* His bloated stomach burst wide open!

Out popped the four perfectly safe, pretty young fawns— and miraculously, a fresh, green saffron plant! 

The overjoyed mother deer tenderly took her babies home. She replanted the brave saffron plant by the door and lovingly watered it with sweetened milk every day. As for the wicked, lame wolf, he was never heard of again.
