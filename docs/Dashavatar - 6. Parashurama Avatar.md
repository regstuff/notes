# Parashurama Avatar

King Arjuna, the ruler of the Haihayas, had propitiated Lord Dattatreya and had secured from him boons which made him invincible. But Arjuna misused his powers and became a merciless tyrant. 
To vanquish him and all such evil Kshatriyas, Lord Vishnu came to earth as Rama, the youngest son of Sage Jamadagni, and his wife, Renuka. 

"It's strange that my son should know so much about weapons and their use," Sage Jamadagni would often think, watching young Rama train. 
It was true. Though Rama was the son of a Brahman, he had an inordinate love for weapons and his absolute favourite was the axe (parashu), which gave him the name Parashurama. 

One day, while Parashurama and his brothers were away, King Arjuna entered Jamadagni's hermitage with his soldiers. 
"Welcome, O mighty king. Rest here with your men and taste the yield of Kamadhenu, my divine cow," Jamadagni greeted them warmly. 
But when Arjuna and his men were fully fed, the greedy king thought, "If Kamadhenu can feed so many in such a short time, I must possess her."
He turned to his men. "Seize the animal and her young one. Take them to my capital."

A few days later, when Parashurama returned to the hermitage, Jamadagni wept, "O Rama, the king has taken away my beloved Kamadhenu and her calf."
"How dare he commit such a sacrilege! I shall deal with him," Parashurama growled. 
Brandishing his heavy axe, an enraged Parashurama stormed into Mahishmati, Arjuna's capital. 
Arjuna was alarmed when he heard the news. "It's Parashurama! Send out seventeen of our most powerful Akshauhinis to meet him," he ordered. (An Akshauhini consisted of 1,09,350 infantry, 65,610 cavalry, 21,870 chariots and an equal number of elephants).

But the terrible Parashurama destroyed the massive army in no time. Not a single man escaped alive. 
Enraged at the total destruction of the core of his army, Arjuna himself rushed forward. But as Arjuna rushed towards him with uprooted trees and rocks, Parashurama swiftly lopped off all his thousand arms with his axe... and then brought his axe down on the tyrant's neck, severing his head. 
Parashurama then gently led Kamadhenu and her calf back to his father, and life in the hermitage went on as before. 

But not for long. The sons of Arjuna were absolutely bent on avenging the death of their father. One day, while Parashurama was away in the woods, they snuck into the hermitage. 
"Lord!" Renuka screamed as they rushed in. 
They fell upon the meditating sage and mercilessly cut off his head. 
"No! Spare my husband! Please spare him!" Renuka cried out. When the deed was done, she wept, "Rama! Oh Rama!"

Meanwhile, Parashurama was on his way back to the hermitage. Suddenly he stopped. "That cry! It's my mother's. She seems to be in distress. I must hasten to her side."
When Parashurama reached the hermitage, he was aghast at the bloody sight. "Mother! Who were the foul murderers? Tell me, mother!"
"Rama! Oh Rama! Rama!" she sobbed inconsolably. 

But Renuka's agony was so deep that she could only beat her breast and cry. Full of burning grief and anger, Parashurama made a terrible vow. 
"Twenty-one times have you beaten your breast in grief, mother. I shall wipe out the vile race of the Kshatriyas as many times!"
And, gripping his blood-stained axe, Parashurama set out to accomplish the rest of the dark task for which he had come to earth. 

Thus did Lord Vishnu, as the fierce Parashurama, exterminate those wicked Kshatriyas who had become a great burden to Bhoomidevi and her children.
