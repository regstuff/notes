# Bheeshma in Command

The Kurukshetra war raged on. As the days progressed, the immense power of the Kaurava Commander-in-Chief, Bheeshma, became terrifyingly apparent to the Pandavas.

On the second day, Arjuna and Bheeshma engaged in a spectacular duel. Bheeshma's arrows enveloped Arjuna, and Arjuna retaliated with equal ferocity. Drona engaged Dhrishtadyumna, while Bheema fought a desperate battle against the Kalinga forces and their war elephants. When Bheeshma attacked Bheema, Satyaki and the Pandava forces rushed to his aid, pushing back the Kauravas. 

By the third day, the Kaurava army was retreating in fear from the sheer fury of the Pandava assault. Duryodhana, watching his army scatter, rushed to Bheeshma and angrily accused him of holding back. "O Grandsire! You are deliberately not using up all your strength. Why did you not tell me before the hostilities commenced that you would not fight the Pandavas?"

Stung by Duryodhana's harsh words, Bheeshma promised to unleash his full wrath. Like a roaring fire, he attacked the Pandava army, slaughtering thousands. Seeing the immense destruction, Krishna turned to Arjuna. "O Arjuna, the time has come to vanquish your enemies! Strike at Bheeshma."

But Arjuna, still fighting out of a sense of duty rather than a desire to kill his beloved grandsire, fought mildly, parrying Bheeshma's arrows but not pressing the attack. Bheeshma's arrows continued to decimate the Pandava ranks and even struck Krishna. 

Unable to watch the slaughter any longer, Krishna's patience broke. He threw the reins of the horses, jumped down from the chariot, and rushed towards Bheeshma, his Sudarshana Chakra blazing in his hand, ready to strike down the patriarch himself. 

Bheeshma calmly dropped his bow and welcomed the attack. "O Krishna! I know not of a higher honour than that of dying at your noble hands. I welcome your attack, O Lord."

Arjuna, horrified that Krishna was about to break his vow of not fighting, chased after him. He caught Krishna by the waist, but Krishna dragged him along for several steps before Arjuna finally stopped him. "O Krishna, do not break your promise! I will not take sides in this battle anymore... I mean, I will do as you bid. I will destroy the Kauravas."

Pacified, Krishna returned to the chariot. Aroused to true fury, Arjuna unleashed the celestial Mahendra weapon. The devastating shower of arrows caused massive casualties among the Kauravas, forcing them to flee in terror as the sun set on the third day. 

Over the next few days, the battles grew increasingly fierce and bloody. Bheema engaged in brutal combat with Duryodhana and his brothers, killing many of Dhritarashtra's sons. In one encounter, Bheema struck Duryodhana with an arrow that made him faint in his chariot, forcing his charioteer to carry him off the battlefield. 

Ghatotkacha, Bheema's Rakshasa son, also fought with terrifying power, using illusions and immense strength to crush the Kaurava forces until Bheeshma intervened to stop him.

On the eighth day, tragedy struck the Pandava camp. Arjuna's brave son, Iravan, fought a magnificent battle against the forces of Shakuni. Duryodhana dispatched the Rakshasa Alambusha to kill him. After a fierce, magical fight where both warriors changed forms, Alambusha managed to behead Iravan. Arjuna was heartbroken. "O Krishna," he wept, "what use are wealth and power gained through large-scale destruction?"

The fighting continued to escalate. On the ninth day, Bheeshma fought like a man possessed. He caused complete panic in the Pandava army, routing their forces and seemingly fulfilling his vow to slaughter ten thousand warriors a day. No one, not even Arjuna, could stand against him.

That night, a deeply depressed Yudhishthira consulted Krishna. "O Krishna, Bheeshma is destroying our army like a raging fire. It is better to retire to the forest than to lead these brave men to their deaths. I see no way to defeat him."

Krishna offered to take up arms again. "I will slay Bheeshma myself, O Yudhishthira, if Arjuna hesitates."

But Yudhishthira refused to let Krishna break his vow. Instead, they decided on a drastic course of action. Yudhishthira, Krishna, and the Pandava brothers went unarmed to Bheeshma's tent in the dead of night. 

They bowed respectfully to the Grandsire. "O Grandsire," Yudhishthira pleaded, "how can we win this war when you are fighting against us so fiercely? Tell us, O wise one, how your own death can be brought about."

Bheeshma, knowing his duty but also desiring the ultimate victory of Dharma, revealed his weakness. "As long as I hold a weapon, no one can defeat me. But I have taken a vow never to strike a woman, or anyone who was previously a woman. Your commander, Shikhandi, was born a female. If you place Shikhandi in front of Arjuna, I will lay down my arms. Then, Arjuna can strike me down."

The Pandavas returned to their camp with a heavy heart, knowing what they had to do. 

On the morning of the tenth day, as the two armies arrayed for battle, Arjuna placed Shikhandi at the vanguard of his forces, directly facing Bheeshma. The stage was set for the fall of the greatest warrior of the Kuru race, as the war entered its most pivotal phase.
