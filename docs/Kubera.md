# Kubera: The Lord of Wealth

Long ago, during the Krita Yuga, the serene ashram of Sage Trinabindu was a paradise of enchanted trees and sparkling pools where celestial Apsaras and Gandharvas sang and danced. One day, Sage Pulastya, one of Lord Brahma’s mind-born sons, came to reside in the ashram. In time, he married Trinabindu’s daughter, Manini.

"Our son will grow up to be a great scholar," Pulastya foretold as Manini held their newborn baby. They named the child Vishrava.

As Vishrava grew, he inherited his father's wisdom and devoted himself to prayer and penance. Impressed by his virtue, Rishi Bharadwaja offered his daughter, Devavarnini, in marriage to Vishrava. Soon, Devavarnini gave birth to a radiant boy.

"The child is exactly like his father," declared Rishi Bharadwaja. "He shall be called Vaisravana."

Vaisravana, who would come to be known as Kubera, grew up gentle and devout. When he came of age, he sought his parents' blessings. "I am going into the deep forest to perform tapasya," Kubera announced.

For a thousand years, Kubera underwent severe penances. He sustained himself first on water alone, then on air, and finally on nothing at all. Pleased by such fierce devotion, Lord Brahma appeared before him in a golden aura.

"My child, I am pleased with your devotion," Brahma spoke gently. "What do you desire?"

"Lord, grant that I become a Lokapala—one of the Guardians of the world," Kubera requested humbly.

"So be it!" Brahma proclaimed. "Henceforth, you shall be the Guardian of all the wealth in the world, equal to Yama, Indra, and Varuna. I also bestow upon you the Pushpaka Vimana, the magnificent flying chariot crafted by Vishwakarma."

Filled with gratitude, Kubera returned to his father Vishrava. "Father, Lord Brahma has appointed me a Lokapala and granted me a magical aerial vessel," Kubera said. "Now I wish to reside in a sanctuary where all creatures may live together in peace."

"There is such a place," Vishrava replied. "It is situated atop Mount Trikuta, and it is called Lanka."

Seeing his son's curiosity, Vishrava shared the origin of Lanka: "Long ago, a fierce rivalry broke out between Vasuki, the king of serpents, and Vayu, the god of wind. Vasuki wrapped his massive coils tightly around Mount Meru, while Vayu blew with devastating fury to dislodge the peak. As the storm threatened to tear the cosmos apart, Lord Vishnu intervened and persuaded Vasuki to yield. When Vasuki uncoiled, Vayu struck with a final gust, snapping off Mount Trikuta and hurling it into the southern ocean. It was there that the divine architect Vishwakarma built the golden city of Lanka for the Rakshasas."

"Why did the Rakshasas leave Lanka abandoned?" Kubera asked.

Vishrava explained the tale of the Rakshasa lineage: "It began when Lord Brahma felt gnawing hunger while reciting the Vedas. From his hunger sprang the Yaksha Praheti, and from his wrath emerged the Rakshasa Heti. Heti’s descendants—Malyavan, Sumali, and Mali—gained boons of invincibility from Brahma and conquered kingdom after kingdom. They commissioned Vishwakarma to build Lanka as their capital. But their arrogance grew boundless, and they challenged the Devas. Lord Vishnu came to the defense of the gods, slaying Mali with his Sudarshan Chakra. Fearing total destruction, Sumali and Malyavan abandoned Lanka and fled to the underworld of Patala."

With his father's blessing, Kubera traveled to Lanka and established his reign. Under his rule, the golden city flourished in unprecedented peace and prosperity. The Yakshas hailed Kubera as a just and generous sovereign, while the Lord of Wealth soared across the skies in his golden Pushpaka Vimana.

Meanwhile, in Patala, Sumali grew bitter watching Kubera's glory. Seeking to reclaim earthly power, he instructed his daughter, Kaikasi: "Go to Sage Vishrava and seek him as your husband, so that your children may match the glory of the Lord of Wealth."

Kaikasi approached Vishrava while he was deep in meditation. Opening his eyes, the sage granted her request but warned, "Because you came to me at an inauspicious hour, your sons will be fierce and cruel, save for one."

Kaikasi bore four children: Dasagriva (who would become Ravana), Kumbhakarna, Surpanakha, and the righteous Vibhishana. As the brothers grew, Kaikasi urged Dasagriva, "Look at your half-brother Kubera. He rules Lanka and commands the Yakshas. I want you to eclipse him in power!"

"I give you my word, Mother," Dasagriva vowed. "I will become more powerful than my half-brother could ever dream to be."

Dasagriva performed extreme penances to Lord Brahma, severing his own heads one by one into the sacred fire. Impressed, Brahma granted him invincibility against Devas, Yakshas, Nagas, and wild beasts. In his supreme arrogance, Dasagriva refused to ask for protection against mortal humans, deeming them harmless. Meanwhile, Vibhishana asked only for lifelong righteousness, and Kumbhakarna was tricked by Goddess Saraswati into asking for eternal sleep.

Armed with his boons, Dasagriva set his sight on Lanka. He sent an emissary to Kubera’s court demanding the surrender of the city, claiming it as his ancestral heritage.

Demonstrating noble grace, Kubera declared, "Dasagriva is my brother. He is welcome to share my kingdom."

However, Sage Vishrava warned his son, "Dasagriva will never be content with sharing power. His boons make him ruthless. You must yield Lanka and seek refuge on Mount Kailasa, the abode of Lord Shiva."

Kubera obeyed his father's counsel. Gathering his subjects and treasures onto the Pushpaka Vimana, he migrated north to Mount Kailasa. High in the Himalayas, Kubera constructed the glorious city of Alakapuri, adorned with the breathtaking Chaitra gardens.

Back in Lanka, Dasagriva was crowned king and unleashed a reign of terror across the three realms, defeating even Indra and looting Devaloka. Distressed by his half-brother's tyranny, Kubera sent a peaceful ambassador to Lanka.

"Tell Dasagriva," Kubera instructed the messenger, "that power should be used to rule wisely, not to strike terror. As his elder brother, I urge him to turn away from violence."

When the messenger read the letter in the court of Lanka, Dasagriva flew into a rage. "How dare Kubera send me advice!" he roared. "Prepare the war chariots! We march on Alakapuri!"

The Rakshasa army breached the gates of Alakapuri. The Yakshas fought back valiantly with sorcery and weapons. Manibhadra, Kubera’s faithful commander, cut down hordes of Rakshasas before falling in battle against Dasagriva himself.

Seeing his commander fall, Kubera stepped onto the battlefield. "You are a fool to think your crimes will go unpunished, Dasagriva!" Kubera cried out.

"Save your breath and fight!" Dasagriva sneered.

Kubera launched the celestial Agneyastra, unleashing torrents of divine flame. Dasagriva countered with the Varunastra, extinguishing the flames with roaring waters. When divine weapons failed, Dasagriva resorted to dark sorcery, shifting forms to overpower Kubera and leaving him grievously wounded on the battlefield.

"Take something precious from his realm as a trophy of your victory," suggested Dasagriva’s minister.

Dasagriva set his eyes on the golden Pushpaka Vimana. Claiming the flying chariot as his own, he returned to Lanka in triumph.

Centuries passed. When Ravana abducted Sita, Lord Rama of Ayodhya built a causeway across the ocean, invaded Lanka, and slew the demon king in a legendary war. Rama placed the righteous Vibhishana upon the throne of Lanka.

To honor Rama, Vibhishana offered him the Pushpaka Vimana to carry Rama, Sita, and Lakshmana back to Ayodhya. Upon reaching Ayodhya, Lord Rama stood before the magical chariot.

"Now that we have reached home, it is time you return to your rightful owner," Rama spoke with reverence.

The Pushpaka Vimana soared back across the sky to Alakapuri. As the golden vessel landed before him, Kubera's heart swelled with joy.

"Lord Rama has conquered evil and restored harmony to the world," Kubera proclaimed warmly to the Vimana. "Go back to him whenever he calls, and carry him wherever he wishes to go."

And so, Kubera, the benevolent Lord of Wealth, resumed his peaceful reign from Alakapuri, forever remembered for his wisdom, patience, and devotion.
