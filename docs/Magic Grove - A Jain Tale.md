# The Magic Grove

In a small village lived a young, hardworking twelve-year-old girl named Vidyutprabha. Ever since her mother passed away four years ago, the entire burden of the household had fallen upon her slender shoulders. One hot day, while exhausted from grazing the cattle under the scorching sun, she made a suggestion to her father, Agni Sharma.

"Father, I find it difficult to run the household alone," she said. "Why don't you marry again?"
Agni Sharma agreed, hoping it would ease her life. "It's a good idea, Vidyutprabha. I'll do as you say."

Unfortunately, his second wife turned out to be lazy and cruel. When the stepmother gave birth to her own daughter, Vidyutprabha's workload only doubled. "Anyway, I have only myself to blame," she sighed one day while grazing the cows. "I wanted more leisure and lost even what little I had."

Suddenly, she heard a hiss. A snake slithered up to her, but to her astonishment, it spoke. "Don't be afraid. I will not harm you. Some snake charmers are after me. Please protect me."
Without hesitation, the kindhearted girl hid the snake in the folds of her skirt. When the fierce snake charmers arrived demanding if she had seen their prey, she calmly replied, "No, sirs. Even if it had passed, I wouldn't know. I was fast asleep."

Once the men were gone, she told the snake it was safe to come out. But the snake vanished into thin air! In its place stood a radiant Deva, a celestial being.
"You were not dreaming, my brave girl," the Deva smiled. "Your courage and kindness are amazing. You deserve a boon. Ask for it."
"O Deva," Vidyutprabha replied selflessly, "if you wish to grant me a boon, please create a shady grove over the heads of my cattle. They find the sun's rays unbearable."

Touched by her unselfish nature, the Deva raised his hand. Instantly, a magnificent, floating grove of trees laden with juicy fruits and fragrant flowers appeared above her head. "Here you are! Whenever you are out of your house, this grove will shelter you," the Deva promised. "Never again will your cattle have to suffer the heat. And if ever you need me, you only have to think of me. I'll be there."

One day, the King of Pataliputra was passing through the forest with his retinue. His men ventured near the floating grove, startling the cattle. Vidyutprabha awoke and ran after her cows. The king and his men watched in absolute awe as the magical grove moved along with the girl! 
"Is she an apsara?" the king wondered aloud. "Whoever she is, her place is in a palace. Certainly not among these cows!"

The king’s minister tracked down Agni Sharma and proposed marriage on behalf of the king. Overjoyed, Agni Sharma accepted. The king granted him the revenue of twelve villages, and Vidyutprabha was given the royal name 'Aramashobha'. 

At Pataliputra, Aramashobha finally lived a life of ease and comfort as the beloved queen. However, back in the village, her wicked stepmother burned with envy. "I should never have let the girl go out to graze the cows," she seethed. As the years passed and her own daughter came of age, her jealousy turned murderous. "If Aramashobha dies, the king might decide to marry my daughter!"

The stepmother prepared a poisoned sweetmeat and placed it in a sealed pot. She handed it to Agni Sharma to deliver to the queen. On his journey to the palace, the tired old man fell asleep under a tree. A watchful Yaksha residing in the tree sensed the stepmother's evil intent. Using his magic, the Yaksha secretly replaced the poisoned sweetmeat with a harmless one. Aramashobha unknowingly ate it and suffered no harm.

Furious that her first attempt failed, the stepmother tried again. But once more, the vigilant Yaksha foiled her plot by replacing the poison. When Agni Sharma returned the second time, he brought news: "The queen is expecting a baby."
"She'll be dead before that happens," the stepmother hissed. She hired a well-digger to dig a deep, treacherous well in their backyard. 

Following tradition, the king allowed Aramashobha to return to her parents' home for the birth of her first child. A few days after her beautiful son was born, the stepmother lured her into the backyard. 
"I had this well specially dug for your safety, my queen," the stepmother lied smoothly. As Aramashobha stepped close to look, the wicked woman violently shoved her into the dark abyss!

"O Deva! Save me!" Aramashobha screamed as she plummeted. 
Instantly, the Deva appeared at the bottom of the well and caught her safely. Enraged, he declared, "I'll slay her this minute and take you back to the palace."
"No! Please don't! Think of my father," Aramashobha pleaded.
"All right," the Deva relented. "Then I'll build a home for you in the netherworld below this well, where you shall know no want."

Up above, the stepmother hastily disguised her own daughter in the queen's veils and smeared her face to look bloated and ill. When the king arrived to take his family back, he immediately noticed the missing magical grove.
"Where is your grove, your majesty?" the king asked suspiciously.
"Oh! It has gone to the well to get watered. It will soon follow," the step-sister stammered. 

When they reached Pataliputra, the grove never arrived. The king realized with sinking dread that he had been duped. "She is not my queen! She's an impostor," he realized. "Then where is Aramashobha?"

Meanwhile, deep in the netherworld, Aramashobha wept for her newborn son. "O Deva, I long for my son. I want to hold him in my arms."
The Deva agreed to transport her to the palace at night but laid down a strict condition. "You must return before sunrise. If you don't... a dead snake will drop from your hair and I can never again come to your aid."

That night, Aramashobha appeared in the royal nursery. She held her baby and left celestial fruits and flowers from her magic grove in his cradle before vanishing. The next morning, the fake queen foolishly claimed she had brought the divine fruits herself. When the king demanded she produce more instantly, she made excuses. Certain of her deception, the king decided to keep watch that night.

At midnight, Aramashobha appeared again. Just as the sun began to rise and she turned to leave, the king leaped out and caught her hand. "My lord, you! Please let me go. I'll come back tonight and tell you all," she begged desperately.
"No, Aramashobha. Now that I've found you, I will not let you go," the king refused firmly. "Tell me all. Till you do, I won't let go of your hand."

Trapped, Aramashobha frantically blurted out her story, rushing to explain the Deva's warning. "...And the Deva said that if I didn't return before sunrise... a dead snake would drop from my..." 
Before she could finish her sentence, the sun breached the horizon. A dead snake tumbled from her hair onto the floor. 

"Alas! It's happened. I am undone. I have lost my Deva!" Aramashobha cried out in despair, fainting into the king's arms. 

When she awoke, the king held her close. "Take heart, my dear. You won't need the Deva anymore. Since I won't let you out of my sight, no one can ever harm you again."

Aramashobha pleaded for mercy for her step-sister. The king agreed to spare her life but permanently banished both the step-sister and the wicked stepmother from the kingdom. Reunited at last, Aramashobha and the king lived happily for many years, enjoying all the luxuries of life. When their son came of age, they crowned him king and peacefully joined the holy order of Acharya Virabhadra.
