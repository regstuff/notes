# Hanuman to the Rescue

During the great war in Lanka, when Rama, the exiled prince of Ayodhya, attacked the city, Ravana, the demon king of Lanka, himself rode out one day to the battlefield. In the course of the day's battle, Ravana hurled his spear at Rama's brother, Lakshmana. 
As Lakshmana fell, Rama came forward and drove away the mighty Rakshasa. 

"Oh, my noble brother... where will I find another like you?" Rama wept, holding the unconscious Lakshmana. 
"Save him, Sushena," Rama begged. "You are a great physician."
"Life has not left him as yet," Sushena examined him. "I could easily revive him with the herb called Vishalya Karani. But I must get it before daybreak."
"Where is this herb to be found?"
"On Mount Gandhamadana. But it would take eighteen years to go there and come back!"
Hanuman stepped forward. "I could do it in much less than eighteen hours. Go, Hanuman. Look for a golden creeper with blue flowers," Sushena instructed. "It grows on either side of the river which flows down one of the peaks of Mount Gandhamadana."
"Cast aside your anxieties, Lord Rama. Lakshmana will be saved," Hanuman promised and left at once. 

Hanuman made himself larger and larger, and with one great leap, rose high up into the sky. As he flew along, Ravana saw him from Lanka. Realising Hanuman was going for the Vishalya Karani, Ravana summoned his uncle Kalanemi. 
Kalanemi was a fearful monster with four heads, eight eyes, eight arms, and was also a magician. 
"Go to Mount Gandhamadana and destroy Hanuman with your magic," Ravana ordered, promising him half the kingdom. 
Kalanemi was terrified. "What use would that be to a dead man? The son of the wind is cunning. He'll dash out my brains." But seeing Ravana's anger, Kalanemi had no choice but to go. "What a predicament. I am dead if I go and dead if I don't."

Kalanemi used his magic to transport himself to Mount Gandhamadana. He transformed himself into a hermit, created a beautiful hermitage out of thin air, and waited. When Hanuman arrived, he warmly welcomed him. 
"Greetings, holy sir," Hanuman saluted him. "I am in great haste. I am looking for a herb called Vishalya Karani."
"Your quest can wait. How can I send you away without feeding you?" the false hermit insisted. He told Hanuman to go bathe in the nearby river to refresh himself, plotting that the ferocious crocodile in the river would tear him to pieces. 

Hanuman waded into the river. The crocodile attacked swiftly, but Hanuman caught hold of the reptile, flung it to the shore, and tore open its breast. 
Instantly, the crocodile's body vanished, and a beautiful celestial dancer appeared. "Once when I was dancing, my foot accidentally touched the person of Indra. He turned me into a crocodile and decreed I should live in this river till liberated by you. Beware of the false hermit, O son of the wind!" she warned before vanishing. 

Hanuman returned to the hermitage where Kalanemi was dreaming of ruling the best half of Lanka. 
"You're back!" Kalanemi stammered. "Please have some fruit."
"Throw aside your fruit, false one... and prepare for your death!" Hanuman declared. A fierce battle ensued. Hanuman flung Kalanemi to the ground, coiled his tail around the monster, and hurled him high into the sky. Kalanemi shot through the heavens like a comet and crashed right into Ravana's court in Lanka. 

Realising Kalanemi had failed, Ravana summoned the sun god. "Listen to me. Unless Lakshmana gets a certain herb, he'll die at daybreak. I want you to hasten that hour. Go and rise from your appointed place. Now!" 
The sun, bound by Ravana's power over the three worlds, had to obey. 

As Hanuman frantically searched for the herb, he was startled to see a rosy glow on the horizon. "Is the sun ready to rise? I must stop him at once!" 
Hanuman darted forward, caught hold of the sun's chariot, and whirled it around. 
"Who are you, great sir? Let me pass," the sun god pleaded. 
"I am the son of the wind. I have come to get a herb to save Lakshmana's life. Do not rise before Lakshmana is restored to health, I beg you."
"My sympathies are with Rama. But how can I save myself from Ravana's wrath if I disobey him?"
"I'll tell you how. Come closer," Hanuman said. As the sun leaned forward, Hanuman caught him in a warm embrace and bundled him securely under his arm! 

Hanuman rushed back to Gandhamadana but couldn't find the golden creeper with blue flowers. He asked some Gandharvas singing and dancing nearby, but they teased him, not knowing who Rama was. Enraged, Hanuman made himself as huge as a hill and fought them, breaking their weapons. Realising he was wasting time, he decided, "If I can't find the herb... I'll take the whole mountain with me!"

He lifted the entire mountain and flew back to Lanka. Rama and the monkey army were amazed to see Hanuman descending with a huge mountain. Sushena went up, gathered the right plants, made a paste, and held it to Lakshmana's nose. Lakshmana stirred and sat up, fully healed! 

"I am indebted to you, O son of the wind. Now please take the mountain back," Rama said. 
Hanuman picked up Mount Gandhamadana again and set off. As he flew over Lanka, Ravana saw him and sent seven huge lion-faced Rakshasas after him. They surrounded Hanuman in the sky, thinking he was defenseless while holding the mountain. But Hanuman shot out his tail, coiled it around six of them, and flung them down to their deaths. The seventh fled in terror. 

Hanuman restored the mountain to its original place. Before leaving, he crushed some leaves of the healing plant, dropped them into water, and sprinkled it over the Gandharvas he had wounded earlier. As they woke up, they tried to attack him again, but Hanuman leapt up and sped back to Lanka. 

His comrades raised a loud cheer. "What is that shining under your arm, son of the wind?" Rama asked. 
"Oh, that is the sun. Ravana had ordered him to rise before the appointed hour. So I had to hold him prisoner," Hanuman smiled. 
"How extraordinary! Let him go at once," Rama chuckled. 

Hanuman lifted his arm and let the sun go. They all paid homage to the sun god as he departed to rise from his usual place. 
"You have saved Lakshmana's life, Hanuman," Rama praised him. "I am ever indebted to you."
