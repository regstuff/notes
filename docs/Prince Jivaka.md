# Prince Jivaka

Based on the epic Tamil poem, *Jivaka Chintamani*. 

King Sachchandan of the mighty Kuru dynasty ruled the prosperous kingdom of Hemangada from his capital, Rajamapuram. Deeply in love with his beautiful Queen Vijaya, the king spent all his time in her company, disastrously neglecting his royal duties. He foolishly entrusted the entire administration of the state to his ambitious and ruthless minister, Kattiyangaran. 

This was a fatal mistake. Seizing the perfect opportunity, Kattiyangaran quietly amassed power, gained the loyalty of the army, and staged a sudden, bloody coup. 

"Your Majesty, all is lost!" a loyal guard gasped, bursting into the royal chambers. "Kattiyangaran has usurped the throne and declared he will wipe out the entire royal family!"

Knowing he had to fight, King Sachchandan first secured his pregnant queen. He placed the weeping Vijaya into a secret, mechanical flying machine—a rare marvel of engineering—and launched it out of the palace windows. He then bravely rode out to face the traitor, but was tragically killed in the fierce combat that followed. 

High in the sky, Queen Vijaya wept uncontrollably. Unable to pilot the complex machine, she crashed heavily into a dark, lonely cremation ground far outside the city. The shock of the crash induced labor, and that very night, amidst the burning pyres, the destitute queen gave birth to a beautiful baby boy. 

"O fatherless child, I must abandon you to save your life," the heartbroken queen sobbed. She slipped her husband’s heavy, royal signet ring onto the baby’s tiny finger, hid behind a tree, and prayed for a miracle. 

Soon after, a wealthy merchant named Kandukkadan passed by. Hearing the cries, he found the radiant infant. "A child... beautiful as the moon," he marveled, taking the baby home and naming him Jivaka. 

Later, one of Kandukkadan's trusted spies informed him of the child's true royal identity, having recognized the king's signet ring. Swearing to protect the rightful heir, the merchant raised Jivaka in absolute secrecy alongside his own newborn son, Nandattan. The two boys grew up as inseparable brothers. 

When Jivaka came of age, Kandukkadan employed a brilliant, wise Acharya (teacher) named Accanandi to educate the boys. Under his strict tutelage, Jivaka mastered all the arts, sciences, and martial skills, growing into a spectacular, unparalleled warrior. 

One day, taking Jivaka to a secluded spot by the river, Acharya Accanandi revealed the explosive truth. "Your education is complete, my child. Now, I must tell you who you truly are." He narrated the tragic story of King Sachchandan, handed Jivaka the royal signet ring, and urged him to prepare to reclaim his throne. His worldly duty fulfilled, the wise Acharya left to seek Nirvana. 

Jivaka kept his identity absolutely secret, waiting for the right moment. During a grand spring festival in the capital, the royal elephant suddenly went completely amok, terrifying the citizens. Showing immense courage and superhuman strength, young Jivaka single-handedly wrestled the massive beast into submission. 

However, Kattiyangaran’s guards arrived to arrest the boy, claiming he had "damaged royal property" by wounding the elephant. Before they could seize him, a sudden, blinding dust storm—secretly summoned by Jivaka’s loyal friends—engulfed the street, allowing Jivaka to escape into the wilderness. 

Thus began Jivaka's years of wandering and adventure. In the kingdom of Hemapura, he impressed King Dadamittan with his flawless, unmatched archery skills in a royal mango orchard. Grateful for his help in defending Hemapura from a sudden enemy siege, the king proudly gave Jivaka his daughter, Princess Kanakamalai, in marriage. 

Meanwhile, Jivaka's loyal foster-brother Nandattan and his friends scoured the lands searching for him. Deep in the Dandaka forest, they stumbled upon an ascetic woman who turned out to be none other than Queen Vijaya! Reunited with his mother, Jivaka joyfully embraced her, promising to fulfill his destiny and avenge his father. 

The perfect opportunity soon arose. In Rajamapuram, Kattiyangaran announced a grand *Swayamvara* (husband-choosing ceremony) for a beautiful princess named Ilakkanai. The impossible challenge was to shoot an arrow through a rapidly revolving mechanical boar. 

Kings and princes from all over the world failed miserably. Finally, Jivaka, disguised as a common suitor, stepped forward. With flawless precision, his arrow pierced the exact center of the revolving boar! 

As the crowd erupted in cheers, the furious Kattiyangaran finally recognized the face of his old king in the young victor. "It is the boy Jivaka!" the usurper screamed. "Kill him!"

A massive, bloody battle instantly broke out in the *Swayamvara* hall. Jivaka, supported by his powerful father-in-law's troops and his loyal friends, fought like a lion. In a fierce, one-on-one duel, Jivaka finally struck down the treacherous Kattiyangaran, avenging his father and liberating Hemangada. 

Jivaka was gloriously crowned King of Hemangada. He married Princess Ilakkanai, richly rewarded his foster father Kandukkadan, and ruled with unmatched wisdom, bringing immense prosperity and peace to his people for many long years. His mother, her life's purpose fulfilled, peacefully returned to her ascetic life in the forest. 

Years later, while strolling through his magnificent royal gardens, King Jivaka witnessed a profound scene. A large, aggressive monkey brutally attacked a smaller monkey, snatching a juicy fruit from its hands. Moments later, a burly human guard walked by, struck the large monkey with a heavy stick, and casually stole the fruit for himself. 

Jivaka stood utterly still, a profound realization washing over him. *"This is the symbol of worldly riches,"* he thought. *"The stronger seize things at the cost of the weaker. I fought a bloody war to seize this kingdom from a usurper. The cycle of power and violence never truly ends."*

Deeply moved by the inherent unfairness and fleeting nature of worldly power, Jivaka immediately summoned his court. He formally crowned his eldest son as his successor, took off his royal robes, and donned the simple white garments of a Jain monk. 

Having conquered his kingdom, Jivaka finally set out to conquer himself, ultimately attaining the supreme, eternal peace of Nirvana.
