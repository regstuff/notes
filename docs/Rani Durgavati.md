# Rani Durgavati: The Brave and Wise Queen

In the sixteenth century, Princess Durgavati was the beautiful, highly spirited daughter of the proud Rajput Chief of Mahoba, belonging to the legendary Chandela dynasty. Unlike most princesses, Durgavati was an incredibly skilled rider, an expert archer, and frequently went on dangerous hunting expeditions with her father. 

One day, she heard fascinating tales of incredible bravery about a young prince named **Dalpat Shah**. Dalpat was the son of Sangram Shah, the powerful ruler of the neighboring Gond kingdom of Garha. Intrigued by his legendary heroism, Durgavati's heart swelled with deep admiration for the Gond prince. 

Hoping to unite their kingdoms, King Sangram Shah formally sent a royal marriage proposal to Mahoba. However, Durgavati's fiercely proud father was deeply insulted. 

"A princess of the famous Chandela dynasty to marry a mere Gond?" he scoffed arrogantly. "Never! It may be an absolute honor for you, Sangram Shah, but it would mean total disgrace for our ancient family."
He rudely rejected the royal proposal, dismissing Dalpat as a man of 'low origin'. 

When Durgavati heard of her father's arrogant rejection, she was absolutely furious. "My father is completely wrong," she told her trusted maid. "A man should be judged not by his birth, but strictly by his true character and incredible achievements!"

Defying her father's rigid, orthodox beliefs, the courageous princess secretly penned a desperate letter to Prince Dalpat. 
*"I will marry Dalpat, come what may!"* she declared fiercely in her letter. *"It is infinitely better to be the proud wife of a brave, low-born hero than the miserable wife of a spineless, high-born fool! Take Sangram in and attend to his needs. Come out and see for yourself, sir. It's unbelievable!"* Wait, no, she simply wrote to Dalpat to come and claim her hand.

When Prince Dalpat read Durgavati's incredibly bold, romantic letter, his father, King Sangram Shah, was absolutely delighted. "I shall immediately order our massive army to march directly into Mahoba!" Sangram declared proudly. 

Seeing the terrifyingly vast Gond army heavily armed and marching aggressively toward his city, the Chief of Mahoba suddenly realized he was completely outmatched. Knowing a bloody war would inevitably result in a humiliating defeat, he quickly swallowed his false pride, threw open the city gates, and unexpectedly gave Prince Dalpat a grand, royal welcome! 

Amidst spectacular feasting and joyous celebrations, the incredibly brave Princess Durgavati was happily married to her chosen hero, Prince Dalpat. 

Their marriage was filled with deep love and absolute happiness. A year later, they were blessed with a healthy baby boy, whom they proudly named Bir Narayan. But tragically, just three short years later, their joy was utterly shattered. King Dalpat fell seriously ill, became entirely bedridden, and died, leaving his young queen a heartbroken widow. 

Though she was expected to immediately perform *Sati* (self-immolation) on her husband's funeral pyre, Durgavati made an incredibly difficult, brave decision. "No!" she declared firmly. "For the sake of my infant son, I absolutely must not give in to my blinding grief. Great, terrifying tasks lie ahead of me. I must prove entirely worthy of my noble ancestry and fiercely rule the kingdom with absolute courage till my son comes of age."

Rani Durgavati took total control of Garha, proving to be an exceptionally brilliant, fiercely capable ruler. When the ambitious Baz Bahadur, the powerful Afghan ruler of neighboring Malwa, aggressively invaded her kingdom, the fearless Rani personally led her troops into battle. She utterly routed Baz Bahadur's massive army! 

Baz Bahadur stubbornly attacked Garha several more times, but the brilliant Rani Durgavati decimated his forces in every single encounter, completely looting his massive wealth and military supplies. Soon, the once-empty coffers of Garha were overflowing with captured treasure, and her fiercely protected people thrived in absolute peace and incredible prosperity. 

Meanwhile, her son, Bir Narayan, grew into a strapping, courageous young lad under her strict, loving supervision. 

However, the immense, unparalleled prosperity of Garha soon attracted the dangerous, greedy attention of the ultimate superpower of the era: the great Mughal Emperor, Akbar. 

Asaf Khan, the ruthless Mughal governor of the adjoining eastern provinces, eagerly approached Akbar. "Alampanah (Refuge of the World), why haven't we conquered and completely annexed the wealthy province of Garha?" Asaf Khan asked greedily. "It shall be done with absolute ease. I will quickly invade Garha and violently bend its arrogant Rani into total submission!"

With Akbar's full imperial blessing, Asaf Khan marched an unbelievably huge, heavily armed Mughal army directly toward Garha. 

When the terrifying news reached her, Rani Durgavati remained utterly undaunted. Ignoring the cowardly advice of her terrified ministers who begged her to hide, she immediately donned her shining royal armor. "Bring me my war-elephant," she commanded fiercely. "Ask Bir Narayan to prepare for bloody battle. I shall personally lead my brave men against the imperial army!"

The Rani brilliantly chose a narrow, highly strategic gorge as her defensive position. When the massive Mughal army marched blindly into the narrow pass, the Rani's hidden soldiers violently attacked from all sides. Caught completely by surprise and unable to maneuver their massive numbers, Asaf Khan's elite army was totally routed by evening! 

Determined to finish the enemy off, the Rani aggressively wanted to launch a surprise, devastating night attack to completely wipe out the retreating Mughals. But tragically, her over-cautious old counselors strongly insisted on waiting until daylight. Yielding to their fatal advice, the Rani waited. 

It was a disastrous, fatal mistake. The delay allowed Asaf Khan to safely bring up his devastating, heavy imperial artillery. 

The next morning, the skies darkened heavily as terrifying Mughal cannons brutally blasted the Rajput lines. As the Rani bravely led a desperate, heroic cavalry charge through the pouring rain, young Bir Narayan was severely wounded in the fierce fighting and had to be hastily carried off the battlefield. 

Seeing their beloved prince fall, many of the Gond soldiers completely lost heart and began to frantically flee in absolute terror. 

Despite being heavily outnumbered and abandoned by her own terrified men, Rani Durgavati fought on like a raging goddess. Suddenly, a stray Mughal arrow violently pierced her temple. She bravely ripped it out, but another deadly arrow instantly pierced her neck, fatally wounding her. 

As the massive Mughal army closed in from all sides to capture her alive, the fiercely proud Rani realized the battle was completely lost. 
"Rani, please let me take you to a safe place," her loyal mahout begged desperately. 

"And perchance fall alive into the filthy hands of the enemy?" the proud Rajput Queen scoffed fiercely. "No! I am completely overcome in battle. God completely forbid that I should also be overcome in name and royal honour!"

Pulling out her sharp, jeweled dagger, the ultimate, fearless Rajputni forcefully stabbed herself directly in the heart, choosing to die in absolute, immortal glory rather than ever live as a humiliated, defeated captive of the Mughal Empire.
