# Varaha Avatar

After Pralaya, at the beginning of the new Kalpa, while Brahma was busy in the work of creation, Bhoomidevi (Mother Earth) was being violently tossed about on the chaotic waves... and eventually sank to the dark bed of the ocean. 

Soon after, Swayambhuva Manu and Shatarupa came into being. They respectfully bowed to Brahma. 
"Father, tell us how we may serve you as well as ensure our happiness in this world and the next," Manu asked. 
"Keep your heart free from jealousy, beget many virtuous children and rule over the earth, for you shall be the ruler of men," Brahma blessed them. 
"I shall obey your wishes. But please let me know where my progeny and I should live. Bhoomidevi lies submerged," Manu pointed out. 

Brahma was troubled. "What shall I do? I will have to bring the earth up. O Lord Vishnu, please help me," he prayed silently. 
As Brahma intensely meditated on the Lord, a tiny boar, hardly as big as his thumb, suddenly emerged from one of his nostrils. 
"What is this weird creature! And coming out of my nostril!" Brahma gasped. 

But even as Brahma gazed at it amazed, it grew... and grew... till it was the size of a large elephant. Brahma was full of wonder. "Could it be Lord Vishnu himself?"
While Brahma pondered thus, the boar, which was now as incredibly large as a huge mountain, roared. 
"It is none other than Lord Vishnu!" the devas cheered. 

Then, emitting another terrifying roar, it took one mighty leap into the air... tore the heavy clouds with its hoofs... and dived straight into the ocean. 
It nosed its way about the murky ocean-bed, in search of the earth... till it reached the other end of that fathomless ocean and discovered in its depths the object of its quest - Bhoomidevi. 

Meanwhile, the mighty and arrogant asura, Hiranyaksha, thundered up to heaven, spoiling for a combat. But finding the devas hiding, he sneered, "Fie on the Lord of Heaven and his devas whom I have scared into hiding!"
Disgusted and angry, he marched up to Varuna, Lord of the Waters, and feigning humility, fell at his feet. 
"O supreme lord! O guardian of a whole sphere! O ruler of great fame! Give me battle," Hiranyaksha demanded mockingly. 
"He mocks me knowing fully well that he is the mightier of the two," Varuna thought. But Varuna curbed his anger. "I have given up fighting. I am too old now. Lord Vishnu is the one you should approach. He alone would be an equal opponent for you. Go seek him out."

Learning from the wandering sage Narada where Vishnu currently was, Hiranyaksha charged eagerly towards the ocean-bed. 
Meanwhile the divine boar had just gently dug his powerful tusk into the ocean-bed... and lifting Bhoomidevi onto it... had begun rising towards the surface, when Hiranyaksha arrived. 
"Oh! So he is now in the form of a wild beast!" Hiranyaksha scoffed. 

Hiranyaksha challenged the beast with a loud roar. "Come on, you beast! Leave Bhoomidevi alone. She has been entrusted to us by the maker of the universe."
When the boar completely ignored him, Hiranyaksha yelled, "You possess little actual power. All your battles are won by Yogamaya (the power of illusory creation), your only strength. But now you are too close to escape unscathed."

The boar steadily continued rising and Hiranyaksha gave furious chase. 
"Wait! Aren't you ashamed of yourself? Running away from a foe who challenges you!"
But the boar did not even look back. "Let him rave," Vishnu thought. "My task is to take Bhoomidevi to safety. She trembles with fright. Ah! We've almost reached the surface."
"Shameless wretch. The sharpest insult fails to pierce your thick hide!" Hiranyaksha spat. 

As soon as he reached the calm surface of the ocean, the boar placed Bhoomidevi gently on it and blessed her. "May you support yourself in keeping with the divine order."
Then he turned to face Hiranyaksha, his speech heavy with sarcasm. "O wretch, scared away by your mace, we who have stolen the earth must take our stand in the battlefield. For, having incurred the wrath of a powerful enemy, where can we go?"

Hiranyaksha became furious. Seething with pure anger, he lifted his heavy mace and sprang towards the animal. But the boar stepped aside quickly and raised his own. 
They fought for a long while, each violently striking out with his mace. 
As the dark hour of twilight drew near, Brahma spoke to the boar from the heavens. "Slay him before he grows formidable with the approach of an hour that is naturally favourable to him. Slay him and rid us of this pestilence."

Hearing Brahma's words, Hiranyaksha roared and hurled his mace at the animal. The deadly mace was effortlessly caught by the boar as if it were a toy. 
Enraged, Hiranyaksha began hitting out wildly with his fists on the broad chest of the boar. 
The boar playfully hit the demon behind his ears. 
With that single, precise blow, Hiranyaksha reeled and fell dead. 

The devas rejoiced and praised the Lord. "O Lord, only you in your immaculate goodness could have lifted Bhoomidevi to the surface of the ocean. Only your invincible self could have saved us from this scourge."
