# The Legend of Lalitaditya - 1. The Legend of Lalitaditya

After the fall of the Gupta Empire, Northern India broke up into minor principalities which were always at war with one another. It was a time of vulnerability, and foreign invaders like the Tibetans and the Arabs were planning to plunder Hindustan. 

But one man stood in their way: Lalitaditya, the powerful ruler of Kashmir, aided by his wise minister, Mitra Sharma. 

When Mummuni the Arab tried to invade, Lalitaditya's valiant Kashmiri soldiers routed the marauders in no time. To commemorate his victory, Lalitaditya built many beautiful cities, but the one he was proudest of was Parihasapura. 

One day, the king visited the older city of Pravarapura. The Nagarseth treated the king to excellent wine. 

"Even poets say there is no city on earth like Pravarapura," the Nagarseth boasted. 
"But Maharaj... it cannot compare with my Parihasapura," a courtier chimed in. 

Drunk and offended, Lalitaditya yelled, "Then they lie! General! Burn down this city! Reduce it to ashes before dawn!"

Mitra Sharma was shocked. He quickly told the General, "Wait, General. Go back to your revelry. I will supervise the operations." 

Mitra Sharma then secretly ordered his men to burn haystacks instead of the city. 

At dawn, Lalitaditya woke up, saw smoke rising into the sky, and remembered his drunken order with horror. "Was the order I issued last night carried out?" he asked Mitra Sharma in a panic. 

"Yes, Maharaj. I supervised the operations myself," Mitra Sharma said calmly. 
"Oh, no! I was not myself... it was the wine... you should have known better, Mitra Sharma..." the king wept. 

Mitra Sharma smiled. "Maharaj, please look out of this window."

The king looked out and saw the city of Pravarapura standing tall and safe. "What you saw were only haystacks in flames, Maharaj," Mitra Sharma explained. "You have saved the city— and my honour!" the king realized gratefully. 

When they were back at the capital, Lalitaditya decided to launch a great campaign of conquest, a *digvijaya*, to unite the warring kingdoms. 

He first marched north and defeated the Turks. There, he recruited an extremely intelligent Turk Buddhist named Chankun to be one of his ministers. 

Next, he turned to the most powerful king of the plains, Yashovarman of Kanyakubja. Not wanting unnecessary bloodshed, Lalitaditya proposed a peace treaty. 

However, during the signing, Mitra Sharma noticed the treaty placed Yashovarman's name before Lalitaditya's. "Our emperor being the more powerful of the two, he must have precedence," Mitra Sharma argued. 

Yashovarman's men refused. "Then the proposal stands withdrawn. Let the battle continue to the finish!" Lalitaditya declared. He swiftly defeated Yashovarman. 

Lalitaditya continued his conquests, marching through Bengal, the southern kingdoms, and the western coast. Soon, his army reached the borders of a great, deadly desert. 

A mutilated man staggered out of the desert. He was Dharmapriya, the minister of the desert chief. 

"I advised my king to surrender to you. So he labelled me a traitor, mutilated me and had me thrown into the desert to die," Dharmapriya claimed. He offered to guide Lalitaditya's army through a secret, short route in the desert to take the enemy by surprise. 

Chankun was suspicious, but Lalitaditya trusted him. They marched into the desert. 

On the fifteenth day, they ran out of water, and the desert seemed endless. Dharmapriya revealed his true colors. "I mutilated myself to trick you into entering the jaws of death!" he laughed. "I am willing to lay down my life to save my master."

The Kashmiri soldiers were furious and wanted to kill him, but Lalitaditya stopped them. "I admire his loyalty to his chief," the king said. 

Despite the scorching heat, Lalitaditya goaded his army on. At the foot of a sand dune, he noticed dried blades of grass. He hurled a spear into the ground. When he pulled it out, damp sand clung to the blade! 

"Dig up the ground at this spot," he ordered. They found fresh, sweet water! 

Having survived the desert trap, Lalitaditya easily defeated the stunned desert chief. 

Lalitaditya spent his entire life in relentless crusades to protect his land and culture. He fought back the Tibetans and the Arabs, and brought all the minor kingdoms under his sway. 

He also improved his kingdom, introducing Persian water wheels (araghats) to irrigate the fields and unearthing ancient, buried temples. 

At last, his ministers told him, "Maharaj, you may now rest your sword. There are no more battles to be fought."

"No, a life of rest is not for me," Lalitaditya decided. 

He sent for his ministers. "I have decided to leave on an expedition to explore the unknown. Don't try to stop me."

Entrusting the kingdom to the care of his ministers, the great Emperor Lalitaditya walked away alone into the mountains, never to be seen again.
