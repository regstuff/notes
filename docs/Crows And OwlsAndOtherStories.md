# Panchatantra: Crows and Owls and Other Stories

## Crows and Owls

A colony of crows dwelt in a great banyan tree in a dense forest. The name of their king was Cloudy. The crows were constantly harassed by their powerful enemies, the owls. Their king, Foe-Crusher, would ruthlessly attack any crow he came across in the dark. 

Then one day, Cloudy called a meeting of his five wise counsellors. "The owls are becoming a menace. They are arrogant and powerful and they attack us at night when we cannot see. We cannot counter-attack during the day because we don't know where they dwell. Advise me, counsellors. What should we do?"

"Let us sue for peace. It is always advisable to make peace with powerful foes," the first counsellor said. 
"I don't agree. One should never make peace with wicked creatures. Let us declare war on the rascals!" the second argued. 
"The enemy is vicious and powerful. I advise neither peace nor war but a change of residence. Let us go away from here," the third suggested. 
"Go away where?"
"A crocodile if he stays at home can defeat an elephant. But if he leaves his swamp, even a dog can make him run. I say, let us stay here and fortify our positions!" 
"In my humble opinion we should form alliances with other friendly birds," the fourth advised. 

Then Cloudy turned to a very old, battle-scarred crow named Live-Strong. "And what do you advise, revered sir?"
"I advise duplicity, my lord," Live-Strong whispered. "Attack me at once with a great show of anger."
"Attack you? My wisest minister?" Cloudy gasped. 
"Yes. We must put up this show for the benefit of our friend over there." Live-Strong pointed a wing at an owl hiding in the branches. 
"An owl!"
"Let him think we've fallen out. Throw me out of the tree. Then, with all your subjects... fly to Antelope Mountain."
"And what will you do?"
"I will befriend the owls and win their trust. And then I'll find a way to bring about their doom."

Cloudy immediately understood. He turned on Live-Strong with fake fury. "Now do as I say, you coward!"
"How dare he speak like that to our king!" the other crows gasped, unaware of the trick. 
"I'll wring his neck for him," Cloudy shouted. "So you have turned traitor! We don't want any traitors here! Get out! And stay out!" 
Cloudy violently tossed Live-Strong out of the tree, and the old crow tumbled to the ground. 

"Ah, they are fighting amongst themselves," the spying owl thought with glee. 
Soon after, the entire flock of crows took to the skies, flying away to Antelope Mountain. 
"The cowards have fled!" the owl hooted, flying off to report to his king. 

Old Live-Strong, who had been watching the proceedings from the ground, was pleased. "The first part of my plan has succeeded. Next comes the most dangerous part... but there's no turning back now. Well, here goes. Caw! Caw!"

The owl soon returned with Foe-Crusher, who was astonished to see the battered condition of the old crow. 
"How have you come to such a sorry state, my dear sir?" Foe-Crusher asked. 
"This is the way I have been rewarded for giving good advice, your majesty," Live-Strong croaked weakly. "My master, Cloudy, became furious with me when I urged him to pay you tribute. He assaulted me and threw me out of the tree. Henceforth I shall work for the destruction of all crows. Now I throw myself at your mercy."
"I will talk it over with my advisers," Foe-Crusher said. 

Foe-Crusher had five ancestral counsellors. Their names were Red-Eye, Fierce-Eye, Hook-Nose, Flame-Eye and Wall-Ear. 
"The crow seeks asylum. What is your opinion, Red-Eye?"
"Slay him without further delay, my lord. He is a crow!" Red-Eye warned. 
But the other counsellors were more charitable. "Grant him refuge, O king! It would be wrong to kill one who seeks your protection. He may prove useful to us in our war with the crows."
"Do not listen to them, your majesty. Slay him! He is up to no good!" Red-Eye pleaded. 
"I have already made up my mind, Red-Eye. The crow stays with us. We shall take him to our fortress."

The owls lifted up Live-Strong and carried him to their fortress. "This is our fortress, sir. Here you may stay in comfort and honour," Foe-Crusher declared. 
"O king, it would not be proper for me to enter your fortress," Live-Strong said humbly. "I am, after all, a crow and unworthy of the honour. Give me a little place near the gate and that is enough."
"You may stay near the gate if it pleases you, my friend."

Live-Strong made himself comfortable near the gate and began to enjoy a life of ease and comfort, unperturbed by Red-Eye's hostility. The owls pampered him with rice and mangoes. 
"How they pamper him. It makes me sick!" Red-Eye fumed. "Your majesty, the crow must go! He is making a fool of you! We are fattening him while he is planning our destruction. He must be laughing at us."
"Leave that poor creature alone. He is harmless," Foe-Crusher dismissed him. 

"They won't listen to me. I can't stay here any longer," Red-Eye decided. He gathered his followers and told them to pack up their belongings. "The end is at hand. I cannot save the king as he refuses to be guided by me. Let us, at least, save ourselves while we can. Anyone who continues to live here does so at his own peril. Let us go forth and seek another fortress in the mountains."

Live-Strong was overjoyed when he saw Red-Eye leaving the fortress with his followers. "Only he could have foiled my plans. The rest of them are numskulls. The days of the owls are numbered. From tomorrow I shall start building my nest of death."

Each day thereafter, Live-Strong went into the forest and returned with a dry twig, which he dropped inside the gate. 
"I am building a nest, your majesty. I hope you do not mind," Live-Strong said. 
"Of course not! Let us know if you need any help," the foolish king replied. 
Soon there was a large pile of dry twigs at the gate, but none of the owls stopped to wonder why their guest required so big a nest. 

Then one morning when the owls were fast asleep, Live-Strong quietly left the fortress. He flew directly to a nearby mountain where Cloudy and the other crows were waiting for him. 
"We are delighted to see you back with us!" Cloudy cheered. 
"I shall give you a full account of my adventures later, my lord. But we have work to do now. Let every crow take up a burning twig and come with us."

Soon, an army of crows led by the tireless Live-Strong was streaking through the morning sky, carrying flaming twigs. 
"There's the cave. Drop your twigs on the nest and set it on fire," Live-Strong ordered. 
The crows did as they were told. *Thud!*

"Fire! The cave is on fire!" the owls shrieked, waking up to thick smoke. 
The owls, still half asleep, tried to rush out of the cave but were beaten back by the blazing flames of Live-Strong's giant nest. 
"The crow has done this! I should have listened to my faithful Red-Eye! We are doomed! Doomed!" Foe-Crusher cried. 

The cave, closed as it was from all sides, became a fiery furnace. Those of the owls who managed to evade the flames were suffocated by the fumes. All of them, including King Foe-Crusher, perished. 
And thus did the crows rid themselves of their powerful but dim-witted enemies, the owls.

## The Noble Enemy

A man was searching for gems on the top of a certain mountain. After a long unsuccessful search, he was exhausted and threw himself on the ground behind a rock. "I'll take a short nap and then try again," he sighed. 

He was awoken by the sound of voices nearby. "...too many dacoits around here..." "...the gems..."
He peeked over the rock. "Good God! Those three young men have found some gems!"
He watched in amazement as the men swallowed the gems. "...would be safest... in our stomachs!" one of them said. 
"They are swallowing them!" the man thought. "This is not fair at all. I should get some gems too for my trouble. I know what! I'll make friends with these fellows and when they are asleep, I'll slit open their bellies and take the gems."

He stepped out from behind the rock. "Good masters, I am fortunate to have met you. I am alone and I was looking for company on this dangerous road."
"You are welcome to travel with us, my friend," they agreed. 
"The more, the merrier," he smiled. "Good. They don't suspect anything. Now all I have to do is wait for the right moment."

But unfortunately for him, that moment was never to come. For as they passed a village that lay along their route, a pet bird in a robber chief's hut began to sing. "They have gems with them... They have gems..."
"Gems!" the robber chief's eyes widened. "Seize those men! Bring them here!"

The three friends and their new companion were captured and brought before him. 
"I know you have gems with you. Hand them over," the chief demanded. 
"What! Gems?" they feigned innocence. 
"Search them!" 

The men searched their captives thoroughly. "There has been some mistake. We don't have any gems with us!" the captives protested. 
"We can't find anything on them, chief," a robber reported. 
"How's that! Oh, well! The bird must have been mistaken. All right. Let them go."

But as soon as they left, the bird began to sing the same song again. "They have gems with them... They have gems with them..."
"What! Have they deceived me, then? Go after them! Bring them back!"
The four men were brought back and searched again. "No! Nothing on them."
"How can that be? My bird insists... Aha!" The chief grinned wickedly. "The gems must be in your stomachs! You have swallowed them! Take them away and cut open their bellies."
"No, no!" the young men cried. 

"I am beyond help," the gem-less man thought. "When they find gems in their stomachs they will cut open mine too. On the other hand, they may cut me first. Either way I am doomed. But I could save the other fellows. Let me try..."
"You won't find any gems in our stomachs, sir," the man stepped forward. "But if you insist on seeing for yourself let me be the first to die. It would be unbearable for me to watch the stomachs of my friends being cut open."
"Take him away and grant him his wish," the chief ordered. 

The man was taken away and killed. Later, a robber returned. "There wasn't a single gem in his stomach."
"What! So my bird can make a mistake after all," the chief frowned. 
"There were no gems in his stomach. There won't be any in the stomachs of his friends either."
"There has been a terrible mistake, my friends. I am sorry that your comrade is dead. You may go," the chief apologized. 

The three friends, unaware that they had been saved by a clever ruse, thought they had just been very lucky, and lost no time in getting out of the village. Little did the chief realise that he had done exactly what the dead man had hoped he would do. 

*Moral: A sensible enemy can often prove to be your best friend.*

## The Bird and the Monkeys

One chilly winter evening, a group of monkeys were shivering in the forest. "Brrr-rrr... It's cold."
"Look, brother! A spark of fire!" one monkey pointed. "I've got it!"
It was a glow-worm going past. 
"Put it under this pile of leaves," the monkey ordered. "We will soon have a big blazing fire to keep ourselves warm."

A bird watching from a branch chirped up. "You are making a mistake, good sirs. That's a firefly you've caught... not a spark of fire."
"Ignore her! Let's blow on the leaves. They'll catch fire faster that way," the monkey said. 
"Listen to me, sirs... you are wasting your time and energy," the bird insisted. "You can't set anything alight with a firefly."
"Why don't you... stop pestering us, you stupid creature!" a monkey yelled, throwing a rock at her. "Don't try to teach us how to light a fire! Come on brothers, let's get back to the task."
"Phoooo! Phoooooo!" the monkeys continued blowing on the glowing insect in the dead leaves. 

*Moral: Don't try to teach those who cannot be taught.*

## The Camel Who Was Beguiled by His Companions

A merchant was leading a caravan of heavily-laden camels through a dense jungle when one of them, overcome by fatigue, collapsed. 
"Let us shift his load onto the others and be off. We must not let this lazy creature delay us," the merchant ordered, leaving the camel behind. 

Later, when the camel recovered his strength, he looked around. "They have gone! And I am alone in this strange jungle. Fortunately, there's plenty of grass here. At least I won't starve."
Days passed and the camel somehow survived the perils of the jungle. Then one day, a lion followed by his courtiers—a leopard, a jackal and a crow—came by. 
"My God! What strange creature is that!" the lion gasped. 
"That is a camel, O king. They are not usually found in jungles. Let us find out what he is doing here," the crow said. 

When the camel told them his sad story, the lion took pity on him. "Poor fellow! He has been treated very badly. You have nothing to fear now, my friend. Henceforth you are under my absolute protection. Come with us."
So the camel joined the lion's entourage and was happy to have found such a strong protector. 

But one day the lion was severely wounded in a combat with a rogue elephant. He had to retire to his cave and there he remained for several days... much to the dismay of the crow, the jackal and the leopard who depended entirely on him for their food. 
"No meat today either," the crow grumbled. 
"We'll starve to death at this rate," the leopard growled. 

The lion noticed their plight. "They look so sad. They must be worrying about my health. I wish I could feed them as always. I am sorry I cannot provide you with food now, my friends. I am too weak to hunt. You will have to fend for yourselves till I recover."
"What are you saying, master?" the sly jackal objected. "How could we even think of eating when you are starving!"
The lion was immensely pleased with the jackal's reply. "You have shown yourselves to be loyal servants. Go and round up an animal and drive it this way so that I may kill it for food."

The jackal and his friends scoured the jungle but could not find any animal. Finally, they found themselves back where they had started. 
"There's really... no need... to exert ourselves like this. What else can we do?" the leopard sighed. 
"Well, there's the camel," the jackal smirked. "His flesh could sustain us all for several days. I'll have a word with him in any case."
"Forget it. The king will never hurt any animal that is under his protection," the leopard scoffed. 

The jackal went to the lion's cave. "O king, we could not find an animal for you. But there's no need for you to starve. You can eat the camel."
"What!" the lion roared. "Are you suggesting that I hurt an animal that is under my protection? Get out of my sight, you wretch!"
"Please don't misunderstand me, O king," the jackal pleaded smoothly. "I was only thinking of your welfare. Our own lives are worthless when yours is at stake. I know it would be wrong for you to hurt the camel in ordinary circumstances. But what if he himself devotedly offered his flesh to you? Then no one could blame you for slaying him."
The lion paused. "I suppose you are right. If the camel were to make such an offer I might accept it."

The wily jackal ran back to his friends who had now been joined by the innocent camel. "Friends, our king is dying of starvation. Let us go and beg him to eat one of us. It is the least we can do for such a noble soul."

They went to the cave. 
"No, O king. We were not able to catch any creature. You may eat me, master, and prolong your life for a day," the crow offered dramatically. 
"No, no, you're too small. The master's hunger would hardly be appeased by eating you," the jackal interrupted. "Eat me, master."
"As if you're very big yourself. It is me you should eat, master," the leopard declared. 

The camel watched them in awe. "All of them have offered to lay down their lives for the king, but he has not hurt any of them," he thought. "Now let me too make a noble gesture. They'll protect me too!"
So the poor, foolish camel stepped forward. "Stand aside, friend leopard. How can the master eat you? You and he belong to the same family - well almost. Eat me, master."

An ominous silence greeted the camel's offer. Then—
"I accept your offer, O noble camel," the lion growled, his eyes flashing. 
"Wha...?!" 
Before the camel could get over his shock, the three animals rushed at him and killed him. Thus did three rogues take advantage of the trust reposed in them by a comrade. 

*Moral: Watch your step with false friends.*
