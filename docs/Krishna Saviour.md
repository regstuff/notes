# Krishna: The Saviour

Pareekshit listened intently as Sage Shukadeva narrated the events following the Rajasooya Yajna of Yudhishthira. 

"While Krishna was at Indraprastha, Dwaraka was attacked by Shalva." Shalva was a friend of Shishupala. He had performed severe penance to Lord Shiva and obtained an indestructible, flying vehicle called 'Saubha' that could travel anywhere in air, water, or land and carry thousands of soldiers. 

Seated in Saubha, Shalva attacked Dwaraka, raining down stones, trees, and serpents. In Krishna's absence, his brave son Pradyumna took charge, backed by Satyaki, Gada, and Samba. Pradyumna destroyed Shalva's illusions with celestial weapons. When Dyuman, Shalva's minister, struck Pradyumna unconscious with a mace, his charioteer carried him away. Regaining consciousness, Pradyumna returned to battle and killed Dyuman. 

After twenty-seven days of fierce battle, Krishna and Balarama returned to find Dwaraka under siege. Krishna joined the fight, penetrating Saubha with a shower of sharp arrows. 

Shalva then created a dark illusion, showing a messenger claiming Vasudeva had been captured, followed by an image of Vasudeva being beheaded. Krishna was momentarily stricken with grief, but quickly realized it was a creation of Shalva's Maya. 

Enraged, Krishna attacked Saubha with his mace until it shattered and fell into the ocean. As Shalva jumped off to confront him, Krishna released the Sudarshana Chakra and severed Shalva's head. 

Soon after, Dantavakra, another ally of Shishupala, arrived to avenge his friends. Krishna struck him in the chest with his mace, killing him on the spot. Viduratha, Dantavakra's brother, also rushed forward and was slain. The victorious Yadava army returned to Dwaraka amidst joyous cheering. 

Later, Sudama, a poor Brahman and childhood friend of Krishna from their days at Guru Sandipani's ashram, was living in dire poverty. His wife patiently bore her suffering, but one day she pleaded, "You told me that Krishna, the Lord of Dwaraka, is your friend... Why don't you go and meet him? He will surely help you." 

"The very thought of meeting my friend Krishna makes me happy. Indeed I must go," Sudama agreed. "Is there anything in the house which I can take as a gift?" 

His wife collected a small handful of Powa (beaten rice) from neighbours and tied it in a cloth. With this small bundle, Sudama set out for Dwaraka. 

When he reached Rukmini's palace in Dwaraka, Krishna saw him from afar. "My dear Sudama!" Krishna rushed forward, embraced his friend warmly, and seated him on his own couch. With his own hands, Krishna washed Sudama's feet while Rukmini gently fanned him. The palace maids were astonished to see the Lord show such reverence to a poor beggar-like man. 

Holding Sudama's hand, Krishna fondly recalled their gurukula days, especially the night they were sent to collect firewood and got caught in a terrible thunderstorm, braving the dark forest together until Guru Sandipani found and blessed them. 

"What present have you brought me?" Krishna asked with a twinkle in his eye. Seeing Sudama hesitate out of embarrassment, Krishna smiled. "When my true devotees offer the smallest gifts with love, I feel pleased." 

Krishna spotted the bundle of Powa. "Why, it's Powa! My favourite food!" He took a fistful and ate it with delight. As he reached for a second fistful, Rukmini gently held his hand. "That is enough, my Lord! It is sufficient to give a man all he needs in this world and the next." 

Sudama spent the night in luxury at the palace. The next morning, he left for home without having asked for anything. "How lucky I am. Lord Krishna treated me as if I were his brother," he thought happily. 

When he reached his village, Sudama was astounded. In place of his humble hut stood a magnificent palace studded with precious stones, surrounded by beautiful gardens! His wife, dressed in fine silk and gold ornaments, came out with servants to receive him. "All this can only be through the benevolence of Krishna," Sudama realized. Yet, amidst all the splendour, Sudama continued to live a simple, devoted life. 

Sometime later, during a total solar eclipse, the Yadavas travelled to the holy spot of Samantapanchaka at Kurukshetra. The Pandavas, Kauravas, Nanda, Yashoda, and the Gopikas also assembled there. It was a grand, joyous reunion. The queens of Krishna—Rukmini, Satyabhama, Jambavati, Kalindi, Lakshmana, and others—narrated the thrilling stories of their marriages to Draupadi and Kunti. 

Revered rishis, including Vyasa, Narada, and Vishwamitra, arrived to pay homage to Krishna. Krishna treated them with great reverence, prompting Narada to remark on Krishna's humility despite being the Supreme Lord. Vasudeva performed a grand Yajna under the guidance of the rishis. 

Upon returning to Dwaraka, Devaki expressed a wish to see her six sons who had been killed by Kamsa. Krishna and Balarama brought them back briefly from the underworld, satisfying Devaki's maternal heart before the sons returned to heaven. 

Finally, Pareekshit asked how Arjuna married Subhadra, Krishna's sister. While on a pilgrimage, Arjuna learned that Balarama planned to marry Subhadra to Duryodhana, though Krishna favoured Arjuna. Disguised as a Sanyasi, Arjuna stayed in Dwaraka for four months during the rainy season. Balarama invited the 'ascetic' for a meal, where Arjuna and Subhadra saw each other and fell in love. 

With Krishna's secret encouragement, Arjuna carried Subhadra away in his chariot. Though Balarama was initially furious, Krishna mollified him, and the wedding was celebrated with great joy.
