# Velu Thampi

In the late 18th century, the beautiful southern land of Travancore (now part of Kerala) was going through an unhappy time. Balarama Varma was only sixteen years old when he became the Maharaja in 1798. Taking advantage of the King's youth, his Diwan (Prime Minister), Jayanathan Sankaran Namboodiri, imposed crushing, unjust taxes on the poor people and merrily pocketed the state's wealth. 

The Diwan was ruthless and arrogant. He even dared to usurp the Maharaja's royal privileges, ordering the guards to carry him around in the King's personal palanquin. 

One day, a young and proud Tehsildar named Velu Thampi saw the Diwan riding in the royal palanquin. Incensed by this blatant disrespect, Velu Thampi boldly stepped forward. "Halt the palanquin at once!" he ordered. "The Diwan has no right to ride in this palanquin!"

The Diwan was furious. "You'll pay for this, Velu Thampi," he threatened. 

"It is you who will soon have to pay for your greed and corruption!" Velu Thampi shot back fearlessly. 

Enraged and humiliated, the Diwan returned to the capital, Trivandrum. He consulted his faithful friend, a wealthy, corrupt planter named Tarakan. "I am in real trouble. I need more money to maintain my power," the Diwan complained. 

"Ha! Ha! That's simple! We shall just levy some more taxes!" Tarakan laughed greedily. "Let the people pay for our pleasures! After all, we enjoy the support of the British Resident, Colonel Macaulay. We need fear no one."

The new, cruel taxes broke the backs of the common people. "This is preposterous! How can they expect us to pay?" they cried. Indignant and desperate, thousands of citizens marched to Velu Thampi, seeking his help against the injustice. 

"It is no use grumbling. Let us take our grievances directly to the Maharaja," Velu Thampi decided, leading a massive, angry protest march to the royal palace in Trivandrum. 

Hearing the cries of his people, the young Maharaja finally realized the depth of his minister's corruption. "I hereby order that the Diwan be removed from office," the King declared from his balcony. "And you, Velu Thampi, shall be my new Diwan."

The corrupt former Diwan was immediately arrested. However, his wealthy crony, Tarakan, managed to flee and sought the protection of Colonel Macaulay, the powerful British Resident. 

Macaulay, angry at losing his puppet minister, saw an opportunity. "The Maharaja's treaty with us is due to be renewed shortly," he told Tarakan. "In return for our 'protection', he usually pays us six lakhs of rupees."

"Increase the levy to eight lakhs," Tarakan suggested wickedly. "That will ruin him. Let the Maharaja and his new Diwan think twice before antagonizing Tarakan!"

When the British demand for eight lakhs of rupees was presented at the court, Velu Thampi advised the King cautiously. "We can't afford to do that now. Let us sign the treaty and then bide our time." 

Faced with the incredibly difficult task of raising such a massive sum, Velu Thampi had to implement strict reforms. He cracked down heavily on corrupt officials, introduced stern measures against hoarders, tightened discipline in the army, and revised land revenue to be perfectly fair, based on the actual yield of the land. 

Velu Thampi was notoriously uncompromising when it came to justice. Once, while visiting his ancestral home, he learned that a local official had done his family a 'favour' by taxing their highly fertile land at a much lower rate. Velu Thampi immediately summoned his brother and the official. "What you did was wrong," he reprimanded them sternly. He ensured his family paid their full dues and severely punished the corrupt official. "Let it be known that henceforth corruption will not be tolerated. No man, rich or poor, is above the law!"

Meanwhile, because he had failed to pay his fair share of taxes, Tarakan's vast estates were confiscated by the state. Tarakan ran crying to Colonel Macaulay once again. 

Macaulay arrogantly summoned Velu Thampi. "I want you to spare Tarakan and return his lands."

Velu Thampi refused point-blank. "For years, Tarakan has oppressed the people. Now he must pay the price."

Infuriated by this defiance, Macaulay sent a threatening message to the Maharaja, demanding the immediate dismissal of Velu Thampi. 

Velu Thampi was furious when he heard this. "Your Highness, the British are mere merchants! The treaty gives them no right to interfere in our domestic affairs."

The Maharaja agreed and bravely rejected Macaulay's demand. Macaulay retaliated immediately: "Inform His Highness that the eight lakhs of rupees he owes us must be paid NOW!"

Realizing the British were simply looking for an excuse to conquer them, the Maharaja and Velu Thampi knew war was inevitable. "We can't be cowed down forever by the Resident. We must act," the King declared. 

Velu Thampi immediately began preparing for war. Distant fortresses were reinforced, and troops were moved to strategic points. He even secured an alliance with the neighboring Maharaja of Cochin. 

"Too long have the British oppressed us. Now we shall be free!" Velu Thampi roared, leading his troops into battle. On the 18th of December, 1808, his forces successfully attacked and captured the British Residency, though the cowardly Macaulay managed to escape in the chaos.

But Velu Thampi's glorious victory was tragically short-lived. The British quickly brought in massive reinforcements from Madras. The mighty British navy mercilessly bombarded the Maharaja's coastal fleets, while their well-armed ground troops overwhelmed the brave but outgunned Travancore soldiers. 

One by one, the towns fell. When the British army finally neared the capital of Trivandrum, Velu Thampi knew the end was near. He hastened to the King's residence in Kilimanoor. 

"The British are nearing Trivandrum. Defeat is inevitable," Velu Thampi reported sadly. But then, he handed the King a parchment. "If Your Highness will be so good as to read this document..."

The King read it, shocked. "Here I have confessed that I alone am responsible for the rebellion against the British, and that Your Highness and the people knew nothing about my plan," Velu Thampi explained. "In this way, I shall save my ruler and my country from the wrath of the British."

Handing over his cherished sword, Velu Thampi bowed deeply. "I pledge undying loyalty. I take my final leave of Your Highness." 

The Maharaja wept as he accepted the heroic Diwan's sacrifice, knowing it was the only way to save Travancore from total destruction. The King officially surrendered to the British to end the war. 

The British took over, but they were determined to have their revenge. "You are to arrest the traitor Velu Thampi, and execute him!" Macaulay ordered the helpless Maharaja. 

A massive manhunt was launched. Velu Thampi, accompanied by his loyal brother Padmanabhan, went into hiding, constantly moving through the dense forests to evade capture. Eventually, exhausted and cornered, they reached a deserted temple at Mannadi. 

"I once had a dream... that we could shake off the yoke of British tyranny," Velu Thampi sighed, looking out into the night. "Today that dream lies shattered. But there will be others to continue the struggle... and eventually, the British will be driven away."

Suddenly, they heard the heavy tramp of soldiers' boots surrounding the temple. 

"The British have found us! All is lost!" his brother panicked. 

Velu Thampi's eyes hardened with resolve. "The British shall never take me prisoner!" He handed his dagger to his brother. "Here, brother. Kill me, and give me the freedom I crave for!"

But Padmanabhan wept and dropped the knife. "Oh, I cannot! Forgive me, I cannot strike!" 

"Coward!!" Velu Thampi yelled. Picking up the dagger himself, he turned his face towards the heavens. "Mother, give me your blessings." 

Before his brother could stop him, the valiant patriot plunged the dagger into his own heart, dying a free man. 

Moments later, the British soldiers burst into the temple. "So he's dead already?" the cruel British officer sneered, seeing the body. "Never mind, we'll hang his dead body up as a warning—and we'll hang you too, for that matter!" he ordered, arresting the grieving brother. 

The British hanged Velu Thampi's lifeless body to terrorize the people, but their cruel actions had the opposite effect. The memory of Velu Thampi, one of India's very first and most valiant freedom fighters, lived on, inspiring generations of Indians to ultimately realize his dream of a free nation.
