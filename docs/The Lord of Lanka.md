# The Lord of Lanka - 1. The Lord of Lanka

Sumali, the king of the Rakshasas from the nether-world, desired a powerful grandson to restore his people's glory. He sent his beautiful daughter, Kaikesi, to the world of men to woo the great sage Vishrava, father of Kubera, the Lord of Wealth. 

Vishrava accepted Kaikesi, but warned her, "Because you approached me at an inauspicious hour, the children born to us will be wicked Rakshasas... but the youngest will be noble."

Kaikesi gave birth to the ten-headed Ravana, the giant Kumbhakarna, the cruel Shoorpanakha, and finally, the righteous Vibheeshana. 

As they grew, Ravana became arrogant and ambitious. Jealous of his half-brother Kubera's wealth, Ravana and his brothers performed severe penances for thousands of years. Ravana even sacrificed his own heads to the fire to please the gods. 

Finally, Lord Brahma appeared and granted them boons. He restored Ravana's heads and granted him invincibility against gods, demons, and spirits (but Ravana, in his arrogance, didn't ask for protection from mere mortals). Kumbhakarna accidentally asked for a boon of eternal sleep, while Vibheeshana asked to always remain righteous. 

Empowered by his boons, Ravana demanded that Kubera vacate the glorious city of Lanka. Kubera, following his father's advice, peacefully surrendered Lanka and moved to Mount Kailas. 

Ravana became the Lord of Lanka, but his arrogance knew no bounds. He harassed gods and sages until Kubera sent a messenger to reprimand him. Furious, Ravana attacked Kubera, defeated him, and stole his magical flying chariot, the Pushpak. 

Flying over Mount Kailas in the Pushpak, Ravana was stopped by Nandi, Lord Shiva's attendant. Angered that a mere mountain was blocking his path, Ravana arrogantly tried to uproot Mount Kailas itself. 

As the mountain shook, Lord Shiva simply pressed his toe down, pinning Ravana's arms under the mountain. Screaming in agony, Ravana realized his mistake and sang hymns of praise to Shiva for a thousand years. Pleased, Shiva freed him and gifted him an invincible sword. 

Ravana resumed his conquests. In the Himalayas, he saw a beautiful maiden named Vedavati meditating to win Lord Vishnu as her husband. Overcome by lust, Ravana grabbed her hair. 

Furious at his touch, Vedavati threw herself into a fire, vowing, "I will be born again and be the cause of your death!"

True to her word, she was reborn in a lotus, which Ravana found. Fearing her curse, he threw the baby into the sea. She washed ashore and was found by King Janaka, who named her Sita. 

Sita grew into a beautiful princess and eventually married Rama, the noble prince of Ayodhya, who was actually an avatar of Lord Vishnu. 

Meanwhile, Ravana's arrogance continued to grow. He even tried to force himself upon Rambha, his own daughter-in-law (Kubera's son's wife). She escaped and told her husband, who cursed Ravana: "If he ever marries a woman against her will, his heads will break into seven pieces."

Years later, seeking revenge against Rama for an insult to his sister Shoorpanakha, Ravana went to the Dandaka forest where Rama and Sita were living in exile. Disguised as a monk, Ravana tricked Sita, assumed his true ten-headed form, and abducted her to Lanka. 

He imprisoned Sita in his Ashoka garden, demanding she marry him. But fear of the curse kept him from forcing himself upon her. 

Rama, aided by an army of monkeys and bears led by Hanuman and Sugriva, marched to Lanka to rescue Sita. 

Ravana's righteous brother, Vibheeshana, pleaded with him to return Sita and make peace. "You are a disgrace to the family!" Ravana roared, kicking Vibheeshana out. Vibheeshana flew across the ocean and allied himself with Rama. 

A terrible war ensued. To break Sita's spirit, Ravana used magic to show her a fake severed head of Rama, but she soon learned the truth. 

In the great battle, Ravana's mighty warriors fell one by one, including his giant brother Kumbhakarna and his powerful son Indrajit. Finally, Rama and Ravana faced each other in an epic duel. 

With a divine arrow, Rama struck down the ten-headed demon king, fulfilling Vedavati's curse. 

As Ravana lay dying, Vibheeshana wept for his brother. "Don't grieve, Vibheeshana," Rama consoled him. "He has died like a brave warrior."

Rama crowned the righteous Vibheeshana as the new Lord of Lanka, and finally reunited with his beloved Sita, returned triumphantly to Ayodhya.
