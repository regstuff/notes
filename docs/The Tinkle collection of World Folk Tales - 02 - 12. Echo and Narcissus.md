# Echo and Narcissus

Echo, the beautiful wood nymph, was very, very talkative. 

"And then I saw the deer running away. I followed it..." she babbled endlessly to her friend. 

"Echo, please stop. You've been talking for hours. I'm tired," her friend sighed. 

"But listen, I haven't told you what happened after that," Echo insisted, grabbing her arm. 

"Echo, please, enough. I'm going," her friend groaned, walking away. 

"She's gone!" Echo muttered, disappointed. 

Just then, Hera, the majestic Mother of the Gods, came walking through the forest. "Echo, have you seen my husband, Zeus?" she asked. 

"No, Mother Hera, I haven't seen Father Zeus. But I'm sure I saw him come here. He hasn't been here, Mother Hera! I saw a deer running near the river and... I was telling my friend what happened this morning," Echo immediately began to chatter without pausing for breath. 

"But I heard voices! What happened?" Hera demanded, trying to interrupt. 

But Echo talked and talked and talked... until—

"Oh do be quiet! See what you have done!" Hera exploded in anger. "You have distracted me with your chatter. Zeus has slipped away!"

Echo was bewildered. "But what have I done?"

"You silly girl! From now on, you'll never be able to talk. You will only repeat the last word of whatever anyone says," Hera cursed her fiercely. 

"...anyone says!" Echo gasped involuntarily. 

Poor Echo could no longer speak her own thoughts and wandered about the lonely woods by herself. 

One day, a remarkably handsome youth named Narcissus came to the forest. Echo saw him and instantly fell deeply in love with him. As she followed him around, he heard rustling leaves. 

"Who are you, girl?" Narcissus asked, turning around. 

"...girl," Echo repeated softly from the bushes. 

"I know that you are a girl. What is your name?"

"...name," she repeated, stepping out. 

"Oh, go away you silly girl. You are stupid," the arrogant youth sneered. 

"...stupid," Echo whispered, tears welling in her eyes. 

"How dare you call me stupid, you wretched creature!" Narcissus shouted angrily. 

"...creature," she sobbed. 

Narcissus stormed away in anger, leaving Echo all alone. Echo, utterly heart-broken, faded away into the shadows until her body melted into a mere spirit, leaving only her echoing voice behind. 

But Nemesis, the fearsome Goddess of Revenge, had watched the whole tragic scene. The goddess sought out the arrogant Narcissus. 

"You wicked boy! You shall suffer for your cruelty!" Nemesis declared. "Look into the pond!"

Narcissus walked to the clear, still water and looked down. He saw his own beautiful reflection staring back at him. 

"Oh, what a charming face!" he gasped, instantly captivated. Narcissus stayed by the pond for a long, long time, unable to look away from his reflection. 

"I shall never leave this place. I want to look at this charming face forever," he whispered to the water. 

So Narcissus remained rooted to the spot by the pond, staring at himself until he wasted away and was magically changed into a beautiful plant bearing a delicate, golden flower that droops its head over the water. 

But Echo, a spirit now, still wanders the world in search of Narcissus. She cannot ask anyone where Narcissus is. She can only repeat what others say. 

"Hello, Echo! Now we know your story," you might shout to the mountains. 

"...story!" her lonely voice will call back.
