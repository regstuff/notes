# The Hidden Truth

Jinadatta was an honest, simple-minded young merchant trying to follow the peculiar final instructions of his late father. However, taking his father's words a bit too literally was driving him straight to ruin. 

One day, walking through the market, Jinadatta spotted Chandrasena, a man who owed him a significant amount of money.
"It's Jinadatta! I hope he hasn't seen me," Chandrasena muttered, trying to slink away.
But Jinadatta cheerfully walked right up to him. "Chandrasena!" 
"Jinadatta! I am very sorry but..." Chandrasena stammered, preparing an excuse.
"Sorry? What for?" Jinadatta smiled, offering a box. "Have some sweets! Always keep your mouth full of sweets. That's what my father advised me on his deathbed. Won't you have some?"
"N-no. Thank you," Chandrasena blinked, bewildered. 
"We must have a long chat some other time. I have to be going now," Jinadatta waved happily and walked off.

Chandrasena stared after him. *He didn't say a single word about the money!*
He hurried to his friend, Suryasena. "Wake up, Suryasena. Have you ever run up against a creditor who doesn't utter a word about the money you owe him? I have. And I am dazed."
"Are you referring to the mad Jinadatta?" Suryasena laughed.
"Yes! But how did you know?"
"Because I also borrowed money from him," Suryasena smirked. "He never asks for it! Right now, he busies himself building random houses in every village he happens to visit!"
"Why does he do that?"
"It seems his father, on his deathbed, asked him to do so."

The two unscrupulous men laughed uproariously. "So let's borrow as much as he can lend before he runs out of money building houses! What do you say?"
Another man nearby joined in the laughter. "Hey, you! What are you laughing about?" Chandrasena asked.
"You too would laugh if you heard our story about Jinadatta," the man chuckled. "Are you talking about his crazy idea of digging under the Ganga?"
"Digging under the Ganga? Whatever for?"
"Because that was the advice his dying father gave him! If he wants more men on the job, I can send them to him," the man guffawed.

***

Meanwhile, at Jinadatta's house, things were dire.
"Business has been terrible, sir," his accountant reported grimly. "I suggest you finally ask your friends to return the money you lent them."
"No, I won't. Never!" Jinadatta insisted stubbornly. "Father told me on his deathbed never to ask for what is given away."
"Well, then, we will have to stop the construction work in the villages."
"And disregard the advice my father gave me on his deathbed? Never! Can't you think of any other solution?"
The accountant sighed. "Well, you could consult the wise Somadatta."
"An excellent suggestion!" Jinadatta agreed. "My father advised me to seek Somadatta's guidance if ever I were in trouble."

Jinadatta immediately set out to see Somadatta, his father's oldest and wisest friend.
"I am happy to see you, Jinadatta," the wise old Somadatta smiled. "But you have put on a lot of weight. Have you been eating too many sweets?"
"Yes. That's what my father advised me to do, uncle," Jinadatta replied seriously.
Somadatta chuckled softly. "When he wanted me to speak pleasant words, he'd say: *Keep sweets in your mouth, my friend.* Did he give you that advice? Well... your father always gave advice in symbolic terms."
"Oh!" Jinadatta's eyes widened.

"How are you faring in business, Jinadatta?"
"Not very well, I am afraid, sir. Even though I have honestly and faithfully been following father's advice."
"What advice?"
"Well, he had advised me *never to ask for money given away*."
"Very sound advice, indeed!" Somadatta nodded. "You should never put yourself in a position where you will have to ask for the money you have lent. It means you should strictly vet who you lend money to, and absolutely never lend money to unscrupulous debtors who won't pay it back."
"Oh! And I took his words literally!" Jinadatta gasped. 

"Now what could father have meant when he said I must *build a house in every village*?"
"He meant that you should make true, reliable friends wherever you go. A true friend's home is as good as your own."
"And I have gone bankrupt actually building physical houses!" Jinadatta wept. "Oh, uncle! I am a fool! I am ruined! I have lost everything! What use is realizing my mistake when I have no capital to start afresh?"

"Didn't your father ask you to dig somewhere?" Somadatta asked kindly.
"He did. And I did as he advised. I dug under the Ganga."
"The Ganga? You mean you actually went and dug under the river Ganga?"
"Yes."
Somadatta shook his head amusedly. "Don't you know any other Ganga?"
"Any other Ganga?"
"Yes. Think it over. When you find the answer, you will find your capital... which I am confident you will now use with wisdom."

***

Jinadatta walked home deep in thought. *Any other Ganga... Any other Ganga... Now let me see... If I concentrate hard I am sure to remember.*

By the time he reached the courtyard of his house, his mind was spinning. The sight of a herd of cattle in his yard diverted his thoughts. 
"Do they belong to us?" he asked his herdsman.
"Yes, sir. All of them. And I know each one of them by name, sir. This is Yamuna! This is Narmada. The one behind her is Gauri."
The herdsman patted a particularly beautiful cow. "And this, the absolute favourite of your late father, is Ganga."

"Ganga!" Jinadatta shouted, the realization hitting him like a lightning bolt.
"Where do you tether Ganga?" he asked breathlessly.
"I will show you, sir. Please come with me. This is where she has been tied since her birth."

Jinadatta immediately grabbed a pickaxe and had the exact spot dug up. 
Within minutes, the pickaxe struck something hard. "I think we have found it! I have found the capital!"
Indeed, Jinadatta had found his true inheritance—a massive, buried urn overflowing with gold coins!
"I will use this gold with great care," Jinadatta vowed, looking at the treasure.

The very next day, Chandrasena knocked on his door. "Jinadatta, I hate to trouble you, but... could you please lend me a thousand gold coins..."
"I am sorry, Chandrasena," Jinadatta replied firmly, his mouth free of sweets. "I don't have a single coin to spare for you."

Jinadatta had finally learnt his lesson. From that day on, he helped only the truly deserving, and used the rest of his great wealth to earn even more through wise, careful investment.
