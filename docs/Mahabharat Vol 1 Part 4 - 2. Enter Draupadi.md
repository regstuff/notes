# Enter Draupadi

While living in disguise as poor Brahmanas in Ekachakra, the Pandavas welcomed a traveling ascetic into their home. The guest entertained them with a fascinating tale from the kingdom of Panchala. 

King Drupada of Panchala, deeply humiliated by his defeat at the hands of Drona and the Kuru princes, had wandered the land in search of powerful Brahmanas who could help him perform a massive sacrifice. His singular goal was to obtain an invincible son who could slay Drona, and a beautiful daughter to marry Arjuna, whom he secretly admired.

Aided by two brilliant sages, Yaja and Upayaja, Drupada performed a grand *Yagna*. From the roaring sacrificial fire, a fully grown, armor-clad warrior emerged—Dhrishtadyumna. Immediately following him arose a breathtakingly beautiful maiden with dark, curly hair and a complexion like a blue lotus. She was named Krishnaa, but became famous as Draupadi. 
"This girl, dark of skin and the best of women, will be the cause of the fall of the Kshatriyas," a celestial voice prophesied as she emerged from the flames. 

Hearing this wondrous tale, the Pandavas grew restless. With Kunti's permission, they decided to leave Ekachakra and travel toward Panchala, hoping to witness the grand *Swayamvara* (husband-choosing ceremony) that Drupada was organizing for Draupadi. 

Their journey took them through a dense forest along the banks of the sacred river Ganga. Led by Arjuna carrying a blazing torch, the brothers walked through the night. As they approached the riverbank at twilight, a furious voice boomed from the water. 

"Keep your distance! Foolish men who venture out during these hours are slain by us," shouted Angaraparna, the haughty King of the Gandharvas, who was sporting in the river with his wives. "Here, leave alone men, even the divines, the Rakshasas and sorcerers fear to tread. I am Angaraparna, and my strength is my faith!"

Arjuna stepped forward fearlessly. "O rover of the skies, the right to approach Ganga is not barred by time," he retorted. "No one can have any exclusive right to the oceans, the Himalayan ranges, or to Ganga—be it night or day or twilight."

Enraged by the mortal's defiance, the Gandharva drew his bow and unleashed a volley of deadly arrows. Arjuna smoothly deflected them with his torch. 
"I know the Gandharvas to be superhuman," Arjuna declared, drawing his own bow. "So I shall fight you with celestial missiles!" 

Invoking the fiery *Agneya* missile given to him by Drona, Arjuna launched it at the Gandharva. The blinding weapon struck the Gandharva's chariot, burning it to ashes and knocking Angaraparna unconscious. Arjuna dragged the defeated king to Yudhishthira by his hair.

Angaraparna's wife, Kumbheenasi, ran to Yudhishthira, tearfully pleading for her husband's life. Touched by her devotion, Yudhishthira ordered Arjuna to release him. "Release him. We would not slay a foe defeated, humiliated, and in need of the protection of a woman."

Deeply humbled, the Gandharva king befriended the Pandavas. Recognizing their immense strength, he offered Arjuna his most prized possessions: the *Chakshushi* lore, a magical science that enabled the user to perceive anything across the three worlds, and a herd of magnificent Gandharva horses that could change color and speed at will. 

In return, Arjuna taught the Gandharva the secrets of the *Agneya* missile. Before parting ways, Angaraparna advised the brothers that no king should travel without a royal priest to guide and protect him. 

Taking his advice, the Pandavas traveled to a nearby ashrama and humbly requested the wise sage Dhoumya to become their royal priest. Recognizing their true noble nature and hidden royal lineage, Dhoumya gladly accepted. 

Guided and protected by their new priest, the Pandavas finally arrived in the bustling, magnificently decorated capital city of Panchala. Still maintaining their disguise, the mighty princes took up residence in the humble house of a potter, living quietly as mendicants, eagerly awaiting the grand spectacle of Draupadi's Swayamvara.
