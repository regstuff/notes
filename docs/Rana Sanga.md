# Rana Sanga

In the early half of the sixteenth century, the glorious Rajput kingdom of Mewar was ruled by the valiant Rana Raimal. Though a great king himself, Rana Raimal was deeply troubled by the bitter, endless rivalry between his three sons: **Sanga**, Prithviraj, and Jaimal. 

As the eldest, Sanga firmly believed he was the rightful heir to the throne. However, his brothers, particularly the incredibly fierce and ambitious Prithviraj, violently disagreed. The brothers constantly bickered, drawing their swords at the slightest pretext, threatening to tear the kingdom apart. 

Seeking to end the bitter feud once and for all, the brothers decided to consult the oracle of Charani Devi, a highly revered prophetess whose predictions never went wrong. 

When they arrived at the holy temple, Sanga unknowingly sat on a tiger skin spread on the floor, while his brothers sat elsewhere. The priestess emerged, took one look at them, and pointed directly at Sanga. "He who sits on the tiger skin is destined to rule Mewar!" she proclaimed. 

Instead of accepting the divine will, the furious Prithviraj instantly drew his sword and viciously attacked his elder brother right inside the sacred temple! During the bloody, desperate scuffle, Prithviraj struck a brutal blow that permanently blinded Sanga in one eye. 

Realizing his life was in immediate danger, Sanga managed to flee the temple. A loyal tradesman named Veeda sacrificed his own life to stop the pursuing brothers, allowing Sanga to escape into the dense jungles on horseback. 

Forced into exile, the rightful Crown Prince of Mewar became a hunted fugitive. Wandering as a poor, one-eyed beggar, Sanga eventually fell in with a band of rebel Rajput dacoits led by a brave chief named Karam Chand. 

Keeping his royal identity an absolute secret, Sanga lived and fought alongside the rebels. During this time, he fell deeply in love with Karam Chand's beautiful daughter, Maru. Recognizing Sanga's inherent nobility and exceptional bravery, Karam Chand happily agreed to their marriage. 

After the wedding, Sanga finally revealed his true identity to his stunned father-in-law. "I am Sangram Singh, the eldest son of Rana Raimal!" Sanga declared. "I am destined to rule over Mewar."
Overjoyed, Karam Chand immediately pledged his absolute loyalty and his entire band of warriors to Sanga's cause. 

Meanwhile, back in Chittor, destiny had taken a dark turn. Both of Sanga's violent brothers, Prithviraj and Jaimal, had tragically died in separate incidents. The aging Rana Raimal was left without an heir. When loyal messengers finally tracked Sanga down and brought him back to Chittor, the old king wept with joy. 

Soon after, Rana Raimal passed away, and Sanga was triumphantly crowned the Maharana of Mewar. 

Under Rana Sanga's brilliant, incredibly courageous leadership, Mewar quickly reached the absolute summit of military power and prosperity. Sanga was a terrifying, unmatched warrior. Though he had lost one eye, one arm, and was covered with eighty terrible battle scars, he fought like a fierce lion. 

When the powerful Sultan Mahmood Khilji of Malwa dared to invade Chittor, Rana Sanga launched a devastating counter-attack, utterly routing the massive enemy army and actually capturing the Sultan himself! 

Proving his legendary Rajput magnanimity, Sanga treated his royal prisoner as an honored guest for several months before generously releasing him, though he permanently annexed four major provinces from the defeated Sultan's empire. 

However, a far greater, far more terrifying threat was brewing in the north. In April 1526, a fierce invader from Central Asia named **Babar** utterly defeated Sultan Ibrahim Lodi at the historic Battle of Panipat, firmly capturing the throne of Delhi. 

Unlike previous invaders who looted and left, Babar made a stunning announcement: he intended to stay and establish a permanent Mughal Empire in Hindustan. 

Rana Sanga immediately realized that Babar was the greatest threat to Indian independence. Uniting almost all the major Rajput kings under his banner, Sanga marched his massive army toward Delhi to drive the foreign invader out. 

The two mighty armies met at the historic battlefield of **Khanwa** in March 1527. 

In the initial, brutal skirmishes, the incredibly fierce Rajput cavalry completely decimated Babar's advance forces. The sheer, terrifying ferocity of the Rajputs completely broke the morale of the Mughal soldiers. Panicking, the Mughal army was on the verge of absolute mutiny. 

Desperate to save his crumbling empire, Babar made a brilliant, highly dramatic move. He summoned all his commanders, dramatically smashed his precious wine flasks, poured the wine onto the earth, and solemnly swore on the Holy Koran never to drink again if God granted him victory. His fiery, religious speech successfully rallied his terrified troops for one final, desperate stand. 

Before the final battle, Babar sent an emissary to sue for peace. The fiercely proud Rana Sanga flatly rejected the terms. However, Sanga made a fatal, catastrophic mistake: he sent his trusted general, Shiladitya, to formally deliver the rejection. 

The treacherous Shiladitya, blinded by sheer greed and seduced by Babar's massive bribes, secretly betrayed his king. He struck a dark, treasonous deal with the Mughal emperor. 

The next morning, the decisive, historic battle commenced. Rana Sanga, leading a powerful flanking attack from the rear, confidently waited for Shiladitya's massive frontal assault to crush the Mughal lines. 

But to Sanga's absolute horror, as the two armies met, Shiladitya and his entire division suddenly switched sides! They openly joined Babar's army and brutally attacked their own Rajput brothers! 

Caught in a devastating crossfire and utterly betrayed by his own top general, the Rajput army was thrown into complete, bloody chaos. Despite fighting with unbelievable, superhuman bravery, the Rajputs were slaughtered. 

A severely wounded Rana Sanga was forcibly carried away from the disastrous battlefield by his loyal remaining officers. 

Waking up in a remote village in the hills, Sanga wept bitter tears for his fallen motherland. "I vow not to return to Mewar until I have vanquished the Mughals!" he roared, refusing to accept defeat. 

But the incredible physical toll of his countless wounds, combined with the agonizing, unbearable heartbreak of such a massive, treacherous betrayal, finally broke the invincible warrior. A few months later, still desperately planning his revenge, the legendary Rana Sanga passed away in a small village, remaining to this day the ultimate, immortal symbol of fearless Rajput pride and unwavering courage.
