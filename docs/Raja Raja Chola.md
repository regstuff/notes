# Raja Raja Chola: Destined for Greatness

About a thousand years ago, the prosperous kingdom of Tanjavur in South India was ruled by the wise Parantaka Chola II. He had three children: the aggressive and hot-headed crown prince Aditya Karikalan, the calm and noble younger prince Arul Mozhi Varman, and a beautiful, highly intelligent daughter named Kundavai. 

Despite being the younger brother, Arul was deeply loved by everyone who met him. His calm demeanor strongly contrasted with Karikalan’s extreme arrogance, which had earned the crown prince many dangerous enemies, particularly the powerful commander-in-chief of the Chola army. 

One day, King Parantaka summoned his sons. "Our counsellors say that Vira Pandya, the king of Madurai, has grown troublesome," the king stated. "Can you teach him a lesson, Karikalan?"
"Willingly, Father," Karikalan boasted. "I shall make you proud of me!"
Arul stepped forward respectfully. "May I accompany him, Father?"
"Certainly!" the king smiled. "That's exactly what I had hoped to hear you say."

Before they could leave, Parantaka asked to speak to them in absolute privacy. His face grew heavy with deep sorrow. "My sons," the king sighed, "years ago, Uttama's saintly father gave up the throne to his brother—my father. Now, Uttama wants it back. My conscience tells me I must return it to him. Besides, he has many incredibly strong supporters among the commanders."

Karikalan was utterly shocked and furious. "But I have already been proclaimed the Yuvaraja (Crown Prince)!" he protested. "This means I shall never succeed you!"
His sister, Kundavai, tried to calm his raging temper. "When you come back victorious from battle, the people will clamour for you," she promised. "The coward Uttama will not stand a single chance." 

The next day, Karikalan and Arul boldly rode out to Madurai with their massive forces. The mighty Chola army clashed violently with the valiant Pandyas. The battle raged fiercely throughout the entire day, but towards sunset, the Chola forces finally gained the upper hand. Realizing his absolute defeat, King Vira Pandya panicked and fled the battlefield. 

Ignoring all honorable rules of warfare, the ruthless Karikalan aggressively pursued the retreating king. Vira Pandya frantically sought shelter in a dilapidated, abandoned house to rest. Karikalan ruthlessly tracked him down, kicked open the door, and mercilessly slaughtered the exhausted, unarmed king in cold blood. 

When Arul arrived and saw the gruesome, unchivalrous murder, he was deeply dismayed. 

When the triumphant brothers returned to Tanjavur, Karikalan expected a hero's welcome. Instead, he faced the intense hostility and absolute contempt of his own soldiers. Even his closest friend, Vandiya Devan, was deeply disturbed. "I wish he had shown more restraint," Vandiya Devan sighed. "He has dangerously antagonized the commander of our army."

To separate the brothers and give Arul a chance to prove his own worth, King Parantaka decided to send Arul on an important military assignment to quell a fierce rebellion in Lanka (Ceylon). Before departing, Arul took a tender, sorrowful leave of Princess Vanati, the woman he deeply loved. 

In Lanka, Arul launched a brilliant, surprise pre-dawn attack. The Chola army quickly overpowered the rebels, forcing the Lankan King Mahendra to flee into the hills. However, unlike his ruthless brother, Arul deliberately ordered his men to let the defeated king escape safely. This incredibly noble, generous act made his soldiers admire him even more. 

Meanwhile, back in Tanjavur, a dark, sinister conspiracy was brewing. The ambitious Uttama had secretly allied himself with the vengeful Chola army commander and his cunning wife, Nandini. 
"Avenge the cowardly death of Vira Pandya," Nandini urged the commander. "Kill Karikalan, and make the way clear for Uttama!"

The commander immediately hired a group of ruthless assassins. He then sent a deceptive, urgent message to Karikalan, asking to meet him alone that night at the old city ruins. The hot-headed, arrogant Karikalan confidently rode out alone—and walked straight into a deadly ambush. He was brutally murdered in the dark. 

When the horrifying news reached the palace, King Parantaka almost broke down completely. He immediately dispatched the loyal Vandiya Devan to sail to Lanka and bring Arul back at once to secure the throne. 

Despite a terrifying, massive storm brewing over the ocean, Arul and Vandiya Devan set sail immediately. Their ship was violently torn apart by the raging storm and sank into the dark depths. 

Back in Tanjavur, the treacherous commander heavily pressured the grieving, ailing king to officially install Uttama as the new Crown Prince. Just as Parantaka was about to helplessly agree, the doors flew open. To everyone's absolute shock, Arul and Vandiya Devan walked in! They had miraculously survived the shipwreck and had been secretly sheltered by Buddhist monks at Nagapattinam. 

Seeing his beloved son alive, Parantaka wept with overwhelming relief. "I shall make immediate arrangements for you to be installed as Yuvaraja!" the king declared. 

But Arul raised his hand. "No, Father," he said softly, looking at the stunned Uttama. "I know my brother's death has made me the rightful heir. But my days are not numbered. What should I do? Your brother is dead. We have no Yuvaraja. And... you made a solemn promise to Uttama. In the absolute interests of our kingdom, let us quarrel no more. Prove yourself a man of your word, Father. Let Uttama be installed."

Uttama could not believe his ears. The entire court was profoundly moved by Arul's incredible nobility, deep respect for his father's word, and absolute lack of greed. 

Uttama was formally crowned as Yuvaraja. Not long after, King Parantaka passed away peacefully, and Uttama ascended the throne. Because Arul had so magnanimously supported him, King Uttama ruled wisely and treated Arul with immense respect. 

During this time of peace, Arul married his beloved Princess Vanati, and his loyal friend Vandiya Devan married Princess Kundavai. In time, a son named Rajendra was happily born to Arul and Vanati. 

Twelve peaceful years passed. When King Uttama lay on his deathbed, he immediately summoned Arul. "I am dying, and I have no strength left," Uttama whispered weakly. "I want to tell you that I am forever indebted to you. I decree that you shall succeed me. You will make a far better king than I have ever been."

Soon after, Uttama died, and the noble, patient Arul Mozhi Varman was gloriously crowned as **Raja Raja Chola**. 

His legendary, brilliant reign had finally begun. When the weak, treacherous King of Madurai rose in arms once again, Raja Raja Chola marched out and decisively crushed the rebellion, capturing their massive forts with ease. Shortly after, when news arrived that the King of Lanka was also planning an attack, Raja Raja Chola launched an unprecedented, incredibly daring naval expedition. 

His sturdy, massive fleet sailed across the ocean, completely overwhelming the Lankan defenses. In a very short time, his highly disciplined troops seized all major forts and captured numerous villages and towns, firmly establishing the absolute supremacy of the Chola Empire across the seas. 

As the glorious years rolled on, Raja Raja Chola's immense prestige as a powerful, brilliant, but profoundly just and noble ruler grew exponentially. Those who sought asylum with him did not do so in vain, and his enemies were thoroughly and permanently quelled. Such was the legendary, unforgettable reign of Raja Raja Chola the Great—the prince who patiently gave up a throne, only to win an entire empire.
