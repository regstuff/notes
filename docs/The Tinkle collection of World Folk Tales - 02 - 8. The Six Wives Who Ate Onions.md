# The Six Wives Who Ate Onions

One day, long ago, six American Indian women were gathering fruits in the forest. 

Suddenly, one of them pulled a bulbous root from the ground. "Oh, look what I've found! It's an onion!" She took a bite. "CRUNCH! It's delicious!"

"I'll have another one," she said. 

"I too," the others agreed. "Mmm... pungent, but tasty! SCRUNCH!"

When they returned home in the evening, each of them was carrying a large bunch of wild onions. Later, their husbands came home, weary from hunting the whole day long. 

"Ah, it's good to be home," a husband smiled, sitting by the fire. 

"I have a surprise for you," his wife beamed, holding up the pungent roots. 

"Look, what we..." she began, exhaling a deep breath. 

"ARRGHH!" her husband choked, covering his nose. "Your breath smells awful! I can't breathe in here! OUT! GET OUT!"

He shoved her out of the tent. "SLAM!"

All the other wives were driven out too by their disgusted husbands, and they had to spend the cold night out in the open, huddled together under the stars. 

"Have you been thrown out too?" one shivered. 

"Yes, she has." 

"I too."

But the next day too, instead of feeling sorry, they stubbornly went back to the forest... and ate several onions each! "Our husbands don't know what they're missing!" they laughed. "If only they would taste one!"

And again that night—"Oh, that odour! OUT! GET OUT!"

For a whole week, the wives would go to the forest and eat onions in the morning. And at night, their furious husbands would push them out of their houses. 

Finally, the women had enough. "Let's leave our husbands and go away!" the prettiest wife suggested. "They're horrid!"

"Yes, let's do that!" the others agreed. 

The next morning, the wives went up a high mountain carrying magic ropes made of eagle feathers. They climbed up to the highest rock and then the prettiest wife whispered a magic word... and threw her eagle feather rope high into the sky. 

Up went the rope... higher and higher... and then it dropped over a soft, fluffy cloud. Both ends of the rope dangled down to the women below. The other women tied their own ropes in a circle to the rope from the cloud. 

Holding hands, they began to chant, "Help us, magic ropes... help us."

The cloud sailed slowly across the sky... carrying the six women away with it into the heavens! 

As they passed over their village, their husbands saw them floating away, but they couldn't do anything about it. So they stubbornly scolded their wives' parents, "You should have kept them at home!"

But soon, the six husbands became very lonesome without their wives. 

"Let's go after them and persuade them to return," one husband finally admitted. 

"That's just what I was about to say too," the others nodded. 

So the husbands went up to the top of the mountain with their own eagle feather ropes. They chanted magic spells and threw their ropes into the clouds. And soon, they too were floating in the sky, chasing after their wives. 

"There they are!" a husband pointed as they spotted the women's cloud ahead. 

Their wives looked down and saw their husbands coming after them. "Should we let them catch up?" one asked. 

"They said they didn't want us. They pushed us out of our homes at night!" the prettiest wife frowned. "We don't want them!"

So the women chanted magic words which made their ropes go much faster. Their husbands too urged their ropes on... "Stay where you are! Wait for us!" they yelled. 

But they couldn't catch up with their wives. The chase went on and on and on. And finally, they all turned into glowing stars in the night sky!

American Indians know the higher group of six stars, 'The Pleiades', as 'Young Women'. And the lower six stars or 'Taurus' are called 'Young Men'. Every clear night, the two groups can be seen moving slowly across the sky, forever chasing, but never catching up.
