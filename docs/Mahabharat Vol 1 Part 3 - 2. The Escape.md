# The Escape

Upon arriving in Varanavata, the Pandavas were warmly welcomed by the citizens and formally received by Purochana, Duryodhana's wicked henchman. Purochana proudly led them to a grand new mansion built exclusively for their stay. 

However, the moment they stepped inside, Yudhishthira's sharp senses detected a strong, unmistakable odor of ghee, oil, fat, and lacquer. Recalling Vidura's cryptic warning, he realized instantly that the entire beautiful structure was a highly flammable death trap. 

"We should continue to live here simulating innocence, and yet be cautious and alert," Yudhishthira whispered to his brothers, warning them that fleeing immediately would only cause Duryodhana to send assassins after them. 

A few days later, a friend of Vidura, an expert miner, secretly arrived at the mansion. He confirmed Yudhishthira's worst fears: "On a moonless night of a waning fortnight, Purochana will set fire to the door of this mansion."
The miner quickly set to work. Starting from the center of the mansion's floor, he excavated a deep, wide subterranean tunnel that stretched far beyond the city walls, concealing the entrance with clever levelling. 

For an entire year, the Pandavas lived in the mansion of lacquer, feigning complete ignorance to lull Purochana into a false sense of security. During the day, they hunted extensively in the surrounding forests, memorizing the treacherous terrain to ensure their eventual escape route. 

When the time was finally ripe, Kunti organized a massive almsgiving ritual, feeding countless Brahmanas and citizens in the mansion. Among the crowd was a destitute Nishada woman and her five sons. Seeking shelter, they consumed a large amount of wine and fell into a deep, death-like sleep within the mansion's courtyard. 

That very night, a fierce windstorm swept through Varanavata. Knowing Purochana intended to burn them alive while they slept, Bheema acted first. He set fire to Purochana's sleeping quarters and then ignited the highly flammable walls of the mansion itself. As the flames roared to life with terrifying speed, the Pandavas and Kunti swiftly descended into their secret tunnel. 

The fire consumed the mansion entirely, burning the wicked Purochana alive. The next morning, the horrified citizens sifted through the ashes and discovered the charred remains of the Nishada woman and her five sons. Believing them to be Kunti and the five Pandavas, they sent grief-stricken messengers to Hastinapura. 

Upon hearing the news, King Dhritarashtra wailed in feigned sorrow, while Bheeshma and the entire Kuru court wept genuine tears of devastation. Only Vidura remained secretly calm, knowing the truth of their escape, though he still prayed for their safe passage through the wilderness.

Meanwhile, the Pandavas emerged from the tunnel into a dense, desolate jungle. The horrific roar of wild animals, the lack of water, and the sheer exhaustion of their sleepless escape quickly took their toll. Kunti and the younger Pandavas collapsed on the jungle floor, completely unable to walk further. 

Seeing their despair, the mighty Bheema hoisted his mother onto his shoulder and gathered his four brothers into his massive arms. With the furious power of the wind god Vayu, Bheema crashed through the dense forest, snapping trees and leaping over rivers, carrying his entire family until they finally reached the safety of a giant banyan tree. 

Leaving his exhausted family to rest, Bheema followed the cries of water birds to a nearby lake, fetching water to quench their desperate thirst as they began their perilous life in exile.
