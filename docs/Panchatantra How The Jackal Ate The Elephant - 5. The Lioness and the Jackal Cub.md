# The Lioness and the Jackal Cub

A lioness once gave birth to two healthy cubs. For a time, she could not go out hunting to feed her family, so her husband, a mighty lion, went out every day to bring home the game he killed.

One day, the lion could not find a single animal to kill. As he was returning home empty-handed and frustrated, he spotted a tiny creature near the path. "What's that? A jackal cub?" he wondered.

He raised his huge paw to strike it, when pity for the tiny, helpless creature overcame him. "No! He's just a cub," the lion thought. 
Picking the cub up gently with his teeth, he took it home alive. 

When he reached their cave, the lioness asked, "What have you brought today?"
"I couldn't find a single animal. Then I saw this cub," the lion explained. "I didn't have the heart to kill him. But you may kill and eat him if you like."
"When you didn't have the heart to kill him, how can I?" the motherly lioness replied. "He shall grow up as my third son."

The three cubs soon grew plump and frisky, playing happily together every day. 

One day, while the three brothers were exploring near their den, they saw a huge elephant approaching. 
"Who is that intruder? Come, brothers, let's go and attack him!" the two young lion cubs roared bravely, getting ready to charge.

But the young jackal panicked. "Wait, brothers. Don't! That's an elephant, an enemy of our race. Let's run away!" 
The two young lions burst out laughing. "Run away? Ha! Ha! What's so funny? I'm going to run away. Let them follow if they want to," the jackal thought angrily, slinking back to the den.

Later at the den, the lion cubs excitedly told their parents about the encounter. "...And, mother, it was so funny! Ho! Ho! The way he put his tail between his legs. Ha! Ha! Ho! Ho! And ran for his life... Ha! Ha!"

The jackal cub was furious. "You are fools!" he snapped at the lion cubs. "I should have let that elephant kill you. I wish I hadn't warned you. I..."
"Calm down, my son. Let us go outside. I want to talk to you," the lioness interrupted gently.

When they were alone, she said, "You must never again speak like that to them. They are your older brothers."
"So what?" the jackal bristled. "Do you think I am inferior to them in any way? Why do they make fun of me? I'm going to kill them!"

The lioness held back the smile that came to her lips. *Poor cub. I will have to tell him the truth—before it's too late.*

"My cub, you are the son of a jackal," she revealed gently. "I brought you up because you were helpless. As long as my sons are cubs, they will not harm you. Run away and join your own pack before they know you to be a jackal. If you don't, my sons will sooner or later fight you and kill you."

The poor jackal was so terrified when he heard this, that without a word he slunk away into the forest to find his own pack.

**Moral: You are best off with your own kind.**
