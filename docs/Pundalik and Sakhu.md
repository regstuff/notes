# Pundalik and Sakhu: Two Famous Saints of Maharashtra

### Pundalik

In a bustling town in Maharashtra, there lived a young man named Pundalik. Being the only child, he was brought up with immense love, care, and affection by his doting parents. However, as he grew older, Pundalik fell into bad company. He spent all his time gambling, drinking, and utterly neglecting his responsibilities. 

Worse still, he became incredibly heartless toward his aging parents. He constantly demanded money from them to fuel his gambling habits, verbally abusing them when they had nothing left to give. Stripped of all their wealth and dignity by their own son, the poor, elderly parents were eventually forced to beg on the streets for a handful of rice. 

Unable to bear the unbearable humiliation and suffering any longer, the heartbroken parents decided to leave their home forever and embark on a long, arduous pilgrimage to the holy city of Kashi to spend their final days. 

Shortly after they left, Pundalik completely ran out of money. When he turned to his so-called friends for help, they brutally mocked him and slammed their doors in his face. In that harsh moment of abandonment, a sudden, piercing realization struck Pundalik. *What have I done?* he wept in deep remorse. *My parents were the only ones who truly cared for me, and I drove them away to starve!*

Desperate to cleanse himself of his terrible sins, Pundalik and his wife packed their meager belongings and immediately set out toward Kashi, hoping to purify themselves in the sacred river Ganga. 

During their long journey, they had to cross a dense, dark forest. One night, while his wife slept, Pundalik kept guard. Suddenly, he heard rustling in the bushes. To his absolute astonishment, he saw three beautiful, darkly complexioned women walking gracefully through the forest. He secretly followed them until they reached a small, peaceful clearing where an old sage named Kukkut sat in deep meditation. 

Pundalik hid behind a bush and watched in sheer awe. The three dark women began sweeping the sage's courtyard and spreading fresh cow dung to purify the ground. As they worked, a miraculous transformation occurred. The dark, polluted aura surrounding the women completely vanished, and they suddenly began glowing with a blinding, divine radiance! 

Unable to contain his curiosity, Pundalik stepped out of the bushes. The radiant women instantly backed away. "Shoo! Go away!" they scolded him. "Do not stand in our way. You are one of the worst sinners in the world!"

Pundalik fell to his knees, weeping bitterly. "Please have mercy on me! I know I have sinned, but I am truly repentant. Please tell me who you are."
"I am River Ganga," the first woman said gently. "I am River Yamuna," said the second. "And I am River Saraswati," said the third. 

"Every day, countless sinners come and bathe in our sacred waters," Goddess Ganga explained. "We absorb their sins and become dark and polluted. But every night, we come here to silently serve Sage Kukkut. The sheer power of his absolute, unconditional devotion to his parents purifies even us! If you truly wish to reform, seek his guidance."

The next morning, Pundalik humbly approached the sage. "First, find your parents," Sage Kukkut advised him gently. "Beg for their forgiveness and serve them with absolute, single-minded devotion. Only then should you worship the Lord."

Filled with new purpose, Pundalik frantically searched the pilgrimage routes and finally found his elderly parents before they reached Kashi. Falling weeping at their feet, he begged for their forgiveness. Touched by his genuine transformation, his parents tearfully embraced him. 

Pundalik brought them to the holy town of Pandharpur. He built a small, comfortable hut and dedicated his entire existence to serving their every need, treating them as living gods. 

The fame of Pundalik's unparalleled devotion to his parents soon reached the heavens. One afternoon, while Pundalik was deeply engrossed in massaging his tired father's feet, Lord Krishna Himself (known in Maharashtra as *Vithoba* or *Pandurang*) miraculously appeared at his doorway! 

"Pundalik! Won't you ask me in?" the Lord smiled radiantly. 
Pundalik looked up, stunned by the divine vision. But his hands did not stop massaging his father's feet. "My duty to my parents comes even before God," Pundalik replied respectfully. "I will not get up to welcome You, lest I disturb my father's much-needed sleep. But please, come in, my Lord. Use this brick to stand upon until I am finished."

He casually tossed a simple clay brick toward the doorway. 

Immensely pleased by Pundalik's absolute, unwavering dedication to his duty, Lord Krishna stepped onto the brick, placed His hands casually on His hips, and waited patiently. 

"Pundalik," the Lord beamed, "I am profoundly pleased by your devotion. Ask for any boon, and I shall grant it."
Pundalik bowed his head. "Never, never leave this place, my Lord. Remain standing exactly here forever on this brick, and bless all the devotees who come to see You."

Lord Krishna joyfully agreed. In His place stood a beautiful, magnificent idol of Lord Vithoba, standing on a brick with hands on hips. A grand, spectacular temple was built around this very spot, and to this day, millions of pilgrims travel to Pandharpur to worship the Lord who stayed behind for His greatest devotee.

### Sakhu

Hundreds of years ago, in the ancient town of Karhad, lived a remarkably pious and gentle woman named Sakhu. She was a staunch, unwavering devotee of Lord Pandurang (Vithal). From dawn to dusk, even while performing the most exhausting household chores, she constantly sang His glorious *bhajans*. 

However, Sakhu's life was a living nightmare. Her cruel mother-in-law and harsh sister-in-law deeply despised her devotion. They constantly nagged her, verbally abused her, and intentionally burdened her with backbreaking labor. When she finally sat down to pray after finishing all her work, they would brutally yank her away, beat her, and intentionally starve her for days. 

"O my Lord Pandurang," Sakhu would weep silently, "give me the strength to bear this injustice."

One day, while fetching water from the river, a *Dindi*—a massive, joyous group of pilgrims singing and dancing their way to the great temple in Pandharpur—passed through Karhad. Hearing the ecstatic chants of "Jai Jai Vithal!", an overwhelming, uncontrollable yearning seized Sakhu's heart. 

Unable to resist the divine call, she dropped her water pot, sent a quick message to her mother-in-law through a neighbor, and joyfully joined the procession of pilgrims. 

When the news reached her house, her mother-in-law exploded in terrifying fury. She immediately dispatched Sakhu's husband to drag her back. The angry husband caught up with the procession, publicly beat Sakhu, and dragged her forcefully back to their house. 

"Tie her to the heavy wooden pillar in the courtyard!" the mother-in-law screamed. "Let's see if her precious Vithoba comes to untie her! Starve her until she begs for mercy!" 

Tied tightly to the rough pillar with thick, biting ropes, the famished, exhausted Sakhu simply closed her eyes and completely surrendered herself to Lord Pandurang. 

Miles away in Pandharpur, amidst the deafening chants of millions of pilgrims, the idol of Lord Pandurang suddenly became intensely restless. "What is worrying you, my Lord?" asked Goddess Rukmini. 
"It is my dearest Sakhu," the Lord replied, His eyes filled with compassion. "I must rush to her help. But my outward form will remain here."

At the stroke of midnight, Lord Pandurang magically flew to Karhad. Assuming the exact physical form of a local woman, He quietly untied the exhausted Sakhu. "Go to Pandharpur, my child," the Lord smiled gently. "I will take your exact form and take your place tied to this pillar until you return."

Sakhu joyfully ran to join the pilgrims, while the Lord of the Universe calmly allowed Himself to be tied to the pillar. 

The next morning, Sakhu's husband, suddenly feeling a strange, overwhelming pang of guilt and compassion, quickly untied the ropes. For the next few days, Lord Pandurang—perfectly disguised as Sakhu—flawlessly performed all the backbreaking household chores, cooking, cleaning, and lovingly serving the very people who had abused Him. The family was absolutely amazed by "Sakhu's" sudden, miraculous efficiency and soothing, radiant presence. 

Meanwhile, the real Sakhu finally reached the glorious temple of Pandharpur. As she fell to her knees before the magnificent idol of Lord Vithoba, tears of ultimate, blissful joy streamed down her face. 
"At last I have seen You with my own eyes," she wept. "I desire absolutely nothing more. I do not wish to live any longer to return to that worldly suffering."

With those final words of pure devotion, Sakhu peacefully breathed her last, her soul merging directly with the Lord. A fellow pilgrim from Karhad immediately recognized her body and, being a good, pious man, respectfully cremated her on the sandy banks of the Chandrabhaga river. 

Back in the temple, Goddess Rukmini realized what had happened. *Sakhu has been cremated!* the Goddess thought in alarm. *If she does not return to Karhad, who will free my husband from His disguise? How can He ever return to me?*

Acting swiftly, Goddess Rukmini rushed to the cremation grounds and miraculously brought Sakhu back to life! "Return to your place among your people," the Goddess smiled, blessing the bewildered Sakhu. 

Sakhu quickly joined the returning pilgrims. When she reached the banks of the river in Karhad, she found the "woman" who had freed her patiently waiting with her water pot. They silently exchanged places, and the Lord instantly vanished. 

Just as Sakhu walked into her house, the pilgrim who had cremated her arrived at the doorstep to deliver the tragic news to her family. When he saw Sakhu standing there, alive and well, he nearly fainted in absolute shock!

"How can this be?!" the pilgrim gasped. "I personally watched her die at the feet of Lord Vithoba in Pandharpur! I cremated her with my own two hands!"
The family was equally stunned. "But Sakhu has been here with us the entire time!" her husband argued. "She has been serving us perfectly for days!"

Then, the profound, staggering truth finally dawned on them all with the force of a thunderbolt. 

"It wasn't Sakhu who was serving us," the husband whispered, falling to his knees in overwhelming shame and awe. "It was Lord Pandurang Himself! The Lord of the Universe took her place, bore our cruel abuses, and washed our feet... all to protect His devotee!"

The cruel mother-in-law and the harsh husband wept bitterly, begging Sakhu for forgiveness. Completely transformed by the sheer, unimaginable grace of the Lord, the entire family became the greatest, most devoted servants of Lord Pandurang, spending the rest of their lives singing His endless glory.
