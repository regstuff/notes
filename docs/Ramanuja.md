# Ramanuja

More than nine hundred years ago, in the small village of Sriperumbudur near modern-day Chennai, a devout Brahmana named Asuri Keshavacharya performed a rigorous fire sacrifice, desperately praying for a son. His prayers were answered when his wife, Kantimati, gave birth to a brilliant, radiant boy. The child's deeply pious uncle blessed him and named him **Ramanuja**. 

Ramanuja grew up alongside his beloved cousin and constant companion, Govinda. Possessing an incredibly sharp intellect and a burning desire for spiritual knowledge, Ramanuja quickly mastered his early studies. When he turned sixteen, shortly after being married to a beautiful young girl, his father tragically passed away. To continue his advanced education, the grieving family moved to the great temple city of Kanchi, where Ramanuja and Govinda enrolled as pupils under the renowned, orthodox scholar, Yadava Prakasha. 

However, Ramanuja’s brilliant, highly compassionate interpretations of the ancient scriptures often clashed directly with his orthodox guru’s rigid teachings. Furious at being constantly corrected by his own young pupil, Yadava’s jealousy slowly twisted into a murderous hatred. 

"We must kill Ramanuja in the absolute interests of our community!" Yadava secretly told his loyal disciples. "He is young, brilliant, and critical of our system. He will establish a rival system and destroy us."

Under the guise of a holy pilgrimage to the river Ganga, Yadava plotted to murder Ramanuja in the remote forests. Fortunately, Govinda overheard the sinister conspiracy. While they were resting under a tree before daybreak, Govinda woke Ramanuja. "You must leave this place at once!" Govinda pleaded. "Save our teacher from the terrible sin of murder!"

Yielding to his cousin's desperate pleas, Ramanuja slipped away into the dark, terrifying forest. Completely lost and exhausted, he suddenly met a strange fowler (hunter) and his beautiful wife, who gently guided him southward through the dense jungle. When they stopped to rest for the night, the fowler's wife complained of intense thirst. Ramanuja walked to a nearby well to fetch water. When he returned, the mysterious couple had completely vanished! 

Looking around in absolute bewilderment, Ramanuja suddenly noticed the towering temple *gopurams* (spires) of Kanchi in the distance. Overwhelmed with profound devotion, he realized the 'fowler' and his wife were none other than Lord Narayana (Vishnu) and Goddess Lakshmi, who had personally guided him to absolute safety! 

Ramanuja immediately rushed to the magnificent temple of Lord Varadaraja, dedicating his entire life to the deity's service. 

Sometime later, Yadava Prakasha returned to Kanchi, utterly shocked to find Ramanuja alive and thriving. Out of sheer, burning jealousy, Yadava formally expelled Ramanuja from his school. 

Undeterred, Ramanuja sought out Kanchi Purna, a highly revered, saintly devotee of Lord Varadaraja. Because Kanchi Purna belonged to a lower caste, orthodox Brahmanas refused to accept him as a teacher. But Ramanuja, who profoundly believed that absolute devotion to God erased all social barriers, humbly fell at Kanchi Purna's feet. 
"Before God, all are absolutely equal!" Ramanuja declared. "Who am I to make a distinction between Brahmana and non-Brahmana? You shall be my Guru!"

One day, Ramanuja heard a passing devotee reciting breathtakingly beautiful verses in praise of the Lord. "Who composed these exquisite verses?" Ramanuja asked in awe. 
"My master, the great Yamunacharya of Srirangam," the devotee replied. 

Eager to meet the legendary saint, Ramanuja immediately traveled to the holy island city of Srirangam. Tragically, he arrived just moments after the great Yamunacharya had peacefully passed away. 

As Ramanuja paid his last respects to the great master's body, he noticed that three fingers of Yamunacharya's right hand were tightly folded inward. "It symbolizes three great, unfulfilled wishes," the grieving disciples explained. 

Standing before the master's body, Ramanuja took three solemn, earth-shattering vows: to widely propagate the path of loving devotion (*Bhakti*), to write a grand, definitive commentary on the *Brahmasutras*, and to pay off the spiritual debt to the ancient Sage Parashara. 
The moment he spoke these vows, the master's three dead fingers miraculously straightened out! The astounded disciples instantly realized that Ramanuja was the true, divinely chosen successor to their great master. 

Ramanuja's fame as a brilliant, incredibly compassionate spiritual leader spread like wildfire. Even his former, arrogant guru, Yadava Prakasha, deeply humbled by Ramanuja's purity, came to him, begged for forgiveness, and willingly became his devoted disciple! 

Eventually, Ramanuja permanently settled in Srirangam. Seeking the ultimate secret to salvation, he approached the reclusive Sage Goshti Purna. After making Ramanuja walk to him eighteen separate times to test his dedication, the sage finally whispered the supreme, secret mantra into his ear: ***"Om Namo Narayanaya."***

"I am now convinced of your absolute devotion," Goshti Purna warned him strictly. "This mantra is the ultimate key to salvation. But you must promise to keep it an absolute secret. If you reveal it to the unworthy masses, you will surely go to hell!"

"I promise!" Ramanuja agreed. But the very next morning, a radically different thought seized him. He climbed to the very top of the towering temple, gathered the massive, bustling crowds of ordinary citizens, and loudly shouted the sacred, secret mantra for the entire world to hear! 

Goshti Purna was absolutely enraged. "You will go to hell for breaking your solemn promise!" he roared. 
Ramanuja smiled peacefully. "I don't mind going to hell, O Guru, if it helps a hundred thousand ordinary people achieve eternal salvation!"
Deeply moved by Ramanuja's unimaginable, selfless compassion for humanity, Goshti Purna wept and tightly embraced him. 

Ramanuja's absolute disregard for rigid caste rules constantly shocked his orthodox disciples. At Srirangam, he noticed a low-caste wrestler named Dhanurdasa who was wildly infatuated with his beautiful wife, holding an umbrella over her head in the scorching sun while the orthodox crowds scoffed at him. 

"Why do you act so shamelessly?" Ramanuja asked him gently. 
"Because my wife has the most beautiful eyes in the world," Dhanurdasa replied honestly. 
"If I show you eyes far more beautiful than hers, will you love them instead?" Ramanuja smiled. He took Dhanurdasa into the dark, inner sanctum of the temple and pointed to the mesmerizing, glowing, eternal eyes of Lord Ranganatha. 

Dhanurdasa was instantly transformed. He and his wife became Ramanuja's most fiercely devoted followers. However, Ramanuja's upper-caste disciples deeply resented Dhanurdasa's presence. To teach them a profound lesson in true detachment, Ramanuja devised a brilliant plan. He asked his disciples to secretly steal Dhanurdasa's wife's expensive gold ornaments while she was sleeping. 

As the disciples crept in and began carefully slipping the bangles off her right arm, she woke up. Realizing they were poor Brahmanas in need, she pretended to stay asleep and gently rolled over, offering her left arm so they could easily take the rest! Frightened by her movement, the disciples fled with only half the jewelry. 

When Dhanurdasa returned, his wife explained what had happened. "You fool!" Dhanurdasa scolded her gently. "Because you moved, you frightened them away. You should have remained perfectly still so they could take everything! We have given up all worldly attachments for the Lord."

When the ashamed disciples reported this, Ramanuja smiled. "Look at your own behavior," he pointed out gently. "Earlier today, I secretly hid your freshly washed clothes. You completely lost your tempers over simple wet rags! Yet, you have seen how this 'low-caste' couple reacted to the loss of highly valuable gold. Dhanurdasa is a true Vaishnava. He has given up all ego and attachment, while you are still trapped by it."
The humbled disciples fell at Dhanurdasa's feet. 

To fulfill his second vow, Ramanuja traveled all the way to Kashmir to study an ancient, rare manuscript of the *Brahmasutras*. Though local scholars fiercely opposed him and violently snatched the book away, Ramanuja's brilliant disciple, Kuresha, had already miraculously memorized the entire text overnight! Using Kuresha's flawless memory, Ramanuja finally dictated his magnum opus: the *Sri Bhashya*. 

At the age of seventy-nine, facing severe, violent persecution from a fanatical Shaivite Chola king, Ramanuja was forced to leave his beloved Srirangam. He sought refuge in Karnataka, where his profound teachings completely transformed the powerful Hoysala King Bittideva, who eagerly became his disciple and took the name Vishnu Vardhana. 

While in Karnataka, Ramanuja miraculously discovered a lost, ancient idol of Lord Narayana buried in an anthill near Yadugiri (Melkote). He built a grand temple there, but learned that a second, vital idol—*Ramapriya*—had been looted years ago by northern invaders and taken to Delhi. 

Fearlessly, the elderly Ramanuja traveled all the way to Delhi and successfully retrieved the sacred idol from a powerful Muslim nobleman. On his dangerous journey back to Yadugiri, Ramanuja's party was suddenly ambushed by heavily armed raiders. 

Just when all seemed lost, a brave group of local "untouchables" fiercely attacked the raiders and successfully drove them away, saving the sacred idol and the great saint's life. 

"We cannot enter your temples, for we are untouchables," the brave villagers bowed respectfully. 
Ramanuja's eyes filled with tears of pure love. "You are not untouchables!" he declared passionately. "You are **Thirukulathar**—the Children of God! Believe me, the all-pervading God resides in you, too!" 

Shattering centuries of rigid, oppressive tradition, Ramanuja proudly threw open the massive wooden doors of the grand Yadugiri temple, allowing the "untouchables" to enter the inner sanctum and worship freely alongside the Brahmanas. 

Having successfully fulfilled all his vows and firmly established the inclusive, profoundly compassionate philosophy of *Vishishtadvaita* (qualified monism), Ramanuja finally returned to Srirangam, where he spent his final, peaceful days surrounded by his loving, devoted followers from every caste and walk of life.
