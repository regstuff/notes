# The Pandavas at Virata's Palace

Having received the boon of anonymity from Lord Yama, the Pandavas discussed where they should spend their thirteenth year of exile. Arjuna suggested the kingdom of the aged Matsya King, Virata, describing him as powerful, virtuous, and charitable. 

The brothers decided to disguise themselves and enter the king's service in different capacities. Yudhishthira would present himself as Kanka, a Brahmana skilled in dice. Bheema chose to become Ballava, a cook who could also entertain the court as a wrestler. Arjuna, honoring the curse given to him by Urvashi, disguised himself as Brihannala, a eunuch skilled in music and dance, to teach the women of the palace. Nakula became Granthika to look after the king's horses, and Sahadeva took the name Tantipala to manage the royal herds. Draupadi resolved to serve Queen Sudeshna as a Sairandhri (maid) under the name Malini. 

Approaching Virata's capital, they decided to hide their celestial weapons. Nakula climbed a massive, sturdy Shami tree near a cremation ground and secured their weapons high in its branches. To deter people from coming near, they hung a foul-smelling animal carcass from the tree, warning locals that it was a cursed corpse. 

Entering the court of King Virata, their majestic bearings impressed the monarch. He gladly employed all of them in their requested roles, completely unaware of their true identities. 

Ten months passed peacefully in the Matsya kingdom. However, trouble arrived in the form of Keechaka, the incredibly powerful commander of Virata's army and Queen Sudeshna's brother. Catching sight of the disguised Draupadi, Keechaka was consumed by lust. He approached her, offering her unimaginable wealth and status if she became his wife. 

"I am a mere maid-servant," Draupadi warned him. "Moreover, I am a married lady. My husbands are five powerful Gandharvas. Anyone who comes near me will be destroyed by them. Rest assured, O Keechaka, if you touch me, you will not live."

Her warnings did not dampen Keechaka's ardor. He colluded with his sister, Queen Sudeshna, to send Draupadi to his chambers under the pretext of fetching wine. Despite Draupadi's fears, the queen ordered her to go. 

Praying to Surya for protection, Draupadi entered Keechaka's room. When he tried to grab her, she violently pushed him away and fled towards the royal court, where King Virata, Yudhishthira (Kanka), and Bheema (Ballava) were present. Keechaka pursued her into the open court, caught her by the hair, and kicked her brutally. 

Bheema, watching from his post, gnashed his teeth and readied himself to tear Keechaka apart. But Yudhishthira, fearing their identities would be exposed prematurely, signaled Bheema to control his anger. He then turned to Draupadi. "Do not interrupt the game of dice," Yudhishthira said coldly. "The Gandharvas will look after you. Go to Sudeshna's apartment."

That night, Draupadi slipped into the kitchens and wept before Bheema. "I cannot endure it when my weak husbands sit back and watch me being kicked!" she sobbed. "I will swallow poison rather than yield to Keechaka."

Wiping her tears, Bheema formulated a plan. "I will kill the wretched Keechaka. Arrange a meeting with him tonight at the dancing hall, and leave the rest to me."

The next day, Draupadi feigned submission and told Keechaka to meet her alone in the dancing hall at night. Overjoyed, Keechaka dressed in his finest robes and sneaked into the dark hall, finding a figure lying on a couch covered with a blanket. 

"O beautiful maiden, I have come," Keechaka whispered, reaching out in the dark. 

Suddenly, a powerful hand gripped his wrist. "You wretch!" Bheema's voice roared. "Today your sister will see you dragged to the ground!"

A terrifying, silent wrestling match ensued in the darkness. The two immense warriors locked arms, hurled each other around, and fought with ferocious strength. Finally, Bheema overpowered Keechaka, wrapping his massive arms around him. With a final, brutal effort, Bheema broke the commander's back, crushing him into a shapeless, lifeless mass. 

The next morning, Keechaka's followers, the Sootas, found his mangled body. Realizing Draupadi was the cause, they decided to burn her alive on his funeral pyre. They seized her and dragged her towards the cremation grounds. 

Hearing her cries, Bheema slipped out of the palace. He rushed ahead to the cremation grounds, uprooted a massive tree, and attacked the Sootas. Terrified by the "Gandharva," the Sootas dropped Draupadi and fled, but Bheema slaughtered all one hundred and five of them. 

The news of Keechaka and his followers being massacred by invisible Gandharvas sent shockwaves through the city. King Virata, terrified, ordered Draupadi to leave the kingdom, fearing her presence would invite destruction. But Draupadi pleaded, "Please wait for thirteen more days. By then, the Gandharvas will have accomplished their task and will repay you for your kindness."

The King reluctantly agreed, unaware that the thirteenth day would mark the exact end of the Pandavas' grueling thirteen-year exile.
