# Narsinh Mehta

"Vaishnava Jana to..." was Mahatma Gandhi's favorite *bhajan* (devotional song). This verse, along with thousands of other beautiful hymns and songs, was composed by Narsinh Mehta, the great poet-saint of Gujarat. Born in the 15th century, Narsinh was a supreme devotee of Lord Krishna whose life became a legendary saga of faith, poetry, and social reform.

Narsinh Mehta was born into a prestigious Nagar Brahman family in the small village of Talaja in Saurashtra. However, his early life was marked by tragedy and hardship. He was born completely dumb, unable to speak a single word. When he was just five years old, both his parents passed away, leaving his loving grandmother, Jay Kunvar, to raise him. 

The other village boys constantly teased and bullied the mute child. Heartbroken but deeply faithful, his grandmother took eight-year-old Narsinh to a local temple on the auspicious day of *Vasant Panchami*. Outside the temple, they met a compassionate, radiant Sadhu. 

"Sadhu Maharaj, my grandson has been dumb since birth," the grandmother pleaded. "Please bless him with speech."
The holy man smiled warmly at the boy. "He is destined to be a great devotee of Lord Krishna. He shall speak." The Sadhu sprinkled holy water from his *kamandalu* onto the boy and whispered into his ear, "Son, repeat with me! Radhe Krishna! Radhe Krishna!"

To his grandmother's utter joy and astonishment, little Narsinh's voice broke free, and he clearly chanted the holy names! The gift of speech had been miraculously bestowed upon him.

A year later, Narsinh and his grandmother moved to Junagadh to live with his cousin, Bansidhar, who served as a head-constable. While Bansi was accommodating, his sharp-tongued wife, Duritgauri, bitterly resented the "useless" additions to her household. Seeking to make Narsinh responsible, she forced him into an early marriage with a young girl named Manekgauri. 

Despite his marriage, Narsinh showed absolutely no interest in worldly pursuits or earning a living. Instead of attending school or finding work, he spent all his days wandering in temples, deeply engrossed in singing devotional *kirtans*. 

Furious at his endless "loafing," his sister-in-law Duritgauri constantly hurled vicious insults at him, feeding him only stale, leftover *rotis*. One evening, unable to bear her cruel taunts any longer, the teenage Narsinh left the house and walked aimlessly into a dense forest. Finding a secluded, abandoned Shiva temple, he collapsed before the *Shiva Linga*. 

"O Lord, I seek refuge in You!" Narsinh prayed desperately. "I will starve myself here until You appear before me and bless me!"

He meditated fiercely, without food or water, for seven continuous days and nights. Finally, Lord Shiva appeared before him in the guise of a Sadhu, pleased with his immense devotion. "Ask for a boon!" Lord Shiva offered.
"What I value most is a vision of Lord Krishna," Narsinh replied humbly. 

Granting his profound wish, Lord Shiva miraculously transported Narsinh to the divine city of Dwaraka. There, on the mystical night of *Sharad Purnima*, Narsinh was blessed to witness Lord Krishna joyously performing the *Rasa Lila* dance with the *gopis* (cowherdesses). 

Standing mesmerized by the divine spectacle, Narsinh was handed a burning torch by Lord Krishna himself to light the dance. Narsinh was so utterly absorbed in the ecstasy of the divine vision that he didn't even notice when the torch burned down entirely, scorching his own hand. 
Smiling at his pure devotion, Lord Krishna touched Narsinh's burned hand, instantly healing it without a single scar. Handing him a pair of *karatals* (cymbals), the Lord said, "Go home, Narsinh. Whenever you need My help, sing the *Kedar Raga*, and I will be there."

Upon returning to Junagadh, Narsinh joyously shared his divine experience with his family. But his cousin Bansi, thinking him utterly mad, angrily threw him out of the house. Unfazed, Narsinh took his loving wife Manekgauri and their children and moved into a local *dharmashala* (free inn). 

Moved by Narsinh's devotion, a wealthy merchant offered to build a beautiful temple for him. Narsinh accepted, and soon, his temple became a vibrant center of worship, flocking with *sadhus* and devotees. Narsinh spent his days composing and singing his immortal *bhajans*, completely unconcerned with money or material wealth. 

This absolute reliance on Lord Krishna was repeatedly tested. When his daughter Kunverbai came of age to go to her in-laws' house, the customs demanded expensive gifts and ornaments that Narsinh simply did not possess. As the impatient priest waited to take the girl away, Narsinh merely closed his eyes and sang. Miraculously, beautiful clothes and precious jewels materialized before them, sent directly by the Lord's grace. 

Later, when Narsinh's son Shamaldas was to be married into a wealthy family in Vadnagar, the jealous, high-caste Nagar Brahmans of Junagadh conspired to ruin the wedding. They secretly wrote to the bride's father, exposing Narsinh as a penniless pauper who couldn't even afford a horse for the wedding procession. 

On the day of the wedding, Narsinh's procession indeed looked like a ragged band of beggars. But before they reached Vadnagar, a magnificent retinue of elephants, golden chariots, and richly dressed nobles suddenly joined them, led by a resplendent merchant on a white horse. It was Lord Krishna in disguise, turning the pauper's procession into a royal parade, ensuring the wedding was celebrated with unparalleled pomp!

Narsinh’s profound spiritual vision saw God in every human being, regardless of their social standing. This was deeply radical in a society strictly divided by caste. One day, he was invited to sing *bhajans* in the colony of the deeply marginalized scavengers (whom Mahatma Gandhi would later call *Harijans*). Narsinh gladly went and spent the entire night singing with them. 

The orthodox Nagar Brahmans were scandalized by this "pollution" and promptly excommunicated him. To humiliate him further, they invited him to a grand community dinner, forcing him to sit entirely apart from the "pure" Brahmans. But as the arrogant priests sat down to eat, a miraculous illusion occurred: every single Brahman looked to his side and saw a scavenger sitting right next to him! Terrified by this divine rebuke, the haughty Nagars begged for Narsinh's forgiveness.

Lord Krishna’s interventions in Narsinh’s life became legendary. Once, a group of pilgrims traveling to Dwaraka needed to deposit their cash safely. As a cruel joke, the mischievous Nagars directed them to Narsinh, claiming he was a wealthy merchant with branches in Dwaraka. Narsinh innocently accepted their 700 rupees and wrote a promissory note addressed to "Shamalsha Vasudeva"—a name for Lord Krishna. When the pilgrims reached Dwaraka, they were shocked to actually find a merchant named Shamalsha, who gladly honored the note and paid them in full!

Despite these endless miracles, Narsinh's enemies, led by the wicked Sarang Mehta, remained fiercely jealous. They approached King Mandlik of Junagadh, accusing Narsinh of being a massive fraud and a charlatan. Sarang also revealed a secret: Narsinh had recently borrowed money from a harsh moneylender named Dharnidhar, pledging that he would never sing his beloved *Kedar Raga* until the debt was fully repaid. 

King Mandlik demanded proof of Narsinh's divine connection. "Put this heavy necklace on the idol of Krishna in the royal temple," the King commanded. "You will be locked outside. If your Lord is real, He must take the necklace from the idol and place it around your neck before dawn. Otherwise, you will be executed as a fraud!"

Locked outside the temple in the freezing night, Narsinh desperately wished he could sing the *Kedar Raga* to summon his Lord, but his honor bound him to his pledge to the moneylender. 

Miles away, in the dead of night, Lord Krishna took the form of Narsinh Mehta. He visited the moneylender Dharnidhar, repaid the entire debt in full, and obtained a signed receipt. 

Suddenly, as Narsinh sat shivering outside the temple, the receipt fluttered down from the sky and fell directly into his lap! Realizing his debt was paid and his pledge fulfilled, Narsinh erupted into ecstatic song, his voice soaring with the majestic notes of the *Kedar Raga*. 

Instantly, the heavy wooden doors of the royal temple burst open with a blinding flash of divine light. The heavy necklace flew from the stone idol and draped itself perfectly around Narsinh Mehta’s neck! 

The King, the conspirators, and the entire city stood utterly stunned, finally recognizing the supreme truth of Narsinh's devotion. From that glorious morning until his passing at a ripe old age, Narsinh Mehta was universally revered as a true saint, his life and songs serving as an eternal testament to the power of pure, unconditional love for the Divine.
