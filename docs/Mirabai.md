# Mirabai: She Gave Her Heart to Krishna

In the kingdom of Kurkhi in Rajasthan, Rana Ratansingh ruled over a prosperous land. He had a beautiful, sweet-natured daughter named Mira. When Mira was barely five years old, she watched a grand wedding procession pass by the palace windows. 

Curious, the little girl turned to her mother and asked, "Mother, where is my bridegroom?"
Smiling gently, her mother led her to a quiet corner of the room where a small, beautiful bronze idol of Lord Krishna was kept. "There is your husband! Gopala himself," her mother said playfully. "Love him and serve him as a good wife would."

Little Mira took her mother’s words with absolute, unwavering seriousness. "From now on, Gopala is mine and I am his," she declared. From that single moment, she abandoned all childish games, spending her days singing devotional hymns, playing her ektara, and worshipping her divine groom.

As the years passed, Mira grew into a radiant young woman. In time, she was married to Prince Bhojraj of Chittor, a brave warrior and heir to a powerful kingdom. Mira proved to be an ideal Hindu wife, managing her household duties dutifully and earning her husband’s deep affection. However, as soon as her daily chores were completed, she would retreat to her private chamber to immerse herself in devotion to her beloved Gopala.

Trouble arose when her mother-in-law demanded that she worship Goddess Durga, the family deity of Chittor. 
"Forgive me, mother," Mira replied humbly but firmly, folding her hands. "I have already offered myself to Lord Krishna. I cannot bow before any other deity."

Her sister-in-law, Uda, also tried to force her to conform, but Mira remained steadfast. Enraged by her refusal, Uda plotted revenge. She went to Prince Bhojraj and whispered malicious lies: "Mira goes every evening to the temple! She has a secret lover!"

Pushed by jealousy, Bhojraj rushed into the temple at midnight with a drawn sword. "Show me your lover that I may kill him!" he demanded.
With a serene smile, Mira pointed to the idol of Krishna. "There he is! He has stolen my heart and will not give it back!"

Relieved and realizing his wife’s devotion was purely spiritual, Bhojraj built a dedicated temple for her within the palace grounds. Devotees began to gather around Mira, and the soulful beauty of her bhajans spread far and wide. Word of her divine music even reached the Mughal Emperor Akbar and his legendary court musician, Tansen. 

Desperate to hear her sing, Akbar and Tansen disguised themselves as Hindu sadhus in saffron robes and secretly traveled into Rajput territory to visit the temple in Chittor. Listening to Mira sing in pure spiritual ecstasy, Akbar was spellbound. Before departing, the Emperor touched her feet and placed a priceless necklace at the base of the Krishna idol as an offering.

When news leaked that the Mughal Emperor had visited the temple and touched the princess's feet, Rana Bhojraj was consumed by outrage. "Did you dare allow a Mughal cur to touch your feet—the feet of a Rajput princess?" he raged at Mira. "For the disgrace you have brought upon Rajasthan, go and drown yourself in the river!"

Without a word of protest, the dutiful wife took her idol of Lord Krishna and walked to the riverbank. As she prepared to leap into the rushing waters, a gentle hand caught her arm from behind. She turned around and fainted into the arms of Lord Krishna himself. 

When she opened her eyes, the Lord smiled radiantly down at her. "Your life with your earthly husband is over. Now you are mine. Go and seek me henceforth in Brindavan."

Mira sang and danced her way across the desert to Brindavan, oblivious to hardship. Meanwhile, back in Chittor, Rana Bhojraj deeply repented his harsh sentence. Learning that Mira had survived, he dressed in saffron robes as a humble sadhu, traveled to Brindavan, and fell at her feet. 
"I beg your forgiveness, Mira," Bhojraj wept. "Please return to Chittor."
"Has Mira ever gone against the wishes of her husband? Yes, I will return," she answered gently.

Mira returned to Chittor, where she was granted full freedom to worship. Almost ten years after her marriage, Rana Bhojraj passed away. His father demanded that the widowed Mira perform *Sati* by throwing herself onto her husband's funeral pyre. 
Mira firmly refused. "As long as the divine Lord lives in my heart, I will not die!"

When Bhojraj's brother became the new Rana, he treated Mira with intense hostility, forbidding her from hosting holy men or worshipping in the palace. Undeterred, Mira left the palace to sing and dance in the public temples. 

Furious at her defiance, the new Rana attempted to murder her three times:
First, he sent her a sealed basket containing a venomous cobra, telling her it was a flower garland. When Mira opened the basket in devotion, the deadly snake miraculously transformed into a garland of fragrant flowers.
Next, he replaced her bedstead with one embedded with sharp, poison-tipped nails hidden under rose petals. When Mira lay down, the nails miraculously turned soft and limp as rose petals, and she slept peacefully all night.
Finally, the Rana mixed deadly poison into a bowl of nectar and sent it to her. Mira drank the potion without hesitation, and the poison instantly turned into harmless, sweet nectar.

Tired of constant persecution, Mirabai wrote a letter to the great saint Tulsidas, seeking his guidance. Tulsidas wrote back: *"Shun those who come in the way of your worship, even if they be your closest relatives."*

Taking the saint's advice, Mirabai left Chittor and went to Merta, her uncle's kingdom, where she was welcomed with love and allowed to worship in peace. As she grew older, she set out on a final, joyful pilgrimage to Mathura, Brindavan, and finally Dwarka. 

Inside the sacred temple of Dwarka, during a grand gathering of devotees, Mirabai danced in pure divine ecstasy. As her songs reached a crescendo, a blinding, heavenly light filled the sanctum. Mirabai collapsed into a trance before the idol of Lord Krishna, and her soul and physical body merged completely into the image of her Lord—reunited forever with the divine groom she had chosen at the tender age of five.
