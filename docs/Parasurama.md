# Parashurama

In ancient times, a good and noble king named Gadhi ruled the land. He had a beautiful daughter named Satyavati, but no son. One day, a great sage named Richika, the son of Bhrigu, saw the princess and immediately wished to marry her. 

When Richika asked for her hand, King Gadhi set a seemingly impossible dowry: one thousand brown horses. To the king's astonishment, the powerful sage produced the horses without difficulty, and he and Satyavati were joyously married. 

Sometime later, Sage Bhrigu visited his son and daughter-in-law. Extremely pleased with Satyavati's devotion and care, he offered her a boon. 
"Father, give me a son, and please give my mother a son as well," Satyavati requested. 
Bhrigu smiled and handed her two separate pots containing sacred rice and milk. "This one is for you, and that one is for your mother," he instructed carefully. 

However, a fateful mix-up occurred. Satyavati's mother secretly took the pot meant for Satyavati, and Satyavati unknowingly consumed the one meant for her mother. 

Through his divine spiritual vision, Sage Bhrigu saw the exchange. "Alas, my child!" he told a devastated Satyavati. "You did not heed my instructions carefully. Because of this mix-up, your mother's son, though a Kshatriya (warrior), will take to the peaceful life of an ascetic. And your son, though born a Brahmana, will become a fierce, bloodthirsty warrior."

Terrified, Satyavati pleaded with her father-in-law. "O revered sire, let my grandson be such a warrior, but please, not my son!"
Moved by her tears, Bhrigu agreed. "So be it."

In due time, Satyavati gave birth to a son named Jamadagni. True to the prophecy, Jamadagni grew up to be a profoundly peaceful, devout Brahmana who mastered the Vedas. He married the chaste and virtuous Renuka, and they settled into the quiet life of forest hermits. 

Meanwhile, the Kshatriya kings of the world had become incredibly arrogant and vicious, heavily burdening Mother Earth. The absolute worst among them was Kartavirya Arjuna, the monstrous, thousand-armed king of the Haihaya tribe. Drunk on his own power, Kartavirya Arjuna even dared to invade the heavens to challenge Indra, the king of the gods. 

Desperate, the gods appealed to Lord Vishnu for protection. "I will take an avatar on earth as a Brahmana with unusual physical prowess to overpower him," Lord Vishnu promised. 

Shortly after, five sons were born to Renuka and Jamadagni. The fifth and youngest was Rama—the promised incarnation of Lord Vishnu. Unlike his ascetic brothers, young Rama was deeply fascinated by weapons and physical combat. As he grew into a sturdy youth, he traveled to the Gandhamadana mountains to perform severe austerities to please Lord Shiva. 

Impressed by his fierce devotion, Lord Shiva appeared and granted him a magnificent, fiery axe called the *Parashu*. From that day forward, the fearsome warrior was known as Parashurama (Rama of the Axe). 

When Parashurama returned to the ashrama, a shocking family tragedy occurred. For a fleeting transgression in her thoughts, Sage Jamadagni furiously condemned his chaste wife, Renuka. When his elder sons refused to punish their mother, the enraged sage cursed them with madness. 

"Kill your mother! She has sinned," Jamadagni ordered his youngest son. 
Without a single moment's hesitation, Parashurama raised his axe and beheaded his mother. 

Deeply pleased by this absolute, unquestioning obedience, Jamadagni offered him a boon. 
"Restore my mother's life and love her as before," Parashurama requested instantly. "And give my brothers back their senses."
The sage happily granted the boons, restoring peace to the family. 

However, their peace was soon shattered by the tyrant Kartavirya Arjuna. While visiting the forest, the thousand-armed king demanded hospitality at Jamadagni's ashrama. The sage graciously hosted the king and his massive army, effortlessly providing endless food using the magical milk of his divine *Homa* cow. 

Driven by intense greed, Kartavirya Arjuna's sons secretly stole the sacred calf that night and fled. 

When Parashurama returned and discovered the theft, he erupted in terrifying fury. Grabbing his divine axe, he charged directly into Kartavirya Arjuna's heavily fortified palace. A dreadful, earth-shaking combat ensued. Moving with the speed of lightning, Parashurama hacked off every single one of Kartavirya Arjuna's thousand arms and finally slew the tyrant, gently carrying the stolen calf back to his father's ashrama. 

But the cycle of violence had only just begun. Seeking revenge, the sons of Kartavirya Arjuna cowardly ambushed the ashrama while Parashurama was away in the forest gathering firewood. Finding the peaceful Sage Jamadagni deep in unarmed meditation, they brutally shot him with arrows from all directions and left him to die. 

When Parashurama returned to find his beloved father's bloodied corpse, his grief transformed into an apocalyptic rage. After cremating his father, he stood before the roaring funeral pyre and took a terrifying, earth-shattering vow:
"I shall not rest, dear father, till I have wiped out the entire martial race of the Kshatriyas from the face of the earth!"

Parashurama first hunted down and annihilated the sons of Kartavirya Arjuna. Then, wielding his bloody axe like the god of death himself, he marched across the world, systematically massacring every Kshatriya king and army he could find. Every time a new generation of Kshatriya sons grew old enough to wield a sword, Parashurama returned to slaughter them too. 

Relentless and utterly ruthless, Parashurama patiently continued his horrifying crusade, single-handedly wiping out twenty-one entire generations of Kshatriyas, until the earth was practically drowning in their blood. 

Finally, Mother Earth could bear the absolute destruction no longer. She appealed to Sage Kashyapa, who used his immense spiritual powers to summon the spirit of Richika, Parashurama's grandfather. 

The glowing spirit appeared before the raging warrior. "Stop, Rama!" Richika commanded. "You have avenged your father many times over. Mother Earth has been relieved of all evil Kshatriyas. Your work is done. It is now time for you to desist."

Bound by his absolute duty to obey his ancestors, Parashurama immediately dropped his bloody axe. "It is my duty to obey my ancestors," he bowed respectfully. "I shall do as you say."

To rid himself of the immense karmic burden of his endless massacres, Parashurama organized a massive sacrifice, distributing all the conquered lands and unimaginable wealth to the Brahmanas. 

Just as the sacrifice concluded, Drona, the son of Sage Bharadwaj, arrived late. 
"Alas! You come late," Parashurama told him. "All that I have left is this body and my weapons. I can give you either."
"I shall receive the weapons with the mysteries of their use," Drona replied eagerly. Parashurama willingly initiated Drona into the ultimate mastery of all divine weapons, making Drona the greatest master of arms of his era. 

Having given away absolutely everything he owned, the terrifying warrior-sage finally retired to the remote crests of the Mahendra Mountains, spending the rest of his immortal days in deep, peaceful meditation.
