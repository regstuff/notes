# Krishna: The Victorious

Shukadeva continued narrating the stories of Krishna's various marriages and exploits to King Pareekshit. 

Krishna visited Indraprastha to meet the Pandavas. Kunti wept with joy to see her nephew. Krishna stayed in Indraprastha for the four months of the rainy season. One day, while hunting by the Yamuna with Arjuna, they met Kalindi, the daughter of Surya, who was performing penance to win Lord Vishnu as her husband. Krishna took her to Indraprastha and later married her in Dwaraka. 

During this time, Vishwakarma built a wonderful city for the Pandavas. Later, Arjuna and Krishna helped Agni consume the Khandava forest. During the fire, Arjuna saved Maya the demon, who built a magnificent assembly hall for Yudhishthira in gratitude. 

After returning to Dwaraka, Krishna married Mitravinda, the princess of Ujjayini, taking her away from her Swayamvara. 

Later, King Nagnajit of Ayodhya announced that whoever could overpower seven wild, ferocious bulls would win the hand of his daughter, Satya. Hearing this, Krishna went to Ayodhya. He assumed seven different forms simultaneously and effortlessly subdued all seven bulls. He married Satya and returned to Dwaraka, while Arjuna protected their procession from envious rival suitors. 

Krishna also married Bhadra and Lakshmana. 

Meanwhile, the demon Narakasura (Bhaumasura) of Pragjyotishapura was terrorizing Devaloka, having stolen the ear-rings of Mother Aditi and the umbrella of Varuna. Krishna, accompanied by Satyabhama, rode on Garuda to Pragjyotishapura. Krishna destroyed the magical barriers of fire, water, and dragnets created by the five-headed demon Mura. He killed Mura and his seven sons, and then decapitated Narakasura with the Sudarshana Chakra. 

Bhoomi, Mother Earth, returned the stolen ear-rings and umbrella to Krishna. Krishna then entered Narakasura's palace and freed sixteen thousand captive princesses. Enraptured by Krishna, all sixteen thousand wished to marry him. Krishna sent them all to Dwaraka. 

After visiting Amaravati to return Aditi's ear-rings and bringing back the celestial Kalpavriksha tree for Satyabhama, Krishna returned to Dwaraka. There, he expanded himself into sixteen thousand forms and married all sixteen thousand princesses simultaneously in separate palaces! 

In time, Krishna's wives gave birth to ten sons each. Pradyumna, Krishna's son with Rukmini, married Rukmavati, the daughter of Rukmi. Their son was Aniruddha. 

Banasura, the thousand-armed King of Shonitapura and a devotee of Lord Shiva, craved a mighty opponent. Shiva foretold that his flagstaff would break when he met his match. 

Banasura's daughter, Usha, had a dream of a handsome prince. Her friend Chitralekha, a talented artist, drew portraits of gods and princes until Usha identified Aniruddha. Using her yogic powers, Chitralekha brought Aniruddha from Dwaraka to Usha's palace. When Banasura discovered Aniruddha, he bound him with Nagapasha (serpent bonds). 

Narada brought the news to Dwaraka. Krishna, Balarama, and Pradyumna led an army against Shonitapura. Lord Shiva and Karttikeya fought on Banasura's side, but Krishna used the Jrumbhanastra weapon to make Shiva drowsy. Krishna then severed all of Banasura's thousand arms except four with the Sudarshana Chakra. Shiva intervened, and Krishna spared Banasura's life. Banasura repented and released Aniruddha and Usha. 

Later, Paundraka, the King of Karusha, claiming to be the true 'Vaasudeva', sent an arrogant ultimatum to Krishna. Krishna marched to Kashi, where Paundraka was staying with his ally, the King of Kashi. Krishna wiped out their combined armies, killed Paundraka, and severed the King of Kashi's head with his Chakra, sending it flying into Kashi. 

Sudakshina, the Prince of Kashi, performed a dark Yajna to avenge his father, invoking a fiery demon to destroy Dwaraka. But Krishna dispatched the Sudarshana Chakra, which chased the demon back to Kashi. The demon burned Sudakshina to death, and the Chakra reduced the city of Kashi to ashes before returning to Krishna's hand.
