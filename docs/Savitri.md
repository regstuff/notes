# Savitri: The Perfect Wife

Long ago, in the glorious kingdom of Madra, ruled a highly righteous and noble king named Asvapati. Though he had a massive, wealthy empire and many wives, the King was deeply sorrowful because he had absolutely no children to inherit his legacy. 

For eighteen long, grueling years, King Asvapati intensely worshipped the Sun God, Savitr, offering daily, sacred fire oblations. Finally, deeply pleased by his incredible devotion, the God appeared brilliantly from the blazing sacrificial fire and granted the King a divine boon. Soon after, the Queen gave birth to a breathtakingly beautiful daughter. In profound gratitude to the God, the King named her **Savitri**. 

Savitri grew up to be a woman of unparalleled beauty, possessing a brilliant, highly educated mind. She effortlessly mastered music, philosophy, and complex astronomy. However, her immense, radiant beauty and profound intelligence were so intimidatingly perfect that no prince in the entire country dared to approach her father to ask for her hand in marriage! 

Deeply concerned, King Asvapati called Savitri to his chambers. "My child," he said gently, "it is time for you to marry. Since no suitor has come forward, I want you to go on a grand journey across the kingdoms and personally choose a husband who is truly your equal."

Accompanied by the royal minister and a grand retinue, Princess Savitri set out on a long, exciting journey. One day, while her golden chariot was passing through a dense, quiet forest, Savitri's eyes fell upon a young, incredibly handsome hermit walking peacefully by the side of the road. In that single, fleeting moment, his serene face was permanently imprinted on her soul. She instantly knew she had found the only man she would ever love. 

Savitri discreetly asked her minister to find out the young hermit's identity. He returned with shocking news: "He is no ordinary hermit, Princess! His name is **Satyavan**. He is the noble son of the great, exiled King Dyumatsena, who tragically went blind and lost his entire kingdom to a cruel usurper."

Overjoyed, Savitri rushed back to her father's palace to announce her final choice. When she arrived, the great celestial Sage Narada happened to be visiting the King. 

"I have chosen Satyavan, the son of the exiled King Dyumatsena," Savitri declared happily. 

Sage Narada's face instantly turned incredibly pale. "Oh, Savitri!" he gasped in absolute horror. "He is indeed the noblest and bravest of all men. But he is cursed by a terrible, unalterable fate! He is destined to die exactly one year from today!"

King Asvapati was absolutely devastated. "My child! You cannot possibly marry him!" he pleaded desperately. "I absolutely forbid you to become a widow in just one short year!"
But Savitri was entirely unshakeable. "Father, I have already chosen him with all my heart and soul. How could I possibly marry anyone else?"

Deeply moved by her incredible, flawless devotion, the King reluctantly agreed. The royal wedding was celebrated with great, joyous pomp in the heart of the forest. Leaving behind all her incredibly expensive royal silks and precious, heavy jewels, Savitri happily donned the simple, rough bark clothes of a forest ascetic and began her new life in her blind father-in-law's humble hermitage. 

For nearly a year, Savitri was the absolutely perfect, deeply devoted wife and daughter-in-law, filling the humble forest hermitage with immense joy and radiant light. But deep inside, a terrifying, agonizing secret slowly consumed her heart. As the fated day of Satyavan's death approached, she began a severe, grueling three-day fast, praying intensely for a divine miracle. 

On the terrifying, fateful morning, Satyavan picked up his heavy axe to go deep into the forest to chop firewood. "Please, let me come with you today," Savitri begged desperately, refusing to let him out of her sight. 

Deep in the woods, as Satyavan vigorously chopped wood, a sudden, blinding pain shot violently through his head. "Savitri!" he cried out in agony, collapsing heavily to the forest floor. Savitri frantically rushed to his side, gently pulling his sweating, feverish head onto her lap. 

Suddenly, the entire forest grew incredibly dark and heavy. A terrifying, giant figure clad entirely in blood-red robes, wearing a shining golden crown and riding a massive, snorting black buffalo, materialized from the shadows. 

It was **Yama**, the fearsome God of Death himself! 

"You are no ordinary person," Savitri whispered bravely, staring directly into the terrifying face of Death. "Why have you come yourself?"
"Your husband's soul is incredibly pure, Savitri," Lord Yama replied solemnly. "That is exactly why I have personally come to collect it."

With a swift, practiced motion, Yama pulled out a small, glowing magical noose, effortlessly pulled Satyavan's soul completely out of his physical body, and began riding silently away toward the dark, terrifying Underworld. 

Gently laying her husband's lifeless, cold body on the grass, the fearless Savitri stood up and began strictly following the God of Death! 

"You must go back now, Savitri," Yama commanded sternly, noticing her following him. "You cannot possibly come any farther. The realm of the dead is strictly forbidden to the living!"
"In life and in death, I am eternally wedded to him!" Savitri replied fiercely, her voice echoing with absolute, unbreakable devotion. "Where my husband goes, I must absolutely follow!"

Deeply astonished and highly impressed by her incredibly pure, fearless devotion, Yama paused. "Your words are profoundly wise, Princess. I am pleased. Ask for any boon you desire—except your husband's life—and I shall instantly grant it."

"Please restore my blind father-in-law's eyesight and brilliantly return his lost kingdom!" Savitri asked immediately. 
"It is done!" Yama promised. "Now, go back!"

But Savitri kept following him relentlessly through the dark, terrifying thorny paths of the Underworld. 
"You are incredibly stubborn," Yama sighed. "Ask for a second boon—anything except his life—and then you must absolutely turn back!"
"My own father has no sons to continue his royal line," Savitri replied. "Grant him a hundred strong, noble sons!"
"It shall be done," Yama nodded. "Now, you must return to the world of the living!"

Still, Savitri continued to follow him, her soft, bleeding feet walking directly on the razor-sharp rocks of the land of the dead. 

Exasperated but deeply moved by her unparalleled, infinite love, Yama offered one absolutely final boon. "This is your last chance, Savitri! Ask for anything at all—except his life!"

Savitri smiled brilliantly, a flash of pure, absolute genius in her eyes. "Then grant me this, O Lord of Death: May I be blessed with a hundred strong, noble sons—children born of me and my beloved husband, Satyavan!"

"Your wish will be fulfilled!" Yama declared instantly, eager to finally be rid of her. 

He took three steps forward before freezing in absolute, stunned realization. He had been completely, masterfully trapped! He had just granted Savitri a hundred sons *with Satyavan*. But how could she possibly have children with Satyavan if Satyavan remained dead? 

To fulfill his own divine, unbreakable boon, the God of Death was absolutely forced to return Satyavan's life! 

Yama burst into loud, joyous laughter, thoroughly delighted by the incredible, brilliant wit and supreme devotion of the mortal princess. "Your devotion is absolutely unequalled in all the three worlds!" Yama smiled warmly. "I give back your husband's life! He shall live long and rule gloriously!"

Yama gently released Satyavan's soul from the magical noose and vanished into the shadows. Savitri frantically ran all the way back through the forest and joyously threw herself beside her husband's body. 

Satyavan's eyes slowly fluttered open. "How long have I been sleeping?" he murmured, completely unaware of his miraculous journey into death. 

Meanwhile, back at the hermitage, another incredible miracle had occurred. King Dyumatsena's eyesight had been miraculously fully restored, and a breathless messenger had just arrived to announce that the cruel usurper had been defeated and the kingdom was theirs once again! 

When Savitri and Satyavan finally returned to the hermitage, the entire family wept tears of absolute, profound joy. Through her infinite, incredibly pure love and her brilliant, unmatched wisdom, the perfect wife had single-handedly conquered even the terrifying God of Death himself, securing eternal happiness and immense prosperity for her entire family.
