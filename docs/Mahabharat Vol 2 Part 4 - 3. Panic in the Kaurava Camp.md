# Panic in the Kaurava Camp

In Hastinapura, King Duryodhana's spies returned after searching far and wide for the exiled Pandavas. They had found no trace of them in the deep woods, but they brought an intriguing piece of news: the mighty Keechaka, commander of the Matsya kingdom, had been killed by invisible Gandharvas over a woman. 

Duryodhana and his allies realized the truth instantly. "Keechaka and his brothers have been slain," Duryodhana declared. "I believe this is the work of none other than Bheema, and the woman Sairandhri is Draupadi."

Seeing an opportunity, King Susharma of Trigarta, a bitter enemy of Matsya, proposed a plan. "Let us invade the prosperous Matsya kingdom," he suggested. "We shall plunder its wealth and cattle."

Karna supported the idea enthusiastically, and the Kauravas launched a two-pronged attack. The Trigartas marched towards the Matsya kingdom and seized the cattle at the northern end. When the news reached King Virata, he gathered his army. Taking his trusted servants—Kanka (Yudhishthira), Ballava (Bheema), Tantipala (Sahadeva), and Granthika (Nakula)—he marched to defend his borders. 

A fierce battle ensued. As darkness fell, the Trigartas gained the upper hand, and King Susharma successfully captured King Virata. 

Seeing the king taken, Yudhishthira quickly ordered Bheema to intervene. "Go and rescue King Virata from the enemy's clutches," Yudhishthira commanded. "This is a debt we have to discharge since we have taken shelter in his kingdom."

Bheema rushed forward, easily overpowering Susharma's guards and unhorsing him. He rescued King Virata, turning a crushing defeat into a glorious victory. 

Meanwhile, Duryodhana, accompanied by Bheeshma, Drona, Karna, and a massive Kaurava army, launched their attack on the southern borders of the Matsya kingdom, driving away sixty thousand head of cattle. The chief cowherd rushed to the capital, pleading with young Prince Uttara for help. 

Uttara boasted loudly in the court. "I would have set out right away if I had a skilled charioteer to guide my horses!"

Hearing this, Draupadi suggested that Brihannala (Arjuna in disguise) was an exceptional charioteer who had formerly served the great hero Arjuna. Eager to prove himself, Uttara commanded Brihannala to drive his chariot, and they set out amidst great fanfare. 

However, when they reached the battlefield and Uttara saw the terrifying ocean of the Kaurava army led by the legendary Bheeshma and Drona, his bravado vanished. "Let us turn back," he trembled. "I dare not fight these heroic warriors."

Before Arjuna could stop him, Uttara leaped off the chariot and ran for his life. Arjuna quickly chased him down and dragged him back. "If you do not wish to fight, come, hold the reins of the horses while I fight the enemy," Arjuna commanded. 

Arjuna directed the chariot towards the cremation ground. Reaching the old Shami tree, he ordered Uttara to climb it and bring down the wrapped bundle. When Uttara unwrapped it, he was mesmerized by the glowing, celestial weapons. 

"To whom do these excellent weapons belong?" Uttara asked in awe. 

"I am Arjuna," he finally revealed. "Kanka is Yudhishthira, Ballava is Bheema, Granthika is Nakula, and Tantipala is Sahadeva. The Sairandhri is Draupadi. Our exile is complete."

Uttara, overwhelmed with reverence, joyfully took the reins. Arjuna replaced his bangles with leather finger-guards, took up the mighty Gandeeva bow, and blew a deafening note on his celestial conch, Devadatta. 

The sound resonated across the battlefield. The Kaurava warriors recognized it instantly. 

"It is Arjuna!" Duryodhana exclaimed gleefully. "The Pandavas have failed to keep their pledge. They will have to go back to the forest for another twelve years!"

But Bheeshma intervened, correcting the King's fatal miscalculation. "The thirteenth year ended five months and twelve days ago! According to the lunar calendar, and even according to the solar calendar, the thirteenth year had ended six days earlier."

With his identity legally revealed and his exile fully complete, Arjuna unleashed a terrifying assault on the Kaurava army. He trounced Karna in a duel, forcing him to flee. He defeated Drona, Kripa, Ashwatthama, and even the great Bheeshma in direct combat. The entire Kaurava force scattered in panic. 

Finally, Arjuna used the mystic *Sammohana* weapon. The entire remaining Kaurava army, including Duryodhana, fell unconscious. Arjuna casually asked Uttara to collect the colorful clothes of the unconscious warriors for the princesses' dolls, then rounded up the stolen cattle. 

As they drove back, Arjuna hid his weapons once again and resumed his disguise as Brihannala, instructing Uttara to take credit for the victory. 

In the capital, King Virata rejoiced at his own victory over Susharma, but was anxious about his son fighting the Kauravas. When news arrived that Uttara had triumphed, Virata boasted proudly. "My son has defeated the Kauravas!"

Yudhishthira smiled knowingly. "He who has Brihannala as his charioteer is sure to succeed."

Enraged that a servant would credit a eunuch for his son's victory, Virata threw a handful of dice at Yudhishthira, cutting his nose. Draupadi quickly caught the blood in a golden bowl, preventing Yudhishthira's wrath from burning the kingdom. 

When Uttara returned, he confessed the truth. "I did not fight the battle. A celestial hero defeated the Kauravas and saved our cattle."

A few days later, the five brothers appeared in the royal court, dressed in royal robes and radiating majesty. King Virata was stunned to realize he had employed the legendary Pandavas as servants, and fell at Yudhishthira's feet, apologizing profusely for his arrogance. 

To cement their new alliance, King Virata offered his daughter, Princess Uttaraa, in marriage to Arjuna. Arjuna politely declined her for himself, having lived as her teacher, but accepted her as a bride for his son, Abhimanyu. 

The Pandavas relocated to the Matsya town of Upaplavya. Soon, the mighty Abhimanyu arrived, and a grand wedding ceremony was held, attended by Lord Krishna, Balarama, King Drupada, and numerous allies. The period of exile and hiding was officially over; the preparations for the great Kurukshetra war were about to begin.
