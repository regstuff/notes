# Vidyut Chora

Once upon a time, Prince Vidyut and his close friend Yamadanda, the son of the kingdom's Kotwal (Chief of Police), were studying together under the same wise Guru at his ashrama deep in the forest. 

One day, when they had some leisure time, Yamadanda suggested a game. "Let's play Thief and Kotwal."

"Is there any other game worth playing?" Vidyut smiled. "No, there isn't. I'll track you down."

"Well, to catch a thief you must know the ways of a thief," Yamadanda challenged him. "So... you be the thief... a thief who is hiding from the Kotwal. Let's see how clever you are at it."

Yamadanda went and hid in the forest, and Prince Vidyut began to look for him. Very quickly, Vidyut spotted a clue. "Aha! A broken twig! Who could have stepped on it except my friend!" He found Yamadanda easily. 

Then it was Yamadanda's turn to be the Kotwal. The prince hid himself so well that Yamadanda searched long and hard, but finally had to admit defeat. 

"You have won, Yamadanda!" Vidyut teased as he emerged from his excellent hiding place. "But one day when you are the Kotwal, I will commit a crime you cannot solve and see that you are hanged. Hanged, do you hear?"

Yamadanda was stung by his friend's boast. "If you ever misbehave when I am the Kotwal... I will see that you are arrested and beaten up!"

Years passed. The two friends finished their studies and were ready to leave their Guru's ashrama. "You will make a good king, Vidyut," their Guru advised. "But don't play pranks, or you will lose men you value."

When Vidyut returned to his capital, he found his father had grown old. "I wish to spend the rest of my years in the forest," the old King announced. "Rule well and wisely, my son." Vidyut was crowned King. At the same time, Yamadanda's father also renounced the world to retire to the forest. 

Vidyut immediately sent for Yamadanda, intending to appoint him as the new Kotwal.

When Yamadanda received the summons, his heart sank. "The day I have been dreading has come," he thought. "He will surely offer me my father's post. But if I accept... one day when I am the Kotwal, he will commit a crime I cannot solve... and see that I am hanged! There is only one course open to me... I must leave his kingdom."

That very night, Yamadanda fled the kingdom in secret. 

When Vidyut was informed of his friend's sudden departure, he understood immediately. "It must have been a hard decision for him... but I would have done the same in his place." But the game was not over for Vidyut. He ordered his spies to scour all the neighboring kingdoms to find his friend. 

Finally, a spy reported back: "He is in the service of the King of Mithila. He is the Kotwal there."

Vidyut's eyes lit up. "It is a long time since we played Thief and Kotwal, Yamadanda," he chuckled to himself. "It's time we played again... This time I will play the thief." 

Vidyut entrusted the care of his kingdom to his faithful ministers and secretly left for Mithila. 

Mithila was a peaceful kingdom of law-abiding subjects where robbery was almost unknown. So Yamadanda, the newly appointed Kotwal, was shocked when a citizen burst into his office one day. "All my wife's ornaments are gone!"

"We will find the thief," Yamadanda assured him. But soon, several more robberies were reported. The Kotwal himself set out to investigate. 

He searched frantically and finally found a clue—a single, faint footprint outside a burgled house. "The footprint of a small foot," Yamadanda mused. 

The people were getting restless, demanding action. Then, the ultimate insult occurred: there was a theft at the royal palace itself! 

The King of Mithila summoned Yamadanda. "My valuable necklace, Sarvarujapahara, has been stolen!" the King thundered. "If you don't bring him before me within seven days, Maharaj... you may hang me."

Yamadanda questioned hundreds of people without success. "Only a person who has mastered the art of Karapatavidya (the highest art of stealing) could be so elusive," he realized. "Could he have followed me here to..."

Following a hunch, Yamadanda visited a local toddy shop. "Has any stranger been here lately?"

"No," the owner replied. "Except, of course, the dwarf. A small, misshapen fellow... he has been coming regularly for the last few days."

"A dwarf?" Yamadanda remembered the small footprint. He raced out of the shop and spotted a dwarf walking down the street. When the dwarf saw Yamadanda, he bolted! 

Yamadanda chased the dwarf out of the city and towards a stream. Amazingly, the dwarf leaped and crossed the stream without even touching the water! But Yamadanda was relentless. He tackled the dwarf to the ground. 

"You are under arrest for robbery!" Yamadanda declared. 

"Robbery? But I've never robbed a soul in my life!" the dwarf cried piteously. 

Yamadanda dragged the weeping dwarf before the King of Mithila. "Here is the thief, Maharaj."

"I am no thief!" the dwarf sobbed. "I may be poor, but I am an honest man."

The King was skeptical. "Do you have any proof, Kotwal? He seems harmless to me."

"He has supernatural powers and he can change his form at will," Yamadanda explained. "I'll show you how he does it." 

The Kotwal took some special paste from a small box and smeared it on the dwarf's eyes. Instantly, the dwarf transformed into a tall, handsome man! It was Vidyut! 

"It is Vidyut, just as I suspected," Yamadanda thought, triumphant. 

"This paste is called Ghutikanjana," Vidyut calmly explained to the astonished King. "It is well known to those who have to deal with thieves... and thieves who have mastered the art." He even demonstrated it on the Queen, safely turning her into a dwarf and back again. 

Yamadanda, however, was not amused. "I'm convinced. Take him away and interrogate him," he ordered his guards. 

In the prison cell, Vidyut tried to end the charade. "The game has gone too far, Maharaj. Let's tell the King the whole story."

But Yamadanda was furious. "You are the Kotwal. I, the accused. Do your duty." He ruthlessly interrogated Vidyut for days, but Vidyut stubbornly maintained his innocence and refused to reveal where he had hidden the King's necklace. 

On the seventh day, the deadline was up. Yamadanda stood before the King. "I've failed to recover the necklace and to bring the thief before you. I am ready for my punishment. My last wish is that the prisoner should be released."

"It shall be done," the King decreed solemnly. 

Yamadanda was led to the gallows. The hangman put the rough noose around his neck. He was about to pull the rope when a man dashed through the crowd, waving his sword. 

"WAIT! HE IS INNOCENT!" 

It was Vidyut. He ran up the steps and quickly sliced the rope. 

"Yamadanda, you have redeemed my pledge," Vidyut said softly to his friend. "You are a consummate thief-catcher."

The confused King demanded an explanation. "What's going on? I don't know."

"I am indeed the thief he said I was," Vidyut confessed to the King. "The treasure is safe. It is in a cave near the river where I was caught. I shall send my men to fetch it."

"But my curiosity is aroused," the King asked. "Why should a thief who has been released come back to confess?"

"I am not a professional thief," Vidyut explained with a warm smile. "I was only redeeming a pledge I made several years ago... in our childhood." 

Vidyut then told the King of Mithila the entire story of their childhood game and his elaborate prank to test his friend. Just as he finished, a royal messenger arrived from Vidyut's kingdom, carrying an urgent message from Minister Vajrasena, begging King Vidyut to return. 

The King of Mithila was amazed. "So he is indeed a King! It is indeed a pity that I have made your acquaintance so late... when you have been in my kingdom for so long." He hosted a grand banquet in Vidyut's honor. 

"But now I must beg a favour of you, Maharaj," Vidyut requested. "Please allow Yamadanda to return with me."

"Then you may do so," the King agreed gracefully. "Well, Yamadanda, would you like to return with your King?"

"I would, Maharaj," Yamadanda smiled, his friendship with Vidyut fully restored. 

Yamadanda and Vidyut returned to their own kingdom. There, recognizing his friend's superior wisdom, dedication to duty, and incredible skill, Vidyut officially crowned Yamadanda as the new King! Having fulfilled his worldly duties and honored his friendship, Vidyut renounced his throne and retired to the forest to seek spiritual peace. From that day on, the playful prince-turned-ascetic came to be affectionately known as the wise Sage Vidyut Chora (The Lightning Thief).
