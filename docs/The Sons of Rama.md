# The Sons of Rama

Rama was the revered King of Ayodhya. Under his righteous rule, the people were happy and prosperous. Every evening, Rama, accompanied by his brothers Laxmana, Bharata, and Shatrughna, and his beloved queen Sita, would hold court to listen to the problems of his subjects.

One evening, a distraught man came before the King but hesitated. "Sire, I cannot! Not in the presence of the queen!" he cried. 

When Sita had withdrawn, the man revealed a bitter truth. "Maharaj, today a launderer was beating his wife, throwing her out of his house. And he said... 'Get out of my house! I'm not like Rama who accepts a wife after she has lived in another's house!'"

The terrible words referred to Sita's captivity in Lanka by the demon king Ravana. Though Rama knew Sita was perfectly pure, the launderer's cruel accusation troubled him deeply. 

*Should I turn my wife out because of what a launderer said?* Rama agonized. *No... no... And she is soon to become a mother! But my first duty is towards my people... I have to send her away...*

Heartbroken, but bound by his duty as a King, Rama made a tragic decision. The following day, he sent for his brother Laxmana. "You will take Sita into the jungles and leave her there."

Laxmana was shocked. "But..."

"No buts, this is my decision!" Rama commanded firmly. 

Laxmana got his chariot ready and drove a pregnant, unsuspecting Sita deep into the wild forest. Leaving her alone, he returned to Ayodhya with a heavy heart. 

"Laxmana, this jungle is so frightening. I'm scared!" Sita cried as night fell. Suddenly, a venomous serpent slithered towards her. "Help! Help!" she screamed.

Fortunately, the great sage Valmiki was nearby. "Don't worry, my child," he comforted her gently. "These animals are from my hermitage. You will live here. Nothing will bother you!" 

Valmiki took Sita to his peaceful ashram, which became her safe haven. There, she was soon blessed with twin sons, Luv and Kush.

As time passed, Luv and Kush grew into strong, spirited boys. Maharshi Valmiki personally attended to their education, teaching them the sacred scriptures, the virtues of compassion, and the masterful use of arms. The boys practiced archery every day, their skills soon matching those of the greatest warriors.

In the evenings, Valmiki would tell them the epic story of the Ramayana—of how Rama broke the bow of Lord Shiva to marry Vaidehi (Sita), how Ravana kidnapped her, and how Rama killed the demon king to win her back. 

"After spending fourteen years in exile, Rama returned to Ayodhya and was crowned king," Valmiki concluded. "But one day, because of what a launderer said, Rama banished Vaidehi!"

The boys were outraged, not realizing that Vaidehi was their own mother and the "cruel" Rama was their father. "Poor Vaidehi! Rama was cruel, indeed!" Luv declared. "If ever we meet her, we'll bring her to the hermitage."

Meanwhile, back in Ayodhya, King Rama prepared to perform the grand Ashwamedha Yajna to assert his suzerainty. The great sages Vishwamitra, Vashishtha, and Narada gathered for the ceremony. 

"How can Rama perform the Yajna without his wife Sita?" Narada pointed out. "He will have to use a golden statue of her!"

The mention of Sita filled Rama with longing. *I should send Laxmana to look for Sita,* he thought sadly. But knowing his duty, he sighed, *No, my people may not like it. A golden statue will have to do!*

The Yajna began. A magnificent white horse with black ears was chosen. Vishwamitra tied a ceremonial gold leaf to its forehead. The horse was then let loose to wander freely, and Rama's vast army followed it. According to tradition, any ruler who stopped the horse challenged Rama's authority and had to fight his army. 

Many rulers accepted Rama's supremacy without a fight. But when the horse wandered near Valmiki's hermitage, Luv and Kush spotted it. 

"Look at this, Luv. 'Anyone who holds this horse will have to fight!'" Kush read the inscription on the golden leaf. 

"Then we will fight!" Luv declared boldly. The two boys caught the horse and tied it to a tree.

Soon, Rama's soldiers arrived. "Children! Give that horse back to me!" a soldier demanded. When the boys refused, a terrible battle broke out. Despite their young age, Luv and Kush's archery skills were magical, and they easily routed the soldiers. 

News of the defeat reached Prince Shatrughna. "I'll teach them how to behave!" he roared, charging at the boys in his chariot. But Luv expertly shot an arrow that shattered Shatrughna's chariot wheel, bringing the prince crashing to the ground. 

Rama's army fled in disarray. When Rama heard that two children had defeated Shatrughna, he was deeply disturbed. "You take over, Laxmana. Go and capture those boys."

Laxmana arrived at the forest. "Go away now, boys! Take a look at him first," he warned them. 

But Kush and Laxmana engaged in a furious duel. Kush was wounded, which enraged Luv. "I won't let you live after what you have done to Kush!" Luv challenged. With a barrage of powerful arrows, Luv overpowered Laxmana, who fell unconscious.

When the news reached Ayodhya, Rama was stunned. "Now Bharata, you must go with the monkey forces," he ordered. The mighty Hanuman prepared for battle and accompanied Bharata. 

But even the legendary monkey army proved unequal to the task. Luv and Kush fought like young gods, their arrows flying with impossible speed and accuracy. Bharata fell, and Hanuman was forced to retreat. 

"A thousand pardons, O Rama!" Hanuman apologized. 

"Don't lose heart, Hanuman. I will come myself," Rama declared, finally leading his huge forces to the forest. 

When Rama saw the two brave children standing fearlessly against his entire army, his heart softened. "We do not know about our father. But Sita is our mother," the boys answered when Rama asked about their parentage.

Hearing Sita's name, Rama recognized his sons. Overwhelmed by emotion and the tragedy of his long separation, Rama's bow dropped from his hands, and he fainted on the battlefield. 

Misunderstanding the situation, Luv and Kush thought they had defeated the great King. They tied up Hanuman and proudly took Rama's golden crown as a trophy. 

"We have brought home precious booty, mother!" they announced, presenting the crown to Sita. 

Sita gasped in horror. "Heavens! No! Did you kill him?"

Just then, Maharshi Valmiki arrived with a recovered Rama. "He did not die, mother, he is alive!" the boys said. 

Valmiki brought the family together. "Vaidehi of the Ramayana is none other than our mother, Luv!" Kush realized in astonishment. 

"Here are your sons, Rama!" Valmiki announced proudly. 

But the joyous reunion was overshadowed by sorrow. Rama gently explained that he still could not take Sita back without proving her purity to the people of Ayodhya. 

Sita, however, had endured enough. She could not bear the humiliation any longer. With tears in her eyes, she folded her hands and prayed, "Mother Earth, if I am pure, gather me to your lap!"

Instantly, the earth rumbled and parted under her feet. "Move away, Mother! The earth is falling apart!" the boys cried.

"You have granted my wish, O Mother," Sita smiled peacefully, and began to sink into the glowing chasm. Before Rama or her sons could reach her, the earth closed over her, taking the pure and noble Queen back into its embrace. 

Rama wept and embraced his anguished sons. "Do not grieve, my sons. Your mother has gone back to her mother."

With a heavy heart, Rama returned to Ayodhya with Luv, Kush, and his army. The boys had proven their incredible worth, and the people welcomed them. Though Rama mourned Sita for the rest of his life, he found solace in knowing that his brave sons would one day make great and righteous rulers.
