# The Pretentious Owl

Kanakaksha, the golden-eyed owl, had an extraordinary and unusual friend in Sumitra, the elegant King of Swans. The two spent many hours talking by the serene lake where the swans lived. 

Kanakaksha, however, harbored a deep insecurity. He desperately wanted to be seen as an equal to his royal friend. "I must do something to prove that I too am a king," the pretentious owl thought to himself. "He will shun me if he knew that I am a poor owl."

One day, Kanakaksha hatched a deceptive plan. "Ah! The King of Varanasi's army is going to camp near my forest tonight," he observed. "I shall invite Sumitra and pretend that these are my men! He will be impressed with my status."

That evening, Kanakaksha flew down to the lake. "Hello, Sumitra! I want you to visit my home today. I'd love you to come some day."

"I'd love to come some day," Sumitra agreed politely. 

"Not some day, today," Kanakaksha insisted eagerly. "No buts, I visit you every day. You should visit me too."

"All right, tomorrow," the Swan King agreed reluctantly.

The next day, true to his word, Sumitra flew into the dark, dense forest to visit Kanakaksha. "Ah! Magnificent," Sumitra admired the massive ancient trees. 

"This is my domain, and these are my men," Kanakaksha boasted grandly, pointing a wing towards the large military camp set up below them. Hundreds of soldiers, horses, and tents dotted the forest floor. 

"Good. Are they proceeding on a march?" Sumitra asked, impressed by the massive army.

"No," Kanakaksha replied quickly, maintaining his lie. "But they are leaving."

"How can they leave without my orders!" Kanakaksha puffed out his chest and loudly hooted, "HO OO HO OO!" 

Down below, the superstitious human army commander heard the call. "Owl hooting! It is a bad omen. Let us postpone our departure," he ordered. 

The army immediately halted their preparations and unpacked their gear. 

Kanakaksha turned to Sumitra, beaming with misplaced pride. "There! You see my men are very obedient."

The Swan King, unaware of the superstition surrounding owls, was genuinely impressed. "Since he is my friend, I will humour him," he thought. "Yes! Yes!" he nodded politely. 

The next morning, the army commander blew his conch shell, signaling the troops to march again. Hearing the commotion, Kanakaksha confidently hooted again. "HOO HOO!"

"Drat that owl," the commander groaned. "We have to postpone our march again! I will pull up my general for indiscipline."

Sumitra, growing slightly uncomfortable with his friend's display of power, thought, "He is going too far."

But Kanakaksha was drunk on his imaginary authority. On the third day, as the army once again prepared to leave, the owl hooted loudly a third time to prevent their departure. 

This time, the commander's patience completely snapped. "Blast that owl! Will someone take care of this ill-omen maker?" he roared furiously. 

An expert archer immediately stepped forward, drew his bow, and aimed carefully into the high branches. "Ah, there he is," the archer muttered, spotting the golden-eyed owl. 

*THWACK!* 

An arrow shot through the leaves and struck Kanakaksha squarely in the chest. With a sharp cry of pain, the pretentious owl tumbled from the branch and fell dead to the forest floor. 

Sumitra, the King of Swans, looked down at his fallen friend in shock and deep sorrow. "Oh! My stupidity has cost my friend his life," he mourned, realizing too late that his polite indulgence of Kanakaksha's dangerous pretense had ultimately led to his tragic end.
