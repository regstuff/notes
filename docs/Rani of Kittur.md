# The Rani of Kittur: The Defiant Queen

In the late 18th century, Chennamma, the beautiful and courageous daughter of the Desai of Kagati, was married to King Mallasarja of Kittur, a powerful and prosperous kingdom near Belgaum in Karnataka. Though she was only sixteen, Chennamma quickly won the deep affection of all her new subjects with her incredible intelligence and gentle, loving nature. 

King Mallasarja already had an elder son, Shivalinga Rudra Sarja, from his first wife. A few years later, Chennamma too gave birth to a brave, strapping son named Basavaraja. However, tragedy struck the royal family when young Basavaraja suddenly died. 

Overwhelmed with absolute, paralyzing grief, Queen Chennamma withdrew from public life. Seeing her profound depression, the King gently gently reminded her of her true royal duty. "Chenna, you weep for one son who is dead, while the rest of your children—the people of Kittur—remain completely neglected," he told her. 

Realizing the profound truth of his words, Chennamma wiped her tears and threw herself entirely into the welfare of her subjects. She toured the kingdom extensively, generously giving shelter and fertile land to desperate, runaway slaves who had escaped brutal Portuguese captivity in Goa, and providing vast royal funds to renovate local mosques and temples. 

However, incredibly dark clouds were gathering over Kittur. The treacherous Peshwa of Pune deceitfully invited King Mallasarja for a friendly visit, only to cowardly imprison him! 

For three long, agonizing years, Queen Chennamma desperately fought for his release. When the King was finally set free, he fell severely ill during his journey back and tragically passed away before reaching Kittur. 

Following his father's death, the elder son, Shivalinga Rudra Sarja, was formally crowned King. Fearing the growing instability in the region, the new King made a fatal, cowardly mistake: he signed a devastating treaty with the British East India Company. In exchange for British "protection," Kittur was forced to pay a massive, crippling annual tribute of 1.7 lakh rupees! 

But the "protection" was a complete, sinister lie. When King Shivalinga suddenly fell terminally ill and died shortly after officially adopting a young heir named Shiva, the greedy British instantly showed their true colors. 

Mr. Thackeray, the arrogant British Political Agent, immediately rode into Kittur. Using the cruel "Doctrine of Lapse," he flatly refused to recognize the legal adoption. "The King is dead, and the new heir is but a little boy," Thackeray declared greedily. "The British Government will take over the total administration of Kittur." 

To prove his absolute power, Thackeray violently seized the keys to the royal treasury and sealed the doors, explicitly forbidding any money to be drawn without his personal permission. 

The people of Kittur were absolutely furious. Loyal, heavily armed subjects began pouring into the Kittur fort from far and near, desperately ready to fight the alien oppressors. Alarmed by the massive uprising, Thackeray ruthlessly ordered the fort gates to be shut and heavily locked, trapping the Rani inside. He then viciously stationed British heavy artillery directly outside the main gates. 

"We'll give you exactly twenty-four minutes," Thackeray issued his final, arrogant warning. "If the gate is not opened by then, we'll launch a devastating cannonade and blast it open!"

Inside the fort, Queen Chennamma briefly wavered, terrified of the horrific bloodshed that a war would inevitably bring to her peaceful people. But as the minutes ticked by, she suddenly remembered the proud, fearless legacy of her late husband. 

Donning her shining royal armor and drawing her sharp sword, the fearless Rani appeared before her restless, heavily armed soldiers. "I have officially decided to lead my men!" she roared fiercely. "Fight, to show the entire world that people of even a small state like ours value incredible honor and absolute independence above everything else! Fight we will!" 

Before the arrogant British could even fire a single cannon, the massive gates of Kittur fort suddenly burst open! 

"Har Har Mahadev!" the terrifying war cry echoed as Queen Chennamma's incredibly fierce soldiers poured out of the fort like a devastating, unstoppable tidal wave. 

Caught completely by surprise by the sheer, sudden ferocity of the unexpected attack, the British forces were utterly, brutally decimated. In the intense, bloody battle, Thackeray was shot dead by the Rani's loyal Muslim bodyguard, Sayid Balappa. The surviving British forces completely panicked, threw down their weapons, and fled in absolute terror, leaving several top British officers behind as prisoners of war. 

It was a spectacular, unprecedented, glorious victory for Kittur. 

But the massive, bruised British Empire could not possibly swallow such a humiliating defeat. Mr. Chaplin, the furious Deccan Commissioner, rapidly assembled a terrifyingly vast, combined British military force from Bombay and Madras, completely surrounding the Kittur fort with thousands of heavily armed troops and devastating heavy artillery. 

"I will brutally stall for time until my heavy battering train arrives," Chaplin planned ruthlessly. He sent a deceptive message to the Rani, promising peace negotiations. 

Believing the British might actually honor a peace treaty, Queen Chennamma generously and unconditionally released all the British prisoners of war. But the moment the prisoners were safely out of the fort, Chaplin instantly dropped his false mask of peace. 

"We will strictly accept nothing less than total, unconditional surrender!" the treacherous British commander declared. 

"Never shall I surrender!" Queen Chennamma replied furiously, realizing she had been completely betrayed. "I fully know I'll be fighting a losing battle. Yet fight I absolutely must, to desperately redeem the sacred honor of Kittur!"

The devastating, entirely one-sided final battle began. The massive British heavy artillery relentlessly pounded the ancient stone walls of Kittur fort day and night. Despite the Rani's incredibly heroic, desperate defense, the walls were finally blown apart by a massive explosion. The overwhelming numbers of the British army poured violently through the smoking breach, brutally overwhelming the brave but exhausted defenders in a fierce, bloody hand-to-hand combat. 

Seeing that all was completely lost, Queen Chennamma was finally captured alive by the British. 

The tragic, defeated Queen was imprisoned in the remote, heavily guarded Bailahongala fort. For five long, agonizing years, she lived entirely in captivity, spending her days staring out of her small window, dreaming of her lost freedom. 

Though she eventually died in prison, her legendary, incredible bravery made her an immortal, eternal symbol of resistance. Decades before the famous Rani of Jhansi even lifted a sword, the fearless Rani Chennamma of Kittur became the very first, glorious Indian queen to actively lead an armed, bloody rebellion against the oppressive British Empire, forever cementing her incredible legacy as India's foremost heroine of freedom.
