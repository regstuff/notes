# The Conspiracy

The Pandava princes grew up to be extraordinary warriors, beloved by the citizens of Hastinapura for their virtue, strength, and humility. Yudhishthira, the eldest, was so scrupulously honest and fair that King Dhritarashtra, recognizing the public sentiment, had no choice but to install him as the heir-apparent. 

The Pandavas continually proved their worth. Bheema mastered the mace and chariot steering under Balarama, while Arjuna mastered diverse weapons under Drona. Nakula excelled in unusual weapons, and Sahadeva immersed himself in spiritual lore. To expand the Kuru empire, Arjuna vanquished the Yavana kings, while Bheema marched South and East, returning with immense wealth and treasures. 

Seeing the Pandavas' phenomenal success and the citizens' adoration, King Dhritarashtra's heart grew heavy with jealousy and fear for his own hundred sons, the Kauravas. His envy was relentlessly stoked by his eldest son, Duryodhana, who constantly whispered venomous thoughts against his cousins. 

Deeply distressed, Dhritarashtra summoned his chief political counselor, Kanika. "The Pandavas are men of might and ample resources. Should I treat them as allies or as rivals?" he asked. 

Kanika’s response was chilling. "O King, slay your foe with gifts and camaraderie. Kill him with discord or with force. A clever jackal once demolished his foes—a strong tiger, a cunning mouse, a swift wolf, and a mongoose—solely through the art of diplomacy and deceit. Be like the jackal."

Convinced by Kanika's ruthless advice, Dhritarashtra threw his full support behind Duryodhana, his brother Duhshasana, and their uncle Shakuni. Together, they hatched a sinister plot to eliminate the Pandavas without drawing suspicion to the Kuru throne. 

Dhritarashtra began to passionately describe the upcoming festival of Pashupati in the town of Varanavata, praising its brilliant attractions. Intrigued, the innocent Pandavas expressed a desire to attend. Dhritarashtra happily granted them permission, instructing them to live in Varanavata with their mother, Kunti.

Secretly, Duryodhana summoned his most loyal and ruthless henchman, Purochana. "The time is ripe for our escape from fear," Duryodhana declared. "Go swiftly to Varanavata. Build a grand mansion for the Pandavas, but construct its walls using highly inflammable materials—hemp, resin, ghee, oil, and lacquer. Welcome them warmly, and when they are fast asleep, set fire to the mansion’s entrance."

Purochana eagerly agreed and sped to Varanavata. 

When the time came for the Pandavas to depart, the citizens of Hastinapura gathered to bid them a tearful farewell. Among the crowd was the wise and deeply concerned Vidura. Knowing of Duryodhana’s lethal plot, Vidura approached Yudhishthira. 

To prevent anyone else from understanding, Vidura spoke to Yudhishthira in the obscure Mlechchha dialect. 
"He who knows that weapons of fire can burn forests but not singe those in forest hideouts, will survive," Vidura warned cryptically. "A burrow with many exits saves one from fire. He who is aware of the planned schemes of foes should protect himself from foreseen calamities."

"I have understood," Yudhishthira replied solemnly, fully grasping that they were walking into a deadly fire trap and that they must dig a secret tunnel to survive. Armed with Vidura's cryptic but life-saving counsel, the Pandavas set forth toward Varanavata.
