# Nala and Damayanti

Thousands of years ago, King Nala ruled over the kingdom of Nishada. He was a generous and noble king, loved by all his subjects. However, he was often lonely and sad, as his father had retired to the forest to spend his last years. His cousin, Pushkara, deeply envied Nala's fame and soon left the capital out of jealousy.

One day, while wandering through his beautiful royal gardens, Nala noticed a flock of graceful swans swimming in a lake. One dainty swan had magnificent golden plumes. Creeping softly forward, Nala caught the golden swan by its leg. The bird cried out in pain as the other swans looked on helplessly.
"Don't be afraid. I won't kill you," Nala assured the bird gently. "I shall keep you in my palace and give you pearls to eat."

The golden swan consoled his wailing mates, "I have to go with the king to keep a promise I made in my last birth. I shall return the moment my work is done."

Nala took the magical swan to his palace. Sometime later, noticing the king's continued melancholy, the swan asked, "Why are you weeping, O king?"
"I have been an unhappy man for many months," Nala confessed. "Once, the sage Narada spoke to me about Damayanti, the charming daughter of King Bheema of Kundanpur. I decided then and there to marry her and no one else."
"Is that all that troubles you?" the swan smiled. "Make arrangements for the wedding and leave the rest to me. I'll return in a week."

The golden swan flew across the sky and reached the kingdom of Kundanpur. Entering the royal gardens, it began chanting, "Nala! Nala!" 
Princess Damayanti looked up in wonder. "Ah! What a dear swan! If only I could have it!" She chased the elusive bird through the gardens until, with a swift movement, she finally caught it. 

The swan immediately began praising King Nala's virtues and handsome features.
"You've been repeating the name 'Nala.' Who is he?" Damayanti asked, captivated by the swan's descriptions. "King Nala must be a wonderful man! I'll marry him and none other."

His mission accomplished, the golden swan returned to Nishada. "I have done my job, O king!" it reported to Nala. "Damayanti will marry only you. I have kept my promise. Let me now go back to my companions."
"I will miss you, my friend," Nala smiled, releasing the bird, "but I don't want to keep you away from your friends."

Soon after, King Bheema organized a grand *swayamvara* (a ceremony where a princess chooses her husband) for Damayanti. Princes from far and wide gathered in the great hall, restless and eager. But when Damayanti entered, she walked straight past them and placed the floral garland around Nala's neck. 
"Long live King Nala!" the people cheered as Nala brought his beautiful bride back to Nishada. 

Twelve happy years passed quickly, and the royal couple was blessed with two children. But their peace was shattered when Pushkara returned. "Don't you remember me? I'm Pushkara, your cousin," he announced. "I am tired of forest life and have come back to enjoy the pleasures of the city."
Nala welcomed him warmly and even built him a new palace. But Pushkara, cunning and greedy, soon invited Nala to gamble in a game of dice. 

Using deceit, Pushkara ensured Nala kept losing. Finally, Pushkara laid down the ultimate wager: "Now for the last round. The winner gets the crown, and the loser spends three years in the forest—agreed?"
"Agreed!" Nala replied. 
The dice were cast, and Pushkara laughed triumphantly. "I have won! Ha! I'm the ruler now!"

Having lost his kingdom, Nala sent his children to Damayanti's parents for safety, and the royal couple walked into the harsh forest, stripped of everything. 
Wandering without food or water, Nala spotted a flock of golden birds. Desperate for food, he threw his only garment over them to trap them. But as he leaned forward, the magical birds flew away, carrying his garment with them!

"Don't worry my king. We can't fight fate!" Damayanti comforted him. 
But Nala was overwhelmed with guilt. "I'm worried about you. Why should you suffer for my sake? Listen, this path leads to your father's kingdom. You should go..."
"Oh, no! I will not leave you here alone," Damayanti refused fiercely.

That night, they slept on the bare ground, sharing Damayanti's sari for warmth. Early the next morning, while she was still asleep, a heartbroken Nala tore a piece from her sari, wrapped himself in it, and quietly walked away, believing she would be better off without him. 

Nala walked for a long time until he heard a cry for help. "Help! Help!" A serpent was trapped in a raging forest fire. Nala bravely jumped into the flames and rescued the creature. 
"You've saved my life," the serpent god announced. "I shall give you something in return. Walk ten steps."

As Nala counted to ten, the serpent bit him! Instantly, Nala's handsome features warped, and he transformed into an ugly, deformed dwarf. "Oh! Serpent god, what have you done?" Nala cried.
"I changed your form so that you won't be recognised," the serpent explained. "You're Baahuk from today. Go to King Rituparna of Ayodhya. He is a master at the game of dice. Serve him, and he will teach you the secret of the game! Take this magic dress; put it on when you want to become your old self."

Meanwhile, Damayanti woke up to find herself abandoned. "Where has Nala gone? Why has he left me alone in the forest?" she wept. Wandering aimlessly, she was attacked by a deadly python. Just as it clamped its jaws around her leg, a hunter shot and killed the beast. 

But the hunter, struck by her beauty, advanced on her. "Who are you? A goddess? Marry me!" he demanded. 
"Stay where you are!" Damayanti cried furiously. "If you take one step further, you will be burnt!" By the power of her curse, the wicked hunter was instantly reduced to ashes.

Stumbling onward, Damayanti met a caravan of traders. That night, a herd of wild elephants trampled their camp, destroying everything. Blaming her for their ill luck, the angry traders chased her away. Running for her life, she reached the kingdom of Viprapur, looking wild and bedraggled. 
Queen Bhanumati of Viprapur saw the children teasing her and took pity. Recognizing her noble bearing, the queen hired her as a maid, not knowing her true identity. 

Sometime later, a minister from Kundanpur (Damayanti's home) visited Viprapur and recognized her. Overjoyed, he brought her back to her parents and children. Though safe, Damayanti remained deeply unhappy, yearning for Nala. "I promise, we shall find out where Nala is, soon," her father vowed.

The king sent ministers in all directions. One minister reached King Rituparna's court in Ayodhya and recited a strange riddle to the assembly: "A king ran away. In dust the jewel lay. Why he ran away, the jewel could not say."
No one could understand it until Baahuk (Nala in disguise), the king's deformed charioteer, stepped forward and answered: "Can a king without a crown, keep a jewel for his own?"

When the minister reported this to Damayanti, her heart leapt. "Who cares if he is ugly and deformed... I am sure, he is Nala," she declared. She hatched a plan to bring him back.

The minister returned to Ayodhya and handed King Rituparna an urgent scroll. "My lord! Damayanti has agreed to remarry. The swayamvara is fixed for tomorrow!"
Rituparna, eager to win Damayanti, panicked. "The invitation comes today! How can I make it?"
Baahuk smiled. "Don't worry, my lord! I will take you as fast as the wind!"

Nala's skill with horses was legendary, and the chariot flew across the land. "Baahuk, teach me the secret of driving horses so fast, and I'll show you my tricks in the game of dice," Rituparna offered. By the time they reached Kundanpur, they had exchanged their secrets.

Upon arriving, Rituparna was confused. "No bride and no swayamvara! Where are the festivities?"
Meanwhile, Baahuk saw his two children playing on a terrace. Damayanti, watching from the palace, rushed down to meet him. As the ugly charioteer hugged the children tightly, she knew. "I'm sure you are Nala! But were you going to marry again, Damayanti?" he asked, hurt.
"No," she smiled through her tears. "It was a trick to get you here. Who else but you could travel such a long distance in one day?"

Nala put on the serpent god's magic dress, and his handsome form was instantly restored. The kingdom erupted in joy to see him safe and sound.

Armed with Rituparna's secret knowledge, Nala returned to Nishada and confronted Pushkara. "I have spent my three years in the forest. Come, let's have another game of dice."
Pushkara arrogantly agreed, but this time, Nala was a master of the game. He swiftly won back his crown, his wealth, and his kingdom. 
"I've won!" Nala declared. But showing his true nobility, he smiled at his trembling cousin. "But, I won't send you to the forest. You may continue to live here!" 

Once again, Nala wore the crown, with Damayanti proudly by his side as his queen. They lived happily for many years, ruling their country with generosity, wisdom, and profound love.
