# Padmavati

Once upon a time, Prince Mukuta-Shekhara of Varanasi set out on a hunting expedition, accompanied by his dearest companion, the brilliant son of the royal minister. After a long morning in the dense forest, they stumbled upon a serene, crystal-clear temple tank. 

"Let us refresh ourselves and worship at the temple," the prince suggested. 
As they washed their hands and feet, a breathtakingly beautiful princess arrived at the opposite side of the tank with her retinue. Captivated by her exquisite beauty, the prince could not take his eyes off her. 

Sensing the prince's intense gaze, the princess turned toward him and performed a highly peculiar sequence of actions. First, she delicately removed a pink lotus flower from behind her ear. Next, she bit into the stalk with her teeth. Then, she took the flower, placed it gracefully under her foot, and finally, she picked it up and pressed it lovingly to her heart. Without uttering a single word, she and her maids turned and left.

"What a beautiful girl! Who could she be?" the prince sighed, utterly heartbroken as he watched her leave. "If I cannot marry her, I do not want to live."
His friend, the minister's son, stroked his chin thoughtfully. "Do not despair, my prince. She has already told you everything you need to know through her actions."

The prince looked at him in confusion. "What do you mean?"
"It was a secret language," the minister's son smiled. "By removing the lotus (*Karna*) and biting it with her teeth (*Danta*), she meant to let you know that she was the daughter of King Dantavat of Karnataka. By placing the lotus (*Padma*) under her foot, she indicated that her name was Padmavati. And by pressing it to her heart, she let you know that she loves you."

Overjoyed by this brilliant deduction, the prince immediately prepared for a long journey. The two friends rode for many days until they reached the magnificent capital of Karnataka. 

Seeking discreet lodgings, they found a kind old woman spinning yarn outside her humble cottage. "My home is open to you, my sons," she welcomed them warmly. 
As they rested, the old woman casually mentioned that she used to be Princess Padmavati's personal nurse. Seeing a golden opportunity, the prince eagerly asked her to deliver a secret message to the princess: *The prince you saw at the tank is here.*

The old woman hobbled off to the palace. However, when she returned, she was weeping bitterly. "I gave her your message," she cried, "but the princess flew into a sudden rage! She slapped me across the face with both her hands covered in white sandal paste and chased me out!"

The prince was utterly crushed. "Your interpretation of her signs was entirely wrong," he told his friend bitterly. 
But the minister's son chuckled confidently. "Quite the contrary, my prince. You have not understood her cleverness. By slapping the old woman's face with all ten fingers covered in white paste, she meant to say that she would meet you exactly ten days hence, when the moon is bright."

The prince waited anxiously. Exactly ten days later, he sent the old woman to the palace once again to ask for a meeting place. This time, Padmavati reacted with even greater fury. Feigning intense anger, she grabbed the old woman by the shoulders, physically pushed her out through a small, hidden wicket gate at the back of the palace, and yelled, "Out with you! Don't you dare show me your face again!"

The terrified old woman swore never to return to the palace. But the minister's son was triumphant. "You are wrong to despair, my prince," he beamed. "It is simple. She wants you to go to her tonight through that exact same wicket gate."

That very night, the prince cautiously pushed open the wicket gate. True to his friend's word, Princess Padmavati was waiting for him in the shadows. She joyfully led him into her luxurious private chambers, where they were secretly married according to the ancient *Gandharva* rites. 

For over a month, the prince lived in absolute bliss, hidden away in Padmavati's royal apartments. But soon, he grew restless and deeply missed his loyal friend, who was waiting for him in the city. When he asked to leave, Padmavati's fierce, possessive love turned into anxious jealousy. Fearing he might never return, she constantly begged him to stay just a few days longer. 

Finally, seeing his deep dejection, she relented. "Go and comfort your friend," she said sweetly. "To show my goodwill, I shall have some special sweets made and sent to him. Come back to me when your mind is at ease."

The prince hurried back to the old woman's cottage and joyfully embraced his friend. Soon after, Padmavati's maids arrived, bearing a silver platter loaded with delicious sweets. "Come, eat some of these as we talk!" the prince urged.

But the minister's son looked at the sweets with profound suspicion. "O prince, it was not wise of you to talk to Padmavati about me. In her intense jealousy and fear that I might take you away, she has planned to get rid of me. These sweets are poisoned."

The prince was outraged. "That is an unkind accusation! My gentle Padmavati would never do such a thing."
To prove his point, the minister's son tossed one of the sweets to a stray dog passing by. The dog gobbled it up and instantly fell dead, convulsing in agony. 

Horrified and enraged, the prince drew his sword. "That evil woman! I will go back and strangle the life out of her!"
"No, you will not," his friend stopped him firmly. "She was prompted by a blind, possessive love for you. Do exactly as I tell you. Go back to her tonight. Be very cordial. When she falls fast asleep, carefully remove all her royal jewelry. Then, strike her firmly on her left foot with this sharp, three-pronged trident, and bring the jewels to me."

Trusting his friend completely, the prince returned to the palace. Relieved that he had come back, Padmavati soon fell into a deep, peaceful sleep. The prince quietly gathered her priceless necklaces and bangles, struck her left foot with the trident, and fled through the wicket gate into the night. 

When he reached his friend, the minister's son had already procured disguises. He dressed himself as an ascetic Guru, complete with matted hair and ashes, and dressed the prince as his humble disciple. 
"Take the bundle of jewelry," the 'Guru' commanded. "We are going to the cremation grounds. I will sit there in deep meditation. You go to the royal goldsmith near the palace gates and try to sell these jewels. When he questions you, bring him to me."

The prince did as instructed. The moment the goldsmith saw the jewels, he recognized them as the unique pieces he had personally crafted for Princess Padmavati. Shouting "Thief!", he immediately alerted the Kotwal (chief of police). 

The prince was arrested and hauled before King Dantavat. "How did these royal jewels come into your possession?" the king demanded furiously. 
"My Guru gave them to me," the prince replied calmly. 

The guards rushed to the cremation grounds and brought the ascetic 'Guru' to the royal court. 
"Master! How did these jewels come into your hands?" the king questioned him respectfully. 

The minister's son, acting his part perfectly, spoke in a deep, booming voice. "O King, I am an ascetic who performs rituals at the cremation grounds. One dark, new-moon night, a wicked witch came to my sacred fire to perfect her dark spells. Disgusted by her evil, I attacked her, stripped her of her ornaments, and struck her left foot with my trident before she fled."

The king was absolutely horrified. He immediately sent the queen to inspect Padmavati's feet. The queen returned shortly, weeping in shock. "It is true! There are three deep, red marks made by a trident on our daughter's left foot. She is a witch!"

Devastated and terrified of the scandal, the king turned to the ascetic. "O Master, what should a wicked woman be punished with? What do the scriptures have to say about it?"
"She should be banished from your kingdom forever," the 'Guru' decreed sternly. 

That very night, to avoid public disgrace, the king ordered his most trusted guards to carry the weeping, confused Padmavati in a closed palanquin and abandon her deep in the treacherous jungles at the border of the kingdom. 

"Alas!" Padmavati sobbed as the guards left her alone in the dark forest. "Why have I been treated thus? My father bears me no love. I should have left with my prince when I had the chance."

But out of the shadows emerged two handsome horsemen—Prince Mukuta-Shekhara and the minister's son, now dressed in their true royal garments. They had secretly followed the palanquin the entire way. 

Overjoyed and profoundly relieved to see her beloved prince, Padmavati threw herself into his arms. Having successfully extracted her from her possessive kingdom without a bloody war, the brilliant minister's son guided the happy couple back to Varanasi, where they lived happily ever after.
