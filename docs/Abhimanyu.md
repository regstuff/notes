# Abhimanyu: Star-Crossed Prince

Abhimanyu, the son of the great Pandava warrior Arjuna and Subhadra (Krishna's sister), is renowned for the extraordinary valor he displayed in the epic Mahabharata war.

When Abhimanyu was just a baby, his father Arjuna could not help loving him more than his other sons. "Abhimanyu shall grow up to be the foremost among the Pandavas as well as the Yadavas!" Arjuna proudly proclaimed.
"Hush! One ought not to praise one's own child thus," Subhadra gently scolded. 

But misfortune soon befell the Pandavas. Their jealous cousins, the Kauravas, plotted to steal their kingdom. Led by the wicked Duryodhana and his cunning uncle Shakuni, they invited Yudhishthira (the eldest Pandava) to a rigged game of dice. Yudhishthira lost his kingdom and was forced into exile in the forest with his brothers and wife for thirteen years.

Though just a child when his father went into exile, Abhimanyu was raised in Dwaraka under the loving care of his mother Subhadra and his uncle Krishna. He grew up to be a valiant, handsome youth, inheriting the best qualities of his family: Yudhishthira's patience, Krishna's conduct, Bhima's strength, the gentleness of Nakula and Sahadeva, and Arjuna's looks, prowess, and scriptural knowledge. 

After thirteen years, the Pandavas spent the final year of their exile in disguise at the court of King Virata. During this time, Arjuna helped Virata's son Uttara defeat a Kaurava attack. Revealing their true identities, the Pandavas were honored by King Virata, who offered his daughter Uttara in marriage to Arjuna.
"I cannot, for I was her teacher," Arjuna respectfully declined. "But my son, the valiant and godlike Abhimanyu, surpasses all in looks and in the knowledge of weapons. He is fit to be your son-in-law."
King Virata joyfully agreed. Abhimanyu and Uttara were married amidst great feasting, attended by Krishna and a host of friendly kings. 

Following the wedding, the Pandavas held a council. Yudhishthira wished to win back their kingdom peacefully, so Krishna went to Hastinapura as an ambassador of peace. But Duryodhana stubbornly refused to give them even an inch of land. War was inevitable.

The terrible Kurukshetra war began. On the very first day, the Kaurava commander, the great grandsire Bheeshma, wreaked havoc on the Pandava army. Hearing of this, young Abhimanyu fearlessly charged at Bheeshma, fighting so brilliantly that he seemed to be everywhere at once. 

The battle raged on for days. Eventually, Bheeshma fell to Arjuna's arrows. The Kauravas then made Drona their new commander. Drona promised Duryodhana that he would capture Yudhishthira alive. However, Drona quickly realized that it was impossible to capture Yudhishthira as long as the invincible Arjuna was by his side.

To achieve this, the Kauravas devised a clever ploy: they lured Arjuna far away from the main battlefield. With Arjuna gone, Drona launched a fierce attack on the Pandava lines using a complex, deadly, circular troop formation known as the *Chakravyuha*. The Pandavas began losing ground rapidly. 

Desperate, Yudhishthira approached young Abhimanyu. "Only you, Arjuna, Krishna, and Pradyumna know how to break through Drona's circular formation. None of them is here, but you. Son, will you lead us?"
"I will gladly try to break through the formation," Abhimanyu replied bravely, "but I do not know how to come out of it, if I am overpowered and in danger."
"Break the formation and make way for us to enter," Yudhishthira assured him. "We will follow close upon your heels and protect you."

Abhimanyu ordered his charioteer to drive straight into Drona's troops. "Think well before you proceed," his worried charioteer warned. "Drona is an adept in the art of warfare, and you are just a youth."
"I am Arjuna's son and Krishna's nephew," Abhimanyu declared fiercely. "Drive on!"

Like a proud young lion falling upon a herd of elephants, Abhimanyu charged into the *Chakravyuha*, successfully breaching the impenetrable formation under the very nose of Drona. 
But alas! Before the Pandavas could follow him inside, the powerful Kaurava warrior Jayadratha effectively closed the breach. Abhimanyu was trapped inside, completely alone. 

Undaunted, the young prince fought like a god, single-handedly massacring the Kaurava warriors and covering the earth with their mutilated bodies. The terrified Kaurava troops tried to flee. Enraged to see his army routed by a single youth, Duryodhana rushed to attack Abhimanyu. Fearing for the king's life, Drona and a host of Kaurava warriors rushed to Duryodhana's rescue. 

Forgetting the righteous code of war, the great Kaurava warriors—Drona, Karna, Duhshasana, Shalya, and many others—surrounded and jointly attacked the young Abhimanyu. Yet, Abhimanyu held them all back with a relentless shower of arrows. He pierced Karna's armor, badly wounded Shalya, shattered the chariot of Shalya's brother, and knocked Duhshasana senseless. 

Watching the boy fight, even Drona was filled with admiration. "He stands unequalled as a bowman," Drona admitted. "If he wishes, he can wipe out this whole host, single-handed."
Hearing this, Duryodhana grew furious and ordered an all-out, ruthless attack to kill the boy immediately. 

Duryodhana's brave son, Lakshmana, rushed forward to duel Abhimanyu, but a sharp arrow from Abhimanyu's bow whizzed through the air and pierced Lakshmana, killing him instantly. Seeing his son dead, Duryodhana screamed in blind rage: "Kill! Kill the wicked Abhimanyu!"

The Kaurava commanders launched a combined, cowardly assault. 
"Tell us how to kill him!" Karna shouted to Drona. "We cannot pierce his armor."
"Aim at the reins of his horses and cut them off. Then attack from behind," Drona advised, abandoning all rules of fair combat. 

Following the cruel advice, Karna cut off the reins of Abhimanyu's horses. From behind, he shot an arrow that snapped Abhimanyu's bow in half. Soon, his horses and charioteer were killed. 
Abhimanyu jumped to the ground, whirling his sword and shield to hold his ground against the horde. But an arrow from Drona broke his sword, and an arrow from Karna shattered his shield. 

Totally unarmed and disabled, the brave prince refused to surrender. He picked up the heavy wheel of his broken chariot and began whirling it at his unjust enemies. But arrows rained down from all directions, shattering the wheel to pieces. 
Finally, Abhimanyu picked up a heavy mace lying on the blood-soaked field. Duhshasana's son charged at him with another mace. They clashed fiercely, and both fell to the ground. But Duhshasana's son was quicker to get up. Before the exhausted Abhimanyu could rise, the Kaurava prince brought his heavy mace down on Abhimanyu's skull, killing him. 

Like savages, the cowardly Kaurava warriors danced around the dead body of the lone, heroic youth. 

But their wicked joy was short-lived. Not one of them outlived the war. Enraged by the brutal, unfair murder of his beloved son, Arjuna, along with Bhima and the mighty Pandavas, hunted down and killed every single one of them in the battles that followed at Kurukshetra. 

The star-crossed prince died a tragic death, but Abhimanyu's unparalleled bravery and ultimate sacrifice are remembered and celebrated to this very day.
