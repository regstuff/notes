# The Nawab's Diwan and Other Tamil Tales - 1. The Nawab's Diwan and Other Tamil Tales

## The Nawab's Diwan
It was a quiet evening at the marketplace in Satyapuri when a frantic horseman suddenly lost control of his steed and was violently thrown to the ground, writhing in pain. 

A young man named Ekambara Mudaliar immediately rushed to help him. 

"Don't touch him, Ekambaram!" the horrified villagers warned. "He is a *fakir* (a Muslim ascetic)! If you touch him, you will be defiled and lose your caste!"

"So what?" Ekambaram replied firmly. "He seems to be in danger of losing something much greater— his life."

Ignoring the warnings, Ekambaram sprinkled water on the fakir's face and tended to his injuries. When the fakir recovered, he thanked Ekambaram and offered him a reward. 

"The opportunity to help a fellow human being is reward enough," Ekambaram smiled. "I do not wish to have any reward. Not even from the Almighty."

"Such insolence, you brat!" the fakir suddenly snapped, and rode away without another word. The villagers mocked Ekambaram, warning him that his kindness would only bring him trouble. 

And they seemed to be right. Sometime later, the royal guards arrived and arrested Ekambaram. He was taken to the capital and presented before the powerful Nawab. 

To Ekambaram's absolute shock, the Nawab was none other than the fakir he had saved! The Nawab had been travelling in disguise to learn the true state of his kingdom. 

"Yes, it is me! And I owe my life to you," the Nawab said warmly. "I knew you were too noble to accept any reward voluntarily. So, I sent armed men to fetch you here."

The Nawab smiled. "I need the services of a man as honest and brave as you. Will you be my Diwan (Chief Minister)?"

Ekambaram gladly accepted. The Nawab noted, "Sometimes we have to use force against good men too... for they do not know what's good for themselves." Thus, Ekambaram Mudaliar became the Nawab's Diwan and earned great fame for his honest, efficient, and selfless service.

## Kanakan Finds a Home
Shantalingam Pillai and his wife were devastated. Their newborn infant had just died, and the poor mother could not be consoled. 

Suddenly, they heard the wails of a baby outside. A stranger was holding a crying infant. "This child has lost his mother," the man said. "Would your wife nurse him? He's hungry."

Pillai's wife immediately took the baby and suckled him. When the stranger tried to take the baby back, the child wailed in protest. 

"Why don't you give him to us?" Pillai asked. "We will take care of him."

"I will give him to you on one condition," the stranger agreed. "You must leave town immediately."

Desperate for a child, the couple agreed. They named the boy Kanakan and moved away that very night. 

Years later, they settled in Satyapuri. Pillai found a job as a tutor for the village chief's son, Pratapan, who was the same age as Kanakan. The chief's strict mother laid down a harsh rule: "If Pratapan makes a mistake, don't punish him... instead punish Kanakan. Pratapan will get the message."

Pillai had no choice but to agree. Poor Kanakan was frequently caned for Pratapan's mistakes. 

When Pratapan's mother found out, she was furious at the injustice. To teach Pratapan a lesson, she served lunch to Kanakan but gave Pratapan nothing. 

"Just keep looking at Kanakan as he eats," she told Pratapan. 
"That is not going to fill my belly!" Pratapan complained. 
"Exactly!" his mother replied sternly. "If your mistakes are corrected by Kanakan being punished, then he alone needs to eat when you feel hungry."

Pratapan realized his foolishness, and from that day on, he and Kanakan became the best of friends, sharing many adventures. 

Kanakan proved to be exceptionally clever. Once, when asked to guide the visiting British Governor to his camp, Kanakan deduced the Governor's identity simply by observing that he was the only man whose hat was not removed. 

Another time, Pratapan tried to show off his "higher math" skills. Pointing to two fruits, Pratapan said, "One... and two... make three fruits!"
Kanakan cleverly replied, "All right! You eat the first fruit, Jnanam (their friend) will have the second... and you can have the 'third' fruit, Pratapan!"

But Kanakan's life was shadowed by a dark secret. He had discovered that Pillai was not his real father. Despondent over not knowing his true parents, he attempted to drown himself in the river. 

He was saved by a passing ascetic. When the ascetic heard Kanakan's name and saw his birthmark, he realized the boy's true identity and rushed to the neighbouring kingdom of Adoor. 

Days later, the Prabhu (ruler) of Adoor, Devaraja Pillai, arrived in Satyapuri with a large retinue. He tearfully embraced Kanakan. "My son!" 

Devaraja explained the tragic tale: Years ago, his jealous younger brother had hired an assassin (the ascetic) to kill the infant Kanakan so he could inherit the throne. The ascetic couldn't bring himself to kill the baby and had handed him over to Shantalingam Pillai instead. The younger brother had recently died, confessing his terrible crime in a letter, leading Devaraja to search for his long-lost son. 

Kanakan was finally reunited with his true parents. Grateful to Shantalingam Pillai and his wife for raising the boy as their own, Devaraja invited them all to live together in the royal palace at Adoor.
