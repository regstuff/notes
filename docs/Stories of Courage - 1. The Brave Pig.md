# The Brave Pig

Long ago, during the reign of King Brahmadatta, there lived a carpenter in a small village near Varanasi. Everyday, he went deep into the jungle to collect wood for his trade. 

One evening, as he was returning home with a bundle of wood, he tripped over something small and squealing. 
"What is this?" he asked, picking it up. "A piglet! Poor thing! It must have been abandoned by its mother."

The kind-hearted man took the tiny, shivering piglet home. 
"It looks so famished!" his wife exclaimed.
"Yes, let us give him something to eat," the carpenter agreed. 
Tenderly, the couple looked after the piglet, feeding it warm milk and caring for it like a child.

The piglet soon grew up to be a huge, plump, fierce-looking pig who followed his master everywhere. The carpenter named him Baddhaki, which means 'carpenter' in Pali.
"Ha! Ha! See how that pig helps his master," the villagers laughed. "He is literally turning the logs for him."
"He has learnt the trade too. He is a carpenter pig!"
Like a loyal pet dog, Baddhaki had learnt to fetch and carry. 
"Get me the saw, Baddhaki," the carpenter would say, and the pig would trot over with the tool in his mouth.

"He has grown quite big and plump, and he's so helpful," the carpenter said one day, patting Baddhaki. "Remember how incredibly tiny he was when I brought him home?"
"You have trained him so well," his wife smiled. "But what is the use? He's so fat now, someone in the village may be tempted to kill him for his meat."

The carpenter paused. He had become so deeply attached to his pet that this dreadful thought had never struck him. "I can't imagine anyone killing him for food."
"Yes! We've brought him up like a child," she agreed. "But to others he is just a fattened pig."
"I feel he is no longer safe here."
"Yes, you are right. Someone may steal him away and kill him."

They decided with heavy hearts that it was best to return the pig to the safety of the deep jungle. 
"There! You will be safer here. Goodbye, Baddhaki," the carpenter said tearfully, leaving him near a thick thicket.

At first, Baddhaki felt completely lost in the wild jungle, having lived so long safely as a pampered pet. For days, he roamed about looking for a safe place to live. At last, he came upon a large, comfortable cave in a hillock. 
"This is a good place," Baddhaki thought. "There is a fresh brook nearby and lots of sweet tubers to eat."

Soon he found he was not alone in his choice of a home. Hundreds of thin, terrified pigs lived nearby. 
"Ah, there you are!" Baddhaki greeted them. "I have been all over the jungle looking for other pigs."
"Are you new to the forest?" one of the thin pigs asked timidly.
"Yes, I used to live in the village with a carpenter. That is why my name is Baddhaki. Now I plan to stay here. It is an excellent spot."
"Yes, but fraught with constant danger," the pigs shuddered.
"I was wondering why all of you look so incredibly thin and worn. After all, there seems to be enough food for all of you around here."
"How can we thrive?" an elder pig sighed. "We are forever anxious and worried. There is a fierce tiger who attacks us every single morning."
"Every single day, exactly at sunrise," another shivered.

"But there are so many of you!" Baddhaki argued. "Can't you get together and overpower him?"
"No, no! How can we ordinary pigs confront a tiger?"
"All right," Baddhaki said firmly, taking charge. "I shall help you to catch him. But you must do exactly what I tell you to do."
"We will," the pigs chorused hopefully. "We need a leader like you."

That night, Baddhaki collected all the pigs for a war council. 
"To confront and overpower our common enemy, we must plan our strategy perfectly," Baddhaki instructed. "He is naturally stronger and more ferocious than us. But our true strength lies in our sheer numbers. We will arrange ourselves in a *Padmavyuha*."
"Padmavyuha?" the pigs asked, confused.
"Yes, a battle formation in the shape of a lotus flower. The women and children will be completely shielded in the centre. The strong young pigs will tightly surround and protect them."

He drew a map in the dirt. "The strongest adults who are fit to fight will be packed together on the outer edge of the Padmavyuha. I will stand right here at the very front as bait. I need you to dig a round ditch in front of my spot. The ground should slope downwards behind me. On the edge of the slope, make another, much deeper ditch. It should be inclined and densely filled with thorny brush and razor-sharp stones."

All night, the pigs worked tirelessly carrying out Baddhaki's precise instructions. 
"Finished at last!" they panted.
"It is almost dawn! Take your positions," Baddhaki ordered.

As usual, exactly at dawn, the hungry tiger arrived to catch his prey. "Oh! I am famished. I must find a fat, juicy pig today."
But he stopped in his tracks. He was completely surprised to see the pigs standing their ground in a tight, impenetrable battle formation. He glared at them in anger. 
*How odd!* the tiger thought. *Everyday they run blindly at the sight of me.*

"Glare back at him. Do not be afraid," Baddhaki commanded the front line.

The tiger opened his massive jaws and roared loudly. "GRRR..." He pretended to pounce, thinking, *I will scare the daylights out of them and pick off the slow ones.* "GRRR..."
But Baddhaki gave a signal. The massive group of pigs aggressively grunted together in reply. "OINK! OINK!" 

The tiger blinked. *What on earth! They are not afraid of me at all. They are imitating me as if we were playing a game!*
The tiger looked closely at the massive, unafraid Baddhaki standing at the front. *It must be that unusually fat pig who is leading them. I dare not attack them today. They look completely united and strong.*

Quietly and cowardly, the tiger slunk away back to his cave. 

A wicked hermit who lived nearby and often shared the tiger's kills saw him returning. "What? You have returned empty-handed? Where's your prey?"
"I caught nothing," the tiger grumbled.
"Yes! And you must be looking forward to your usual share of pig meat," the tiger sneered at the hermit. "But both of us have to fast today."
"Why are you looking scared?" the hermit taunted.
The tiger spoke to him about the bizarre behaviour of the pigs. 
"Don't tell me the little pigs scared you off, ha! ha!" the hermit mocked.
"Don't laugh at me. You should have seen them today. All these days they used to scuttle off to hide in their caves as soon as they saw me. Today, they had arranged themselves in battle formation and fiercely glared and grunted back at me."

"Come, come, don't give up so easily," the wicked hermit encouraged. "After all, what can fat pigs do to a fierce tiger like you? Go back again and attack them. They are bluffing."

Fired up by the hermit's mocking words, the tiger returned to the hillock.
"See, the big crook is back," a pig squeaked in fear. "What should we do now?"
"Don't be afraid. Stay in formation! We will get him today," Baddhaki ordered.

The tiger stood on a rock above the ditch. *That exceptionally fat pig is their leader. I'll leap and get rid of him first,* the tiger decided.
Taking careful aim, the tiger leapt through the air directly at Baddhaki's throat.

But in the exact nick of time, Baddhaki ducked smoothly and jumped into the shallow ditch ahead. 
Having completely missed his target, the heavy tiger could not control his momentum. Down he went, rolling wildly all along the slippery slope Baddhaki had designed... and fell headlong with a tremendous crash into the deep ditch filled with sharp stones and thorns.

The tiger was badly hurt and impaled by the fall. 
Before the stunned tiger could even recover, Baddhaki and the other strong pigs ruthlessly swarmed into the ditch and attacked him with heavy stones and their sharp tusks... and killed him.

When they saw their terrible enemy finally dead, the pigs shouted for pure joy. 
"Bravo Baddhaki!"
"You helped us to kill the tiger."
"You are our true leader, wise and courageous!" they cheered, safe at last.
