# Rajbala

This Amar Chitra Katha retells two exciting folktales from the historic region of Sind (now in Pakistan). 

### The Secret of Gulab Singh

One bright morning, two ruggedly handsome horsemen rode up to the magnificent palace of Rana Jagat Singh of Udaipur, seeking an audience. 

"Who are you?" the Rana asked kindly. 
"We are Rajput warriors in need of work, Your Majesty," the older youth bowed respectfully. "My name is Ajit Singh, and this is my younger brother-in-law, Gulab Singh."
Impressed by their strong bearing, the Rana immediately hired them, offering them a generous salary of five rupees a day. 

The two newcomers quickly proved their worth. Shortly after joining, they accompanied the Rana on a royal hunt. Suddenly, a massive, ferocious lion ambushed the royal party and charged directly at the Rana's elephant. Taken completely by surprise, the Rana dropped his bow. Before the beast could pounce, young Gulab Singh fearlessly lunged forward, plunging a heavy spear deep into the lion's heart, killing it instantly. 

Immensely grateful to the brave youth for saving his life, the Rana richly rewarded Gulab Singh by appointing him the Captain of the Royal Guard, and made Ajit Singh the Captain of the Sleeping Apartments. 

For a long time, the two captains served the Rana perfectly. However, one cold, stormy day, while Gulab Singh was on guard duty near the royal chambers, 'he' suddenly broke into an incredibly sweet, melodious, and deeply sorrowful song. The Rana's wife overheard the beautiful voice and was intensely puzzled. 

"Ranaji," the Queen asked her husband that night, "of the two new captains of your guard, one is surely a woman! No man could possibly sing with such a delicate, feminine voice."

The very next morning, the Rana summoned Ajit Singh to his private chambers. "Ajit, is one of you a woman? Answer me truthfully!" the Rana demanded softly but firmly. 

Realizing the secret was out, Ajit Singh immediately fell to his knees. "Forgive us, Ranaji," he confessed. "You are right. Gulab Singh is actually my beloved wife, Rajbala."

"But why the heavy disguise?" the astounded Rana asked. 

Ajit Singh sighed deeply. "My father was the Raja of Umarkot. Tragically, our lands were violently seized by the cruel Raja of Sind, and my father died a penniless, broken man. I was raised in extreme poverty by my widowed mother. However, when I was an infant, I had been formally betrothed to Rajbala, the daughter of the wealthy Thakur Pratap Singh. 

"When I came of age, I sent a relative to remind the Thakur of his solemn pledge. Being an honorable Rajput, the Thakur could not possibly go back on his word, but he was deeply unhappy about marrying his precious daughter to a landless pauper. Hoping to legally break the engagement, he set an impossibly high condition: I could only marry Rajbala if I immediately paid him a massive dowry of twenty thousand rupees! 

"Determined to win my bride, I rode for days across the desert to Jaisalmer and successfully begged a wealthy, old moneylender—a former friend of my late father—for a massive loan. I paid the staggering dowry, and Rajbala and I were finally married. 

"However, my massive debt hung heavily over my head. My sweet wife noticed my deep depression. When I finally confessed the truth about the crushing loan, she absolutely refused to let me shoulder the burden alone. She cleverly disguised herself as a young man, and we traveled together to your generous court to earn enough money to repay the debt."

Deeply moved by Rajbala's incredible courage, fierce loyalty, and brilliant resourcefulness, the Rana smiled warmly. He immediately summoned his royal treasurer. 

"You have more than earned this by saving my life," the Rana declared, handing the stunned couple exactly twenty thousand rupees from the royal treasury. Not only did he clear their debt, but the grateful Rana also gifted the brave couple a beautiful palace and a large, fertile estate, where the courageous Ajit Singh and his brilliant wife lived happily for the rest of their days.

### Bir Singh and Sunderbai

Deep in a beautiful, shaded forest garden, a group of royal women sat discussing the arrogant nature of men. 
"Without a mother or a wife, men are completely lost," one woman stated firmly. "And yet, they have absolutely no respect for women."
"How men treat women depends entirely on the women themselves," another replied wisely. "I have decided to marry Bir Singh, the famously arrogant Crown Prince of Vallabhipura. I'll teach him a lesson by proving that a woman can be just as clever, brave, and strong as any man!"

Little did she know, Prince Bir Singh himself was resting behind a nearby tree, having lost his hunting party! Highly amused and intrigued by the girl's incredibly bold boast, he quietly asked a passing gardener who she was. 
"That is Princess Sunderbai, daughter of Raja Kesari Singh," the gardener replied. 

A few days later, acting on Bir Singh's eager request, the Raja of Vallabhipura formally sent a marriage proposal to Raja Kesari Singh. The proposal was joyfully accepted, and the grand wedding was celebrated. 

However, when Sunderbai arrived at her new palace in Vallabhipura, Bir Singh deliberately ignored her for weeks, maliciously hoping to break her proud spirit. Finally, losing her patience, Sunderbai secretly followed him to the temple. 

When Bir Singh noticed her, he smirked arrogantly. "Where is the legendary strength and valor of which you boasted so loudly in your garden?" he taunted. 
Furious, Sunderbai raised her chin. "Until I prove my words to you, my Lord, I will never step foot into your palace again!" she vowed fiercely. 

Returning to her lonely chambers, Sunderbai secretly dispatched her most trusted maid to her father in Shalya. *Send me a man's dress, a suit of armor, and a swift horse,* she wrote, *but let no one know of it.*

Dressed impeccably as a handsome, rugged young Rajput warrior, Sunderbai secretly rode out of Vallabhipura in the dead of night. Two days later, she confidently presented herself at the royal court of Vallabhipura—her own father-in-law's court! 
"I am Ratan Singh," she introduced herself in a deepened voice. "I seek employment in your royal army."

Deeply impressed by the young man's confident bearing, the Raja immediately hired 'him'. Over the next few weeks, 'Ratan Singh' and Prince Bir Singh became incredibly close, inseparable friends, with Bir Singh completely failing to recognize his own disguised wife! 

One day, Bir Singh confessed his deep secret to his new best friend. "I truly love my wife, Sunderbai," Bir Singh sighed, "but I want to test her boast. I want to see if she is truly as brave as she claims."
"If she is a true Rajput woman," Ratan Singh smiled knowingly, "she definitely will be."

Soon, a terrifying crisis struck the capital. A massive, ferocious man-eating lion began creating absolute havoc on the outskirts of the city. The desperate Raja offered a huge reward to anyone who could kill the beast. 

"I will kill it, Maharaj," Ratan Singh volunteered bravely. "But first, make me a hollow iron image of a man."

When the hollow iron statue was placed in the forest, Ratan Singh secretly climbed inside it at midnight. When the ferocious lion arrived and aggressively pounced on the metal statue, breaking its own claws and teeth, Ratan Singh thrust 'his' sword out from a hidden slit, expertly killing the exhausted beast with a single, lethal blow. The Raja was so thrilled by the incredible display of courage that he rewarded Ratan Singh with a massive estate. 

A few weeks later, while the Raja and Bir Singh were hunting in a far-off jungle, a massive army of ruthless marauders suddenly attacked the royal party. Outnumbered and overwhelmed, both the Raja and Prince Bir Singh were taken captive, and Vallabhipura was violently seized! 

Hearing the disastrous news, Ratan Singh did not panic. 'He' immediately galloped at lightning speed to Shalya and mobilized Raja Kesari Singh's fierce, elite lancers. Acting as the supreme commander, Ratan Singh brilliantly divided the massive cavalry into three groups, launching a devastating, coordinated mock attack on the city gates to completely distract the enemy forces. 

While the marauders were entirely focused on defending the main walls, Ratan Singh personally led an elite strike force through a forgotten, secret underground tunnel that led directly into the heart of the royal palace. 

Bursting into the main hall where the unsuspecting enemy commanders were celebrating, Ratan Singh's forces slaughtered them completely, throwing open the main gates for the rest of the Rajput army. The enemy was entirely routed, and the Raja and Prince Bir Singh were joyfully rescued! 

"Ratan, how can I ever reward you for saving my entire kingdom and my heir?" the weeping Raja asked gratefully. 
"Maharaj, I would like to meet some old friends first," Ratan Singh smiled gently. "May I go?"

Ratan Singh immediately rode back to the secluded royal apartments, swiftly changed back into her elegant bridal clothes and precious jewelry, and calmly waited. 

When Prince Bir Singh rushed into the apartments looking for his brave best friend, he found his beautiful wife instead. He stared at her, utterly perplexed. 
"Where is Ratan Singh?" he asked. 

Sunderbai smiled, a brilliant, knowing twinkle in her eye. "Look deeply into my face, my Lord," she said softly. "You might find your brave, dearest friend right there."

In a sudden, overwhelming flash of realization, the incredible truth dawned on Bir Singh. The fearless lion-slayer, the brilliant military commander, the ultimate savior of his kingdom—it had been his own wife all along! 

Overwhelmed with profound awe, deep respect, and immense love, Bir Singh fell to his knees before her. "Forgive me, my brilliant Sunderbai," he wept, completely humbled. "You have truly proven that women are as clever, resourceful, brave, and infinitely strong as any man on earth!"
