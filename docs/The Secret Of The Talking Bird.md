# The Secret Of The Talking Bird

Timma, a poor fowler, used to catch birds and sell them for a living. One evening, he cast his net in the forest and caught a brilliant green parrot. 

"A parrot! It's my lucky day!" Timma exclaimed happily. 

To his absolute shock, the parrot spoke in a clear, human voice. "Let me go, my friend."

"It can talk!" Timma gasped. He thought for a moment. "You will make my fortune if you set me free," the parrot urged. 

"I'd be a fool to free a bird that I've caught," Timma replied, shaking his head. 

"And yet, I'll set you free," the parrot said cryptically. "If you wait there for some time, a rare bird will land in your net—a bird you will be proud of."

Intrigued, Timma let the green parrot fly away. He waited patiently by his net. A short while later, a breathtakingly beautiful bird with feathers of five brilliant colors fluttered down and landed right in his trap!

"A parrot again! And what a colourful one!" Timma rejoiced. 

Knowing the bird's worth, Timma bypassed the market and took it straight to the royal palace. However, he was stopped at the gates by the King's arrogant and envious minister. 

"The Maharaj will not be able to see you. He is busy," the minister sneered. 

"When will he be free, sir?" Timma asked politely.

"Never! At least not for any and every fowler who comes here with a parrot."

Just then, the Maharaja himself walked out. Hearing the commotion, he looked at the cage. "A rare parrot? Let me have a look at it." The King's eyes widened in wonder. "It has five colours! I have heard about such a bird, but I had never seen one."

"It's for you, Maharaj," Timma offered.

"I'll accept it on one condition," the King smiled. "You must teach it to speak."

"It would be a pleasure, Maharaj," Timma agreed. 

The Maharaja commanded his minister to give Timma a heavy bag of gold coins. The greedy minister was furious. *What! A bag of gold coins for a simple parrot?* he fumed inwardly. 

Timma began to visit the palace frequently to train the bird. Soon, he brought good news. "The parrot has begun to talk, Maharaj," he reported. 

The King was delighted and began to spend hours talking to Timma, treating the humble fowler like a dear friend. This infuriated the minister even more. *The King calls that wretched fowler a friend! And he has no time to talk to me,* the minister thought bitterly. *I must persuade him to send Timma on an expedition... an expedition from which he will never return.*

The next day, the minister approached the King with a cunning suggestion. "Maharaj, you have a rare bird... but in such an ordinary cage! What about an ivory tower, Maharaj? No one has built an ivory tower for a parrot."

The King loved the idea. The minister immediately summoned Timma. "I want you to bring a cartload of ivory to build a tower," he ordered maliciously. "I will give you four weeks' time. If you fail to bring it... your head will be cut off."

Poor Timma went back to the forest in despair. "To get the ivory, I'll have to capture elephants. But how does one trap elephants? I only know how to trap birds."

Suddenly, a familiar voice called out from the branches above. "Why are you looking so worried, friend?" It was the green talking parrot!

Timma told the bird about the impossible task of the ivory tower. "Don't despair, my friend," the parrot comforted him. "Go eastwards. You will come across a forest. Walk through it till you reach a tank. All the elephants of the forest come there to drink water. Speak to the King Elephant. He will help you."

Timma followed the instructions and reached the tank. In the evening, as the herd left, one massive, majestic elephant remained. Timma ran up and fell at his feet. "Help me, O King of Elephants."

"Stand up, child. Who are you? What do you want?" the King Elephant asked gently. 

When Timma told his sad story, the King Elephant sighed. "I understand. You need my help. But I myself need some help. You humans are clever. We elephants are being terribly harassed by a wicked, man-eating lion. I want you to drive away the lion."

"Do it, and you shall have all the ivory you want," the King Elephant promised.

Timma sat by a stream, wondering how a poor fowler could tackle a fierce lion. As he bent down to take water in his palms, he saw his reflection. "What do I see here? I've got it!" he exclaimed. 

Timma rushed back to the city and returned the next morning with two huge mirrors. He placed the mirrors on the forest path, directly facing each other. Then, feeling absolutely confident, he sat down nearby and began to sing loudly. "Tara la... la... la."

Soon, he had company. A massive lion prowled out of the bushes with a terrifying roar. "G-R-R-R! Young man, even veteran game hunters run away when they see me. Aren't you scared?"

"Scared? Who, me? Hah!" Timma laughed dismissively. "Do you know to whom you are talking? I am a big game hunter—a hunter of lions."

"You liar," the lion snarled. "Do you think you can get away with that bluff? I'll eat you!"

As the lion leapt towards him with claws bared, Timma deftly stepped aside. The beast sailed past him and landed squarely between the two giant mirrors. 

Startled, the lion looked to his left and saw a fierce lion staring back at him. He looked to his right, and an infinite line of lions stretched into the distance! 

"Do you see your friends?" Timma asked casually. "Would you like to join them?"

"NO!" the terrified lion roared. Believing he was surrounded by a massive pride of rival lions, he turned tail and fled as fast as his legs could carry him, never to be seen in that forest again.

The King Elephant was overjoyed. "Thank you, my friend. And now I shall keep my part of the bargain." He led Timma to a hidden cave filled with the skeletons of elephants previously killed by the lion. "You may take all the tusks."

Timma tied the tusks into huge bundles, and the grateful elephants carried them all the way to the city. When Timma arrived at the palace at the head of a caravan of elephants loaded with ivory, the King himself came out to receive him. 

"Welcome, my friend!" the King beamed. "This man has achieved the impossible. I appoint you the chief of my mahouts." 

The minister watched in silent fury. "Oh no!"

The magnificent ivory tower was built, but the beautiful five-colored parrot remained silent and depressed within it. 

"A happy bird sings, Maharaj," the minister pointed out slyly. "This parrot does not. The parrot must be missing its old master. But how are we to trace its old master?" The minister smiled wickedly. "Our Timma should know, Maharaj."

Once again, the minister summoned Timma and gave him a deadly ultimatum. "The King wants you to bring the owner of our parrot here. If you don't bring the owner here within four weeks, your head will be cut off."

Timma trudged back to the forest, seeking out his old friend, the green parrot. 

"I will help you. Follow me," the green parrot said, leading Timma to a mysterious, abandoned temple. Inside the courtyard stood a beautifully crafted mechanical horse. 

"Mount this horse and turn the key," the parrot instructed. "Fly westwards. When you fly over the sea, you will come across an island. The owner of the bird lives there."

Timma mounted the horse, turned the key, and to his amazement, the mechanical beast soared up into the air! He flew over forests and oceans until he spotted the island. He landed the flying horse gently in the royal gardens of the island's palace. 

A group of maidens saw the marvelous flying horse and begged for a ride. Timma obliged them, flying them around the garden. Soon, the commotion attracted the island's beautiful Princess. 

"I too would like to fly," the Princess requested. 

Timma helped her onto the horse. But as soon as they were airborne, Timma turned the horse eastwards and flew straight over the ocean, heading back home! 

"Where are you taking me, young man?" the Princess asked, startled but not afraid.

"To someone you'd love to meet," Timma replied with a smile.

As they finally neared the ivory tower in Varanasi, the sad five-colored parrot suddenly perked up and began to sing a joyous, melodious song. 

"It's my parrot!" the Princess cried in delight. 

The King rushed to the tower upon hearing the song. "Meet the owner of the parrot, Maharaj," Timma presented the Princess. 

Captivated by her beauty and charm, the King married the Princess a few days later and appointed the loyal Timma as the Commander of his army. The minister gritted his teeth. *My plan has misfired again!*

A few weeks later, the new Queen suddenly fell mysteriously ill. The kingdom's best physicians were baffled. 

"You must send for a physician from the land of our Queen, Maharaj," the minister suggested smoothly, seeing a final opportunity to get rid of his rival. "We could send our Commander to the island."

"I agree," the King nodded. 

But the weak Queen shook her head. "There is only one person who knows the remedy, and you won't be able to find her. She was my close companion. Once, in a fit of temper, I used my magic and turned her into a parrot. No one can find her now."

Timma instantly knew what he had to do. He rode out to the forest and found his old friend, the green parrot. When he explained the Queen's mysterious illness, the parrot agreed to come. "Take me to your Queen and leave the rest to me."

Timma rushed the green bird into the royal bedchamber. The moment the ailing Queen reached out and touched the green bird's feathers, a magical transformation occurred. In a swirl of light, the green parrot turned back into a beautiful young woman! 

"Pardon me for turning you into a parrot," the Queen wept, embracing her long-lost companion. With the curse broken and her guilt relieved, the Queen was instantly cured. 

The beautiful companion, grateful for her rescue, fell in love with Timma, and they were soon married. Recognizing his immense wisdom, loyalty, and courage, the King finally dismissed his wicked advisor and appointed Timma as his new Chief Minister. Seeing that his dark schemes had only elevated the humble fowler to the highest position in the land, the envious former minister left the city forever, never to return.
