# Prabhavati: The Asura Princess

In the deeply fortified, magical city of Vajra lived a tremendously powerful Asura (demon) king named Vajranabha. Through centuries of severe, punishing penances, Vajranabha had won a seemingly invincible boon from Lord Brahma: his magnificent city of Vajra would be absolutely impenetrable. Not even the wind could enter its gates unless Vajranabha explicitly willed it. 

Vajranabha had a beautiful, intelligent daughter named Prabhavati. Though she was an Asura princess, she grew up listening to the breathless, admiring gossip of the elderly palace women. They spoke constantly of the Yadavas of Dwaraka, particularly praising the handsome and incredibly valiant Pradyumna—the son of Lord Krishna—who had recently slain the mighty demon Shambara. 

Listening to these tales, Prabhavati secretly gave her heart entirely to Pradyumna, stubbornly refusing the marriage proposals of all the handsome, brave Asura princes who sought her hand. 

One day, while walking through the palace corridors with her favorite cousins, Chandravati and Gunavati, Prabhavati overheard a disturbing conversation between her father and the celestial sage Narada. 

"The Yadavas get their immense power directly from Krishna, who is Lord Vishnu himself incarnate," Narada warned the Asura king. 
Instead of feeling reverence, Vajranabha's eyes burned with hatred and ambition. "If Vishnu is here among men, then I shall destroy him and his entire race!" the Asura king boasted arrogantly. He immediately began drawing up massive plans to conquer Indra’s heaven and wipe out the Yadavas. 

Alarmed by Vajranabha's terrifying ambitions, Lord Indra rushed to Dwaraka to seek Lord Krishna's help. "Brahma's boon makes his city impenetrable to armies," Indra despaired. "How can we possibly defeat him?"

Krishna smiled mysteriously. "Armies cannot enter, but art and illusion can. Pradyumna is a master of illusion. He shall enter the city of Vajra disguised as a brilliant actor. And we have the perfect excuse: Vajranabha has a beautiful, unmarried daughter named Prabhavati."

To set his divine plan in motion, Krishna dispatched a flock of magical, highly intelligent celestial swans to the heavily guarded city of Vajra. Because they were mere birds, the magical defenses of the city let them pass. 

The swans landed gracefully in the royal garden pools, where Prabhavati and her friends were bathing. Recognizing them as divine creatures, the princess spoke to them. Suchimukhi, the cleverest of the swans, gently told Prabhavati that the incredibly handsome Pradyumna was currently unmarried and seeking a worthy bride. 

Overjoyed that her secret dream might come true, Prabhavati begged the swan to fly back to Dwaraka and convey her deep, undying love to Pradyumna. 

When Suchimukhi delivered the sweet message, Pradyumna immediately sprang into action. Disguising himself as a highly talented traveling actor named Bhadra, and accompanied by his equally skilled uncle Gada and brother Samba, Pradyumna journeyed to the impenetrable gates of Vajra. 

Hearing of a magnificent acting troupe waiting outside his city, King Vajranabha—who considered himself a great patron of the arts—willingly lowered his magical defenses and invited them inside to perform. 

That evening, the entire Asura court watched spellbound as 'Bhadra' and his troupe delivered a spectacular, flawless performance. Watching him move, Prabhavati instantly recognized the handsome actor as her beloved Pradyumna. 

Unable to meet him openly, she devised a clever plan. She gave her trusted maid a beautiful, fragrant flower garland. "Take this to the actor Bhadra’s chambers," she ordered, "and hang it perfectly upon the wall nail."

As the maid carried the garland down the corridor, Pradyumna used his magical powers of illusion to instantly transform himself into a tiny, buzzing beetle. He flew quietly behind the maid and hid himself perfectly within the soft petals of the garland. 

When the maid hung the garland in Prabhavati's private, heavily guarded bedchamber and left, the beetle dropped to the floor and instantly transformed back into the gloriously handsome Pradyumna! 

Prabhavati was overjoyed. That very night, using a magical, sacred ring of fire to witness their vows, the two were secretly married. In the days that followed, Pradyumna brought his uncle Gada and brother Samba into the inner apartments, where they fell in love with and secretly married Prabhavati's beautiful cousins, Chandravati and Gunavati. 

For several blissful months, the Yadava princes lived entirely hidden within the women's heavily guarded apartments, their presence completely unknown to the Asura king. 

However, the secret could not be kept forever. When Vajranabha finally discovered that his daughter and nieces had been secretly living with the hated Yadava princes, his roaring fury shook the very foundations of the palace. 

"Arrest the trespassers! Kill them all!" Vajranabha bellowed, unleashing his entire, massive royal guard upon the women's chambers. 

"Do not worry, my love," Pradyumna smiled fiercely at a terrified Prabhavati. "We are Yadavas. We do not hide when battle calls."

Using his immense magical powers, Pradyumna instantly created millions of illusory, heavily armed clones of himself, plunging the Asura guards into total, panicked confusion. He then conjured a magnificent, shining war chariot and fearlessly charged directly into the heart of the massive Asura army alongside Gada and Samba. 

Sensing the battle had begun, the faithful swan Suchimukhi flew at lightning speed to Dwaraka to alert Lord Krishna. 

Back in Vajra, the battle raged fiercely. Vajranabha was a terrifying, immensely powerful foe. Wielding a massive, heavy mace, he struck Pradyumna a devastating blow that knocked the young prince completely unconscious to the ground. 

Just as Vajranabha raised his weapon for the killing strike, the sky split open with a blinding light. Riding upon the magnificent giant eagle, Garuda, Lord Krishna and Lord Indra descended upon the battlefield! 

Krishna raised his legendary divine conch, *Panchajanya*, and blew a long, earth-shattering note. The pure, divine sound instantly revived Pradyumna, filling him with fresh, boundless energy. 

Pradyumna leaped to his feet. Seeing his son ready, Lord Krishna raised his hand and sent his ultimate, spinning divine weapon—the *Sudarshana Chakra*—flying directly into Pradyumna's waiting hand. 

Catching the blazing, sun-like discus, Pradyumna hurled it with all his might at Vajranabha. The divine weapon sliced through the Asura king's defenses and instantly struck him down, ending his tyrannical ambitions forever. Seeing their invincible king fall, the remaining Asuras, led by his brother Nikumbha, fled the city in absolute terror. 

With the threat completely neutralized and the impenetrable city conquered from within, Lord Krishna smiled warmly at his brave son. Rather than annexing the city, Krishna decreed that the vast, wealthy kingdom of Vajra would be equally divided and ruled by the newborn sons of Prabhavati and her cousins. 

Their mission perfectly accomplished, Pradyumna and his relatives stayed in Vajra for a few more months to ensure their children were firmly established on the throne, before finally returning home to Dwaraka in glorious triumph.
