# Krishna and Narakasura: Confidence Versus Arrogance

In his Varaha avatar (the boar incarnation), Lord Vishnu lifted Bhoomi Devi (Mother Earth) from the depths of the ocean. Soon after that, a son was born to Bhoomi Devi. He came to be known as Narakasura. Though he was the son of a divinity and a virtuous mother, Naraka unfortunately turned out to be a vicious asura who took pleasure in terrorising the inhabitants of all three worlds.

Kings, sages, rakshasas, and devas alike feared him. Whenever his ruthless hordes approached a city, panic spread rapidly. "It's Narakasura! He's back!" people would scream, rushing to guard their families. But their guards were always helpless against Naraka.

"Round up the chariots and horses!" Naraka would command his men. "Seize the maidens. They shall adorn my palace."

Later, at Naraka's impregnable capital, Pragjyotisha, he sat proudly on his throne. "How many maidens have we captured so far?" he asked his commanders.
"Sixteen thousand and one hundred, O King."
"Enough! How many four-tusked elephants does Indra have?"
"Thousands, my lord—all descendants of Indra's mount, Airavata."
"Then on to Amaravati! Those elephants shall adorn my stables!"

Narakasura rode boldly into Amaravati, the capital of the devas, and marched right up to Indra's stables. Indra could only fume with impotent fury. "He's taking away my elephants! But what can I do? I dare not oppose the asura. He is invincible." 

Thus did the wicked Naraka harass the devas. But then, for some reason, the asura lay low for a while.
"Naraka seems to have tired of his wicked ways, at last," the devas whispered among themselves. 
"Yes, we have not known such peace for a long while," Indra agreed. "We shall hold a feast to celebrate our new-found peace!"

A few days later, Amaravati wore a festive look. The sweet scent of Parijata blooms filled the air. All the devas, wearing their best ornaments, assembled to watch the apsaras perform. 
Suddenly, chaos erupted. "It's the evil asura! Flee!" 

As the devas tried to escape, Narakasura's hordes swooped down on them. "Don't spare them," Naraka ordered. "Attack, while I go and seize Varuna's umbrella."
As Naraka charged towards Varuna, the Lord of the Hydrosphere, a dazzling flash blinded him for a moment. It was Aditi, the mother of the devas, wearing her precious celestial ear-rings. 
"Those ear-rings lack lustre on you, O mother of the cowardly devas!" Naraka mocked. He rushed forward, ruthlessly snatched the ear-rings from her ears, and declared, "I shall keep them."

Mounting his chariot, Naraka headed towards Varuna. Before the deva even knew what was happening, Naraka seized his majestic umbrella and rode furiously towards Indra. 
"He's coming for me! I'd better flee," Indra panicked, mounting his elephant Airavata and rising into the air. Safe in the clouds, Indra seethed with anger. "The arrogant asura has dared lay his impure hands on my mother's ear-rings! He has gone too far. He must be taught a lesson, and there is only one who can do it. I will seek his help."

***

An agitated Indra burst into the palace at Dwaraka, where Lord Krishna was peacefully seated with his wife, Satyabhama. 
"What's the matter? Why do you look so pale?" Krishna asked.
"It's the asura, Naraka," Indra panted.
"Bhoomi Devi's son? What has he done?"
"He snatched my mother's ear-rings from her very ears, took away Varuna's umbrella, and drove me out of Amaravati! You must kill that sinful wretch. Only you can do it!"
"Where is he now?" Krishna asked, his expression hardening.
"He has gone back to his impregnable capital, Pragjyotisha," Indra explained. "You will have to surmount the barriers of mountains, weapons, water, fire, and wind that encircle his capital. And the five-headed asura, Mura, guards the gates of his palace."
"Narakasura has invited his own death by daring to lay his hands on Mother Aditi's ear-rings," Krishna declared, picking up his conch and his weapons. Instantly, his mighty mount Garuda appeared before him. "Mounted on Garuda, I'll finish off the evil one."

As Krishna prepared to leave, he noticed Satyabhama pouting. *Satyabhama resents this rude interruption,* he thought. *The gentle one cannot bear this sudden separation. I'll take her with me.* 
Without uttering a word, Krishna caught the pleasantly surprised Satyabhama by the waist and swung her onto Garuda, seating himself securely behind her.

Garuda soared into the air. As they neared Pragjyotisha, Garuda's beak suddenly hit something hard. Whichever way the great bird turned, huge mountains magically rose before him, blocking his path. 
"Lord, I cannot move forward!" Garuda cried.
Unperturbed, Krishna swung out his mighty mace, and within minutes, the mountains were shattered into dust. "Fly on, Garuda! We've broken the first barrier."

Filled with renewed energy, Garuda flew on. Suddenly, a barrage of weapons of every description rained down upon them. 
"Wait, Garuda. Don't try to break through," Krishna instructed. Drawing his bow, Krishna shot arrows from his inexhaustible quiver in such quick succession that every single weapon was destroyed before it could reach anywhere near Garuda or Satyabhama. "That's the last of them! You may proceed."

Garuda flapped his wings and flew on, only to be pushed back by a sudden surge of huge, crushing waves of water. 
"Garuda! Lord! It's the deluge!" Satyabhama cried out.
Still unperturbed, Krishna simply raised his finger. His glowing discus, the Sudarshana Chakra, manifested and whirled forward, cutting through the violent waves and breaking them into a gentle, life-giving rain.

Refreshed by the cool showers, Garuda was about to fly on when a huge blaze erupted, evaporating the rain and sending out leaping tongues of flame to scorch the intruders. But the whirling discus, with increasing momentum, sliced straight through the magical flames. 

Garuda darted forward, only to be confronted by the wildest of gales that kept sweeping him back into the fire. But the discus matched the momentum of the cyclonic winds, whirring through the currents in the opposite direction. The stormy winds were swiftly tamed, and Garuda flew on until the outer gates of Naraka's palace came into view.

"Ah! There lies Mura, deep in sleep," Krishna noted. "I shall rouse him out of his complacency." Krishna raised his conch and sent out a triumphant, earth-shaking blare.

Awakened with a start, Mura, Naraka's five-headed asura guard, saw Garuda hovering above. "It's Krishna! He's come to slay Naraka. But he'll have to kill me first!" Raising his glittering trident, Mura charged.
With two arrows shot in quick succession, Krishna split Mura's trident into pieces before it could even touch them. Then, he sent stinging arrows straight into the five gaping mouths of the asura. Roaring with pain, Mura flung his heavy mace at Krishna, only to have it completely blasted apart against Krishna's own raised mace. 

Inside the palace, the clash of weapons awoke Narakasura. He rushed to the window, alarmed by the sight. "It's Krishna! Will Mura be able to vanquish him?"
As Naraka watched in suspense, Mura rushed forward blindly, hoping to crush Krishna with his bare hands. Smiling all the while, with one arm protectively around Satyabhama, Krishna raised his finger. The Sudarshana Chakra came sailing through the air, and the next moment, all five of wicked Mura's heads were cleanly severed from his body.

Hearing their father's roars as he fell, Mura's seven sons came rushing out with maces, swords, and clubs, but Krishna routed them all in no time.

"Enough! I have permitted enough carnage," Narakasura roared from his balcony. "Let us ride out and finish that impudent Yadava who dares displease me—the invincible Narakasura! Follow me on your elephants!"
Mounted on his colossal war elephant and leading his hordes, Narakasura thundered towards the palace gates. Garuda instantly flew at the elephants, attacking them fiercely with beak, wing, and claw. Terrified by the divine bird, the elephants turned and panicked, running back towards the palace.

Narakasura was livid with rage. "You vile bird! You will not escape this powerful weapon of mine!" He hurled a mighty, glowing spear directly at Garuda. But when the weapon struck, Krishna's divine presence ensured it did not even have the force of a flower garland. 
"Impossible!" Naraka gasped. "That bird is endowed with greater strength than I thought." 

Desperate, Naraka took up his mace to hurl it at Krishna. But before he could even lift his arm, a terrifying whirring sound distracted him. He looked up just in time. "The discus! It's flying towards me!" 
With a final cry, the mighty Narakasura was struck and rolled off his elephant, dead.

From the heavens, the devas cheered, and a shower of fragrant flowers fell upon Krishna and Satyabhama. "Well done! Ah, well done!"

Meanwhile, Bhoomi Devi herself approached Krishna, followed by a young asura. She carried Aditi's precious ear-rings and Varuna's umbrella. 
"Come, Bhagadatta," she gently told the young asura. "Let us praise the Lord and seek his protection."
Placing the stolen articles at Krishna's feet, Bhoomi Devi sang hymns in his praise. "O Lord! This is Naraka's son, Bhagadatta. He places himself at your feet. Please protect him."
Krishna smiled warmly. "Do not fear, Bhoomi Devi. He shall be installed on his father's throne. Come, let us enter the palace."

When Krishna entered the grand palace, the sixteen thousand, one hundred maidens captured by Naraka rushed to greet him. 
"Protect us, O slayer of the evil Naraka!" they pleaded. "Lord Krishna, take us with you, away from this place!"
"So shall it be," Krishna promised. He turned to Bhagadatta. "Have these damsels sent to Dwaraka, properly escorted."

Krishna then inspected Naraka's vast treasury. "Have all this wealth sent to Dwaraka as well." Finally, he went to Naraka's stables and found the magnificent elephants. "These four-tusked descendants of Airavata should be returned to Indra."

His mission complete, Krishna turned to his wife. "Satyabhama, now let us go and delight Mother Aditi by returning her stolen ear-rings. Varuna, too, shall have his celestial umbrella back." 

Mounted on Garuda once more, they flew gracefully towards Amaravati to deliver the stolen goods and restore peace to the heavens.
