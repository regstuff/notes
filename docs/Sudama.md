# Sudama: The True Friend of Krishna

Sudama was a poor Brahman boy who formed a deep and lasting friendship with Krishna when they both studied at Guru Sandipani’s ashram. They spent their days together, studying the scriptures and serving their guru. 

Sudama often practiced reciting the scriptures with Krishna. "Your rendering of the Vedic hymns is almost perfect," Krishna once complimented him.

"I have got it from my father," Sudama replied with a smile. "Guru Sandipani and he were good friends."

One day, Sandipani’s wife called the two boys. "Will you go and bring me some firewood from the forest?" she asked.

Soon after they entered the deep forest to gather wood, a terrible, howling storm broke out. Rain poured down in torrents, and as the storm finally cleared, night had fallen. The boys were stranded in the dark woods.

"We have lost our way," Sudama said, his voice trembling slightly in the cold. 

"Do not be frightened, Sudama," Krishna reassured him gently. "I am with you."

Guru Sandipani, deeply worried about his students, found the boys only after a long and desperate search. Touched by their devotion, the sage blessed them. "Life is dear to all, and yet you have disregarded that life for my sake. I grant you a boon: you will not lose your knowledge of the Vedas in this world or the next."

When the period of their studies finally came to an end, it was time for them to part ways. "Tomorrow we part and go our ways," Sudama said sadly.

"But I shall always remember you," Krishna promised. 

"And I you," Sudama replied. 

Years passed. Krishna became the mighty Lord of Dwarka and married Princess Rukmini, the Goddess of Prosperity incarnate. Sudama, on the other hand, married a good Brahman girl. He did not care for worldly pleasures and lived a life of dire poverty. Everyone loved Sudama for his gentle nature, and he had no enemies. His wife was sorely tried by the austere life she had to lead, but she patiently bore it all, for she looked upon Sudama as her god. "The love of my lord sustains me," she would often tell herself.

Soon, children were born to them, and they too had to endure the harshness of poverty. 

"Mother, I am still hungry. I want some more rice," one child cried.
"Mother, I feel cold," shivered another. "Why don't I have a shawl like other children?"

Listening to her children's pleas, Sudama's wife felt her heart break. *I can no longer bear to see my children suffer for want of food and clothing,* she thought. 

That night, when the children had finally slept, she approached her husband. "I beg you to be patient with me, my lord."

"Do not fear. Speak out your heart, my good lady," Sudama encouraged her gently.

"You have told me that Krishna of Dwarka is your friend... and his wife is the Goddess of Prosperity incarnate," she began, the legendary prosperity of Dwarka flashing before her mind's eye. "Go my lord, I beseech you, for the sake of our dear children, and meet Krishna."

Sudama pondered for a moment. "The very prospect of meeting my dear friend after so many years makes me happy. I will go and see him, but I will not ask for anything."

"My lord! Even a visit to Krishna will bless us. I am content," his wife smiled, silently hoping, *Lord Krishna, the all-knowing, will understand. My husband need not even ask. He will give.*

Then, a thought occurred to both of them. "I cannot send my husband empty-handed," she realized. 
"Do you have anything in the house?" Sudama asked. "Anything worthy of being presented to Krishna?"

She suddenly remembered something. "My lord used to tell me how Krishna loved to eat *powa* (flattened rice)." 
Sudama smiled, remembering how Krishna used to snatch *powa* right from his hand when they were boys. 

"I'll go to our neighbour and borrow a few handfuls of *powa*," his wife decided. She hurried next door. "My husband is going to visit a friend and I want him to take a gift. Can you give me a little *powa*? I have none left at home."

"Can I ever refuse you anything?" the kind neighbour replied, handing her the rice. 

When she returned, she found she had nothing to tie the *powa* in. Resourceful and devoted, she simply tore the end of her own garment, tied the rice into a small bundle, and handed it to her husband. "Here, my lord."

Sudama took leave of his wife and set out on the long journey to Dwarka. Before he knew it, he had reached the magnificent golden city. When he came to the open gates of the grand palace, no guards stopped him. He walked freely into the palace grounds, passing through room after lavish room, until at last, he found Krishna and Rukmini.

"Look! Who is that?" Rukmini asked, noticing the frail, ragged man entering their chambers.

Krishna's eyes lit up with unbridled joy. "My dear friend—Sudama!" He rushed forward and embraced the poor Brahman warmly. Turning to his wife, he said, "This is the friend I've talked so much about."

"Come, sit down," Krishna urged, leading Sudama to a luxurious couch. "Let me bathe your tired feet." As Krishna applied soothing sandalwood paste to Sudama's tired limbs, Rukmini offered him pieces of betel nut. 

The palace attendants watched in utter disbelief. *What great act of merit has this dirty beggar in tattered garments performed to be so delightfully welcomed?* they wondered.

"We must do everything possible to make his stay here comfortable," Krishna told Rukmini. 

After Sudama was refreshed, the two friends sat together, reminiscing. "Tell me, have you married?" Krishna asked.

"Yes, my wife is a good Brahman woman," Sudama replied.

"You lead a householder's life, but your heart is free from attachment," Krishna observed with a smile. "Does your wife accept your way of life?"

"She has never questioned it," Sudama said simply.

Just then, Krishna's eyes fell on the small, ragged bundle tied to Sudama's waist. "Ah! You have brought a present for me," Krishna said eagerly, reaching out. 

Sudama hesitated, suddenly deeply ashamed. *How can I give my rag bundle to one who has all?* he thought, trying to hide it.

But Krishna gently took the bundle. *He has not come to ask anything for himself,* Krishna thought warmly. *He has come out of love for his wife and me!* 
Aloud, he said, "The poorest of gifts given to me with love is dearer to me than the richest of gifts given without love. What is this, Sudama?"

Krishna untied the knot and his face broke into a delighted grin. "Powa! Dear Sudama, my favourite eatable! You remembered!" 

*As if I could ever forget,* Sudama thought, moved by his friend's genuine happiness.

Krishna enthusiastically tossed a handful of the dry rice into his mouth. But just as he was about to pick up a second handful, Rukmini playfully caught his hand. "Enough, my lord! One mouthful is enough to give him all he needs in this world and the next." 

Sudama looked confused. *What does she mean? I don't understand,* he wondered. He did not realize that it was the duty of the Goddess of Prosperity to carefully control the distribution of wealth, and Krishna's first handful had already granted Sudama boundless riches. 

Krishna asked Sudama to spend the night in the palace. As Sudama lay on the unimaginably luxurious bed, he sighed happily, "I am indeed in heaven."

The next morning, Sudama took his leave. "Don't forget us, Sudama, when you go back home," Krishna said fondly as they parted.

On his long walk back, Sudama relived the magical experience. *I did not ask and he did not give. But I am content. He received me with great warmth. He embraced me because I was his friend. He sat me on the same couch as the Goddess and it was she who fanned me. I was received with hospitality and he washed my feet.*

*He must have thought that if he gave me wealth, I would forget him,* Sudama reasoned to himself. *He has saved me. I am fortunate indeed.*

But when Sudama finally neared his home, he stopped dead in his tracks. Where his dilapidated hut used to stand, there was now a sprawling, magnificent palace surrounded by lush gardens. "What is all this? To whom does it belong? Where is my hut?" he gasped.

Before he could make sense of it, a beautifully dressed woman adorned in jewels came out to receive him, followed by healthy, richly clothed children. 

"My lord, don't you recognise me?" his wife asked, her eyes shining with tears of joy. 
"Father, look at my new clothes!" his son cheered. 

And then, Sudama understood all. *For a handful of powa given with love, he has showered me with riches.*

As he stepped into his new, opulent home, Sudama looked around at the luxury but kept his heart grounded. *I must be careful not to let all this turn my head!* he reminded himself.

"Lord, tell me all," his wife asked eagerly. "How did this happen? What did you ask Krishna?"

"Believe me! I asked for nothing," Sudama smiled softly. "He showered his love and affection on me. But... I couldn't ask for anything. Krishna knows all that is in our minds."

And together, surrounded by their new blessings, they talked endlessly about the glory and greatness of Lord Krishna.
