# Tales Of Arjuna - 1. Arjuna, the Monkey and the Boy

Once, while the great Pandava prince Arjuna was on a pilgrimage to various holy places, he arrived at the sacred shores of Rameswaram in coastal Tamil Nadu. Looking out at the ocean, he observed the remnants of a great structure. 

"And that is the bridge Rama built with the help of the monkeys," Arjuna murmured to himself. He frowned, his pride as an archer welling up. "Monkeys? But why did a great archer like him need the help of monkeys? He could have built a bridge of arrows! Why didn't he?"

A Brahman passing by heard him and went his way, but a monkey, which had been quietly following Arjuna, suddenly burst out laughing. "HA! HA! HO! HO! HA!"

"What is so funny?" Arjuna demanded.

"A bridge of arrows would have collapsed under the weight of the monkeys!" the monkey chuckled, pointing at the sea.

Arjuna was a little annoyed by the monkey's audacity. "Mere monkeys could break a bridge of mighty Rama's arrows? Impossible! Why, no monkey could crush a bridge of even *my* arrows!"

The monkey grinned mischievously. "I challenge that! A bridge made with your arrows won't withstand even my weight, puny as I am."

"And if it does?" Arjuna challenged. 

"If it does, I am willing to be your slave!" the monkey retorted. 

"And if it doesn't, I am willing to burn myself on a pyre!" Arjuna proudly declared.

So Arjuna set to work. He began to bridge the waters between Lanka and the mainland with a rapid shower of arrows. *With the quiver of inexhaustible arrows that Lord Agni, the God of Fire, gave me, I should have no problem in building a strong bridge,* he thought confidently. 

As soon as the gleaming bridge of arrows was ready, he turned to the monkey. "There! You can test it."

The monkey hopped forward. But he had barely touched the bridge... when it completely collapsed into the waters!

"No! I can't believe it! I'll try again," Arjuna said, his pride stung. 

This time, Arjuna shot the arrows much closer, leaving absolutely no gaps between them. The bridge looked incredibly solid. "Now test it!" he challenged.

The monkey stepped onto it. "This is stronger than I expected! I'll walk a bit further." But he had hardly taken a few more steps when the bridge shattered again, sending the monkey splashing into the sea.

Arjuna hung his head in defeat. "I have lost! I shall get the pyre ready." 

True to his word, the pyre was built, and a blazing fire was lit. Arjuna stood before the flames, ready to jump in and end his life. 

"WAIT!" a young Brahman boy suddenly cried out, running towards them. "Why do you want to give up your life?"

Arjuna and the monkey explained the terms of their bet to the boy. 

The boy listened carefully and shook his head. "But a bet that had no witness is not valid."

"I don't know who you are and why you want to save my life," Arjuna sighed. "But... I am afraid your words are born more out of kindness than out of love for truth."

"How can you be sure that the bridge collapsed because of the monkey's weight?" the boy countered wisely. "There was no one present to judge that! Why don't you two compete again and let me be the judge?"

"That's a better suggestion," Arjuna agreed, and the monkey nodded.

Arjuna built the bridge of arrows for a third time. As soon as it was ready, the monkey confidently began walking across it. But this time, something felt wrong.

*What's happening to me? My strength is leaving me! I feel weak!* the monkey panicked. Determined to win, the monkey made himself as large as a mountain. *I'll take on the form I took when I leapt across the ocean in search of Sita,* he thought. 

The gigantic monkey jumped up and down on the delicate bridge of arrows with all his might. But miraculously, the bridge did not even tremble. 

*What! The bridge still stands! I can't believe it!* the monkey thought in awe. Then, he looked at the smiling young boy who was acting as their judge. *Only Krishna... Why, of course the boy is Krishna!*

At the exact same moment, the truth dawned on the humbled Arjuna. *What a vain fool I have been. Of course it is the Lord himself!*

"Rama!" the monkey cried out, bowing deeply.

"Krishna!" Arjuna gasped, falling to his knees.

And lo! In the place of the young boy stood Lord Vishnu in all his divine majesty. "Yes, it is I!" Lord Vishnu smiled. "Both of you needed to be taught a lesson in humility. You, Arjuna, were too vain about your archery. And you, Hanuman, were too proud of your strength."

Vishnu then turned to the monkey god. "Hanuman, to fulfil the conditions of your wager, you shall be on Arjuna's flag in the great battles to come."


# Tales Of Arjuna - 2. Arjuna Defeats Indra

A certain king once performed a massive sacrifice for twelve long years, during which vast offerings of ghee were continuously poured into the holy fires for Agni, the God of Fire. 

When at last the magnificent sacrifice was completed, Agni felt strange and perturbed. *Why do I feel so dull and weak?* he wondered. *Why am I so pale? Why don't I shine as I did before?*

Seeking a cure, he traveled to the celestial abode of Brahma. "O Lord, what should I do to regain my lost splendour and strength?" Agni asked.

Brahma smiled. "All the ghee you have had for so many years has made you ill. But don't worry. Consume the Khandava forest, and you will regain your lustre."

Rejuvenated by the advice, Agni hastened to the Khandava forest and began to blaze furiously, much to the dismay of Indra, the king of the gods. 

"If I don't stop this, my friend Takshaka, king of the Nagas, and his followers will perish in the burning forest!" Indra cried. Determined to protect them, Indra sent down incredibly heavy showers of rain, instantly dousing the flames. 

Frustrated, Agni went back to Brahma. "Lord, Indra is bent on thwarting me! What should I do?"

"There are only two persons who can help you: the valiant heroes, Arjuna and Krishna," Brahma advised. "Go to them. They are now near the Khandava forest."

At that very moment, Arjuna and Krishna were relaxing on the peaceful banks of the river Yamuna. 

"This has been an unusually hot summer," Arjuna remarked, wiping his brow. "I feel as if I am being scorched by Agni!"

Indeed, Agni was nearby, having taken the guise of an old, frail Brahman. *Ah! Those two must be Arjuna and Krishna, the foremost among men and warriors,* the disguised god thought.

Approaching them, Agni said, "I am a hungry Brahman. Gratify me by giving me a lot of food."

"Tell us what you wish to eat, holy one, and we shall try to give it to you," the two heroes replied respectfully. 

Agni then revealed his true, blazing form and told them who he was and why he had come. 

"We can tackle many Indras," Arjuna promised. "But we do not have the weapons to match our skill and speed. We also need a good chariot."

"Then I shall give you all these," Agni declared. He closed his eyes and thought of Varuna, the god of the oceans. "O Varuna, bring the Gandiva bow, with its inexhaustible quivers, for Arjuna. Bring for him also a chariot; and for Krishna, the Sudarshana discus."

In a flash, Varuna appeared and placed all the magnificent weapons and a majestic chariot before them. 

"All this is yours," Agni smiled.

"O Agni! With these weapons no one can withstand us. Now eat as much as you like! Surround the forest with fire," Arjuna encouraged.

Urged on by the heroes, Agni changed his form, breaking out into seven towering flames and surrounding the Khandava forest, setting it ablaze on all sides. As the flames rose high into the sky, Krishna turned to Arjuna. "I'll take on the denizens of the forest, and you take on Indra and the celestials."

As they posted themselves on opposite sides of the burning forest, the skies suddenly darkened ominously. 

"Thunderclouds!" Arjuna shouted. "Indra has struck again!"

The next moment, rain came down in massive torrents. The forest, choked with thick smoke and struck by lightning, was fearful to look at. 

*I must be quick! At this rate Agni will be smothered,* Arjuna thought. Drawing the mighty Gandiva bow, Arjuna stopped the shower with a spectacular shower of his own arrows, firing so rapidly that the entire forest was soon covered with a thick canopy of arrows.

"There! Not a drop of rain shall touch Agni. Not a single creature shall escape!" Arjuna declared triumphantly.

But the next moment, an angry Indra raised a violent, magical gale that hit Arjuna directly, knocking him unconscious. When Arjuna finally regained consciousness, he was furious. "I will not rest till I've vanquished you, O king of the devas!" he roared, aiming his arrows directly at Indra in the skies.

Indra retorted with deafening thunder and blinding flashes of lightning. But suddenly, all went quiet. 

"The energy has gone out of my thunder!" Indra gasped in shock. "The wind has blown away all the clouds. Arjuna has used the Vayavya weapon against me!"

The next moment the sky was clear. The sun shone brightly and a cool breeze fanned Agni as he blazed afresh. But the battle was not over.

"Krishna! Look! It's Indra and the devas!" Arjuna yelled. "And look! On the other side the asuras and nagas are charging forward!"

"Arjuna, you take on the devas... and I'll deal with the asuras and nagas," Krishna commanded. 

Krishna sent his gleaming Sudarshana discus flying through the air, and the terrified nagas and asuras fled in terror. Meanwhile, Arjuna countered the attack of the celestials so skilfully that they retreated in panic. 

Even as they fought, Indra could not help but admire his son's prowess. *My devas are turning away in fear! Arjuna has surpassed himself,* Indra thought proudly. *But, all the same, let me see the extent of your might.*

Indra sent down a massive shower of stones. But Arjuna quickly warded off the shower with his arrows, pulverizing the rocks in mid-air. Indra then tore up the peak of the Mandara mountain and hurled it at Arjuna, but Arjuna effortlessly smashed the mountain peak into dust with his arrows.

*I am pleased! His valour is unmatched! And Krishna has vanquished the asuras and nagas with his discus!* Indra thought, thoroughly defeated. *If it were not for Takshaka I would...*

At that moment, a divine voice echoed from the heavens. "Your friend, Takshaka, is safe. When the fire broke out, he was at Kurukshetra, O Indra! No one can defeat Arjuna and Krishna. And the destruction of Khandava has been ordained by fate. So give up the fight."

As they saw Indra gracefully retreating, Arjuna and Krishna leapt for joy. 

But suddenly, Krishna spotted movement. "Look, it's the asura, Maya! He was hiding in that cave! I'll get him with my discus." 

He raised his weapon, but the terrified asura sprinted toward Arjuna and fell at his feet. "O Arjuna, protect me!" Maya begged.

Arjuna lowered his bow. "Since you ask for my protection, you shall have it."

"Arjuna has given his word. Leave him alone, Agni," Krishna smiled, lowering his discus.

When quiet was finally restored, Agni blazed happily for fifteen days till he fully regained his lost lustre. 

Indra then came down from the heavens, his face beaming with pride. "For your valour and might, you deserve a boon each. Ask for one that is not available to men."

"Bestow all the weapons on me with the knowledge of how to use them," Arjuna requested.

"May my friendship with Arjuna last for ever," Krishna asked warmly.

"So be it, Krishna," Indra smiled. "And you Arjuna, shall receive the weapons when you need them most."

As Indra returned to the heavens, it was Agni's turn to bestow a reward. "I have been gratified by you. You shall, at my command, be able to travel anywhere at will."

When Agni left, Arjuna, Krishna, and the asura Maya relaxed on the bank of a river. 

"O Arjuna, you protected me from the wrath of Krishna and Agni. Tell me what I can do for you," Maya pleaded gratefully.

"Be ever well-disposed to us, Maya, as we are to you," Arjuna said kindly.

"But I must do something for you," Maya insisted.

Arjuna turned to his friend. "Well, Krishna, what can we ask of our friend?"

"Maya, you are the great architect of the asuras," Krishna noted. "Why don't you build a *sabha* (grand hall) for Yudhishthira? Build one that will be unsurpassed in splendour."

"It shall be done," Maya promised eagerly.

"Then, come, let us consult Yudhishthira," Krishna said. 

And that was how Yudhishthira acquired the magnificent, magical hall of illusions—a hall that became the last straw for his already envious cousins, the Kauravas, eventually inducing them to deceitfully deprive Yudhishthira of his kingdom.


# Tales Of Arjuna - 3. Arjuna Humbled

"Ah! Krishna, now that the Gandiva bow is mine, no one can defeat me," Arjuna boasted as he stood on a magnificent balcony. "Even Indra was helpless against my arrows!"

Arjuna, the proud Pandava prince, had come to visit Krishna at his palace in Dwarka. But his boasting was suddenly interrupted. 

From the streets below, they heard a loud, heart-wrenching cry. Seeing an old Brahman weeping outside the gates, they went down to him. 

"What is troubling you, friend?" Krishna asked gently.

"Eight times have I appealed to our king to save my dead sons, and eight times has he ignored me!" the Brahman sobbed. "Now I have lost my ninth son!"

He gestured bitterly toward the palace. "Though he is a warrior, he is fond of pleasure. If he had control over himself, would he be so callous? A king should protect his subjects from every calamity, including death. Restore my son to me, I implore you!"

Arjuna glanced sideways at Krishna, expecting him to act, but Krishna remained perfectly silent and unmoved. *Why doesn't Krishna respond? Has he lost all compassion?* Arjuna wondered.

When the Brahman continued to lament, Arjuna could no longer bear it. His pride swelled. "Don't grieve, my friend," Arjuna stepped forward confidently. "I will protect the next son born to you. If I fail, I will burn myself on a pyre for making a false promise."

The Brahman looked skeptically at him. "When Balarama, Pradyumna, and Aniruddha—all mighty warriors—could not protect my sons, how can you?"

Arjuna's vanity was sharply stung. "I may not be your Balarama or Krishna," he declared haughtily, "but I am Arjuna, the Pandava, and I wield the mighty Gandiva bow. I will save your son for you. I will defeat even Yama, the god of death, if necessary. Now go home and let me know when your wife is about to deliver her tenth child. I will wait in Dwarka till then."

Some time later, the Brahman returned, frantic. "Arjuna! Come! It is time! Please save my son from the clutches of death."

"I am coming," Arjuna said. He snatched his prized Gandiva bow and rushed with the Brahman to a small hut on the edge of the city. 

"There! My wife is lying in that hut," the Brahman pointed.

Arjuna raised his bow and fired an incredibly rapid volley of arrows, completely covering the hut with a thick, impenetrable network of steel and wood. Then, he stood guard outside, his bow drawn. *Now let us see if the Lord of Death can dare enter the hut,* he smirked.

Suddenly, a cry echoed from inside. "Wah-ah-ah!"

"Ah! A baby's cry! My tenth son has survived!" the Brahman wept with joy. But a moment later, his wife screamed from within the arrow-covered hut.

"Where's the baby? Why are you weeping?" the Brahman shouted.

"He... he... cried a little after he was born... and... then... just vanished!" she wailed.

The Brahman rushed out of the hut like a man possessed, his face contorted with grief and rage. "Where is my son? Before, I had at least beheld my dead children. This time I didn't even see my child! You had promised to save my child! You have broken your promise!"

"Be patient, friend. I'll bring your son back to you," Arjuna insisted, though his heart hammered with panic.

Mounting his divine chariot, Arjuna searched high and low across the earth. But nowhere could he find the Brahman's newborn son. Defeated, exhausted, and utterly humiliated, he returned to Dwarka. 

"I'd better return and give up my life. I have failed," Arjuna muttered.

The Brahman sneered as he approached. "I can see you have failed. Why have you come back?"

"To fulfil the second half of my promise," Arjuna said grimly.

Arjuna soon had a massive funeral pyre prepared. The flames roared high into the sky. He closed his eyes, preparing to jump into the fire and end his life.

"Wait, Arjuna!" Krishna's voice suddenly rang out. "Do not punish yourself. I will show you where the Brahman's sons are. Come with me."

The completely bemused Arjuna stepped back from the flames and got into Krishna's chariot. 

"We will go towards the west," Krishna commanded.

They drove on rapidly until they left the realms of light and entered the deep, endless regions of the night. It was pitch black.

"Oh! I can't see a thing! Are you there Krishna? Has the chariot stopped?" Arjuna called out, completely blind.

"The horses have stopped because of the blinding darkness," Krishna replied calmly. He then raised his arm. "Go, Sudarshana! Light our path."

Krishna's divine discus whirred out of his hand, flying ahead and cutting a brilliant, radiant path through the absolute darkness with its own blazing lustre. 

Following the light, they soon reached the majestic Ocean of Milk—the celestial abode of Lord Vishnu. When they arrived, Krishna bowed deeply to the Lord, of whom he was but a human avatar. 

Arjuna stood dazed for a moment, completely overwhelmed by the divine presence, before he too bowed in deep reverence. 

Sitting safely near Lord Vishnu were the ten missing sons of the Brahman.

Vishnu smiled warmly at Krishna. "I had these Brahman boys brought here only to give you an opportunity to come here. Slay the asuras and help preserve the divine law. That is your mission. After finishing it, come back quickly to me."

As they listened, everything suddenly clicked into place for Arjuna. He looked at Krishna, deeply humbled. *Ah! Krishna, you knew all along that they were here! No wonder you were silent and unmoved when the Brahman cried.*

Arjuna finally realized why this entire drama had been orchestrated. His vanity was shattered, replaced by a profound sense of awe. *O Krishna, I now understand. Whatever manliness and powers a man may have, it is all through the grace of God. Forgive me my vanity.*

Taking their leave of Lord Vishnu, Krishna and a newly humbled Arjuna gently placed the ten rescued sons of the Brahman into the chariot and began the long journey back to Dwaraka. 

It is faith in God and humility that ultimately wins our battles for us, Arjuna learned—not vanity or skill alone.
