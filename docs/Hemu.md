# Hemu: Brilliant Strategist, Shrewd Statesman

Hemu was born in the small town of Rewari, in present-day Haryana. As his father was a grocer, he was brought up to be a shopkeeper. However, Hemu had ambitions beyond his trade. "I don't plan to remain a grocer for the rest of my life," he decided. 

He saw an opportunity when Sultan Islam Shah (son of the famous Sher Shah Suri) came near Ferozepore. Hemu bought up all the grain in the area, forcing the army to come to him for supplies. Impressed by his foresight and resourcefulness, the Faujdar invited him to Agra. Sultan Islam Shah, planning a campaign against a rebel governor in the Punjab, appointed Hemu as the superintendent of bazars. 

Hemu successfully managed the army's supplies during the campaign. He also proved his shrewdness when Humayun's brother, Mirza Kamran, sought asylum. "An enemy's foe is a friend, your majesty. By helping Kamran you can upset Humayun's plans," Hemu advised. Intrigued, the Sultan appointed him to lead the secret service. 

After Islam Shah's death, the young prince was murdered by his uncle, who ascended the throne as Sultan Muhammad Adil Shah. Hemu pledged his loyalty to the new Sultan and was appointed his minister. "I know you will be loyal. Besides, you, a humble grocer, can never be a threat to me," Adil Shah reasoned. 

When Junaid Khan of Ajmer rebelled, Hemu volunteered to lead the army. "My common sense and intelligence can lead the soldiers to victory," he promised. Using defensive tactics, he lured Junaid Khan's general, Daulat Khan, into a false sense of security, then launched a surprise night attack and crushed the rebellion. The Sultan rewarded him richly, but Hemu insisted the rewards go to his fearless soldiers, winning their loyalty. 

However, Afghan nobles resented Hemu's growing influence. Intrigue and violence plagued the court, leading to the murder of nobles right in front of the Sultan. Hemu warned Adil Shah of an impending rebellion by Taj Khan Kermani. The Sultan marched against him, and Hemu brilliantly led a surprise attack across a river with elephants, defeating the rebels. "The cunning grocer is a shrewd general too," his enemies admitted. 

The empire began to fall apart as various governors declared independence, and Ibrahim Khan took Delhi and Agra. Humayun seized the opportunity, defeated the Afghans, and occupied Delhi. "We have lost everything Sher Shah worked for and we have no one to blame but ourselves!" Hemu lamented. 

A year later, Humayun died accidentally, leaving his young son Akbar as successor. Adil Shah sent Hemu to recapture Delhi. Hemu easily took Agra when the Mughal garrison fled in panic. He then marched on Delhi. 

The Mughal governor of Delhi, Tardi Beg, decided to stand and fight. Hemu charged into the thick of the battle, encouraging his men, and completely routed the Mughals. All the Mughal wealth fell into Hemu's hands. He distributed the booty generously among his soldiers. 

Having captured Delhi, Hemu realised the Afghans were divided and the Mughals leaderless. "I shall proclaim myself king and found a new dynasty!" he declared. On ascending the throne, Hemu assumed the legendary title of Vikramaditya. 

His first concern was to defend his new kingdom against the Mughal army despatched by Akbar and led by the veteran Bairam Khan. The two armies met on the historic plains of Panipat. 

Just before the attack, the leading elephant in Hemu's army was struck by lightning, which many saw as a bad omen. But Hemu rallied his troops, "No power can defy our will. Victory goes to those who fight for it!" 

Hemu led a spirited charge that scattered the Mughal forces. Bairam Khan was uneasy, thinking only a miracle could save them. Just then, a chance arrow pierced Hemu's eye. He swooned and collapsed in his howdah. 

Seeing their leader fall, Hemu's army panicked and scattered. What was certain victory turned into a crushing defeat. Hemu's elephant slowly wandered into the shelter of the nearby jungle, carrying the unfortunate hero who, but for a stray arrow, might have founded a great dynasty and changed the course of Indian history.
