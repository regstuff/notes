# Malavika

During the second century B.C., a fierce struggle for the throne of Vidarbha raged between two cousins, Yajnasena and Madhavasena. Sensing imminent danger, Prince Madhavasena fled the kingdom at dusk in a pair of chariots. Accompanying him were his beautiful sister, Princess Malavika, his loyal minister Sumati, and Sumati’s sister, Kaushiki. Their destination was the neighboring kingdom of Vidisha, ruled by King Agnimitra.

"I wish I could fly to Vidisha, Sumati," Madhavasena sighed nervously as their chariot bumped along the forest road. "Until I succeed in my mission, I'll know no peace."

Suddenly, armed soldiers of the rival cousin blocked their path. Realizing escape was impossible, Madhavasena made a swift decision. "Get off here with our sisters and trek through the jungle towards Vidisha," he ordered Sumati. "Go. It's an order... and remember my mission. Promise that you'll fulfil it should anything happen to me."
Reluctantly, Sumati promised and slipped into the jungle with the women just as the soldiers captured Madhavasena and his chariots.

The three fugitives joined a passing merchant caravan heading for Vidisha. However, disaster struck that very night when ruthless robbers attacked the camp. Sumati fought valiantly to protect the women but was brutally killed in the skirmish. In the chaos, the women were separated. When the robbers finally fled, Kaushiki emerged from hiding only to find her brother dead and Princess Malavika nowhere to be seen.

Unknown to Kaushiki, the terrified Malavika had wandered deep into the forest, where she fainted from exhaustion. She was discovered by Virasena, the frontier commandant of Vidisha. When the frightened girl awoke, she refused to reveal her royal identity. "My name is Malavika. Please do not ask me anything else," she pleaded.

Assuming she was a commoner, Virasena sent her to Queen Dharini, King Agnimitra’s chief queen. Struck by the girl's grace, Queen Dharini assigned her to the royal dance teacher, Ganadasa, to be trained as a court dancer. Thus, destiny landed Princess Malavika in the palace of Vidisha—not as a royal guest, but as a lowly maid.

A few days later, a female mendicant arrived at the palace seeking an audience with the queen. It was Kaushiki in disguise! Recognizing each other immediately, Kaushiki and Malavika made a silent pact to keep Malavika's true identity a secret for the time being.

One day, while King Agnimitra was admiring a newly painted mural with Queen Dharini, his eyes locked onto a breathtaking face in the background. "Who is this girl, my queen?" he asked, instantly smitten.
"Just one of my maids," Dharini replied evasively. But her young sister playfully blurted out, "I know! It's Malavika."

The king could not get the beautiful face out of his mind. Desperate to see her in person, he enlisted the help of his clever jester, Gautama. The jester cunningly provoked a fierce rivalry between Malavika's dance teacher, Ganadasa, and another court teacher, Haradatta. They took their quarrel to the king, demanding a trial of their skills. 

The king eagerly arranged a royal dance contest, appointing Kaushiki as the judge. Malavika was the first to perform. As she danced gracefully, singing a song of yearning, the king’s heart was completely captured. The feeling was mutual; Malavika had also fallen deeply in love with the handsome king.

Their secret romance blossomed, but it was not without obstacles. During the spring festival, Queen Dharini injured her ankle in a swing accident caused by the mischievous Gautama. Unable to perform the traditional spring ritual, the Queen summoned Malavika.
"Malavika, if the golden Ashoka tree blossoms within five days of you striking it with your decorated foot, I will grant you any boon you desire," the Queen promised.

Later, in the palace gardens, Malavika confided her love for the king to her friend Bakulavalika while preparing for the ritual. Overhearing them from behind a bush, the king stepped forward and declared his love. Unfortunately, the king's jealous second wife, Queen Iravati, was also watching. Furious, she reported the lovers' meeting to Queen Dharini.

Angered by the betrayal, Queen Dharini ordered Malavika and Bakulavalika to be locked in the dark treasure-cellar. "They must not be released until the guards are shown the seal of my serpent-ring," she commanded.

Once again, the jester Gautama came to the rescue. While the king was visiting Queen Dharini, Gautama burst into the room screaming, "Help! I've been bitten by a snake in the garden!" The royal physician, bribed by Gautama, declared that the only cure was a charm bearing the image of a serpent. Alarmed, Queen Dharini immediately handed over her serpent-seal ring. 
Gautama dashed straight to the cellar, flashed the ring to the guards, and freed the two girls, leading them to a secret rendezvous with the king in the garden.

Their romantic meeting was interrupted again by Iravati, but before a fight could break out, a maid rushed in screaming that the young princess had been frightened into unconsciousness by a monkey. The king and queens ran off, leaving Malavika and Bakulavalika alone. 

"Look!" Bakulavalika gasped, pointing at the garden. The golden Ashoka tree had burst into magnificent red blooms, well before the five days had passed.

The next morning, King Agnimitra and Gautama walked into the garden and stopped in sheer amazement. Standing beneath the blossoming Ashoka tree was Malavika, dressed in radiant bridal finery, accompanied by Queen Dharini.

Before the king could ask questions, a royal messenger arrived from the frontier. "Victory to the King! King Yajnasena of Vidarbha has been defeated, and Prince Madhavasena has been liberated!" he announced. He then presented two maids sent by Madhavasena as a gift. 

As the maids entered, they gasped in shock. "Our Princess! Alive!" they cried, falling at Malavika’s feet. 
King Agnimitra was stunned. "Princess? How do you know Malavika?"

Kaushiki stepped forward, throwing off her mendicant's disguise. "The moment has come to tell all," she announced. She revealed Malavika's true identity as the Princess of Vidarbha and explained their tragic journey. 
"But why didn't you tell us this earlier?" the king asked.

"When Malavika was a child, a wise sage prophesied that she would have to endure the life of a servant for exactly one year before marrying a great king," Kaushiki explained. "That is why I kept her royal blood a secret for a whole year."

Queen Dharini, bound by her promise to grant Malavika a boon for making the Ashoka tree bloom, stepped forward with a warm smile. Taking Malavika's hand, she placed it in the king's. "My lord, please accept Princess Malavika as your royal bride." 

Thus, through a twisting path of destiny, disguises, and prophecies, King Agnimitra and Princess Malavika were united forever.
